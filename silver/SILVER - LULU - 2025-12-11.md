# LULU — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-12-11)

**The key idea:** This is a company narrating its own turnaround while its CEO exits mid-execution. International strength (China +46%) is subsidizing a US business that's still shrinking and losing share in performance apparel, and the entire investment thesis for 2026 rests on a product-refresh pipeline the outgoing CEO insists is unaffected by his departure — but that claim is untestable until spring, and by then a new CEO search will be resolving in parallel.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Leadership transition lands at the worst possible moment for credibility.** Calvin McDonald steps down January 31, right as the company pivots to a formalized 3-pillar action plan (product creation, activation, enterprise efficiency) it only unveiled last quarter — a co-CEO interim structure (Meghan Frank + Andre Maestrini, with Marti Morfitt as Executive Chair) will run the company through the CEO search.
2. **International is doing the heavy lifting.** China Mainland comps up 25% and revenue guided to "at or better than the high end" of 20-25% for the year, versus US revenue down 3% and comps down 5% — the global growth story is now almost entirely ex-US.
3. **Tariffs have become a genuine structural drag, not noise.** Q4 gross margin is guided down ~580bps, with 410bps of that specifically tariff/de-minimis related; the Canadian DC network built partly around de minimis-era assumptions is now "deep in the work on evaluating," per Frank.
4. **The product-refresh thesis has a hard deadline it hasn't met yet.** New style penetration is targeted at 35% for spring 2026 — the whole inflection story is deferred to a season that hasn't happened, while current shelves admit to carrying "styles that are not representative of our go-forward vision."
5. **Inventory discipline is the one concrete lever pulled today.** 2026 inventory units will run below sales growth — a bet that chase capability (6-8 week reorder) can substitute for building buffer stock, reducing markdown risk but raising stockout risk if the new product actually works.

## Economy & consumer
- **Guests are trading down under promotional pressure.** McDonald: "they're definitely looking for ways in which they can save in value" — behavior he says has persisted "throughout the year" and continued into Q3.
- **Guest acquisition isn't the problem; guest activation is.** Total and retained guest counts are growing across all age demographics — the gap is frequency and spend among high-value guests.
- **Post-Thanksgiving momentum already faded.** Black Friday was the biggest e-commerce volume day ever, but Frank flagged "some pullback in demand post Thanksgiving in terms of traffic," baked into Q4 guidance before the quarter even finished.
- **China's consumer behaves differently — less promotional dependence.** Lululemon shows up there "with the least amount of discounting" and still gains share across all city tiers, a structurally healthier demand backdrop than the US.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US comps down 5%, share lost in performance apparel.** McDonald conceded the company "lost some slight share in the performance apparel as we see guest behavior and trading down," while holding share in premium athletic.
- **Gross margin missed its own bar for the wrong reason — but beat guidance anyway.** 290bps of Q3 compression came from tariffs and higher markdowns (+90bps), though the miss was smaller than the 410bps originally guided, thanks to top-line leverage and "prudent management of the fixed expenses."
- **Lounge is the specific wound.** Scuba and lounge franchises "have been our core franchises where we've seen the greatest headwind" domestically — though the same franchise "activated" well in China, suggesting a US-specific fatigue rather than a global product problem.
- **Store channel comps are effectively flat while digital carries growth** — digital revenue +13%, now 42% of total revenue, versus flat total store sales despite 12% more square footage.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Speed-to-market is the single biggest structural investment.** Cutting mainline development from 18-24 months to 12-14 months, plus building 6-8 week "chase" reorder capability — a direct admission that the existing product engine was too slow to react to guest signal.
- **Store curation is being tested, not yet rolled out.** De-assortment pilots in LA and Miami are showing "very good results" per McDonald but remain small-scale tests ahead of a broader rollout.
- **The scarce resource is design relevance, not capital.** With $1B cash and no debt, capital isn't the constraint — the company explicitly frames its problem as failing to "inspire our high-value guests to purchase as we had in the past."
- **DC network flexibility is now a live open question**, not a settled multiyear plan — the Canada distribution center project, built under different trade-policy assumptions, is under active reevaluation because of de minimis removal.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** 2026 is the inflection year, with new-style penetration hitting 35% and "newness" driving outsized US growth as it already has in early tests. **Action:** concrete moves so far are limited to speed-lane infrastructure, two city store pilots, a website redesign, and incremental price actions on a small share of the assortment — the flagship product overhaul itself won't be visible until spring 2026, meaning the "action" investors can see today is almost entirely process/infrastructure, not yet product.
- **Belief:** tariff mitigation is achievable through vendor negotiation, DC efficiency, and enterprise savings. **Action:** Frank would not reconfirm or update the $320M gross 2026 tariff estimate given last quarter, only that "the team continues to work" on it — the belief in mitigation is stated with more confidence than the numbers behind it.
- **Belief:** the CEO transition won't alter product strategy given decisions are largely locked for H1 2026 ("the teams are just completing the winter buy for '26"). **Action:** the company still hasn't named a permanent CEO and is running commercial/product reporting lines through a co-CEO structure — a stated belief in continuity backed by an organizational design built for uncertainty.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 operating margin guidance** — Frank repeatedly said only that "the negative factors would outweigh the positives," deferring specifics to the March call.
- **No updated tariff dollar figure for 2026** despite offering one last quarter, and no confirmation the $320M estimate still holds.
- **No quantification of the Amex Platinum partnership's revenue or margin impact** — described only as "a relatively small part" of the business with "pleased" profitability, credits noted as a revenue reduction.
- **No commitment on further pricing action**, framed as "no imminent plans," decided "item-by-item" going forward.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered heavily on **succession mechanics** — who product, merchandising, and design report to now, whether the leadership change delays the 2026 refresh, and what the Board is screening for in a permanent CEO.
- A second cluster probed **tariff/de minimis mechanics and DC network implications**, pressing for updated dollar figures Frank declined to give.
- A third cluster pushed on **trade-down behavior and pricing power** — whether elasticity data supports further price increases, which management answered by reaffirming past actions performed in line with expectations but declining to signal more.
- Every margin-related question got the same deflection: specifics arrive in March. The compressed worry underneath all three threads: **is there an actual operator steering the 2026 product bet, or is the company asking the market to trust a plan through a leadership vacuum it created itself?**
