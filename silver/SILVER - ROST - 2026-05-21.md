# ROST — fiscal 2027-Q1 call (2026-05-21)

**The key idea:** Ross delivered the strongest same-store comp in its 40-year history (17%) and framed it not as a sugar high from tax refunds and pent-up spring demand (though both helped), but as early proof of a deliberate transformation — modernized marketing, brand reintroduction, store labor reallocation — that is structurally expanding its customer base, especially among the young and previously-hard-to-reach shopper. The tension: can a company built on risk-averse "chase inventory" discipline sustain double-digit customer growth into tougher compares without giving any of it back, and is management's refusal to detail second-half comp assumptions ("at the risk of laying out new second half guidance") confidence or caution in disguise.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **This was a transactions story, not a pricing or basket story.** Comp growth was "primarily driven by an increase in the number of transactions," units per transaction were flat, and the average-basket contribution was "a significantly lower proportion of the sales growth" — the growth engine is pure new-customer acquisition and increased shopping frequency, not upselling.
2. **Off-price is positioning consumer stress as opportunity, not risk.** CEO Conroy: "the silver lining for off price is that any uncertainty in the macro environment could lead to customers seeking more value when shopping and create closeout opportunities for us from the supply side" — Ross explicitly wants a softer macro because it feeds inventory supply and drives trade-down traffic.
3. **The company is quietly repricing its market position.** Conroy: "the market is starting to see the transformation of Ross going from a very good company, and accelerating from there, and it's getting noticed... when someone has a good deal or more closeouts, we're getting calls" — vendors and even competitors' cancellations are increasingly flowing to Ross first, a supply-side network effect that's hard for peers to replicate quickly.
4. **Guidance implies a sharp, unexplained deceleration.** Full-year comp guide of 6-7% against a 17% Q1 print and 6-7% Q2 guide mathematically implies roughly 2-3% comps in the back half — management wouldn't defend or explain that gap beyond generic "tougher compares" language, leaving a wide unexplained band in the model.
5. **Store refresh program paused mid-rollout.** After refreshing half the chain (new signage, wayfinding, cosmetic repairs) and measuring a positive sales and customer-survey impact, management stopped rather than finish the other half — they want to evaluate a bigger prototype change before committing more capital, an unusual pause on a program that's already shown ROI.

## Economy & consumer
- **Tax refunds and calendar shift inflated the base, but management insists the underlying business was "extremely healthy" independent of those effects.**
- **Customer count grew double-digit across every income level, ethnicity, age group, and geography** — including a standout in the 18-24 cohort, which Conroy called part of the deliberate brand strategy taking root.
- **No observed correlation between rising fuel/gas prices and sales, including in California specifically** — "California performed in line with the chain during the quarter," despite higher regional gas prices.
- **Management explicitly frames macro softness as good for Ross**, both via trade-down traffic and increased closeout supply from vendors and rival retailers under pressure.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Every major merchandise category comped in the teens or higher**, broad-based across geography, with ladies, cosmetics, and juniors outperforming; the Midwest was the strongest region.
- **Cosmetics strength tied to specific new/hot brand wins and a Korean beauty consumer trend**, not a space-allocation change — sales productivity per square foot rose without more shelf space.
- **Operating margin expanded 120bps to 13.4%,** merchandise margin +85bps, occupancy leverage +60bps, distribution and freight both improved — but SG&A de-levered 25bps purely on higher incentive compensation tied to the outperformance, and buying costs also rose 25bps for the same reason.
- **Packaway inventory ratio fell to 36% of total inventory from 41%,** with consolidated inventory up 12% — a shift toward more current, faster-turning goods rather than reserve stock.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Store growth holds at 5% unit pace (~110 new stores) with CapEx up to ~$1.1B from $819M last year,** and management said it "wouldn't hesitate" to raise the unit target further if a bankruptcy-driven real estate opportunity emerged.
- **New York/Northeast expansion (12 stores exited 2025) is "far exceeded" underwriting pro forma expectations,** with more Northeast rollout planned for 2027; only ~20% of current new-store growth is in newer markets, so the core footprint still dominates.
- **The binding constraint is supply of closeout goods, and it's currently loose, not tight** — "the availability of closeouts in the marketplace is still outstanding," aided by vendor relationships and rising deal flow from cancellations across mainstream and off-price retail.
- **Store refresh program paused after completing half the chain** despite proven sales lift, to reassess format before further capital commitment — a scarcity-of-conviction pause, not a scarcity-of-capital one.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Conroy frames the growth as durable and early-stage — "we just have gotten started on many of these initiatives" — driven by a repeatable flywheel of marketing, merchandising, and store execution that compounds over time.
- **Action:** Full-year guidance raise (comp to 6-7%, EPS to $7.50-$7.74) and continued 5% unit growth and $1.275B buyback back this up with real commitments — but the store-refresh pause and refusal to give explicit H2 comp assumptions show the company hedging its own optimism operationally even while touting it verbally.
- **Gap:** Management talks a "growth orientation" and cultural shift toward "offense" but structurally guides to a comp deceleration from 17% to an implied 2-3% in the back half — the qualitative confidence and quantitative guidance are in tension, and management declined to reconcile the two beyond citing tougher compares.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No explicit second-half comp number was given** — Conroy said giving new guidance "at the risk of" doing so, then declined, leaving only the implied 2-3% embedded in full-year math.
- **Declined to quantify future marketing spend increases** despite repeated analyst prompts, saying only that "over time we will" possibly spend more, with "nothing that will move the needle in a material way" this year.
- **Tariff refund claims were explicitly excluded from all guidance** given "ongoing uncertainties related to the timing and ultimate amount of the reimbursement" — a real potential earnings tailwind management chose not to bank.
- **Fuel/freight cost impact on H2 was described only qualitatively** ("some pressure"), with no oil-price assumption disclosed despite a direct analyst request to quantify it.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Durability of the comp was the dominant theme** — multiple analysts pressed on whether the 17% (and 9% trailing) comp versus a historical ~4% pre-pandemic norm is sustainable or must mean-revert, essentially asking what has to be "given back."
- **Inventory/chase capacity** was scrutinized directly — whether an "unprecedented" comp was fed by an unsustainable inventory chase that can't be repeated at 6-7% comp levels; management pushed back firmly on any resource constraint.
- **Cost structure questions clustered around SG&A leverage/de-leverage mechanics, freight and fuel cost trajectory, and flow-through math**, reflecting analyst focus on whether the margin expansion is repeatable or was a one-quarter incentive-comp/opex anomaly.
- **New-store productivity beating the recently-raised 70-75% benchmark** drew follow-up on whether the underwriting model itself is now understated.
- **The compressed worry: can Ross convert a 17% comp anomaly into a structurally higher run-rate without either giving back share once compares normalize or the company itself admitting it doesn't know where the ceiling is.**
