# MU — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-06-24)

**The key idea:** This call is Micron declaring a business-model pivot: instead of the historic boom-bust commodity memory cycle, they're locking roughly half of future revenue into non-cancelable, take-or-pay Strategic Customer Agreements, betting that HBM/DRAM scarcity is structural (node migration yielding less, HBM trade ratios eating more wafers, greenfield fabs taking years to contribute bits) rather than cyclical. The tension: they're raising CapEx aggressively even while insisting supply won't catch demand through 2028 — either genuine conviction that AI-driven memory demand is durable, or a management team taking out insurance against ever having over-invested at the top of a cycle.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **SCAs are the new central mechanism, and they're deliberately punitive to customers.** These are 5-year (ex-auto), non-cancelable, take-or-pay contracts with price floors/ceilings and $18B in cash deposits already collected across 16 signed deals. Sadana: "There is no provision in this agreement to enable a customer... to walk away." Micron is using its scarcity leverage to convert what used to be spot/contract commodity sales into locked annuities.
2. **HBM TAM guidance pulled forward a full year** — from crossing $100B "in 2028" to now "easily crossing $100B in 2027" — the single most concrete forward datapoint on the call, and a meaningful raise.
3. **Bit growth is now supply-gated, not demand-gated, industry-wide.** Sadana explicitly abandoned providing CAGR forecasts because "the shipment growth for bits is not really determined by demand anymore... it's actually more determined by the supply." That's a structural claim worth stress-testing against every other memory/compute name.
4. **Capital return signaling was aggressive but qualitatively hedged.** With ~$30B+ quarterly free cash flow implied, Murphy committed to "100% back to shareholders" primarily via buyback starting after the December 9 CHIPS anniversary, but gave no rate/pace, leaving the buyback math (an analyst floated ~10% of the company annually) unconfirmed.
5. **Greenfield fabs bring their own cost drag before they bring bits.** Startup costs step up in FQ4 and through 2027 (~$100-200M/quarter effect), and Bhatia flagged that higher HBM trade ratios plus greenfield's lack of leverage vs. brownfield expansion will structurally raise DRAM bit costs near-term — a cost headwind layered on top of a supply-shortage tailwind.

## Economy & consumer
No consumer-facing commentary — Micron's "economy" read is entirely about enterprise/hyperscaler AI capex cycles and industrial end-markets (automotive, aerospace/defense, medical), not the household consumer. Notably absent: any read-through on PC/smartphone demand, historically Micron's bread-and-butter cyclical indicator — an omission that itself signals where management's attention has shifted.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record cash generation, two quarters running,** driven by "record cash flow numbers" from combined technology/manufacturing execution — Murphy: "the last two quarters, we've generated as much as the company's history."
- **Enterprise SSD is a genuine bright spot**: a $5B quarter within $25B total data center revenue, with Micron claiming "record share after record share of data center SSDs" — validates the NAND side of the portfolio, not just HBM/DRAM.
- **Diversification outside data center still matters**: AEBU (automotive/embedded) and MCBU (mobile/client) together are "almost 40% of our company revenue" — a hedge against pure AI-cycle concentration that management proactively highlighted.
- **What's not working / cost pressure ahead**: startup costs from Idaho One, Tongluo, and Idaho Two will bite starting FQ4 and through 2027, and higher HBM trade ratios plus greenfield's inherent inefficiency will raise DRAM bit costs — an acknowledged near-term margin headwind layered onto an otherwise triumphant supply story.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx raised to ~$27B for FY2026**, with FY2027 spend confirmed to exceed the "low-to-mid 40%" growth rate implied by prior commentary — "we will be spending above that level as we look at it today." More than half of the FY2027 increase is pure construction, meaning it won't produce bits within the year.
- **DRAM (HBM and non-HBM) is the binding constraint, ahead of NAND.** Sadana: "DRAM is extremely constrained... HBM is very constrained. All the segments are seeing those challenges." Customer asks for HBM volume through 2027 and even 2028 are "far in excess of our ability to support."
- **New capacity doesn't intercept demand even by 2028.** Bhatia: greenfield capacity "really starts to contribute to bits in calendar 2028," and "even with that supply improvement, we don't see... an intercept for supply with demand."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** tightness persists "beyond 2027," HBM TAM crosses $100B a year earlier than prior guide, and supply structurally can't catch demand through 2028.
- **Action:** CapEx raised and explicitly guided above the trajectory analysts had modeled, three greenfield fab sites accelerated, and roughly half of future revenue being locked into 5-year non-cancelable contracts.
- **The gap:** if management truly believed supply would remain that far behind demand indefinitely, the more aggressive CapEx path would be the obvious call — yet Murphy refused to confirm even a 55-60% CapEx growth number when an analyst floated it directly, despite the underlying scarcity thesis implying it might be warranted. That's either capital discipline or a hedge against being wrong about how long the shortage lasts.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No specific FY2027 CapEx figure** despite direct, repeated analyst pressure — Murphy: "No. We're not going to give a CapEx number," even after confirming spend would exceed the analyst's mid-40% estimate.
- **No blended customer fulfillment-rate number.** Sadana refused to give a single percentage (70%? 90%?) for how much of customer-forecast demand Micron expects to satisfy through 2028, instead describing a segment-by-segment approach that avoids a quotable, trackable metric.
- **No forward bit-demand CAGR guidance** — abandoned entirely on the grounds that supply, not demand, now governs shipment growth, conveniently removing a number management would otherwise be held to.

## The street — what analysts asked
Questions clustered almost entirely around two anxieties: (1) whether the SCA contract structure is as ironclad and durable as management claims — cancelability, deposit treatment, pricing bands, and what happens if a customer actually can't/won't buy — and (2) whether the capital-return and CapEx math implies discipline or a peak-of-cycle land grab (the $1.2T buyback math, the "above mid-40%" CapEx dodge). One question probed China competition (CXMT/YMTC) and got a confident dismissal — most of their output stays domestic, no share pressure seen outside China. The compressed worry: **is this "unbreakable contract" architecture genuine structural pricing power, or a very sophisticated way to lock in today's scarcity premium right before supply catches up?**
