# PCAR — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-27)

**The key idea:** PACCAR closed out a soft-freight, tariff-disrupted 2025 with record Parts and Financial Services results propping up a truck segment that took a real (but undisclosed) margin hit from scrambling to relocalize manufacturing ahead of Section 232 tariffs. The call is a bet that 2026 becomes a "year of accelerated growth" as tariff and EPA'27 emissions clarity — both now resolved — unlock pent-up freight and pre-buy demand, with PACCAR positioned to convert a former tariff *disadvantage* into a margin and share *advantage* as competitors lag on pass-through.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Tariff policy flipped from headwind to tailwind, but the transition cost real money nobody will quantify.** Q4 truck margins fell to 12% (from a 12.5-13% Q1 guide) largely because factories were rushed into "local-for-local" builds — Chillicothe, Denton, and Sainte-Thérèse absorbing new model mixes with overtime and inefficiency. Feight admitted it was "significant" but refused a number: "there is a lot of gray in that number, so I would be taking a number that has multiple inputs to it."
2. **PACCAR believes it now has a structural cost edge over rivals building outside the USMCA footprint.** Competitors haven't yet passed Section 232 costs to market, keeping Q1 "in a bit of a very competitive state," but management expects to "gain in terms of margin and market share" as the year progresses and rivals catch up on pricing.
3. **EPA'27 (35mg NOx limit, effective January) is being sold to customers as a ~$10,000/truck cost increase**, with management deliberately vague because "useful life and warranty" impacts are still "subject to change" — a live regulatory risk still embedded in the 2026 guide.
4. **Parts and Financial Services are quietly becoming the stabilizer of the business** — both posted record quarterly and annual revenue in a "soft parts market," with Parts margin resilient at 29.5% even as customers shifted spend toward required maintenance over discretionary/proprietary parts.
5. **Order books are already tight**: Q1 is "mostly full" on strong December-January order intake from vocational buyers, bodybuilders replenishing inventory, and steady LTL — but fleets are spreading deliveries across the year, so the order surge won't show up in build volume until later quarters.

## Economy & consumer
- **Freight is inflecting from a trough, not booming.** "The truckload segment is beginning to accelerate, with industry customer demand and spot rates picking up in December" — vocational and LTL were merely "steady" through the downturn.
- **Driver supply, not just freight volume, may be repricing the market.** Asked about CDL/non-domicile enforcement reducing low-cost drivers, Feight agreed established carriers are gaining "better rate positioning," which should eventually translate into fleet cash flow and truck orders.
- **Used truck values are troughing and expected to rise** — up 4% year over year already — partly *because* EPA'27 will make new trucks meaningfully more expensive, a classic pre-buy/residual-value dynamic.
- No traditional end-consumer segment; PACCAR's "customer" is the trucking fleet, addressed under business and street themes instead.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Parts and Financial Services are structurally strengthening the mix**: Parts revenue hit a record $6.9B (+3% YoY) with $1.67B pretax profit; Financial Services hit record $2.2B revenue with pretax income up 11% and market share up two points to 27%.
- **Truck segment execution was messy in Q4**: margins slipped to 12% on Section 232 transition costs and elevated overtime — Poplawski noted overtime "should not be recurring in the first quarter."
- **Inventory discipline looks intentional, not accidental**: PACCAR sits at 2.2 months of Class 8 inventory versus an industry average of 3.2 months, giving room to flex into 2026 demand without an overhang.
- **DAF's European product cycle is paying off**: DAF XF/XD won International Truck of the Year (third time in five years) and Fleet Truck of the Year in the UK, with European heavy-duty share at 13.5%, up alongside a raised 2026 European market forecast.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex and R&D guidance is essentially flat** ($725-775M capex, $450-500M R&D vs. $728M/$446M in 2025) — funding next-gen diesel/hybrid/battery powertrains, the autonomous vehicle platform, ADAS, and "agentic AI" tools that Kevin Baney credited with improving parts mix identification during the soft market.
- **The binding constraint is supplier capacity, not PACCAR's own factories.** Feight: suppliers are aware of PACCAR's sequential-acceleration forecast, but "if we get into a third, fourth quarter where build is significantly higher, then it puts stress on their systems... we do not rule out that that could happen in the second half of the year." That's the tell for where price could firm later in 2026.
- **Material costs dominate the cost structure** — Poplawski put raw materials/components at "80, 85%" of cost of goods sold, meaning tariff and commodity swings flow through almost directly to margin.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes**: 2026 will be a year of sequential, accelerating build and margin improvement, tariff clarity converts to share gains, and EPA'27 drives a second-half pre-buy.
- **Is doing**: Held capex/R&D roughly flat rather than surging investment to front-run the believed acceleration; kept inventory lean rather than building ahead of the pre-buy; dropped 2026 tariff surcharges now that Section 232 costs are embedded rather than passing through further price increases.
- **The gap**: management talks confidently about an accelerating, tariff-advantaged 2026 but is hedging its own balance sheet — flat capex, lean inventory, no guidance on how big a second-half pre-buy build could get ("a lot of speculation that we cannot really get to yet"). The conviction is in the rhetoric more than in the commitments.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Would not quantify the Q4 local-for-local margin drag**, despite three separate analyst attempts, calling any number too dependent on "multiple inputs."
- **Would not project Q4 2026 dealer inventory levels or confirm/deny an "overhang" risk**, saying it's "a little early to predict."
- **Would not give a numeric read on second-half build ramp or supplier bottleneck risk** beyond acknowledging it "could happen."
- **Declined to break out monthly margin cadence** within the quarter, only confirming directionally that margins improved sequentially.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: (1) how much of the Q4-to-Q1 margin bridge is real cost improvement versus one-time noise, (2) whether PACCAR's tariff-driven cost advantage over Freightliner/International will actually convert into market share before competitors reprice, and (3) how much of the strong order intake is genuine freight recovery versus an EPA'27 pre-buy pulling forward demand.
- Multiple analysts tried and failed to get a dollar figure on the local-for-local transition cost and on Q4-end dealer inventory positioning.
- One analyst raised a structural question — whether a *driver-supply-constrained* cycle (CDL enforcement) behaves differently than a demand-led one — that management engaged with substantively rather than deflecting, a notable exception to the pattern of hedged answers.
- **Compressed worry**: is PACCAR's 2026 acceleration story real freight-cycle recovery, or mostly regulatory pull-forward and a temporary tariff-timing advantage that fades once rivals catch up on pricing?
