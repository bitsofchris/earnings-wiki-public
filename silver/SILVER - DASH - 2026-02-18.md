# DASH — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-18)

**The key idea:** This is a "trust the reinvestment" call — DoorDash just posted a record year and is now asking the market to absorb a year of visibly elevated, self-described "not ideal" spend (three parallel tech stacks, AV fleets, fulfillment warehouses) as the price of becoming the "operating system for local commerce." Management is confident and specific about 2026 economics but conspicuously vague on 2027, leaving the central tension unresolved: is this a one-time platform reset or the start of a permanently heavier cost structure.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Deliveroo is outperforming on the metric that matters least to skeptics and most to Tony.** CEO Tony Xu says Deliveroo is "growing much faster at the same profit contribution that we expected before the acquisition" — i.e., they bought growth without paying for it in margin, at least so far, five months in.
2. **The tech-stack unification is being paid for now, explicitly framed as redundant spend.** CFO Ravi Inukonda concedes the current three-platform setup means shipping "that 3x," and says redundant running costs are "majority... in '26, some of that will be in '27, which will come off" — a real, quantified admission of self-inflicted inefficiency, not market pressure.
3. **New verticals (grocery/retail) is being walked to profitability on a promise, not a lever.** Inukonda repeatedly insists there's "no one thing, which is a step function change," just accumulated basis points — a soft, hard-to-verify claim ahead of the promised H2 2026 gross-profit-positive milestone.
4. **Tony's agentic-commerce answer is a deliberate non-panic stance built on history, not data.** He compares AI chat/agent interfaces to Google Food Ordering and Amazon-vs-search-engines precedent, arguing DoorDash wins because it owns "the end-to-end job" (damaged orders, substitutions, late drivers) that a pure discovery layer can't solve — a well-rehearsed answer to a question every retail/marketplace company is now getting.
5. **Coding-agent productivity claim is a striking aside, not the focus.** Tony volunteers "90-plus percent daily active usage" of AI coding agents across engineers, calling the pace of change in coding tools "astonishing and almost breathtaking" — relevant color for anyone tracking enterprise AI-tool adoption curves, DoorDash included.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro headwinds cited anywhere on the call.** Every soft spot (Q1 EBITDA dip, Dasher costs) is attributed to seasonality or the winter storms (~$20M hit), not consumer pullback.
- **Engagement metrics are all at highs:** "we hit an all-time high in terms of MAUs and order frequency continues to be quite strong," with DashPass posting "a record year as well as a record quarter."
- **Category expansion is the real consumer story:** ~30% of US MAUs now order outside restaurants, and Xu frames the explicit long-run goal as getting "that 30% to be 100% over time."
- **Grocery basket behavior is maturing into a second use case** — customers now use DoorDash for both "middle of the week runs" and larger weekend "stock-up" baskets, with share of wallet rising cohort over cohort.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US restaurants: strong and still improving, but decelerating margin pace.** Contribution margin was up y/y in Q4 and 2025 grew faster than 2024 at larger scale, but Inukonda flags margin improvement "will be at a lower pace compared to prior years," partly because DashPass economics favor volume/gross-profit dollars over margin rate.
- **New verticals: real but unprofitable, on a stated timeline to fix that.** Grocery/retail is "the fastest growing in the U.S." among third-party peers, and management expects the entire retail and grocery business to be "unit economic positive in the second half of the year."
- **International: Deliveroo/Wolt gaining share while integration work compounds.** "We're gaining share in its largest markets" on both Deliveroo and Wolt; cross-market feature porting (US → Wolt → Deliveroo) is already showing "immediate impact," per Tony.
- **Ads/Symbiosis is a genuine bright spot understated in the framing.** Symbiosis "doubled the number of advertisers... as well as tripled the spend" — a strong result Tony almost tacks on as an afterthought rather than leading with.
- **OpEx discipline holds at ~2% of GOV target for 2026**, per Inukonda, despite the ROO-driven bump to 211bps in Q4.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Three declared investment buckets for 2026, unchanged in size from the prior call:** global tech-stack replatforming, autonomous delivery (Dot + drones), and merchant/fulfillment services (DashMart Fulfillment Services with Kroger and CVS named as partners).
- **The binding constraint is self-inflicted technical/organizational debt, not external scarcity** — running "3 tech platforms, pretty much a very similar business" is explicitly called "not ideal" and blamed for shipping features "3x."
- **Dasher pay models are being restructured** to reflect longer, more complex grocery/retail trips as more volume shifts into non-restaurant categories — a direct labor-market/incentive investment, not just software.
- **AV strategy is fleet-agnostic and hybrid, not a bet on full automation:** "there will be a collection of different vehicles... some of which we'll build inside our 4 walls and others of which we will partner," with Dashers, AVs, and drones handling different delivery types by design, including explicit suburban/rural use cases beyond the robotaxi dense-city model.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Tony argues owning the "end-to-end job" (damage, substitutions, lateness, support) insulates DoorDash from agentic-AI disintermediation, treating chat/agent platforms as new top-of-funnel "channel partners" like Google/Facebook before them.
- **Action gap:** there's no described product investment to actually integrate with or defend against agentic commerce flows beyond this framing — it's a thesis, not a roadmap item, unlike the AV and tech-stack investments which come with dollars and headcount attached.
- **Belief:** management expects grocery/retail to eventually rival restaurants in scale and profitability if selection and quality keep improving.
- **Action:** DashMart Fulfillment Services (forward-deployed inventory, Kroger/CVS partnerships) is the concrete mechanism — a real capital commitment behind the belief, not just a stated hope.
- **Belief:** tech-stack unification will materially lift feature velocity and margins starting in 2027.
- **Action:** the redundant spend is happening now, in 2026, with benefits described so far only anecdotally ("features from one market... working into a different market") rather than quantified.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2027 investment or incremental-margin framework offered**, despite being asked twice; Inukonda repeats "we're not guiding the business towards incremental margins" and Tony's replatforming-timing answer stays qualitative ("majority... completed this year," no hard date).
- **No commitment on AVs displacing Dasher labor** — Tony insists on a hybrid, orchestration-first model where "it's not going to be immediately or at least not immediately obvious... that autonomous vehicles are going to perform every single type of delivery."
- **No specific competitive response detailed against Amazon in grocery/perishables** beyond asserting no growth impact — a confident denial without supporting data disclosed on the call.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Investment intensity and its 2027 tail dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pushed on whether elevated 2026 spend (tech stack, ROO, AVs) is truly one-time or will "bleed into next year," and Inukonda's answers stayed consistently non-committal on 2027 specifics.
- **Competitive positioning came up from multiple angles** — Europe intensity, Amazon in grocery, agentic AI disintermediation — with management giving uniformly confident, no-material-impact answers in each case.
- **Deliveroo integration and replatforming timing were treated as linked stories**, with analysts probing for early wins (SevenRooms venue-add speed, Deliveroo order acceleration) as proxies for whether the broader platform bet will pay off.
- **Margin cadence questions (Q1 dip, Dasher costs, OpEx as % of GOV) sought reassurance that seasonal/one-time items explain near-term softness** rather than a structural cost creep — management attributed all of it to seasonality, weather, and front-loaded ROO investment.
- Compressed worry: **is 2026's "setup year" spend a bridge to higher margins, or the first year of a permanently heavier cost base that never fully comes off.**
