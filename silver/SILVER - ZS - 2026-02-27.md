# ZS — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-02-27)

**The key idea:** Zscaler is repositioning itself from "Zero Trust for users" to "the security layer for the agentic AI era" — betting that as AI agents proliferate and talk to each other, all that machine-to-machine traffic will need to route through its Zero Trust Exchange the same way user traffic does today. The quarter's headline numbers (25% ARR growth, record deal counts) are real, but a meaningful slice of the raised full-year guide comes from Red Canary, an acquisition management itself flagged as carrying "elevated" churn — a tension analysts spent much of the call trying to untangle.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI security is shifting from narrative to bookings, but still small.** New AI Protect logo wins (an 8-figure Fortune 500 semiconductor deal, a 7-figure construction-company upsell) are real, and MCP request volume went from "literally nothing a couple of quarters ago" to millions monthly — but this remains early-innings relative to the $3.4B ARR base.
2. **Non-seat-based/metered pricing is becoming the growth engine.** Metered usage (Branch, Cloud, AI, token-based) hit just over 25% of new ACV with ARR up over 100% YoY — a structural shift away from per-user pricing that CFO Kevin Rubin called out as material enough to change "the growth algorithm."
3. **Zero Trust Everywhere adoption is compounding fast but concentrated.** Enterprises buying all three pillars (Users, Branch, Cloud) grew from 130 to over 550 in a year, with a claimed 2x-3x ARR lift on conversion — yet this is self-reported anecdote, not a disclosed cohort metric.
4. **Red Canary is the quarter's real asterisk.** Its ARR guide jumped from $95M to $130M for the year (nearly 30%), but management admits "churn for MDR businesses is higher than we experienced in our Zscaler business" and post-acquisition churn "has been elevated" — a red flag partially obscured by the guidance raise headline.
5. **Component cost inflation is on the radar, not yet in the model.** Kevin Rubin proactively flagged rising memory, storage, and processor prices as a factor that "could become a factor in the future" for data centers and branch appliances — an unprompted, forward-looking disclosure worth tracking next quarter.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro headwinds cited.** The call contains no discussion of enterprise IT budget tightening, layoffs, or demand softness — the only forward-looking cost risk raised is component/hardware inflation (memory, storage, processors) for Zscaler's own infrastructure and branch appliances, not customer demand.
- **Consumer key omitted** — Zscaler sells to enterprise IT/security buyers, not end consumers.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Core ZIA/ZPA (Zero Trust for users) is stable, not exciting.** Growth held "mid-teens" in ARR — the CFO added the extra disclosure this quarter specifically because analysts wanted more visibility into the foundational business obscured by faster-growing new pillars.
- **Record execution metrics.** Record Q2 pipeline conversion, record $1M+ new ACV deals, and the Americas closed twice as many $1M+ deals as a year ago — genuine go-to-market strength, not just M&A-driven optics.
- **Red Canary churn is the clear soft spot.** Rubin's own words: "churn for MDR businesses is higher than we experienced in our Zscaler business... post acquisition, Red Canary's churn has been elevated" — a rare instance of a company flagging integration trouble on its own earnings call rather than burying it.
- **Free cash flow margin compressed** to 20.7% from 22.1% a year ago, attributed to "timing of cash collections" — a soft explanation for a real number.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Two acquisitions this quarter:** SquareX (Feb 5, browser-extension-based Zero Trust for unmanaged devices, avoiding a "full-blown third-party browser" Chaudhry says the company evaluated and rejected years earlier) and continued Red Canary integration ("next phase" as of Feb 1).
- **CapEx stayed light** at $18M, just 2% of revenue — Zscaler's model remains capital-efficient even as it expands AI security infrastructure, unlike compute-heavy AI infrastructure plays.
- **The binding constraint is category creation, not resources.** With only 4,400 of 20,000+ target enterprises as customers and over half of the Fortune 500 still unpenetrated, the gating factor is land-grab execution and trust-building at the C-suite level, not capital or engineering capacity.
- **Hardware inflation flagged but not yet priced in** — Rubin: "we haven't seen a meaningful impact to our operations. However, it could become a factor in the future... We'll continue to monitor our costs and adjust customer pricing if needed."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** AI agents will generate "billions" of autonomous, machine-to-machine interactions, and Chaudhry called this "probably the biggest upside for growth of Zscaler business."
- **Action:** The company has real product motion behind this — a newly launched, integrated AI Protect portfolio, Red Canary folded into Agentic SecOps, an upcoming ZDX AI agent for IT ops — but no unit economics, pricing model, or revenue-recognition detail was given for how agent-to-agent traffic actually converts to dollars beyond "token-based" pricing in early, small deals.
- **Gap:** The scale of the stated opportunity ("billions of agents") far outpaces the disclosed evidence (millions of MCP requests/month, "starting small but growing pretty rapidly") — this is a thesis the company is executing against, not yet a proven revenue line.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No long-term monetization framework for AI-agent traffic**, despite it being called the single biggest growth driver — Chaudhry's answer to a direct question on monetizing agent traffic ("the more agents, the more agentic traffic, the more value we deliver") restated the thesis rather than quantifying it.
- **Red Canary's elevated churn was acknowledged but not quantified** — no churn rate, cohort detail, or timeline to stabilization was given, even under direct analyst pressure to reconcile the guidance raise against the flagged risk.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Organic vs. inorganic disentangling dominated the Q&A.** Multiple analysts (Deutsche Bank, Citi, RBC) pushed on how much of the raised full-year ARR guide was Red Canary versus organic strength, forcing Rubin to repeatedly restate the "9.5% organic net new ARR growth" figure separate from Red Canary's mechanical contribution.
- **Z-Flex's real substance was probed directly** — whether the flexible-commitment program was inflating RPO/booking optics versus reflecting genuine incremental demand; Rubin's answer conceded Z-Flex deals show up in ARR "no differently" than non-Z-Flex deals of similar structure, implicitly confirming there's no unique accounting benefit, just packaging.
- **AI monetization mechanics were asked twice** (KeyBanc, Mizuho) and got directional but non-quantitative answers both times.
- **Compressed worry:** is Zscaler's reported growth acceleration substantively organic, or is it increasingly a blend of M&A ARR injection (Red Canary) and deal-structuring innovation (Z-Flex) dressed up as platform momentum?
