# KDP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-23)

**The key idea:** This is a company mid-transformation telling investors to trust the phasing math: Q1 EPS fell and coffee profit cratered, but management insists it's all cost-timing lag that reverses hard in the back half, right as they're simultaneously closing the $18B+ JDE Peet's deal and standing up two separate C-suites for a 2027 split. The tension is real — strong beverage momentum is carrying the story while coffee is asked to take it on faith.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Two companies are already being built inside one.** Tim Cofer will run Beverage Co., Rafa Oliveira (ex-JDE Peet's CEO) will run Global Coffee Co., and a "transformation management office" is running parallel workstreams toward an early-2027 separation — this isn't exploratory, it's operational.
2. **Coffee's Q1 misses were pre-announced, but the back-half math is aggressive.** An analyst noted consensus implies ~1,000bps of coffee margin recovery and high-teens/20% back-half EPS growth off high-single-digit Q2 — management didn't push back on the framing, just reasserted "high degree of confidence" without new numbers.
3. **Refreshment Beverages' strength is partly a one-time comp.** CEO admitted Ghost distribution gains added "a couple of points" to Q1 growth on a lapping basis — real momentum is there (energy, CSD innovation, Electrolit) but the printed growth rate won't repeat.
4. **Hedging insulates 2026 from the Middle East commodity shock, not 2027.** Aluminum, resins, and diesel exposure is "largely hedged" for this year; beyond that, KDP would need "mitigating action plans" it hasn't detailed.
5. **Capital structure is deliberately complex and pre-positioned for separation.** The JDE Peet's deal used a $4.5B convertible preferred (BevCo) plus a $4B pod-manufacturing JV minority stake (Coffee) instead of straight debt — structuring value creation and leverage exposure separately by future entity before the split even happens.

## Economy & consumer
- **Affordability is the active battleground.** "Consumers seeking affordability in the current environment" prompted refined promotional strategies and pack-size work, even as net price realization kept offsetting inflation.
- **SNAP watched but not yet a drag.** "The SNAP impacts to date have been manageable and largely consistent with our expectations," with state-by-state monitoring built into RGM response.
- **Category volumes stayed positive despite price hikes** — CSD and broader LRB volume growth even after inflation-driven pricing, which management reads as validation of value delivered, not fragility.
- **Mexico beverage tax dented International volume** in the quarter, a policy-driven headwind distinct from consumer weakness — underlying demand was called "resilient."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US Refreshment Beverages is the clear engine:** double-digit net sales and operating income growth, CSD category "healthy" at mid-single-digit retail growth accelerating from Q4, energy share gains led by Bloom and Ghost as "two of the top three fastest-growing major trademarks."
- **US Coffee absorbed the pain on schedule:** net sales -2.3%, operating income -21.3%, driven by peak green coffee hedge costs, tariffs, and a 7% pod shipment decline from trade inventory drawdowns that "lagged point-of-sale trends."
- **International grew top line but profit fell:** net sales +8.5% constant currency on pricing, but operating income -15.1% on cost pressure, the Mexico beverage tax, and higher marketing — a "seasonally smaller profit quarter" bearing outsized cost impact.
- **C4 is the soft spot inside a strong energy portfolio:** management confirmed deliberate SKU rationalization with partner Nutrabolt (dropped the "Smart" sub-line, repositioned "Ultimate") — a controlled retreat, not organic weakness, but still the one energy brand not gaining share this quarter.

## Investing & scarcity
- **JDE Peet's closed April 1** with a structured financing mix ($4.5B BevCo convertible preferred, $4B coffee JV minority investment, ~$6B new senior debt) explicitly designed to route leverage to the right future entity.
- **Marketing spend rose across all three segments** in Q1, with coffee notably front-loading investment (Keurig Coffee Collective, "Keurig Anthem" campaign) *during* its worst margin quarter of the year — a deliberate choice to protect long-term brand position over near-term profit.
- **The binding constraint is commodity cost lag, not capacity or people:** green coffee costs take 6-9 months to flow through the P&L via hedges and inventory cycles, meaning Q1's cost pain was locked in well before the quarter started and 2026 pricing decisions are already largely determined by 2025 hedges.
- **Alta system (next-gen Keurig brewer) is being prepped for direct-to-consumer launch later this year**, positioned to eventually carry partner brands beyond Keurig/Peet's — a platform bet, not yet a shipping product.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** coffee margins inflect sharply upward in H2 as hedged costs roll off and green coffee prices (already pulling back) become a "tailwind going into 2027." **Action so far:** none of that improvement has shown up yet — Q1 was the trough by design, and the only concrete moves are cost-side (hedging discipline) and demand-side investment (innovation slate), not yet realized results.
- **Belief:** BevCo will have "additional strategic optionality" post-separation on route-to-market (testing beyond owned DSD) and portfolio moves (earlier-stage partnerships, new geographies). **Action:** nothing concrete committed yet — Suntory Europe was restructured to a capital-light concentrate model, the one tangible proof point of this philosophy pre-separation.
- **Belief:** deleveraging to 3.5-4x (BevCo) / 3.75-4.25x (Coffee Co.) targets by separation is achievable mainly through free cash flow. **Action:** actively "looking at" non-core asset and minority-investment divestitures to accelerate this, but declined to name any — optionality flagged, not exercised.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No specifics on which non-core assets or minority stakes are divestiture candidates**, only that "a number of things" are being evaluated to accelerate deleveraging.
- **No commitment on post-Middle-East-conflict commodity exposure beyond 2026** — hedged for this year, but longer-tail resin/aluminum inflation would only trigger unspecified "mitigating action plans."
