# APP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-06)

**The key idea:** AppLovin is on the cusp of ending 14 years as a closed platform, betting that a still-young consumer/e-commerce ad product (18 months old) can scale the way gaming did while gaming itself keeps accelerating rather than maturing. The tension analysts kept probing: is this expansion — consumer verticals, lead gen, Connected TV, social — disciplined sequencing of a proven playbook, or scope creep diluting the narrow focus that made them dominant in mobile gaming ads in the first place?

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Consumer vertical inflected sharply.** March grew roughly 25% over January and April became a record month exceeding any prior Q4 peak — in an ad business, beating Q4 in Q1 is unusual ("I don't know of another advertising business that can grow Q1 over Q4," Foroughi said), and management credits it to a single "quite substantial" model release rather than broad-based demand.
2. **Gaming shows no maturity slowdown.** Growth has stayed "way over" the long-stated 20-30% target every quarter since Axon 2.0 launched (12 straight quarters), with hybrid ad+IAP monetization from previously IAP-only studios cited as a multi-quarter tailwind still in early innings.
3. **June self-serve launch is the real catalyst, but sized cautiously.** Management projects $70K+ first-year spend per new customer and near-zero churn past 30 days live, implying a large TAM if onboarding scales — but explicitly frames 2026 upside from new verticals (lead gen, CTV) as not material this year, i.e. don't extrapolate the big numbers yet.
4. **AI creative tooling is becoming core infrastructure, not a feature.** Interactive page generator is live and widely adopted; video ad generation (using third-party models like Sora and Seedance) is "days away" from broad rollout and is explicitly not expected to compress margins since it's a pass-through cost, not owned compute.
5. **No cannibalization between gaming and consumer ad dollars**, per management — the single-auction model is said to expand the pie (previously wasted impressions get monetized) rather than reallocate it, though this is self-reported with no independent verification offered.

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro-insulated by design, per Foroughi.** "We're selling revenue and profit to advertisers... if they're buying profit, they're not gonna come cut the dollar" — the pitch is that performance-marketing ROI protects them from cyclical ad-spend cuts, though he admits limited visibility since most customers remain Western/self-serve is not yet global.
- **Small-business flywheel example reused from last quarter**, an Israeli cookware company scaling from $4M to a projected $80M in revenue, offered as the template management wants to replicate "thousands of times."
- **A Turkish hybrid-monetization game reached a nine-figure annual run rate in six months** and sold for roughly $1B about six months post-launch — cited as proof the hybrid category's growth potential is real, not theoretical.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Beat guidance again**: revenue $1.84B (+59% YoY, +11% sequential), adjusted EBITDA $1.56B (+66% YoY, 85% margin, ~400bps of YoY expansion). Free cash flow $1.29B, described as temporarily elevated by tax-payment timing and expected to normalize to ~75% of EBITDA for the full year.
- **Onboarding friction ("breakage") is still being worked, not solved.** Foroughi declined to quantify a target breakage rate and said the main fix — out-of-box video ad creation — is still "in the midst of resolving," rolling out over the coming weeks ahead of general release.
- **App Store fee cuts are not moving the needle, by their own account.** Asked directly if developer margin gains from bypassing App Store fees were flowing into more ad spend, Foroughi said flatly, "We don't notice it" — a notable dampener on a thesis some investors hold about platform-fee relief boosting ad budgets.
- **Video ad-generation quality is described as near-indistinguishable from human-made creative** and "exceptionally low cost" relative to human production — positioned as the unlock for long-tail consumer advertisers who currently lack video assets.

## Investing & scarcity
- **GPU capacity is being added continuously via Google Cloud (multi-cloud capable)**, but Foroughi is explicit that raw GPU count isn't the differentiator in this market — "different businesses use infrastructure for different purposes" — and that model/product quality plus proprietary advertiser data is the actual moat versus scale peers like Google or Facebook.
- **Video generation compute is a third-party pass-through cost, not internal infrastructure** — Sora 2's deprecation "didn't impact us at all" since they're model-agnostic across open- and closed-source options (explicitly namechecking Seedance). No margin impact expected; potential future monetization (credit caps, charging for overage) flagged as a possible revenue stream, not a cost risk.
- **$1B in buybacks this quarter, $2.3B authorization remaining, $2.76B cash on hand** — capital return pace unchanged, framed as "conviction in the value and durability of our business" alongside continued organic investment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Foroughi expects hybrid ad+IAP monetization to keep converging toward the ~$100B IAP market size over "the next five years." **Action:** courting IAP-only studios post-Gaming CEO Summit and pointing to a consumer-vertical roster (non-gaming advertisers) as the reason IAP developers no longer fear running competitive ads — real but early-stage groundwork, not a market-sizing commitment.
- **Belief:** management is "most excited" about agentic advertiser workflows — AI agents managing ad spend end-to-end. **Action:** building Axon to be natively agent-accessible alongside the June self-serve launch — a real infrastructure bet, though still described in future tense with no adoption metrics yet.
- **Belief:** Connected TV is called "the holy grail of advertising" and "massively under-monetized." **Action:** effectively paused in 2025-2026 — "we went all hands on deck" on consumer instead — with no timeline given and an explicit refusal to discuss it further until it's proven, a clear talk/action gap on a previously stated top priority.
- **Belief:** lead-generation categories (insurance, fintech, food delivery) represent a large untapped audience on their >1B DAU base. **Action:** a model is "in the midst of testing," compared to where the e-commerce/consumer vertical stood six quarters ago — real but nascent, explicitly not expected to matter financially in 2026.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to size the IAP-to-hybrid monetization mix** despite being asked twice with an offered "pick a number" framing — "it's really, really hard to do that, especially on the fly" — offering only qualitative color instead.
- **No Connected TV timeline or financial framework**, more than a year after naming it a top-five 2025 priority; Foroughi explicitly said they withhold detail "until we know that things are gonna work."
- **Ruled out brand advertising entirely** as a strategic boundary — "we're never gonna get into branding" — keeping the story strictly performance-marketing, which also caps the TV opportunity's near-term shape.
- **No hard commitment to social-media product or acquisition**, despite Foroughi's own podcast comments fueling speculation — framed instead as a low-cost talent-attraction and experimentation vehicle ("we can vibe code products") rather than a stated roadmap item.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Repeated attempts to get a hard number on the IAP-to-hybrid monetization shift — consistently declined in favor of qualitative framing.
- Sustainability of the model-improvement cadence and whether the next big uplift could come faster — answered with confidence but no specific mechanism beyond "the team is getting smarter."
- GPU/compute cost exposure as video generation scales — addressed by clarifying it's third-party spend, not owned infrastructure, so no margin drag expected.
- Connected TV's status against its prior billing as a top strategic priority — deflected with no new detail.
- Whether expanding across consumer verticals, lead gen, and CTV simultaneously repeats a focus mistake, given their edge came from single-vertical dominance in gaming — answered by asserting the initiatives are low-incremental-cost extensions of shared infrastructure rather than true diversification.
- The compressed worry: **can a company built on narrow, obsessive focus in one vertical scale a half-dozen adjacent bets at once without diluting the model-improvement flywheel that made gaming the moat in the first place?**
