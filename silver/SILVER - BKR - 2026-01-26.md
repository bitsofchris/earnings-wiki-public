# BKR — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-26)

**The key idea:** Baker Hughes is repositioning from an oilfield-services cyclical into an industrial power-infrastructure company riding the AI/data-center power buildout — record IET orders, record backlog, and a pending Chart acquisition are the vehicle — while the legacy OFSE (oilfield services) business grinds through a soft 2026 with an oil-price inflection pushed out to 2027. The tension: can power-systems and LNG growth outrun a genuinely weak oilfield cycle long enough for the "20% company EBITDA margin by 2028" target to land.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Power systems is now the headline growth story, not oilfield.** Orders more than doubled to $2.5B in 2025 with $1B directly tied to data centers, and management now guides to $3B of data center orders across 2025-2027 — "over 150% growth compared to last year's power systems orders." NovaLT turbine capacity is described as "effectively full through 2028" even after a planned doubling of capacity by 1H27.
2. **Oilfield services softness is real and multi-region, not a rounding error.** OFSE 2025 revenue fell 8% with the fourth quarter hit by "seasonal declines in the North Sea and Asia Pacific, continued softness in Mexico and weaker year-end product sales as customers remained cautious with capital deployment" — margins held only via aggressive cost discipline, not volume.
3. **The inflection they're waiting for is a full year further out than the growth story implies.** CEO Lorenzo Simonelli explicitly said a broad upstream activity inflection "is likely a 2027 catalyst," with 2026 global upstream spending still expected to decline low-single-digits and North America mid-single-digits.
4. **Chart is the linchpin of the whole power-systems pivot**, adding thermal-management and trigeneration capability; management is "increasingly confident" in the $325M cost-synergy target, and divestitures (PSI sale, SPC JV formation) generated ~$1.5B explicitly to shore up the balance sheet ahead of that close, expected Q2 2026 pending regulatory review.
5. **Venezuela is a real but entirely unquantified call option.** BKR has a large legacy installed base there (1,200+ oil production systems, historically $0.5B revenue in 2012) but management would commit to no dollar figure or timeline, citing safety and unresolved "legal and regulatory framework."

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro framed as resilient but not roaring**: "modestly stronger year-over-year GDP growth in 2026, supported by continued investment in generative AI, easing inflation and a supportive fiscal backdrop."
- **No traditional consumer exposure** — BKR's end markets are industrial operators, utilities, and data-center developers, not individual consumers; this key is effectively N/A for this call.
- **AI infrastructure spend cited as a macro tailwind**, not just a company story: "industry estimates suggest that AI infrastructure spending totaled more than $500 billion in 2025 and is expected to approach $1 trillion annually in the late 2020s."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **IET is firing on all cylinders**: record full-year orders ($14.9B), record backlog ($32.4B, book-to-bill >1x), and EBITDA margin up 160bps to a record 20% in Q4, "sixth consecutive year of IET RPO growth."
- **OFSE is managing decline, not growing through it**: revenue down 8% for the year, margins essentially flat (18.3%) purely via "cost discipline and structural actions to remove duplication," not demand recovery.
- **Free cash flow beat its own target range**: full-year conversion of 57% against a 45-50% target, driven by "enhanced working capital efficiency and higher customer down payments" — a genuinely strong, not manufactured, cash result.
- **Subsea stood out within the soft OFSE segment**: SSPS booked a near-record $1.1B quarter (book-to-bill 1.4x) and captured roughly 25% of the global subsea tree market in 2025.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is visibly rotating toward power systems and Chart integration**: three closed portfolio transactions (PSI sale, SPC joint venture, CDC acquisition) plus the pending Chart deal are described as the core of the "comprehensive review" of the portfolio.
- **NovaLT capacity is the binding constraint on the growth story**: CFO Ahmed Moghal said Nova slots are "effectively full through 2028" even after a planned capacity doubling — meaning near-term data-center order upside is capacity-gated, not demand-gated.
- **Balance-sheet capacity is explicitly being preserved for Chart**: net debt/EBITDA at 0.5x, liquidity of $6.7B, with a stated post-close target of 1-1.5x within 24 months — management is intentionally under-levering now to have room to absorb the deal.
- **New Energy (CCUS, geothermal, flex-fuel) is a real, growing sub-scarcity bet**: record $2B of orders in 2025, "well above our $1.4 billion to $1.6 billion target," with 2026 guide of $2.4-2.6B.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: global power demand doubles by 2040 (>4% CAGR) with data-center power demand growing ~12% CAGR through 2040, LNG demand up "at least 75% by 2040."
- **Action behind that belief**: doubling NovaLT capacity by 1H27, pursuing Chart to add thermal management, and setting a concrete $40B+ 3-year IET order target — these are funded, dated commitments, not just narrative.
- **Belief on oil/OFSE**: a cyclical inflection is coming but "likely a 2027 catalyst."
- **Action gap here is the tell**: no comparable capacity investment or order target is being made for OFSE in the meantime — 2026 guidance for OFSE is essentially "hold margin flat via cost cuts and wait," which is a materially different posture than the aggressive capacity build-out happening in power systems.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **The "comprehensive strategic evaluation"** was described only in process terms — "a disciplined, ongoing process" considering "a broad range of strategic options" — with no scope, timeline, or specific actions disclosed beyond the already-announced Chart deal and divestitures.
- **Venezuela upside was quantified historically (2012's $0.5B) but not prospectively** — management explicitly said they'll "be obviously programmatic as we go back" and are waiting on "clarity on legal and regulatory framework," declining to attach any near-term revenue number.
- **NovaLT capacity expansion beyond the already-announced doubling was left conditional**: "any decision to further expand Nova capacity... will be based on a disciplined assessment... guided by very clear return thresholds" — no commitment despite slots being sold out through 2028.
