# TTD — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-25)

**The key idea:** TTD delivered a decent but decelerating quarter (14% headline growth, 19% ex-political) while explicitly calling out CPG and auto — a quarter of its business — as a macro drag it says isn't structural. The real tension: is this a temporary two-vertical air pocket that a go-to-market reorg and new AI products (Kokai, Audience Unlimited, Deal Desk) will fix in 2026, or is it the leading edge of Amazon/Google closing the AI-scale gap that TTD's "objectivity moat" thesis depends on staying open?

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **CPG and auto are doing the damage, and management quantifies it.** Green states plainly: "we would have been at least 5% higher in growth rate" excluding those two verticals, which together are "roughly 1/4 of our business." That's a large, explicit number for a company that has never broken out vertical performance this specifically before — the disclosure itself signals how much it's weighing on the narrative.
2. **The "objectivity moat" argument is being pressure-tested by the market, not just competitors.** Green leans hard on Vinny Rinaldi's (Hershey's) "fallacy of cheap reach" quote and on named brand tests (an appliance maker's head-to-head vs. Amazon DSP: 70% more reach, 30% lower cost, 6x better performance) to argue TTD wins when data and decisioning matter more than owned inventory. That's real, but it's also survivorship-selected anecdote in a quarter where growth actually decelerated.
3. **2026 EBITDA margin is flat by design, not by drift.** Tahnil Davis is explicit that Q1's lighter EBITDA guide is "primarily a timing thing" tied to infrastructure investment (completing the shift to owned data centers, AI/ML buildout), with full-year margin held "approximately in line with 2025" — a deliberate reinvestment year, not margin erosion.
4. **Leadership transition adds uncertainty at a sensitive moment.** The CFO seat is filled on an interim basis by the Chief Accounting Officer, whose opening remarks emphasize "continuity" — a soft signal worth tracking given this is also the year TTD says it needs disciplined reinvestment to pay off.
5. **Amazon competitive threat is reframed, not dismissed.** Green's core argument — Amazon can't be an objective DSP because its money is made on O&O and it would create "channel conflict" — is a structural bet, but it dodges the more immediate question of whether Amazon's superior AI/compute scale erodes TTD's execution edge before that channel conflict ever bites.

## Economy & consumer
- **CPG and auto are absorbing macro pressure that other categories aren't.** Green: those companies "have tough choices ahead" from "tariff uncertainty and uneven volumes in addition to persistent inflationary pressures as more consumers deal with cost of living challenges" — and "those trends have continued into the beginning of this year."
- **Tech, travel, pharma, and communications had a strong 2025** even amid "a greater degree of macro uncertainty," per Green — a bifurcated advertiser economy rather than broad softness.
- **Brands are choosing budget cuts over layoffs.** Green: when uncertainty hits, "it's often the thing that gets paused, that they would rather adjust marketing budgets than lay people off" — ad spend as the shock absorber for corporate cost pressure, a signal about labor-market tightness underneath.
- **Record ad supply flipped the market to a buyer's advantage.** Green: "we estimate that more supply was added to the global market than in any year before," which he frames as validating TTD's non-owned-inventory model — more supply than demand structurally favors objective decisioning platforms over walled gardens.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Headline growth decelerated to 14% (19% ex-political)** in Q4, down from a stronger comp base, with full-year revenue at $2.9B (+18%). CTV and audio outgrew the overall business; audio grew faster than any other channel in Q4.
- **International keeps outpacing North America** — international was ~16% of Q4 revenue but "growth across our international business continues to outpace growth in North America," attributed to multi-year EMEA/APAC investment.
- **Medical health, technology, and business/finance verticals were standout strength**, explicitly offsetting CPG/auto softness — real diversification, not just a story.
- **JBPs (Joint Business Plans) now exceed half of revenue** and the pipeline "has more than doubled over the past year" — the clearest concrete evidence the go-to-market reorg is landing with large advertisers.
- **Q4 adjusted EBITDA margin held at ~47%**, with $423M spent on buybacks in the quarter alone and a new $500M authorization — capital return continuing even during a reinvestment year.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Headcount growth stays below revenue growth for a third straight year**, reinforcing that the reinvestment is capital/infra-led, not headcount-led.
- **The real scarce resource, by Green's own framing, is trusted proprietary data — not compute.** "Advertisers are becoming more selective with their data... AI companies without access to scaled quality data or amazing levels of trust will not last long." Audience Unlimited exists specifically to solve underutilization of third-party/retail data via flat-cost pricing because "there is no price discovery for data."
- **Retail media data usage hit record levels in 2025**, with retail partners in TTD's marketplace representing "more than half of global retail sales" — a genuinely large, differentiated data asset if the claim holds.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: growth reaccelerates through 2026 as the reorg and product cycle mature.** Action behind it: doubled JBP pipeline, brand-first unified coverage teams, and named product rollouts (Audience Unlimited, Deal Desk, new measurement/agentic frameworks) — this is real money and headcount reallocation, not just talk.
- **Belief: Agentic AI strengthens rather than disintermediates trusted platforms.** Action is thinner here — Green describes Kokai injecting AI "across nearly every part" of the platform and cites productivity gains from internal AI coding tools, but gives no concrete agentic-AI monetization metric yet; it's positioned as 2026-and-beyond payoff.
- **Belief: CPG/auto weakness is cyclical, not structural** ("does not reflect a diminished long-term opportunity"). Action: the Q1 guide is explicitly built conservatively around this vertical softness rather than assuming a snapback — management is hedging operationally even while stating optimism verbally, a real gap between rhetoric and the guide.
- **Gap to flag:** management claims conviction that "the decisions we're making today position us... to emerge from this period even stronger" (Davis), yet the only hard 2026 target given is that margin stays flat and growth "should improve over time" — no specific reacceleration quarter or number was committed to.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Q1 EBITDA softness is attributed to "timing"** rather than quantified against a specific spend plan, keeping the infrastructure investment number opaque.
- **Competitive-intensity question was reframed rather than answered directly** — asked how competitive pressure has evolved, Green spent most of the answer arguing the premise ("has the competitive pressure gone up? I would say not really") rather than detailing market-share dynamics.
