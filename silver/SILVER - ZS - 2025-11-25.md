# ZS — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-11-25)

**The key idea:** Zscaler posted an unambiguously strong quarter — ARR accelerating to 26%, RPO to 35%, all three growth pillars accelerating together — built on a bet that Zero Trust plus AI security consolidation beats point-product proliferation. The tension: management repeatedly declined to isolate how much of the acceleration is organic versus stitched together from three recent acquisitions (Red Canary, SPLX, Avalor), and analysts spent the entire Q&A trying and failing to get that number.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI security is now the standout pillar, and Zscaler is selling the anxiety, not just the product.** The segment grew over 80% YoY and blew past its full-year $400M target three quarters early; Jay's framing leans hard on fear — citing a hijacked AI coding assistant used for autonomous attacks as proof that "AI agents are also becoming the weakest link in their security."
2. **Zero Trust Everywhere hit its target a full three quarters ahead of schedule** (450+ enterprises vs. a 390 goal set only three quarters prior), and Jay is candid that this creates pull-through demand for the other two pillars — the platform-land-and-expand thesis is visibly working, not just aspirational.
3. **Z Flex is the quiet engine reshaping the sales motion.** Bookings under the flexible-commitment program grew over 70% sequentially to $175M TCV, roughly 20% of RPO bookings — a structural shift toward larger, longer, swap-friendly commitments that gives Zscaler better forward visibility, per Kevin Rubin.
4. **The organic-growth number is conspicuously withheld.** Multiple analysts (Tal Liani, Brian Essex, Joshua Tilton) tried different angles to get a clean ex-Red Canary growth rate; every answer was the same scripted line — "organic growth was consistent compared to Q4" — with no percentage ever given.
5. **SAP RISE migration is being pitched as the next Microsoft-365-scale tailwind.** Jay explicitly compares the SAP on-prem-to-cloud migration to the Exchange-to-Office-365 wave, framing Zero Trust Cloud as the natural replacement for ExpressRoute/Direct Connect firewall sprawl in that transition.

## Economy & consumer
- **IT budgets are tight, cyber is protected.** "There's no significant growth in the back environment. IT budgets remain tight. There is pressure on CIOs... There is far less pressure on the cyber side of it," Jay said — cybersecurity is holding up better than broader enterprise IT spend.
- **Deal scrutiny persists but hasn't worsened.** Jay noted continued "scrutiny from our deals, similar to what we shared in the past" — no incremental deterioration, but no easing either.
- No traditional consumer read-through; Zscaler sells to enterprise IT/security buyers, not consumers directly.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broad-based acceleration.** ARR growth hit 26% YoY (from 3.2B+ ARR base), RPO 35%, and Kevin Rubin explicitly said "ARR from each of our three growth pillars accelerated in the quarter, including on an organic basis" — the qualifier "including organic" is doing a lot of the reassurance work here given how much M&A is layered in.
- **Margin trade-off flagged openly.** Gross margin slipped to 79.9% from 80.6% a year ago; Rubin was upfront that "we are introducing new products that are experiencing strong growth and are optimized for faster go-to-market rather than margins" — a deliberate growth-over-margin choice on new AI products, to be revisited "over time as they scale."
- **Core ZIA/ZPA business is decelerating relative to the platform.** Jay conceded "the core business by itself will grow at a smaller rate than the rest of the overall business" — the legacy product is being deliberately subordinated to the Zero-Trust-Everywhere upsell motion.
- **Red Canary integration reads as smooth but is being kept opaque.** Jay claims pipeline is now "vast majority... coming from Zscaler customers," and a Fortune 100 CSO reportedly said Red Canary is "finding a few things every month that we aren't able to find" — but the company won't disclose numbers.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Three acquisitions in rapid succession are being fused into one platform, not run as separate businesses**: Avalor (data fabric/entity relationships) → Red Canary (agentic SecOps, closed August 2025) → SPLX (AI red-teaming, unifying LLM/workflow/MCP-server discovery). Jay: "Avalor gave that stuff. We have the data. Red Canary gave us a gigantic AI technology on top of it."
- **The constraint that binds is trust, not capacity.** Customers are explicitly reluctant to spread AI security across many vendors — "I don't want to share my data with a startup that started ten months ago" — which is the stated rationale for consolidating red-teaming/discovery/guardrails under one roof rather than best-of-breed.
- **Capital discipline stated but thin on detail.** Rubin says the company will "invest aggressively to position us for long-term growth and profitability" while also promising back-half margin expansion — the balance point wasn't quantified beyond guidance ranges.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** AI security ARR will cross half a billion dollars by fiscal year-end, and Zscaler will "exceed $10 billion in ARR" long-term. **Action:** three acquisitions bolted onto the platform in roughly a year, plus a dedicated Zero Trust Branch specialty sales team — commitments with real headcount and cash behind them, not just guidance talk.
- **Belief:** SAP RISE migration will be "a similar impact on our business" to the Exchange-to-365 wave. **Action:** so far this is narrative and a single 8-figure TCV customer example (Fortune 500 healthcare) — no dedicated product SKU or team announced yet, so this is more forward-looking thesis than committed build-out.
- **Belief:** Z Flex materially improves close rates and deal size. **Action:** formalized into a named program generating $175M TCV and growing — genuinely operationalized, not just talked about — though management admits "I'm not sure we have quantified exactly how much impact it is happening."

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Red Canary's ARR contribution, refused outright.** Rubin: "we don't believe that Red Canary's contribution is material to our overall business. So as we go forward, we don't intend to provide specific color on Red Canary" — said despite disclosing it's trending "slightly better" than internal guidance, a data point offered without the number behind it.
- **Clean organic growth rate, never given.** Every attempt (three separate analysts) to pin down ex-M&A revenue/ARR growth got the same qualitative deflection — "consistent compared to Q4" — with no percentage.
- **Average Z Flex deal economics, declined.** When asked to size the typical Z Flex upsell, Jay said plainly "I'm not sure we have quantified exactly how much impact it is happening."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Organic growth transparency was the dominant anxiety**, raised from at least three different angles (revenue-dollar delta framing, direct Red Canary ARR ask, and a follow-up asking whether full-year guidance raise equals Q1 organic beat) — none got a clean number, only "consistent" language.
- **Competitive positioning against resurgent SASE/firewall vendors** (Cisco, Check Point, Palo Alto claiming their own wins) was raised twice; Zscaler's response both times was to dismiss "SASE" as a meaningless marketing term rather than address specific competitive claims.
- **M&A integration mechanics** (Red Canary + data security synergy, SPLX + AI security completeness) got detailed, technical answers from Jay — management was far more forthcoming on integration narrative than on integration financials.
- Compressed worry: **is this 26%-growth quarter real organic acceleration, or a beat manufactured by stacking three acquisitions on top of a decelerating core business — and why won't management just say the number?**
