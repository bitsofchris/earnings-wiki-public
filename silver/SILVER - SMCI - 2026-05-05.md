# SMCI — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-05-05)

**The key idea:** SMCI reported a revenue miss it insists is pure timing (customer sites not ready) alongside a sharp gross-margin recovery it attributes to mix and one-time cost relief — all while opening the call addressing a DOJ indictment of former employees for alleged product diversion to China, an independent board investigation, and a balance sheet that swung to $7.5B of net debt in a single quarter. The tension: management's growth story (DCBBS attach, enterprise diversification, record backlog) requires the market to trust guidance and inventory accounting from a company mid-investigation that just burned $6.6B in operating cash flow.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The indictment dominated Q&A, not the numbers.** Multiple analysts opened on the DOJ case, NVIDIA relationship risk, restatement risk, and 10-Q timing before touching margins or revenue — a sign the market's real question is trust, not demand. CFO David Weigand: "the company was not named in this," and "we do not believe we will need to restate," but both hedged as based on "what we know at this moment."
2. **Gross margin nearly doubled quarter-over-quarter, but the drivers are largely non-repeating.** The jump from 6.4% to 10.1% non-GAAP came from customer/product mix, lower tariffs, and expedite fees that "did not recur in the March quarter" — yet Q4 guidance calls for margin to fall back to 8.2-8.4%, undercutting the "sustainable double-digit" framing management used minutes earlier.
3. **Customer concentration didn't go away — it just changed shape.** One customer was 27% of Q3 revenue (down from 63% previously reported), and management confirmed guidance assumes this customer stays a 10%+ customer — concentration risk is being managed, not eliminated, even as enterprise channel mix rose to 28% of revenue from 15%.
4. **Working capital deteriorated sharply.** Cash conversion cycle nearly doubled (54 to 106 days), inventory days rose 43 days, and operating cash flow went from -$24M to -$6.6B in one quarter, funded by a new $1.8B Taiwan credit facility — a real cost of chasing "record" backlog amid supply shortages.
5. **DCBBS is the pitch for a re-rating from box-builder to solutions provider**, with software revenue scaling from under $10M/quarter to $46M booked, but Liang wouldn't quantify DCBBS's actual dollar contribution to gross profit when directly asked twice.

## Economy & consumer
- **Component shortage is industry-wide, not SMCI-specific.** Liang: "In last six months...memory, SSD price grow so much, double, triple, more than triple, and some CPU shortage, especially from Intel and also some GPU shortage...we, like other competitor, other system company, yes, we suffer a lot from those shortage."
- **No visibility on when shortages end.** "Those shortage may continue for we don't know how long, like memory and SSD" — an open-ended constraint baked into guidance.
- No end-consumer exposure; SMCI sells into enterprise/cloud/sovereign AI infrastructure buildouts.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Revenue miss attributed entirely to customer readiness, not demand.** "Several customer sites were not yet equipped with the power and networking required for their cloud deployment" — Liang frames this as pure timing, with deferred revenue expected "in the coming quarters."
- **Margin recovery is real but partly non-recurring.** Weigand: expedite fees from a large December-quarter deployment "did not recur in the March quarter," and tariff relief followed a Supreme Court ruling — both framed as tailwinds that may not repeat at the same magnitude.
- **Balance sheet stress is the flip side of the "record backlog" story.** Net debt swung from $787M to $7.5B; cash position ended at just $1.3B (though $2.7B of AR collections landed in early April); a new Taiwan RC facility was stood up mid-quarter specifically for working capital.
- **AI GPU platforms remain the core**, at over 80% of revenue, with two customers each above 10% of revenue (one at 27%).

## Investing & scarcity
- **Aggressive US and Asia/Europe capacity expansion** — the new Silicon Valley DCBBS campus (largest US site, ~4M sq ft total Bay Area footprint, 8 buildings) is being built with dedicated clean rooms for DLC-2 liquid cooling and photonics/optical networking, explicitly to handle "much higher density in power, in computing density."
- **Scarcity binds on components (CPU/GPU/memory) and increasingly on working capital**, not on demand or manufacturing capacity — Liang says capacity is "already...big, but we continue to build more."
- **DCBBS positioned as the structural growth/margin lever**: cooling, networking, power shelf, battery backup, and management software bundled with hardware; segment margins reportedly exceed 20% versus hardware-only sales, and Liang expects DCBBS (with software/service) to deliver "at least 20% of our net income" within two years.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Liang is "very bullish" on AI/data center demand, targeting $12B next quarter and $40B for full fiscal 2026, and expects gross margin to keep trending toward a "sustainable double-digit" model.
- **Action:** Capital is going into new manufacturing campuses, a Taiwan credit facility for working capital, and continued platform co-development with NVIDIA (GB300 NVL72, Vera Rubin prep), AMD (MI350/MI400), and Intel/Arm (Xeon 6+, Arm AGI GPU solutions).
- **The gap:** Despite the bullish framing, Q4 guidance calls for gross margin to step *down* to 8.2-8.4% from Q3's 10.1%, and management repeatedly declined to quantify backlog or DCBBS's actual profit contribution — the stated confidence isn't matched by disclosure that would let investors verify it.
