# MAR — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-06)

**The key idea:** A geopolitically clean quarter turned messy overnight — Q1 beat on every metric with surprisingly broad strength (select service finally joining luxury), but the Middle East conflict is now a modeled ~100-125bps full-year RevPAR drag, worst in Q2 at roughly -50% regional RevPAR. Management is using the noise around World Cup and AI disruption to reassert scale as the moat, while quietly conceding that near-term U.S. demand visibility is thin — a ±3-week booking window standing in for a read on the whole summer.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Select-service inflection is the real story of the quarter.** Select service RevPAR flipped from down over 1% in Q4 to +3.5% in Q1 — the broadest chain-scale strength Tony Capuano says he's seen in a while, driven by a "pivot to domestic travel," drive-to substitution for pricier airfares, and tax refunds.
2. **Middle East is a modeled, quantified shock, not a vague risk.** Guidance bakes in a 100-125bps full-year RevPAR hit, a ~50% Q2 RevPAR decline in the region specifically, and CFO Jen Mason's blunt framing: "the hardest hit quarter we are looking at... is Q2," with recovery only "sequential" thereafter.
3. **Conversions and mid-scale are doing the heavy lifting on unit growth**, not new-build luxury — 35% of signings and 40% of openings were conversions, and the two-year-old mid-scale push already has 500 hotels between open and pipeline, described as "incremental" rather than cannibalizing higher tiers.
4. **World Cup guidance is a hill management is choosing to die on.** Despite press reports of cancellations and softer demand, Jen Mason held the line on 30-35bps of RevPAR benefit, citing group occupancy expectations closer to 15% (vs. ~40% for a Super Bowl) and benchmarking against past citywide events — a specific, testable claim worth tracking against actuals.

## Economy & consumer
- **Broad-based Q1 strength across every segment and chain scale** — leisure +6% globally, group +5%, business transient +1% (+2% ex-government) — that management explicitly resists framing as K-shaped, even while attributing gains to consumers trading down (drive-to vs. fly-to, tax refunds).
- **Booking visibility is short**: a ±3-week transient window means Q1/April strength doesn't translate into hard summer demand data — "that short transient window is really making it a little blurry right now."
- **Government room nights remain a soft spot**: down 12-13% in Jan/Feb before easing to -8% in March on easier comps, the one demand segment still shrinking.
- **International travel patterns "normalized" post-conflict-shock**: after an initial pullback in U.S. outbound bookings when the Middle East conflict began, domestic-vs-international booking mix is "back to kind of pre-conflict trends."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Fee-based model firing on multiple engines**: gross fees +12%, co-branded card fees +37%, residential branding fees +70%+, adjusted EBITDA +15%, EPS +17% — genuine multi-line strength, not one prop holding up the print.
- **Owned/leased upside is largely non-recurring**: the 21% jump there was driven by "higher termination fees" and one-off strength at specific hotels — worth flagging as the softer-quality line in an otherwise clean beat.
- **Middle East is now a real P&L drag, not just headline risk**: March RevPAR there fell over 30%, and IMFs are guided flat for the year specifically because Q1 outperformance gets offset by "significant declines in the Middle East" in the back three quarters.
- **1,000th hotel migrated to the new tech platform** — a scale milestone in Marriott's multiyear systems overhaul, framed as freeing staff time for guest service rather than a cost story per se.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The investment-spend increase is a single-item story**: $1.05-1.15B guide (up from prior), and both Tony Capuano and Jen Mason attribute the entire increase to the new Lefay luxury-wellness brand, not broader capex inflation.
- **Tech transformation stays the second-largest capital bucket** (30-35% of spend) but is structured so "the overwhelming portion" gets reimbursed by owners over time — Marriott isn't carrying the balance-sheet risk of its own digital buildout.
- **The actual constraint this quarter is corridor/connectivity, not capital**: Middle East airlift loss (Emirates, Etihad capacity cuts) is rippling into India and the Maldives specifically, since "the Middle East accounts for 10% of global transit traffic demand" — a supply-chain-style bottleneck in travel rather than a demand problem.
- **Capital return unchanged and rising**: $4.4B+ to shareholders guided for 2026 via buybacks plus a "modest" dividend — capital discipline holds even amid the Lefay and tech spend increases.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: Middle East recovery will be gradual and back-half weighted; conflicts don't map cleanly to historical recovery curves ("I wish that you could look at a single or a couple of regional conflicts and draw deep conclusions"). **Action**: guidance still assumes hotels "generally proceed as planned" there, and Marriott has kept opening properties "even since the war started" — a bet that disruption is temporary enough not to pause committed development.
- **Belief**: AI will reshape travel search/booking and Marriott's scale is a durable advantage. **Action**: the actual commitments are modest and defensive — a Q2 conversational-search rollout on marriott.com, pilots with OpenAI's Ad Pilot and Google's AI mode — with Capuano declining to share specifics on projected cost benefits, i.e. conviction stated, capital committed is still small and experimental.
- **Belief**: credit-card renegotiation (Visa, Chase, Amex) will meaningfully lift loyalty economics. **Action**: talks are "going well" and deals expected "later this year," but the CFO is explicit the real financial benefit is a 2027+ story once cards relaunch — this year's guidance excludes any new-deal impact entirely, so the upside is stated but not yet priced in or funded.
- **Belief**: mid-scale is a large incremental growth vector. **Action**: already at 500 hotels open/pipeline after ~2 years, framed as additive rather than reallocated resources — one of the few areas where stated ambition and actual build-out are moving in lockstep.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2027 investment guidance** — Jen Mason would only say spend "categories... relatively stay the same," explicitly declining to quantify.
- **No specifics on AI pilot economics** — Capuano said pilots "will have favorable cost benefits for our owner and franchise community" without sharing numbers, calling it not yet ready to "share really detailed specifics."
- **No firm view on how AI-mediated distribution monetizes** — "None of us know exactly how the online booking space will evolve," a rare admission of genuine uncertainty rather than a talking point.
- **Credit card deal terms undisclosed** — confirmed active negotiations and a timeline ("later this year") but no detail on fee structure or magnitude of loyalty/royalty impact.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **World Cup skepticism**: two separate questions pressed on reported cancellations and softer demand; management repeatedly defended the 30-35bps guide with the same benchmarking argument, suggesting this is the print's most contested number.
- **Middle East mechanics**: multiple questions dug into recovery timing, airlift substitution, and which APAC markets are most exposed — the conflict is clearly the analyst community's top concern this quarter.
- **Underlying worry, compressed**: is Marriott's demand strength durable or just a short booking-window mirage, and will AI intermediation quietly tax or bypass the loyalty-driven direct-booking model that underpins its fee economics?
