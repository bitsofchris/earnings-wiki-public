# BIIB — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Biogen is trying to prove it has escaped its MS-legacy decline by stacking three growth waves — a growth portfolio that now outsizes legacy MS, an Apellis bolt-on to accelerate the base, and an imminent five-trial registrational cycle (lupus, AMR, Dravet) meant to convert a "flat through 2030" consensus into a growth story. The tension: the company is spending heavily and taking real dilution now against data readouts still months to two years out, while quietly shelving a once-promising BTK asset and treating its most novel Alzheimer's bet (an anti-tau drug) as explicitly outside the near-term equity story.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **A five-trial registrational cycle is the whole near-term thesis.** SLE (x2), CLE, AMR, and Dravet syndrome readouts are now expected within the next four quarters, with felzartamab-AMR and litifilimab-CLE both **pulled forward** to first half of 2027 on faster-than-planned enrollment — management is visibly trying to convert "flat through 2030" consensus before it hardens.
- **Apellis is being run as a proof case for post-merger revenue continuity**, not just cost synergies — CEO Viehbacher called retained revenue through integration turbulence "the number one measure of the success of an integration," and framed it as almost entirely a U.S.-team retention story.
- **High-dose SPINRAZA is outperforming its own original launch** — starts and vials in the first 13 weeks are exceeding the initial SPINRAZA launch, and the company is seeing **anecdotal switchbacks from oral competitor Evrysdi (risdiplam)** back to SPINRAZA, which they're using as an efficacy signal ahead of salanersen.
- **LEQEMBI's subcutaneous IQLIK format is being positioned as a market-expander, not just a convenience feature** — management believes it makes previously-ineligible patients (those without a caregiver who can get them to infusion centers) treatable, and could blunt competitor Kisunla's once-monthly-dosing advantage now that home subcu exists.
- **Early-stage pipeline rebuild is explicitly a 2030s bet**, not a near-term lever: three years with no IND filed, now three INDs this year plus the RayThera acquisition (already in phase I) and six new programs expected in 2026 — Viehbacher: "you've got to start today if you want to have that growth tomorrow."
- **Brain shuttle / CNS delivery is a standing, multi-year internal priority** predating any recent data — both Viehbacher and R&D head Singhal describe active internal and external work on tissue-delivery modalities, with an eventual anti-tau + anti-amyloid combination product under "war gaming."
- **M&A posture is shifting from intentional to opportunistic** now that Apellis fills the near-term growth gap — future deals are expected to target earlier-stage assets (development-candidate to IND stage) rather than more commercial-stage bolt-ons.

## What's changing

- **The growth portfolio has now overtaken the legacy MS franchise** — Biogen's standalone growth products (excluding Apellis) hit $933M, up double digits, "in excess of our legacy MS portfolio" for a second straight quarter, while TYSABRI is merely holding flat against a live biosimilar launch.
- **SYFOVRE's underperformance was reportedly self-inflicted and is being unwound** — the newly integrated commercial team says free-drug program usage has been **cut by half** by tightening eligibility, and June was "the best month in the brand's history"; a premature direct-to-consumer campaign was shut down and is being relaunched now that the team believes physician sentiment has caught up.
- **Biogen now claims to understand SYFOVRE's real discontinuation problem** — patients were dropping off after the *first* injection, not gradually over a year as the top-line one-year attrition numbers suggested; this reframes the retention fix as an early-education problem rather than a durability problem.
- **The therapeutic footprint has broadened well past neurology** — from "only visiting the neurologist" three years ago to now calling on rheumatologists, dermatologists, nephrologists, epileptologists, and transplant nephrology, which raises real execution risk across simultaneous launches.
- **A prefilled syringe for SYFOVRE is coming**, projected to save physicians ~15 minutes per injection and increase injection throughput — a workflow argument as much as a clinical one.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **The BTK inhibitor BIIB091 hit its efficacy bar and is being shelved anyway.** Despite "compelling efficacy" in phase II relapsing-remitting MS, management is "taking a pause" given "increasingly competitive" BTK landscape dynamics — a tacit admission that a scientifically successful readout can still lose the capital-allocation argument.
- **Diranersen (anti-tau) is being carefully walled off from the growth story.** CEO: "not something that's going to affect Biogen's growth over the rest of this decade" — a deliberate expectations-reset after AAIC data, with unresolved dosing hypotheses ("maybe too little tau is also not good — we just don't know") left open rather than answered.
- **Apellis dilution is real and immediate**: ~$0.85 of non-GAAP EPS dilution in 2026 from financing costs alone, plus $120–130M/year hit to other income/expense through 2027, with accretion only promised starting 2027 — the growth narrative is being bought with near-term earnings.
- **$164M of IPR&D/milestone charges this quarter** (TJ Bio felzartamab China rights, Ionis salanersen milestone, Ionis ALS opt-in) plus a further ~$3 EPS-worth of such charges baked into full-year guidance — a recurring, guidance-distorting cost bucket that makes headline EPS comparisons noisy quarter to quarter.
- **Conspicuous silence on CD40 competitive positioning** — asked directly how Biogen's phase-I-ready anti-CD40 differs from Amgen's phase III CD40L antagonist in Sjögren's, Priya Singhal offered only "we remain excited... we haven't shared that yet."
- **No disclosed BIIB091 dose or clear go/no-go timeline** despite direct analyst pressure on liver-safety implications — management declined to share the dose "at this stage."
