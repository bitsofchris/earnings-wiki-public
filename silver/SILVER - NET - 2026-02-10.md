# NET — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-10)

**The key idea:** Cloudflare is pitching itself as the neutral infrastructure layer for an "agentic Internet" — the network agents must pass through and the platform developers build agents on — and the quarter's numbers (record ACV, record $1M+ customer adds, accelerating net retention) back that narrative with real enterprise bookings, not just talk. The tension: gross margin is quietly eroding (74.9%, below target range, down 270bps y/y) even as management insists AI traffic is a pure tailwind, and the company has yet to say anything concrete about how it will monetize the "content creators get paid by AI companies" business model it says will define 2026.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Enterprise sales motion has genuinely inflected.** New ACV bookings grew ~50% y/y to a record, sales productivity hit an all-time high for an eighth consecutive quarter of y/y gains, and the company closed its largest-ever ACV deal ($42.5M/year) on top of last year's largest-ever TCV deal ($130M over five years) — this reads as a real structural shift from product-led growth to true enterprise sales, not a one-quarter blip.
2. **Agent traffic is an early but sharply accelerating phenomenon.** "Over the month of January alone, the number of weekly requests generated by AI agents more than doubled across the Cloudflare network" — a genuine leading indicator of a new traffic regime, and directly relevant to anyone tracking infrastructure demand from agentic workloads.
3. **Gross margin is the one number moving the wrong way.** Down to 74.9% (below the 75-77% long-term target, -270bps y/y) as "paid versus free customer traffic again increased significantly," pushing more network expense into COGS — management frames this as mix shift with stable unit economics, but it's worth watching whether "Act four" and agentic workloads structurally pressure margin further.
4. **Cloudflare is positioning as the "honest broker" for AI content monetization**, working both sides — 80% of AI companies use Cloudflare, and a growing share of publishers do too — but this is entirely a narrative and pipeline story for 2026 with zero financial specificity offered.
5. **CapEx discipline is the explicit contrast to hyperscalers.** Prince argues Cloudflare gets up to 10x the useful work per GPU versus hyperscalers because it charges for work done rather than leased capacity, and that hyperscalers have "no incentive" to run AI workloads efficiently — a direct, structural critique of the hyperscaler CapEx supercycle from a company sitting adjacent to it.

## Economy & consumer
No discussion of the macro environment or end consumer — this is a B2B infrastructure call focused entirely on enterprise IT spend patterns and developer adoption, not the health of the broader economy.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Revenue accelerated for a third consecutive quarter to 34% y/y ($614.5M); large customers ($100K+/year) grew 23% y/y and now contribute 73% of revenue, up from 69% a year ago; net retention rose to 120%, up both sequentially and y/y; free cash flow nearly doubled to $99.4M (16% of revenue) from $47.8M a year ago.
- **Not working:** Gross margin came in at 74.9%, "just below our long-term target range of 75% to 77%," down 270bps y/y — CFO Seifert attributes this to the mix shift toward paid traffic increasing "the highest allocation of network expenses to cost of goods sold... ever," an honest disclosure rather than a dodge, but a real drag worth tracking.
- Customer count hit a record with nearly 37,000 net paying-customer adds in the quarter (+40% y/y), driven partly by free-to-paid graduation on the developer platform — a healthy top-of-funnel signal for the workers business.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx stayed modest relative to the AI narrative:** network CapEx was 13% of revenue in Q4, guided to 12-15% for 2026 — a far cry from hyperscaler-scale AI CapEx ramps, consistent with Prince's framing that Cloudflare "invest[s] behind the demand" rather than pre-building capacity.
- **Capital is going into team acquisitions, not GPUs:** Humenated (ex-Google team focused on the "future business model of the Internet") and ASTRO (a developer framework underlying vibe-coding platforms) were both folded in to extend the workers/agentic strategy.
- **The stated constraint is go-to-market and trust, not compute** — Prince repeatedly frames Cloudflare's edge as efficiency per GPU-dollar (up to 10x versus hyperscalers) rather than a scarcity of compute itself; the binding constraint on growth appears to be enterprise sales capacity and channel maturity, both of which the company says it's still building out ("we will get to" $100M-deals-per-quarter, "not up to a point" yet).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management describes a "fundamental replatforming of the Internet" where agents replace human seat-based software consumption, calling agents "the ultimate infrastructure multiplier," and frames 2026 as the year a new AI-content monetization business model gets defined.
- **Action:** concretely, this shows up as record enterprise bookings, a channel-first go-to-market rebuild (published price lists, SI/VAR enablement) now bearing fruit after two years of work, and two bolt-on acquisitions aimed at agentic commerce tooling — but CapEx guidance (12-15% of revenue) shows no dramatic scaling of infrastructure investment behind the "replatforming" thesis, consistent with the efficiency argument but notably conservative next to hyperscaler spending.
- **Gap:** the AI-content-monetization business model — repeatedly flagged as a major 2026 theme, with publishers and even bank research teams reportedly calling Cloudflare for help — has zero committed dollars, headcount, or product specifics attached yet; it remains entirely a talking point pending a 2026 reveal.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No financial framework for the content-monetization business.** Prince says "2026 will be the time that we start really talking about what this future business model looks like and how that is going to impact us financially" — an explicit deferral with no numbers, timeline, or structure offered despite direct questioning about the marketplace model.
- **No commitment on channel mix ceiling** beyond ruling out the extreme ("I don't think we'll ever get to 90% plus of business going through the channel"), leaving the actual target range unspecified.
- CFO Seifert flagged that pool-of-funds/variable revenue (~20% of Q4 ACV bookings, "mid-teens" for full-year 2025) will introduce sequential volatility — "bigger Q4 and sequentially smaller Q1s" — without committing to a specific magnitude investors should model.
