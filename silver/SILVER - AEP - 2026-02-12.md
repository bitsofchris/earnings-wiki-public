# AEP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-12)

**The key idea:** AEP is riding an unprecedented, hyperscaler-and-industrial-driven load growth wave — contracted incremental demand doubled to 56 GW in a single quarter — but management is deliberately keeping guidance and the capital plan unchanged "for now," banking upside for future disclosure rather than re-rating expectations today. The tension: investors want to know if this converts to real capital and real earnings growth, and AEP keeps answering "ask us next quarter."

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Load growth is genuinely historic, not incremental.** Contracted load additions doubled from 28 GW to 56 GW since the prior quarterly update, with total system sales crossing 200 million MWh for the first time in company history — commercial/industrial sales (mostly data centers) grew nearly 10%.
2. **AEP is deliberately sandbagging guidance.** CFO Trevor Mihalik called the $72B capital plan "somewhat conservative" and said the $5-8B incremental project pipeline plus the 28 GW reforecast are *not yet* in guidance — the company is choosing to wait until Q3 2026 (its stated annual cadence) to formalize the upside rather than raise numbers now.
4. **Transmission scale is the moat.** AEP owns ~90% of 765kV transmission in the U.S. and just landed new 765kV project awards across PJM, SPP, and MISO (~$4.7B), reinforced by a strategic build partnership with Quanta Services — a structural advantage competitors can't easily replicate.
5. **Generation-as-a-service is a new adjacent business.** The $2.65B Bloom Energy fuel cell deal (Wyoming, 20-year investment-grade offtake) is framed by management as "very similar to a regulated return" — a contracted-cash-flow bridge product for customers who can't wait years for standard grid interconnection.

## Economy & consumer
- **Demand is being pulled by AI/data center buildout, not broad economic growth.** CEO Bill Fehrman frames the environment as "accelerating electrification, rapidly expanding AI-driven and industrial demand" — nearly all incremental C&I growth traces to data centers in Indiana, Texas, and Ohio plus Texas industrial load.
- **Residential customers are treated as a constituency to protect, not a growth driver.** Residential sales grew only ~3%, and management repeatedly emphasizes tariff design (data center tariffs in Ohio, large-load tariff modifications in Indiana/Kentucky/West Virginia, pending filings in Michigan/Oklahoma/Texas/Virginia) explicitly to keep large-load infrastructure costs off residential bills.
- **No signal of consumer-side stress or pullback** — the "consumer" story here is entirely about protecting affordability optics rather than observed demand weakness.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Earnings beat and ROE gap is closing.** 2025 operating EPS of $5.97 topped the high end of $5.75-$5.95 guidance; earned ROE reached 9.1-9.2%, up 30bps in two years, trending toward a targeted 9.5% by 2030 on the back of Texas UTM, Oklahoma SB 998, and Ohio's forward-facing test year mechanisms.
- **Balance sheet metrics are mixed across raters.** S&P FFO-to-debt sits at 15.2% (above the 14-15% target), but Moody's FFO-to-debt is "just under 14%" — management flags this divergence itself rather than glossing it.
- **Generation & Marketing outperformed on contract optimization**, not core volume — CFO/Controller attribute the beat to retail margin strength and wholesale "contracts moving in and out," i.e., trading/optimization gains rather than organic growth, expected to persist into 2026.
- **"No plugs" discipline is a repeated talking point** — Fehrman explicitly contrasts AEP's guidance philosophy ("there has to be something behind it") against peers who "just put numbers out there," a pointed jab at less conservative competitors.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital plan has three separate, stacking layers**: the $72B base five-year plan (built on the *original* 28 GW forecast), a newly disclosed $5-8B of "confirmed or endorsed" incremental generation/transmission projects, and — separately again — capital tied to the *new* 28 GW of load growth that doubled the forecast to 56 GW, which isn't even sized yet.
- **The binding constraint is equipment and contractor capacity, not capital or demand.** AEP says its competitive edge is buying gas turbines and 765kV equipment "well ahead of time" — it has secured 10+ GW of gas turbine capacity — precisely because peers without that scale will hit supply chain walls first.
- **SMRs are explicitly speculative and gated.** Two early site permits (Indiana, Virginia) are underway, but Fehrman is explicit AEP "will only move forward with the appropriate returns and risk mitigating structures" — an early-stage optionality play, not a committed investment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: this load growth is durable and enormous** — 56 GW contracted plus 180+ GW still in the queue, spread across Texas, Ohio, Oklahoma, and Indiana, framed as multi-decade infrastructure need extending "well past 2030."
- **Action matches on transmission and generation procurement**: real dollars committed — Quanta partnership, $2.65B Bloom Energy contract, $4.7B in awarded 765kV projects, 10+ GW of gas turbines secured — genuine capital and contractual commitment, not just talk.
- **Action lags on financial guidance**: despite the demand doubling, EPS guidance and the long-term 7-9% growth CAGR are unchanged, explicitly deferred to Q1/Q3 2026 calls "when we have a little bit of greater line of sight." This is the clearest talk-vs-action gap on the call — operational commitments are already made, but the earnings-growth translation is being held back for a later, more controlled reveal.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Would not raise 2026 EPS guidance or long-term CAGR now**, even when directly asked if the doubled load pressures the growth rate upward — CFO repeatedly redirected to "keep an eye out on the formal process" at Q3.
- **Would not commit to converting LOAs into a stated conversion-rate rule of thumb** when asked directly by an analyst — Trevor Mihalik deflected to "quality of counterparties" rather than giving a hard data point on LOA-to-signed-customer conversion history.
- **Would not give a firm read on federal permitting reform** — Fehrman says AEP is engaged with DOE and Interior secretaries but "who knows if it will get traction," an explicit non-forecast on a policy tailwind they clearly want.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Multiple analysts converged on one anxiety: is the 56 GW real commitment or optionality?** Questions repeatedly probed ESA/LOA firmness, referencing a peer data-center project that walked away from a signed ESA elsewhere in the industry, and whether Texas LOAs (which only require land/interconnection studies/forecasts, not full construction spend) risk being "options" rather than binding projects.
- **A second cluster asked whether generation can physically keep pace with load** — one analyst bluntly compared the incremental load added in three months to New York City's entire peak demand (13 GW), questioning whether PJM/ERCOT reforms (reliability backstop auctions, SB 6) will deliver generation fast enough.
- **A third cluster pushed on timing of guidance updates** — whether the CAGR or capital plan could be revised before the promised Q3 cadence, given the scale of the reforecast — met with consistent "wait for Q1/Q3" deferrals.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts don't doubt AEP can build what's contracted — they doubt whether "contracted" really means committed, and whether the grid's generation side can show up in time to avoid the whole growth story becoming a bottleneck story.
