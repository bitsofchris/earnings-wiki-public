# FANG — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-05)

**The key idea:** Diamondback flipped from "yellow light" to "green light" mid-cycle, adding rigs and a frac crew within weeks of a geopolitical oil-supply shock, betting that its cost/inventory advantage justifies growing into a market that's short barrels — while insisting, almost defensively, that this isn't 2018 all over again. The tension: management wants credit for being the fast, disciplined mover, but every answer about growth ceilings, mid-cycle pricing, and capital allocation is hedged to "quarter by quarter."

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A war made this an easy decision.** Two months into what Van't Hof calls "the world's largest oil supply disruption in history," Diamondback greenlit 2-3 incremental rigs and pulled forward its 5th frac crew, arguing "if this isn't the time to grow now, then I don't know when it is." The company frames itself as merely reacting to a clear market signal, not chasing price.
2. **Operational execution is genuinely running hot.** Well performance beat expectations "even to us internally," credited to completion redesign and — repeatedly — machine learning applied to workover/downtime reduction post-Endeavor integration. Drilling costs already hit year-end targets early ($300/ft Wolfcamp D, sub-$400/ft Barnett), which management uses to argue the growth capital is being spent efficiently, not sloppily.
3. **Balance sheet, not buybacks, is the near-term priority.** With oil elevated, Diamondback is accelerating its $10B net debt target (from "12-18 months" to "a couple months") and explicitly slowing buybacks, aiming to convert "debt value to equity value in our NAV." CFO Jere Thompson wants the balance sheet to graduate from "strength" to "fortress."
4. **Negative Waha gas is a live drag, not a footnote.** Waha prices deeply negative are wiping out NGL value and, in spots, cutting into oil economics — Diamondback has curtailed ~2,000-3,000 bbl/d before under similar conditions. Relief depends on two new pipelines expected in H2 2026 shifting exposure from financial to physical hedges.
5. **Management is deliberately not giving a growth ceiling.** Asked directly for an upper bound on oil growth, Van't Hof declined a number, citing limited investor appetite for anything beyond mid-single-digit growth and explicit avoidance of "the go-go days of 2017, 2018" with multiple CapEx raises in a year.

## Economy & consumer
- **Oil supply shock is the entire macro frame.** "We're 2 months into the world's largest oil supply disruption in history" — Diamondback calls itself a "tourist" in the situation by virtue of being purely West Texas-based, but treats the disruption as license to grow.
- **No end-consumer exposure** — this is an upstream E&P; the relevant "customer" is the global oil market, not a retail or corporate buyer base. No consumer section content beyond commodity demand signals.
- **Mid-cycle pricing assumption held steady** despite the rally: still "mid-$60s WTI... it's a little too early for us to go higher on mid-cycle pricing today," even with the 2026/2027 strip near $90/$75.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Production beat driven by both subsurface and surface gains.** COO Danny Wesson credited perforating strategy, rate design, and sand-loading changes on new completions, plus "layering on that machine learning" for workover/downtime optimization — explicitly tied to post-Endeavor knowledge-sharing between legacy teams.
- **Drilling cost targets already beaten.** Wolfcamp D drilling hit $300/ft (down from $360/ft) ahead of schedule; Barnett drilling already put a well in the ground under the $400/ft threshold needed to make that play competitive.
- **Surfactant program is early-stage and uneven.** ~50 wells tested last year averaged a 100 bbl/d uplift, but results ranged from 0 to 400-500 bbl/d — "this is version 1.0" — with the next deployment phase this quarter aimed at understanding what drove the winners vs. the duds.
- **DUC balance is being actively managed as a buffer, not a stockpile.** Peaked over 200 DUCs in Q1, will draw down in Q2, then rebuild via the 2 backfill rigs — target is roughly a quarter-to-a-quarter-and-a-half of inventory ahead of each frac crew for schedule flexibility.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Green light = 2-3 rigs + 5th frac crew reactivated early**, primarily to accelerate Barnett JV obligations (net ~1.5 rigs to Diamondback despite the larger gross headline number), adding an estimated 20-30 wells to the year within the original guidance envelope.
- **Waha gas weakness is the binding near-term constraint on gas economics**, not rig or crew availability — service pricing and OFS capacity are explicitly described as still slack ("haven't seen industry activity ramp aggressively... there's still quite a bit of capacity out there").
- **Power/data-center gas monetization project still in progress** after "almost a year" of work, framed as a way to capture in-basin gas value — no timeline given, "once we do get a project finalized, we'll be able to talk about it in more detail." Notably Diamondback's own words connect gas monetization directly to compute/power demand, relevant context for infrastructure buildout even though it's the supply side, not the AI/software side, of that trade.
- **M&A largely on pause**: "this volatility is kind of difficult to get deals done... M&A is probably fairly quiet at Diamondback for the foreseeable future," aside from small Midland bolt-ons.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** higher-for-longer pricing could justify shifting Diamondback from a "bond-like" flat free-cash generator to a free-cash-per-share growth compounder over the coming years, and see the current disruption as evidence energy security/US-barrel importance is structurally rising.
- **Do:** so far this converts to a modest, reversible move — 2-3 rigs, one reactivated frac crew, guidance still "quarter by quarter." No CapEx guidance raise beyond the top end of the existing range, no committed multi-year growth plan.
- **Gap:** management explicitly declines to translate the "higher for longer" belief into a stated growth ceiling or long-range plan, preferring optionality — a real divergence between the stated conviction (production growth is now justified) and the actual commitment (small, hedged, quarter-by-quarter moves).

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No oil growth ceiling given**: "I don't wanna get into a specific number... there's a lot of noise in the system."
- **No upward revision to mid-cycle price assumptions** despite the strip rally — held at mid-$60s WTI, calling it "too early" to move.
- **No commitment on out-year (2027-2029) production trajectory** — framed only as "if we are in a higher for longer world... an advantaged company... should answer the call," not as guidance.
- **No specifics on the power/data-center gas project** beyond "moving forward."

- Balance sheet questions (net debt targets, potential zero-debt endgame, liability management) reflected interest in how much of the windfall goes to debt paydown vs. buybacks vs. growth capital.
