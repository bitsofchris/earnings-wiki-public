# XEL — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-30)

**The key idea:** This call is Xcel's coming-out party as the "safe," customer-protected way to serve hyperscaler data center demand — the Google ESA is the proof point, structured so customers get $1B-$1.5B in savings while Google eats all the infrastructure cost. The tension underneath: a 20 GW+ backlog and a firming $7B+ incremental capex opportunity are exciting, but they all have to be financed with more debt and equity into a balance sheet already absorbing a record $14B annual capital program, and half the "line of sight" projects are still unbid RFPs, not signed contracts.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The Google deal is the template, not a one-off.** A 15-year ESA where Google pays for 1,900 MW of new wind/solar plus a Form Energy 100-hour iron-air battery, with credit protections that management says save existing customers $1B-$1.5B over the contract term — Frenzel: "we believe this is a model for large load development that benefits customers and communities."
2. **The incremental opportunity is getting firmer, not just bigger.** Management moved from a vague "$10B+ opportunity" flagged last October to "line of sight to at least $7+ billion" of it this quarter, anchored to a specific SPP transmission award, Colorado-approved generation, and Google-related builds.
3. **Supplier lock-in is the real defense against an industry-wide capacity crunch.** With 24 gas turbines already slotted through Siemens and GE, plus framework deals with NextEra and Tier-1 EPCs, Frenzel argues scale is what lets Xcel out-execute peers on speed to power — the market read this positively enough that an analyst opened his question by calling it a compliment.
4. **Rate case settlements are proceeding on the company's preferred script.** A constructive Minnesota ALJ recommendation (9.8% ROE, 52.5% equity ratio) and a "decent starting point" in Colorado intervener testimony both point toward the negotiated outcomes Xcel has a track record of reaching.
5. **Financing is running ahead of plan, which is itself a tell on how much capital this requires.** Half of the five-year plan's $7B equity need is already locked down after one quarter — proactive, but also a signal of how large and front-loaded the funding requirement is.

## Economy & consumer
- **Weather, not demand, explains the quarter's softness.** Colorado's warmest winter on record cut EPS by $0.09; weather-adjusted electric sales still grew 2.8%, and full-year guidance holds at 3% weather-adjusted growth.
- **Affordability is the explicit governor on data center growth.** Bob Frenzel repeatedly ties large-load contracts back to protecting "existing customers" from cost increases — bills are cited as ~30% below the national average, and management wants that gap to widen, not narrow, as load grows.
- **No mention of broader macro conditions** (inflation, rates as a macro variable, recession risk) — the only economic signal offered is state-level industrial/oil-and-gas activity in the SPS territory.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Ongoing EPS of $0.91 beat the prior-year quarter's $0.84**, and guidance was reaffirmed at $4.04-$4.16 with a claimed 22-year streak of hitting annual guidance.
- **Financing cost is the real drag.** Higher interest charges and common equity financing cut EPS by $0.18 — nearly offsetting the $0.23 gain from rate case outcomes and sales growth — a direct consequence of funding the largest capital program in company history.
- **Legacy liabilities are being worked down methodically but not painlessly.** A fresh $37M charge hit for additional Prairie Island outage disallowance, and Smokehouse Creek wildfire claims sit at $460M low-end accrual with $397M already committed in settlements.
- **Rate case cadence itself may be changing.** Brian Van Abel floated using comprehensive capital riders to move toward "a longer-term path to not filing rate cases maybe every year," contingent on a constructive Colorado settlement.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Record capex, and it's still growing.** Q1 alone was over $3B against a $14B 2026 plan; the incremental opportunity above the $60B base plan firmed to $7B+ of a flagged $10B+, driven by the 765kV SPP transmission line, ~1,200 MW of Google-related generation, and 800 MW of Colorado-approved generation.
- **Supply chain and labor scarcity is named directly** as an industry-wide risk ("supply chains and qualified labor for generation, transmission, and distribution projects will become more constrained") — the stated countermeasure is multi-year framework agreements with GE Vernova, Siemens, and NextEra rather than spot procurement.
- **Tax credits are a load-bearing part of the affordability pitch**, not just the returns pitch: $7B+ in aggregate PTC/ITC benefits to customers 2026-2030 is cited as helping keep bills low while capex balloons — worth flagging as a policy-dependent assumption analysts didn't press on.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** 6 GW of contracted data center load by year-end 2027, a 20 GW+ inbound backlog, and 9% average EPS growth through 2030. **Action:** one Google ESA signed, a non-exclusive NextEra JDA covering "at least" 2 GW ("unbounded" per Frenzel), and state-by-state large-load tariff filings (Minnesota done, Colorado filed, Texas/New Mexico/Wisconsin pending) — real commitments, but the gap between a 20 GW backlog and a handful of GW under contract is still wide.
- **Belief:** the incremental $7B+ investment layer will convert cleanly into rate base. **Action:** several of the named projects (Colorado JTS RFP, 765kV SPP competitive bid, NSP/SPS RFPs) are still in bid or evaluation stages with decisions expected later in 2026 or 2027 — the "line of sight" language is doing real work covering unresolved procurement.
- **Belief:** the balance sheet can absorb this cycle at a "rule of thumb" 40% equity funding ratio. **Action:** they're running ahead of that ratio already (50% of five-year equity need funded in one quarter) — arguably de-risking, but also evidence the 40% figure understates true funding pressure in a front-loaded build.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **In-service dates for Google-serving generation remain undisclosed** — Van Abel: "we haven't specified the in-service dates on the assets to serve Google. That's still not public."
- **Company-owned vs. PPA mix for upcoming SPS and NSP renewable RFPs** was deflected to a generic "50/50" guide despite one analyst directly asking whether they simply don't know yet.
- **No firm cadence on remaining equity issuance** for the five-year plan — Van Abel declined to give specific timing, citing flexibility from forward contracts that can be pushed out multiple years.
- **No customer-benefit quantification framework** offered for future data center deals beyond the Google case's "probably in that zip code" estimate — Bob Frenzel acknowledged peers disclose this more systematically and said only that Xcel "can probably work on something like that" later.

- **Data center execution risk was the other major cluster** — geographic mix of the NextEra JDA, financing structure for large loads, equity-financing cadence, and supplier/turbine availability, with one analyst explicitly framing the question as "the market isn't thinking about the execution risk" behind the growth story.
- Smaller threads covered the Colorado PUC sunset legislation (securitization authority), wildfire season preparedness, MISO capacity auction read-through, and Smokehouse Creek claims resolution — all handled without pushback or follow-up tension.
- **Compressed worry:** can Xcel actually convert a fast-growing, still mostly unsigned data center backlog and a firming multi-billion-dollar capex opportunity into contracted, financeable, on-time megawatts without breaking its balance sheet or its customer-affordability pitch.
