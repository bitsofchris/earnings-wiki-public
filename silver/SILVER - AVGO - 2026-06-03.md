# AVGO — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-06-03)

**The key idea:** Broadcom just posted the biggest AI semiconductor quarter in its history and used it to reveal the mechanism for the next leg: a financing vehicle with Apollo and Blackstone to fund frontier labs' compute purchases, because the real constraint on the AI buildout is no longer chips — it's power, infrastructure, and capital structure. The tension of the call is that management's visibility window keeps sliding outward (2027 → 2028) while its four largest disclosed commitments (Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, Meta) mostly don't start delivering material volume until 2027-2028, meaning today's record numbers are really a preview, not the payload.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Bookings are running 3x shipments.** $30B in AI semiconductor bookings against $10.8B shipped this quarter — Tan attributes this to customers front-loading orders for lead time on power and infrastructure, not chip scarcity: "It's not because of shortage of our components, it's also the other elements that need to be put in place."
2. **Broadcom is becoming a financier, not just a fab-less chip designer.** The AI XPU platform with Apollo and Blackstone — $35B first tranche toward >20GW through 2028 — exists specifically because frontier labs "otherwise might have difficulty getting access" to Broadcom's technology; Broadcom is underwriting demand it can't otherwise monetize.
3. **Networking's share of AI revenue (40%) is likely at its peak, not a new baseline** — Tan explicitly guides it back toward 30% as a "stars align" quarter, not the trend.
5. **CFO transition mid-cycle.** Kirsten Spears retires June 12; Amie Thuener steps in during the most consequential quarter in company history — a leadership-continuity risk worth flagging even though the call gave it only a courtesy mention.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read given** — this call lives entirely inside the AI capex cycle; the only cyclicality comment is non-AI semis (broadband, server storage, enterprise networking) turning up with bookings over $6B, which Tan calls "a clear indication we're on a path towards a full cyclical recovery," implicitly confirming that segment had been depressed.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record quarter, no asterisks in headline numbers** — $22.2B revenue (+48% y/y), AI semis $10.8B (+143% y/y), 67% operating margin, $10.3B free cash flow (46% of revenue).
- **Gross margin compression is structural mix, not weakness** — down 230bps as ASIC/TPU business (lower margin) outgrows higher-margin networking and software; both Tan and Spears stress "no structural change in semiconductor margin," but the trend will continue as AI semis keep outgrowing everything else.
- **Inventory build signals confidence, not caution** — days on hand jumped 68→86 "in anticipation of accelerating AI semiconductor growth in the second half," a deliberate working-capital bet on the H2 doubling.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is flowing into an off-balance-sheet compute-funding vehicle**, not just R&D or capex — Broadcom's own capex was a modest $231M; the real dollars ($35B+ tranche) are being mobilized through Apollo/Blackstone rather than Broadcom's own balance sheet.
- **The binding constraint has moved upstream from silicon to power** — wafer and HBM supply for 2026-2027 is "very comfortable," with 2028-2029 "working on" now; but customers are booking early specifically to align power shell and infrastructure timelines, not chip availability.
- **Supply access looks less like a bottleneck than a relationship game** — asked directly whether Broadcom can match rivals "dropping $20 billion" for incremental wafer/HBM allocation, Tan says customers "have been coming to us incrementally... by and large, yes," sidestepping the dollar-figure comparison.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: 2027 AI semi revenue "easily exceeds $100B," 2028 grows substantially further.** Action behind it: named, GW-denominated multi-year commitments to Google, Anthropic, OpenAI, and Meta, plus $6B in POs from two more unnamed customers — this is one of the more concrete believe-vs-back-it-with-money alignments in the note; the guidance is not just talk, it maps to specific contracted GW figures.
- **Belief: enterprise/consumer AI is a "second wave" of demand not yet arrived.** Action: none directed at enterprise directly — all capital and platform-building is aimed at the same six frontier-lab customers, meaning the stated future opportunity (enterprise) has zero dedicated investment behind it yet; the money is entirely still behind serving the labs that resell to enterprise.
- **Belief: content (dollars) per gigawatt will keep rising via denser, multi-die XPUs with HBM/SRAM/embedded CPU.** Action: this is a technology-roadmap claim, not a financial commitment disclosed on this call — worth watching for capex/R&D follow-through in future quarters.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to size the Google deal in dollars**, calling it only "very substantial," while simultaneously admitting Google will diversify chip sources — an unusual pairing of "strategic and very substantial" language with an explicit concession of non-exclusivity.
- **Declined to give a 2027 AI-revenue number beyond "over $100 billion," repeatedly**, resisting pressure (including an analyst's implied $200B backlog math) to commit to anything more specific: "We're not trying to guide you every quarter what 2027 would be like."
- **Would not confirm networking's 40% AI-revenue share as a new run rate**, walking it back to ~30% as the more durable expectation.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Q&A clustered around three anxieties: (1) **backlog-to-revenue bridge math** — is the $73B 18-month backlog understating a much larger 2027 number, and does H1→H2 doubling actually reconcile to the guided $56B full-year figure; (2) **customer concentration/share risk**, especially at Google, where the new long-term agreement raised questions about whether Broadcom is losing exclusivity; (3) **supply parity with rivals**, specifically whether Broadcom can secure incremental wafer/HBM the way competitors "dropping $20 billion" reportedly can.
- One analyst's mischaracterization of the Anthropic deal as Broadcom chips being used as debt "backstop" financing was corrected on air by Tan, clarifying it is a straightforward compute-capacity supply arrangement, not a financing structure.
- Compressed worry: **is the eye-popping backlog real, converting, and exclusive to Broadcom — or is it front-loaded optionality from customers hedging their own power and infrastructure timelines, with share increasingly shared across suppliers?**
