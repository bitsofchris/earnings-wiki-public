# HON — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-23)

**The key idea:** This is Honeywell's next-to-last quarter as a combined company, and the call is really two stories layered on top of each other: a clean portfolio-transformation execution story (spin-off pulled forward, two divestitures signed, $20B financing done at strong ratings) riding alongside a "trust the backlog" story, where both Aerospace (supplier-driven shortfall) and Process Automation (Middle East conflict) took real Q1 hits that management insists are temporary and fully offset by H2 backlog conversion. The bet embedded in maintained full-year guidance is that near-term disruption is noise and the underlying order book — up 7% organically, book-to-bill above 1.1 — is signal.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Aerospace hit a supply wall, not a demand wall.** Jim Currier was unambiguous: "our growth is constrained by supply and not demand across the board," attributing the January-February slowdown to acute shortages at specific, identifiable mechanical suppliers in engines/power systems, explicitly ruling out customer destocking. March was the best revenue month of the quarter and the recovery is said to be carrying into April.
2. **The spin-off just got pulled forward and made concrete.** The Aerospace separation now has a hard date (June 29, technically "Q3" by Honeywell's calendar), $20B of financing is raised at investment-grade ratings (A3/A-/BBB+, positive outlook), and both held-for-sale businesses (PSS to Brady, Warehouse/Workflow to American Industrial Partners) are signed — this is a company executing a checklist, not still deciding.
3. **The Middle East conflict is a real but bounded and quantified drag.** ~0.5% of Q1 total revenue, ~1% modeled for Q2, concentrated in PA&T aftermarket/services (~$50M of lost revenue in Q1). Management's framing is that this is deferred, not destroyed, demand — plant refurbishment work hasn't even started yet and will layer in over H2 and into 2027 as catalyst demand.
4. **Margin trajectory has a Q2 wrinkle that requires taking management's word for it.** Q1 margin beat expectations (23.3%, +90bps) but Q2 guide is sequentially down (22.2%-22.5%) purely on catalyst sales mix/timing — full-year guide is unchanged and reaffirmed with more confidence, but this is exactly the kind of "trust the bridge" claim that needs H2 delivery to validate.

## Economy & consumer
- **Geopolitics, not the consumer, is the swing variable this quarter.** No end-consumer discussion at all — this is an industrial/enterprise-demand business, and the operative macro factor is the Middle East conflict and elevated oil (above $100), which management frames as a near-term revenue drag but a medium-term tailwind via better crack spreads and catalyst demand.
- **Short-cycle demand in China and Europe is recovering** after early-year concern, per Vimal Kapur: "That certainly is recovering, which is very positive for us." Q2 short-cycle is guided to mid-to-high-single-digit growth.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: Building Automation, orders, backlog, pricing.** BA sales +8% organically, orders +9%; total company orders +7% with backlog +15% (PA&T backlog +22%); pricing running toward the upper end of the 3%-4% signaled range, closer to 4%.
- **Working: Industrial Automation margin expansion.** IA segment margin expanded 260bps on structural cost simplification post-divestitures, improved pricing execution, and NPI-driven share recovery — Mike Stepniak called it "a margin expansion driver for us for the next year or so."
- **Not working: Aerospace mechanical supply chain and PA&T aftermarket.** Aero sales growth slowed to 3% organically (vs. double-digit exit rate in Q4 2025) on supplier constraints; PA&T sales down 6% organically on catalyst reload timing delays and Middle East disruption; PA&T aftermarket specifically down ~10%.
- **Free cash flow softened.** ~$100M in Q1, down from ~$200M a year ago, on Middle East collections timing and aerospace inventory buildup tied to the supply chain dynamics — though management says collections "improved meaningfully in April."

## Investing & scarcity
- **The binding constraint in Aerospace is supplier capacity, not Honeywell's own capacity or demand.** Company has invested over $1B over three years into supplier onboarding, internal capability build, and engineering support for supply partners, and is continuing that elevated spend through 2026.
- **PA&T's constraint is geopolitical/logistical, not structural** — Vimal Kapur: "near-term headwinds are already reflected in our numbers... long term, the trend suggests that it's going to be a favorable outcome."
- **Capital is rotating out of non-core assets and into core automation/aerospace bets:** the $500M DoD framework agreement, the Johnson Matthey catalyst acquisition, and continued LNG capacity investment (Commonwealth LNG, NextDecade/Bechtel) versus the two divestitures (PSS, Warehouse/Workflow) exiting the portfolio entirely.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** H2 PA&T ramps to high-single-digit growth on backlog conversion (LNG, modular equipment) and Middle East rebuild demand; Aerospace returns to double-digit output growth as the supplier fix takes hold. **Doing:** locking in firm, FID-funded backlog rather than assuming unbooked demand — Mike Stepniak: "This is a very solid backlog that's going to start converting in Q3" — and continuing supply-chain capex specifically targeted at the constrained suppliers.
- **Believe:** LNG is a multi-year secular growth vertical, expanding beyond the U.S. into Africa and elsewhere for energy-security diversification. **Doing:** the Air Products liquefaction and Sundyne acquisitions from the past 18 months are already integrated and cited as "performing extremely well," and new LNG contracts (Commonwealth, NextDecade/Bechtel) are being signed now rather than promised for later.
- **Gap worth flagging:** management is confident the Q1/Q2 Aerospace and PA&T weakness is fully temporary, yet the Q2 margin guide is sequentially down and the full-year PA&T outlook is only "roughly flat" for the year — the H2 acceleration is asserted with high confidence but the actual converting-backlog evidence (beyond order growth and book-to-bill) won't be visible until Q3 results.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
