# CMCSA — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-23)

**The key idea:** Comcast is paying cash today to buy a broadband future it isn't sure it wins — EBITDA fell in the segment being pivoted, ARPU declined, and share buybacks are paused — all three weeks after announcing the NBCUniversal/Sky split, while parks softened and management leaned hard on "trust us, the AI traffic wave is coming" as the long-term justification.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **The wireless land-grab is the whole broadband strategy now.** Comcast crossed 10 million mobile lines (still just 7% penetration of its footprint) on a "best quarter ever" of 448,000 net adds, with roughly half of postpaid phone connects coming from customers taking a **free line** — a loss-leader designed to convert into paid relationships as the free cohort rolls off later this year. Premium unlimited plans hit ~30% of connects, which management used to argue they aren't just winning on price.
- **The convergence bet is explicit: broadband is the anchor, wireless is the wedge.** Convergence ARPA sits at roughly $85, which Jason Armstrong repeatedly called "well below levels reported by telecom competitors" — the pitch to investors is that this gap is pure runway, not evidence of underpricing.
- **The separation is proceeding with real urgency**, targeting completion in about a year, with Michael Angelakis brought back specifically to accelerate execution. Both resulting companies are meant to launch with **investment-grade balance sheets**, which is already shaping capital decisions today (see buyback pause below).
- **NBCUniversal is positioning itself as the un-walled-garden media company.** Mike Cavanagh's framing: "we take all these great assets and we are willing to partner with others. Other strategies are a little more walled gardens" — a direct jab at Disney/Netflix-style bundling as NBCU preps to compete standalone.
- **Sky is buying ITV's media business in the UK**, pairing Sky's content/connectivity/sports with ITV's reach (40M weekly UK viewers) — a bolt-on scale play ahead of the split, folded into Sky's side of the ledger.
- **Peacock crossed into profitability** for the first time ($189M EBITDA), adding 2 million subscribers for a second straight quarter (48M total) on World Cup and "Love Island," which management is using as proof the six-year streaming build finally pencils out.

## What's changing

- **Broadband ARPU declined 3.8%** and **Connectivity & Platforms EBITDA fell 5.8%** — the direct cost of the pricing simplification, no-rate-increase decision, and free-wireless dilution strategy Comcast began about a year ago. Management is promising "modest improvement" starting in Q3 as free lines convert to paid.
- **Broadband subscriber losses improved year-over-year** (loss narrowed by 34,000 to 167,000) — still losses, but framed as the pivot "working."
- **Sky Germany was sold off** (closed May 31), removed from pro forma comparisons — a >$2B revenue business exited because it had "immaterial EBITDA contribution," a quiet admission that scale in that market never translated to profit.
- **Share buybacks paused as of July 1** and expected to stay paused through separation — a full stop on a $900M/quarter capital return lever, redirected toward setting up two investment-grade balance sheets.
- **Business services mix is shifting hard toward higher-margin solutions**: the advanced-solutions-to-connectivity revenue ratio moved from $0.20-per-dollar three years ago to $0.70 today — one of the few clean secular-growth data points on the call.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **Theme park attendance "softened more than we anticipated"** starting in June and continuing into Q3, blamed on "higher fuel prices and weaker consumer sentiment" — a discretionary-spending warning sign management is careful to frame as temporary and macro-driven rather than product-related, even as Epic Universe itself is reportedly performing fine.
- **Osaka remains impaired by China-related travel restrictions**, and Beijing is fighting "a challenging macroeconomic backdrop" — a multi-quarter overhang on the international parks book with no resolution timeline offered.
- **EBITDA declined 5% overall this quarter**, masked in the headline by 5% revenue growth from World Cup timing — a case of top-line strength propping up a period where the core connectivity engine is shrinking.
- **On Starlink, the tone was "not a threat yet, but no comfort taken"** — Jason Armstrong conceded satellite will become "more of a competitor over time," particularly in rural/underserved markets that overlap with Comcast's weakest footprint, and admitted Comcast already partners with Starlink in its own business unit, an odd position for a company that spent the rest of the call talking up wireline's durability.
- **Conspicuous absence: no forward guidance on synergy costs, stranded corporate overhead, or dis-synergies from the split** — the call stayed at the "excitement and conviction" level three weeks post-announcement with capital structure, dividend policy, and exchange ratio all deferred to "coming months," leaving investors pricing a breakup without the numbers that actually determine its value.
- **"Some of the behavior has been irrational"** (Steve Croney, on competitor promotional intensity) — a tell that Comcast itself may be participating in or provoking pricing behavior that's compressing the whole market's returns, not just absorbing it passively.

## Street anxiety (compressed)

Whether Starlink is a rural niche or "fixed wireless 2.0" that eventually erodes the core wireline moat · whether the promised H2 abatement in broadband ARPU/EBITDA pressure actually materializes or is the second consecutive quarter of "next quarter it gets better" · how much of parks softness is temporary macro versus a read on Epic Universe's true demand ceiling · whether NBCUniversal has the standalone scale to compete against larger consolidated platforms or whether separation is really about creating acquisition currency · no clarity yet on target leverage, dividend policy, or exchange ratio for the two post-split entities — the underlying worry is that Comcast is asking the market to trust a breakup thesis and a broadband turnaround simultaneously, without giving numbers to underwrite either one.
