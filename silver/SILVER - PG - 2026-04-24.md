# PG — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-04-24)

**The key idea:** P&G delivered its best organic growth quarter in a while (3%+, broad across categories and regions) just as a fresh, unbudgeted ~$1 billion after-tax cost shock from the Middle East conflict landed on top of it. The tension: management is doubling down on innovation-led investment and productivity to absorb the hit rather than lean on broad pricing, betting that "pricing power has to be earned" — while declining to say what that means for margins or FY2027 guidance until markets settle.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth finally broadened, but roughly a third of it was pull-forward.** All 10 categories and all 7 regions grew organic sales — a genuine inflection after a rough stretch — but CFO Andre Schulten conceded Easter timing and defensive retailer trade-inventory building pulled about a point of growth from Q4 into Q3: "we would have rounded to 3%... instead of having a strong 3%."
2. **A geopolitical shock hit mid-quarter and the company chose to eat it rather than pass it through.** The Middle East conflict adds a ~$150M after-tax headwind this fiscal year alone and implies ~$1B after-tax if oil stays near $100/barrel — "nothing to sneeze at" — but management explicitly ruled out broad price increases to offset it, instead leaning on productivity and selective, innovation-paired pricing.
3. **The cost shock is a supply-chain and reformulation problem more than a raw-commodity problem.** Force majeure declarations from suppliers and their upstream suppliers, longer sourcing lanes, and product reformulation "upcharges" are named as the biggest drivers beyond the direct oil price — Southeast Asia is flagged as the most exposed upstream sourcing region.
4. **China is the clearest proof point for the "superiority over price" thesis** — a shrinking, low-confidence market where P&G still grew SK-II 18% and Baby Care 19% by out-innovating in a category most peers are ceding, while the U.S. — a market roughly the opposite in wealth terms — remains P&G's one weak Baby Care region.
5. **Management is deliberately decoupling margin from strategy.** Asked point-blank whether gross margin will be down next year, Schulten answered "I don't know... and I don't really care" — a striking rejection of the metric analysts fixate on, arguing margin is an output of getting growth and productivity right, not a target in itself.

## Economy & consumer
- **Inflation fatigue meets a fresh shock.** "The consumer has been hit with cumulative inflation beyond anything that they've seen in recent history," compounding onto a Middle East-driven energy/logistics cost spike that just began flowing through.
- **Underlying category growth is tepid (~2% value) but stabilizing**, with the two-month trend improving before the conflict added uncertainty to near-term consumer spending via gasoline/energy costs.
- **No evidence yet of consumer stockpiling** in response to the conflict — "I don't think the consumer is loading pantries at this point in time, nothing visible to us" — the inventory build seen was attributed mostly to retailers and base-period effects, not fear buying.
- **China's consumer is bifurcated**: overall confidence and category value are still down, but the segment that engages is willing to pay a premium for genuine superiority, illustrated by SK-II and Baby Care outperformance amid negative category growth.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Skin & Personal Care and China led** (high-single and double-digit growth respectively); nine of ten categories posted low-to-mid-single-digit growth, and share metrics improved to flat year-over-year globally with 26 of 50 top category-country combos holding or growing share.
- **U.S. Baby Care is the standout laggard** — growing share in five of seven global regions but not in the U.S., attributed to weaker product/communication/trial execution versus international markets; leadership says the fix is underway but "takes some time" given complex manufacturing.
- **Margins absorbed the investment**, not just the cost shock: core gross margin fell 100bps and operating margin fell 80bps even with 330bps of productivity, because the company chose to reinvest savings into innovation and demand creation rather than bank them.
- **The restructuring program (announced June 2025) is ahead of schedule** on portfolio actions (Bangladesh/Pakistan go-to-market exits, APAC portfolio pruning) and on pace for its 15% non-manufacturing headcount reduction target, with automation (unattended night shifts) now live across nine categories.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is chasing proven momentum, not spread evenly**: incremental investment was approved mid-quarter specifically for Tide (evo/Boosted), SK-II/Beauty Care, and the U.S. Baby Care turnaround — CFO declined to detail the full list, saying only that the portfolio share earning continued investment is growing.
- **The real constraint is supply continuity, not capital.** Force majeure from direct and upstream suppliers, compromised manufacturing facilities in-region, and longer/costlier sourcing lanes are the acute bottleneck — addressed via "rapid product reformulation and supplier diversification" enabled by the company's integrated data platforms.
- **Capex is rising** to fund both new capacity additions and restructuring cash costs, pushing free cash flow productivity guidance toward the lower half of its 85-90% range for the year.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes:** growth momentum is broad enough now to sustain itself — "I'm more confident than I've been in a long time" that outperformance versus category continues, though not guaranteed every quarter.
- **Acting:** protecting demand-creation and innovation spend through the cost shock rather than cutting to defend margin, and pulling forward Supply Chain 3.0 automation timelines to accelerate productivity into FY2027 — a real commitment of capital and management attention.
- **Gap:** management says it wants innovation-led pricing to fully offset the ~$1B headwind but admits productivity "likely" won't be sufficient alone and gave no committed magnitude for the pricing offset — the confidence is qualitative, the financial plan is still "a work in progress."
- **Tariff refunds** ($150M after-tax potentially recoverable from IEEPA tariffs) are being pursued procedurally but the company won't count on the money until the government process is defined.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No FY2027 guidance** and explicit relief at not having to give it: "I'm very happy that I don't have to give guidance today, because what do we know what the world looks like three months from now?"
- **No margin target or directional promise** — repeatedly deflected gross margin questions as the wrong focus versus top-line/earnings outcomes.
- **No specifics on which brands get incremental investment** beyond broad category hints (Baby Care US, Beauty Care, Fabric Care) — "you will understand I won't give away where we're going."
- **No commitment on the price/productivity split** used to offset the $1B headwind — framed as still being built over the next 90 days.
- **No promise of quarter-over-quarter category outperformance** — explicitly declined to guarantee it despite high confidence in the trend.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Pricing power skepticism dominated**: multiple analysts pressed whether CPG pricing power is structurally impaired after years of cumulative inflation, and whether P&G specifically can price through this new shock — management's answer reframed pricing as something that must be "earned" via innovation, not taken broadly.
- **Margin trajectory into FY2027** was probed repeatedly and consistently deflected — CFO would not commit to a gross margin direction, calling the underlying growth engine more important than the margin outcome itself.
- **Middle East conflict spillover** — questions on regional demand impact (Southeast Asia, Middle East enterprise markets) and on the cost breakdown beyond raw oil price — management confirmed no demand impact yet but real upstream supply chain exposure in Southeast Asia.
- **Sustainability of trial-driven volume gains** (affordability interventions, SKU/price-point tuning) was questioned; management said it's tracked rigorously business-by-business but wouldn't generalize the recipe.
- **Underlying worry, compressed:** can P&G's innovation-led model actually absorb a full unbudgeted $1B cost shock without either sacrificing the fragile growth inflection or ceding more gross margin than the Street is currently modeling?
