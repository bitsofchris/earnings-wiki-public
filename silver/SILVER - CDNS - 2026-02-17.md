# CDNS — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-17)

**The key idea:** Cadence closed 2025 with record backlog and reaccelerating recurring revenue, and spent the call making one core argument: agentic AI doesn't shrink demand for its "physically accurate" simulation engines, it multiplies it, because AI-written chip designs still have to be verified by the same base tools. The real tension is monetization — management is confident demand is real but is still working out how much of the new agentic value (ChipStack, Cerebrus AI Studio) gets captured via subscriptions versus usage-based or outcome-based pricing.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The "3-layer cake" is the entire competitive thesis.** Devgan repeatedly frames Cadence's moat as physics-grounded engines underneath AI: "any AI tools that we are developing or our customers are using basically in the end, call our software to get the job done properly." This is a direct rebuttal to the bear case that AI coding tools could disintermediate EDA vendors.
2. **Hyperscaler custom silicon (COT) is now "irreversible."** A marquee hyperscaler's first fully customer-owned-tooling AI chip tape-out was the quarter's standout win. Devgan says the shift from ASIC to hybrid-COT to full COT "is even more firm now than 1 or 2 years ago," with each hyperscaler running at least 3 major chip platforms — a durable tailwind for EDA/IP/hardware consumption regardless of which merchant or system company wins.
3. **Hardware and IP are both compounding, not one-off.** Hardware delivered a third consecutive record year (7 of top 10 2025 customers were repeat "Dynamic Duo" buyers); IP grew ~25% and Devgan explicitly says he waited for "multiple years" of performance before calling it a trend — a tell that he's normally cautious about declaring wins.
4. **Monetization design is deliberately conservative on pricing model.** CFO Wall: "we don't see AI forcing a wholesale change from subscriptions to consumption... multiyear subscription remains at the core." ChipStack will be priced like a "virtual engineer" on top of base licenses rather than replacing the subscription model — a hedge against undermining recurring-revenue durability.
5. **China has stabilized, not accelerated.** Design activity is "good" and "stabilized" but guidance holds China at 12-13% of revenue for a third straight year, with explicit second-half conservatism due to short pipeline visibility.

## Economy & consumer
- **Semi industry pulled forward a trillion-dollar milestone.** "A lot of the projections are that we might hit as the industry, semi might hit $1 trillion this year... which is like it used to be 2030" — a 4-year acceleration Devgan attributes broadly to AI, not just hyperscaler capex.
- **Broad-based cyclical recovery, not just AI/memory.** Devgan: "the regular semi companies are also, I think, have a better outlook for '26 than '25" — signaling the traditional/mixed-signal semi segment (~55% of Cadence's business) is turning up alongside AI-driven demand, a read-through for broader industrial/auto semi health.
- No direct end-consumer commentary — Cadence sells to engineering organizations, not consumers, so this key is appropriately thin.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Recurring software reaccelerated to double-digit growth in Q4**, reversing a comp-driven SD&A slowdown; CFO attributes most of the drag to a deliberate shift of BETA CAE customers from multiyear to annual license terms, which depressed 2025 SD&A growth to 13% despite genuine strategic momentum.
- **Share gains claimed across nearly every product line** — hardware, IP, 3D-IC, digital/verification — with Devgan noting the shift is new enough to call out explicitly: "our competitive position has improved... you can see it in the results as well."
- **Record $7.8B backlog, 67% of 2026 revenue already booked**, giving unusually strong forward visibility for a software company this early in a fiscal year.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Foundry partnerships widened, not just deepened**: TSMC N2/A16, Intel Foundry Accelerator Design Services Alliance, Rapidus (broad EDA commitment), and expanded Samsung Foundry collaboration — hedging exposure across the leading-edge foundry landscape rather than betting on one.
- **Pending Hexagon D&E acquisition** (~$200M annualized revenue, not yet in guidance) targets physical AI, autonomous vehicles, and robotics — extending the SD&A/BETA CAE physical-simulation strategy into a new vertical.
- **The real constraint is exploration capacity, not seats.** Devgan directly challenges the "seat-limited" framing: agentic flows run "10 or 100 experiments in parallel" versus 3-5 for a manual user, meaning compute/tool-usage — not headcount — is what actually gates how fast customers can iterate. DRAM/memory price increases were raised by an analyst as a hardware-margin risk but not substantively addressed by management.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: agentic AI will spawn new monetizable product categories.** Action: ChipStack shipped as a discrete "Super Agent" with its own pricing (virtual-engineer model) rather than folded into existing licenses, and 4-5 more agentic flows (physical design, analog migration, packaging) are already in development — a real budget/roadmap commitment, not just talk.
- **Belief: system companies designing their own silicon is a multi-year structural shift.** Action: expanded footprint at "multiple marquee hyperscalers" across EDA, hardware, IP, and system software simultaneously — betting on the same customers needing the full stack, not picking off point products.
- **Gap worth flagging**: management is bullish enough to call broad share gains and a "much more healthier" 2026 outlook, yet the formal guide embeds explicit prudence (2-quarter hardware pipeline visibility, flat China mix, 51% incremental margin vs. 59% delivered in 2025) — the stated confidence runs ahead of the numbers they're willing to commit to.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No segment-level 2026 guidance** for SD&A, IP, or hardware growth rates despite specific, bullish qualitative commentary on each ("IP will be good this year," "yet another record year for hardware") — commitments in words, not numbers.
- **Second-half hardware and China contribution deliberately under-guided** due to limited pipeline visibility beyond two quarters; Wall says this is standard practice, not new caution, but it means the full-year guide leans on H1 strength materializing.
- **Declined to frame incremental margin expansion as durable**: Wall calls 2025's 59% incremental margin evidence "there's no near-term ceiling," yet guides to 51% for 2026 — an explicit gap between what they believe is achievable and what they're promising.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Dominant anxiety: does AI cannibalize EDA demand, or amplify it?** Opened the Q&A and recurred in multiple forms (ChipStack vs. Cerebrus overlap, start-up competition, pricing model). Management's answer was consistent and rehearsed — AI increases tool usage via exponential workload growth — but the repetition of the question across several analysts signals it's not fully bought yet.
- **Monetization mechanics**: several questions pressed on whether agentic AI pricing would be subscription, usage-based, or outcome-based, and whether it would be margin-accretive — Cadence gave a hedged, multi-model answer rather than committing to one structure.
- **Mix-shift questions** (semi vs. systems 55/45, upfront vs. recurring, SD&A license-term transition) probed whether recent growth drivers are durable or one-time comp effects. One-sentence worry: is Cadence's AI-driven growth story fully priced in, or is monetization of agentic tools still unproven relative to the multiple the stock commands?
