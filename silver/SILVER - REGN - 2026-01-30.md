# REGN — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-30)

**The key idea:** Regeneron closed 2025 with genuine commercial strength (Dupixent, Libtayo, EYLEA HD all growing double digits) but is explicitly reframing itself around a much bigger, genetics-driven 2026 pipeline bet — 18 new Phase III studies, several first-in-class antibodies, and a novel GLP-1/PCSK9 combo — while quietly managing legacy EYLEA erosion, a looming biosimilar wave, and an unresolved government drug-pricing negotiation.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The franchise handoff from EYLEA to EYLEA HD is now the main commercial story.** EYLEA HD grew 66% and now represents "nearly half" of the retinal franchise, but legacy EYLEA 2mg fell 15% sequentially and management guided to "double-digit" further decline in Q1 2026 "based on competition and importantly, ongoing conversion to EYLEA HD" — a manageable transition today, but 2026 brings biosimilars that "is expected to intensify."
2. **Dupixent is still the crown jewel and management is defending its moat aggressively.** Schleifer emphasized the "incredible safety profile" versus JAK inhibitors and OX40 approaches ("somewhat disappointing... perhaps even most importantly from the safety side") and revealed a next-gen "soupy doopy" molecule in development, while declining to add detail on Sanofi's extended IP-runway comments.
3. **Patient affordability, not demand, is the drag on the anti-VEGF category.** Category growth was just ~5% for 2025 with the innovative branded segment down ~12%, explicitly attributed to "continued patient co-pay affordability issues" — prompting REGN to extend its donation-matching program to $200M through 2026.
4. **The obesity strategy is a deliberate non-compete play.** Rather than chase weight-loss percentage points, REGN is betting on a GLP-1/GIP + PCSK9 co-formulation targeting the "50% or more people who are obese and have high cholesterol" — Yancopoulos called it a "Trojan horse" to drive cardiovascular benefit, explicitly ceding the weight-loss efficacy race to competitors.
5. **CMS pricing negotiation looms unresolved over the whole print.** Management says it's "actively engaging" and "anticipate[s] reaching agreement in alliance with the framework's previously established by other companies" — vague language that signals REGN is a price-taker following precedents set by peers, not shaping the outcome.

## Economy & consumer
- **No general macro read** — REGN's only "environment" commentary is government/payer-specific: ongoing CMS negotiations aimed at "reducing drug costs for American patients" while "maintaining the United States leadership in biotechnology innovation."
- **Co-pay affordability, not clinical demand, caps category growth** — anti-VEGF category grew only ~5% in 2025, with the innovative branded segment (ex-Avastin/biosimilars) down ~12%, a gap management attributes squarely to patient cost-sharing, not efficacy or competition.
- **REGN is subsidizing affordability directly** — matched a $60M Q4 donation to a retinal disease fund and extended matching up to $200M through 2026, an operating cost taken on to preserve demand rather than cut list price.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Dupixent (+32% global, +36% U.S.) remains the growth engine** — "the most widely used innovative brand in antibiotic medicine" (sic, immunologic) with 1.4M patients across 80 indications, "most of which remain significantly underpenetrated."
- **Libtayo's adjuvant CSCC launch is "off to a great start,"** now the only NCCN category-one preferred immunotherapy in that setting, and is the #2 most-prescribed first-line immunotherapy in advanced NSCLC with "new patient market share... greater than Opdivo, Tecentriq, and Imfinzi combined."
- **EYLEA HD's growth is real but Q1 2026 will look worse on paper** — management flagged ~$30M of elevated wholesaler inventory at year-end plus typical Q1 reauthorization headwinds that will "negatively impact" reported Q1 net sales even as underlying demand grows high-single-digits sequentially.
- **Linvoseltamab (myeloma bispecific) launch is early but encouraging** — "we expect adoption to continue to steadily build over time," with the bigger opportunity still ahead in earlier treatment lines.

## Investing & scarcity
- **R&D spend steps up to $5.9-6.1B and capex to $1.1-1.3B for 2026**, funding 18 new Phase III studies (~35,000 patients) across myeloma, anticoagulation, complement disease, and obesity, plus expansion of Tarrytown R&D facilities and manufacturing/fill-finish capacity.
- **The binding constraint is regulatory/manufacturing execution, not capital or pipeline ideas** — the EYLEA HD prefilled syringe approval depends on a new-manufacturer inspection with a decision "expected in late April," and Catalent Indiana remains a backup contingent on resolving prior FDA inspection findings.
- **Gross margin guided down slightly (83-84%)** on "changing product mix" and costs to expand bulk manufacturing and fill-finish — a direct cost of supporting the broader pipeline and multiple simultaneous launches.
