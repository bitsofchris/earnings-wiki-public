# CTSH — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Cognizant's pitch has fully shifted from "we do AI-assisted delivery" to "we are an AI builder" — a bet that as models commoditize, the money moves to the applied layer (context, governance, workflow embedding), and that consulting/SI firms rather than hyperscalers are structurally positioned to capture it. The tension: management's own research says two-thirds of large enterprises haven't realized measurable AI productivity gains and a quarter have paused AI deployments — the thesis and the demand environment are pulling in opposite directions.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Cognizant AI Delivery Operating System**, a three-pillar stack (engineering harness, business-operations harness, "Intelligence Spine") built around **context engineering** — assembling a client's "work graphs, guardrails, and tribal knowledge" — positioned as the actual moat once model choice stops mattering.
- Betting the next leg of growth is **business process operations, not software engineering** — CEO Ravi Kumar: "software engineering is very mature. Business operations is not as mature" — reframing the addressable market from "$1 trillion system integration" to "$5 trillion-$6 trillion enterprise operations," with $4.5 trillion of labor exposed to AI.
- New talent architecture: **"frontier-certified engineers" and "frontier business operators"** (blended human/digital teams), targeting 5,000 and 10,000 headcount respectively, layered onto claims of the largest pool of Claude-certified architects globally and mass OpenAI/Gemini badge hackathons.
- **TriZetto held up as the proof-of-concept for platform economics** — $1.1B+ annual revenue, growing faster than the company average at "substantially higher margins" — explicitly called "a blueprint for how we intend to scale platform-led growth across other industries."
- Launched a **Physical AI platform-as-a-service**, explicitly framed as positioning ahead of "this iPhone moment for robotics and infrastructure," leveraging 10,000+ associates who previously trained models for the Magnificent Seven, now repurposed to train enterprise-specific models.
- Deepened frontier-lab dependence: dedicated Gemini Enterprise practice, OpenAI Daybreak Consortium membership, and expanded Anthropic partnership as a "global premier partner" in the Claude Partner Network — Cognizant now explicitly offers to **bundle pre-training and inference costs into its services contracts**, tying its economics directly to model-provider pricing.
- Completed the **Astreya acquisition** (IT managed services, tied to Google's corporate engineering environment) for $1.3B in year-to-date M&A, continuing an inorganic build-out (following Belcan, 3Cloud) to diversify beyond financial services/healthcare into public sector and physical/digital infrastructure.

## What's changing
- **Pricing model is shifting from labor/effort to outcomes** — fixed-price and transaction-based work has grown three straight years, and management is now signing engagements that "underwrite results" (one insurance brokerage deal commits to 50%+ productivity improvement over five years).
- **Productivity gains are increasingly being kept rather than fully passed through** — Kumar says the historical "circuit breaker" on how much labor productivity you could monetize is gone: "you could pass on that productivity to clients and outbid what you have actually committed to clients and keep some for yourself," which is why large-deal margins are "trending much better" than originally underwritten.
- **Client conversations have moved from raw AI adoption to "token economics"** — model routing between expensive/cheap/open-weight models, avoiding token spend where not needed — described as a maturing, still-unresolved discipline that will drive services demand for the next 12 months.
- One-time **India Labour Code regulatory change** produced an $81M one-time benefit (partial reversal of a 2019 liability) that flatters reported margin — management is transparent about backing it out, but it's worth flagging as a non-operating prop.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- Management's own data: **two-thirds of the Global 2000 haven't realized measurable AI productivity gains, and one in four have paused AI deployments** — a striking admission sitting inside a call otherwise selling AI-builder momentum.
- **Discretionary/short-cycle spending remains "cautious" and "cost-driven"** across health sciences, comms/media, retail, and travel/hospitality — guidance was cut mid-year because the anticipated "improved discretionary spending environment" never showed up; full-year revenue guidance was walked down even as EPS guidance went up (financially engineered via buybacks/tax rate, not demand).
- **$1B was drawn on the revolving credit facility** to fund the Astreya acquisition and buybacks, adding interest expense that partially offset EPS gains — a leverage-funded capital return program layered on top of an acquisition spree, in a quarter where organic demand is explicitly described as cautious.
- **Project Leap restructuring** cost $84M in the quarter (roughly half severance), with no change to full-year cost or savings estimates — a multi-quarter cost-cutting program running in parallel with "AI builder" growth messaging, i.e., headcount reduction and AI-driven headcount reduction are the same motion described two different ways.
- Bookings growth decelerated sharply against a distorted comp — down from 22% a year ago to 5% TTM — management leans hard on "tail velocity" and mix-shift narratives (more $50-100M deals, fewer mega-deals) to explain away the deceleration rather than address it directly.
- **India IPO/secondary listing decision remains unresolved** for another quarter — "we will make a decision on this once we have visibility of the revised regulatory framework" — a placeholder answer repeated with no new information.
