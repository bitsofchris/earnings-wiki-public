# TMUS — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-23)

**The key idea:** T-Mobile's pitch is that network superiority is now compounding across every product line — postpaid, fixed wireless, fiber JVs, enterprise — so it can raise prices, hold the line on device subsidies, and still take share. The unspoken question underneath the whole call is whether that flywheel is durable once rate-plan "modernization" (a euphemism for a price hike) shows up as churn in Q3, and whether the AI-driven network traffic story is real or just a hedge nobody can yet falsify.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Live translation on beta is their "first network-native AI application"** — AI models embedded directly in the core network rather than bolted on, with the stated long-term ambition of the network becoming **"the connective tissue for physical AI with inferencing at the edge,"** including a partnership with Figure AI.
- **Fixed wireless stays capped by design, not by capacity constraint**: the "fallow-capacity model" forecasts traffic at the hex-bin level (36 million of them), caps FWA market share, and sells only the leftover capacity — management reiterated the 15M-subscriber-by-2030 target is unchanged even as new spectrum (upper C-band, 2.7 GHz) becomes available, because that spectrum was **excluded from the original guidance** and just adds more fallow capacity on top.
- **Fiber JVs are being run for equity value, not homes-passed vanity metrics** — ~20% penetration in the first 12 months in markets where they start from zero customers, and crucially framed as complementary to FWA, not competing with it: winning a fiber customer off HSI frees fallow wireless capacity elsewhere.
- **Satellite/direct-to-cell is being downgraded from "differentiator" to "table stakes utility"** — usage is roughly 0.0002-0.0003% of network traffic, and the strategic bet is now on a pooled-spectrum JV rather than the existing Starlink exclusivity (confirmed not expiring this year), because a carrier-agnostic satellite layer is judged more valuable than owning it exclusively.
- **They're leaning into the 2027-2028 spectrum auctions (upper C-band, 2.7 GHz) as a repeat of the 5G Standalone moment** — Srini explicitly frames this as the next inflection where T-Mobile "steps in" ahead of competitors, tying capital allocation (buybacks intentionally kept judicious/lumpy) to preserving flexibility for these auctions.

## What's changing
- **Device subsidy strategy: deliberately not increasing subsidies even as component costs (memory) push smartphone prices up** — management is explicit that "customers will have to pay more" as a result, betting that network/brand value outweighs a subsidy war.
- **Rate-plan "modernization" is a forward-guided churn event**: Q3 postpaid net account adds are guided down sharply to ~250K (from ~277K in Q2, against a full-year 950K-1,050K range), explicitly because migrating legacy accounts to modern rate plans will cause a **temporary elevated churn spike concentrated in low-line-count accounts** — phone churn impact is smaller.
- **ARPA growth is being talked up, but the comp is messy**: reported postpaid ARPA growth was 2% year-over-year, but management immediately reframed it as **3.7% ex-M&A**, stripping out the UScellular and fiber JV acquisitions' lower-ARPA subscriber base from the prior-year comparison — a real dynamic, but also a metric redefinition mid-answer.
- **Postpaid phone churn dropped to 0.85%**, a meaningful year-over-year improvement management links directly to the NPS gains and premium-plan mix shift (60%+ of new account lines choosing top-tier plans).
- **Bundling framing shift**: T-Mobile now claims ~70% mobile/broadband bundle attach (vs. AT&T's cited 45%) by counting a broader mix of multi-line and device bundles — again, a metric constructed to look favorable relative to a peer's narrower definition.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **The AI-traffic-surge narrative other carriers are pushing (AT&T specifically) is directly contradicted here**: T-Mobile's own network chief says "we have not seen any surge in mobile traffic due to AI" — training and agentic workloads live in wireline/data-center infrastructure, not on the mobile network. This is a notable divergence worth flagging for anyone modeling telco capex off AI-driven demand.
- **EBITDA growth deceleration (12% H1 to high-single-digits implied H2) is explained away as an acquisition-comp timing artifact** rather than any softening — plausible, but the explanation was volunteered defensively before being pressed hard.
- **Prepaid is quietly stagnant-to-declining and treated as a feeder, not a business** — Peter Osvaldik expects a "similar trend" of minimal/flat service revenue into Q4, with the more premium prepaid customers being cannibalized upward into postpaid (framed as a feature, but it means the prepaid line itself isn't growing).
- **Buyback pace is being deliberately throttled and cushioned by "capital envelope" language** ahead of the 2027-2028 spectrum auctions — management won't commit to a run-rate, an implicit signal that auction costs could be large enough to compete with shareholder returns.
- **No mention of network capex needs if the AI-traffic story does eventually materialize** — the confidence in "sufficient runway" is asserted but not stress-tested against a scenario where physical AI/edge inferencing actually shows up at scale, which is the same edge-AI story Srini uses to justify the network build in the "where they're going" section. The two halves of the pitch (no AI traffic yet / edge AI is our future) sit in some tension.
