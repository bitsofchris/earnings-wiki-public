# TXN — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-22)

**The key idea:** After two years of the industrial/data-center recovery being the only story, TI is now describing demand as broad across every end market simultaneously — including a sudden, self-surprising automotive inflection — and is starting to push pricing up for the first time in years. The subtext: this is the cycle TI spent three years and a capacity buildout preparing for, and management is visibly relieved to finally be pulling the "lead time" lever instead of the "inventory glut" lever.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **TI is positioning idle clean-room capacity as its core competitive weapon this cycle**: with Sherman 1, the Sherman 2 shell, and Lehi 1 already built, the CEO said equipping fabs (not building them) is now the only gating step — "we have done the hard work ahead of time, and we have the capacity to build into it."
- **Data center is framed as a structural, multi-year tailwind, not a cyclical bump**: revenue in that segment **doubled year-over-year**, and the CEO ties future growth to the industry's shift to 800V architectures, which he says multiplies the number of power-conversion stages (and thus TI content) per rack rather than replacing existing sockets.
- **The company is explicitly playing a "lead time arbitrage" game against competitors**: when a rival quotes 52-week lead times, TI treats that as an opening to win new sockets — "that's an opportunity for TI, because that means there is a little bit less visibility for customers, and sometimes they will start a new design, even on a very complex part."
- **Pricing increases are being reintroduced after a multi-year down-cycle norm**, negotiated customer-by-customer rather than as a blanket list-price move — analog first, embedded expected to follow more in 2027 annual pricing talks.
- **Silicon Labs acquisition is still on track**, regulatory approvals "moving as planned," expected to close first half of next year, funded via cash on hand and debt with no change in structure.
- **CFO transition**: Rafael Lizardi retires end of August after nearly a decade as CFO; Julie Knecht (25-year TI veteran, former chief accounting officer) takes over August 1 — a low-drama succession, telegraphed well in advance.

## What's changing
- **Automotive went from absent to a named growth driver within a single quarter**: "when we came to the call in April, we didn't have the same visibility" — the CEO attributes the swing to China EV/hybrid demand plus customers who had run inventory "to very low levels" now scrambling to restock.
- **The whole demand picture flipped from concentrated to broad**: industrial, data center, and automotive all now growing together, with personal electronics expected to add to (rather than lag) the mix in Q3 — guidance for Q3 revenue and EPS both moved above normal seasonal patterns.
- **Lead times are ticking up off historic lows** — "a couple of weeks higher" versus a ~13-week floor — read internally as evidence of tightening rather than a warning sign.
- **Free cash flow inflected hard**: trailing-twelve-month free cash flow is up nearly 4x from a year ago, aided by **$1.6 billion in CHIPS Act incentives** over the period — management reaffirmed its capital-management-day framework ($8-9B FCF at $20B revenue, $9-10B at $22B) as still valid.
- **Backlog is visibly rebuilding**, both near-term shippable orders and longer-dated backlog, described as building "throughout the quarter."

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **The pricing story is being carefully undersold in the near term**: management said the vast majority of Q3 growth is unit volume, with pricing contribution "almost insignificant" — a striking hedge right after announcing pricing increases are underway, suggesting they don't want the Street modeling a margin-expansion story yet.
- **Personal electronics remains the soft spot inside an otherwise broad recovery**: flat year-over-year in Q2, and management dodged a direct request to confirm whether it would be up or down year-over-year in Q3, repeatedly redirecting to "sequential" framing instead — an analyst had to ask the same question three times without getting a clean answer.
- **Gross margin fall-through is being quietly capped by depreciation**: incremental margins guided at 70-85% *excluding* depreciation, an admission that the 300mm capacity buildout is a real, ongoing drag on reported profitability even as utilization improves.
- **No mention of AI PC, on-device AI, or any direct "AI chip" positioning** — notable for an analog/embedded supplier whose entire data-center pitch is being laundered through "power delivery infrastructure" language rather than any AI-specific narrative, keeping TI's story adjacent to the AI buildout without exposure to its volatility.
- **CapEx-to-ITC mechanics were flagged as "choppy"**: the CFO warned that assembly/test capacity (increasingly built outside the U.S.) won't qualify for the 35% investment tax credit, so the outsized ITC benefit seen this quarter shouldn't be extrapolated forward.
