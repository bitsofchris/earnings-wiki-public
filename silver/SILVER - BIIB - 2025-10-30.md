# BIIB — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** Biogen is racing legacy MS erosion against launch-product growth while simultaneously trying to rebuild an early-stage pipeline through cheap, pre-IND business development. The quarter's "beat" leaned on one-time gross-to-net and inventory tailwinds in MS, and management is candid that TECFIDERA's European erosion is about to accelerate — the real story is whether LEQEMBI's diagnostic-capacity unlock and 10 Phase III programs can outrun that decline before the runway narrows.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The MS "beat" was partly manufactured.** CFO Robin Kramer disclosed $38 million of favorable gross-to-net adjustments and $22 million of VUMERITY inventory dynamics inflating U.S. MS this quarter, even as TECFIDERA's European sequential erosion is guided to **roughly double** in Q4 — the underlying legacy trend is worse than the reported number.
2. **Diagnostic capacity, not demand, is the real Alzheimer's bottleneck.** Alisha Alaimo: "about half of the patients who are able to get an appointment with their neurologists are not actually eligible" — management is explicitly building blood-biomarker triage and subcutaneous dosing to relieve a *throughput* constraint, not a demand constraint, ahead of potential prevention-trial readouts (Lilly, Eisai/Biogen) that could add an asymptomatic population overnight.
3. **The BD strategy has flipped to buying early and cheap.** CEO Viehbacher: "one of the best times to bring in assets is actually pre-IND... you can take advantage of the fact that a lot of companies have venture capital backed financing" — Alcyone and Vanqua Bio are the first moves in a stated pipeline of "at least another one, if not two" more research-stage deals by year-end, funded by a pipeline where R&D spend is down while program count is up.
4. **Litifilimab's fast enrollment is being read as a demand signal, not just a trial-ops win.** Viehbacher explicitly ties competitive enrollment speed in lupus to future commercial success — a case where operational data is used as leading indicator for a market that hasn't launched yet.
5. **ZURZUVAE's commercial fit is genuinely contested and management's defense is thin.** Asked why Biogen keeps a women's-health asset now 50%-owned by Supernus, Viehbacher's answer leaned on back-office synergy and omnichannel/AI tooling rather than strategic fit — a real portfolio-logic gap the company hasn't fully resolved.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read at all** — the call is 100% disease-area, reimbursement, and diagnostic-pathway specific; the closest thing to an economic comment is an offhand CEO joke about AI data-center spend during a technical outage.
- **Patient access reads as the demand-side constraint**, not affordability: SKYCLARYS ex-U.S. growth is gated by country-by-country reimbursement negotiation, and LEQEMBI's Medicare Part D full-formulary access isn't expected until **2027**, with non-formulary exceptions bridging the gap in the meantime.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Launch products are genuinely working**: $257 million combined launch revenue, 67% YoY growth, now offsetting the MS decline on a YTD basis — LEQEMBI up 82% YoY globally, ZURZUVAE up 150% YoY with 80% of scripts written first-line.
- **VUMERITY is a real bright spot inside a shrinking franchise**: nearly $0.5 billion in 9-month sales with patent protection into the next decade, the only protected oral MS option — but Q3 growth was partly inventory- and gross-to-net-driven, so the underlying run-rate is softer than headline.
- **TECFIDERA's European collapse is accelerating**, not stabilizing: sequential net decrease of $28 million in Q3, guided to roughly double in Q4 as generics spread into new markets including Germany.
- **SKYCLARYS discontinuation dynamics are a live risk worth watching**: Alaimo disclosed some patients who discontinued after ~1-1.5 years "declined actually quite quickly" before restarting — a signal that the drug's disease-modifying benefit is hard for patients/physicians to perceive, threatening persistence.

## Investing & scarcity
- **R&D efficiency claim is stark and worth scrutinizing**: development costs cut ~25%, research costs cut ~40% vs. three years ago, while simultaneously running 10 Phase III/Phase III-ready programs — management attributes this to disciplined portfolio selection under Priya Singhal rather than under-investment.
- **The stated binding constraint is diagnostic/referral throughput**, not capital, compute, or people: blood-based biomarkers (up to 350,000 Alzheimer's blood tests expected this year) and subcutaneous LEQEMBI are framed as tools to relieve neurologist bottleneck capacity, with PET/CSF positivity rates rising as a direct result of better upstream triage.
- **Capital allocation logic favors buying early-stage assets pre-IND** to arbitrage venture-backed sellers' financing risk, reserving later-stage/expensive M&A for only the highest-conviction opportunities — a deliberate scarcity-of-capital discipline stated explicitly by the CEO.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **They believe** prevention-stage Alzheimer's treatment (asymptomatic patients) is a major future opportunity if EVOKE/AHEAD 3-45-style trials read positive — **they're acting** by building blood-biomarker triage and subcutaneous administration capacity now, ahead of any readout, to pre-solve the capacity problem Paul Matteis explicitly challenged them on.
- **They believe** immunology is a decade-long growth vector beyond current lupus/nephrology work — **but the actual commitment** is described as "relatively small" headcount additions in nephrology, transplant, and lupus, with broader immunology indications explicitly pushed out to a 10-year, not 5-year, horizon — a real gap between stated ambition and near-term resourcing.
- **They believe** litifilimab and felzartamab de-risked Phase III programs are highly likely to succeed based on Phase II data — **they're acting** by already building out commercial teams, pricing, and market-access infrastructure ahead of any Phase III readout, effectively pricing in success.
