# WBD — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-26)

**The key idea:** This call is less an earnings report than a pitch deck for the Discovery Global spin-off and the ongoing sale process — management spent the open on a highlight reel (box office records, HBO hits, Olympics) and then spent Q&A defending the leverage, sports, and international assumptions underpinning the separation math, all while explicitly refusing to discuss the one deal (Netflix) analysts actually wanted to ask about.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The Netflix gag order was the loudest thing on the call.** IR opened by telling analysts not to ask about "the Netflix transaction," and management never addressed it — every other answer (leverage, sports rights, cost savings) reads as a proxy fight for a deal nobody was allowed to name.
2. **Discovery Global's leverage story leans on trust, not new information.** Wiedenfels called ~3.3x net leverage "absolutely sustainable" and predicted "single-B, maybe low double-B ratings," but the underlying $0–$2B range from the proxy is explicitly a soft estimate — "wiggle room," in his words — not a firm number.
3. **The games business admits a real strategic failure.** JB Perrette conceded 2025 was "a year of reset" after WBD "allowed ourselves to get distracted going after too many IPs," and flagged that the 2026 pipeline wasn't replenished — a rare unforced admission of a broken bet.
4. **Discovery+ narrative reversal.** Wiedenfels now touts Discovery+ as a profitable, re-opening asset with millions of engaged viewers — the same product WBD "were trying to shut down" when the merger happened, per his own words.
5. **The streaming profit story is entirely lever-driven, not results-driven for 2026.** JB Perrette's "triple by 2030" case rests on five levers (content, new-market launches, password-sharing enforcement "second inning," retention tweaks, ad monetization) that are mostly still ramping, not yet showing up in reported numbers.

## Economy & consumer
- **International ad market diverges from domestic.** Wiedenfels: expects "flat to slightly up" international ad sales in 2026, driven by heavy free-to-air presence — a structurally different setup than the U.S., where the message was "relatively consistent" demand, not growth.
- **Password-sharing enforcement is still early.** Perrette says WBD is in "the second inning of our password sharing enforcement," with no global rollout yet — meaning a meaningful subscriber lever hasn't been pulled outside initial markets.
- **CNN ad sales flagged as a headwind even as viewership recovers.** Wiedenfels noted CNN "has seen headwinds in terms of ad sales" despite "an even more pronounced uptick" in audience — a monetization lag versus attention.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Motion picture business is the standout.** Seven straight $40M+ openings and nine #1 debuts in 2025, "a first for any studio," per Zaslav, with 2027 slate (Batman 2, Minecraft 2, Superman: Man of Tomorrow) positioned as the next leg.
- **HBO content engine is firing.** The Last of Us and Euphoria drove some of the strongest audiences "in HBO history," with new seasons of The Pit and Industry described as "cultural sensations."
- **Games division took a real strategic hit.** Perrette: 2025 was reset after chasing "too broad a set of studios" and "too many IPs" post a 2024 "unsuccessful launch" — 2026 will "look similar to 2025," with only two major titles (a TT Games console/PC release, and mobile sequel Dragonfire) rather than a rebuilt pipeline.
- **Linear networks holding share amid secular decline.** Portfolio still captured 30% of U.S. primetime cable viewing, with 17 of 2025's top 25 new cable series — framed as resilience, not growth, against acknowledged "secular headwinds."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Content spend was reallocated, not simply increased.** Zaslav: WBD "canceled a lot of stuff that was down 50% or 60%" to redirect economics into original film and prestige TV — investment funded by cuts, not fresh capital.
- **Talent retention is treated as the scarce resource.** Zaslav emphasized WBD "did not lose any creative talent in the last four years" as a core competitive claim — the constraint being fought over is creative talent and franchise IP, not cash.
- **Capital structure discipline for Discovery Global.** Wiedenfels: targeting no debt reshuffling and relying on "long-dated low-interest capital structure" to support the spin — a scarcity-of-flexibility argument dressed as strength.
