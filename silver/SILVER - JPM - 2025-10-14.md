# JPM — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-14)

**The key idea:** JPMorgan posted broad-based strength — record AWM revenue, a 17% CIB revenue jump, resilient consumer credit — while simultaneously absorbing two idiosyncratic wholesale fraud losses (Tricolor, and proximity to First Brands) that put NDFI/private-credit exposure at the center of every analyst's mind. Management's message is a split screen: the data says "fine," but Dimon's own language ("cockroach," "worse than people expect") concedes the non-bank lending complex is a genuine known unknown after a benign decade of credit.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Dimon contradicts his own CFO on NDFI risk.** Barnum spent several answers normalizing non-bank financial institution lending as mostly secured, sophisticated counterparties, no more elevated a risk than any other book. Dimon then volunteered, unprompted in tone: "when you see one cockroach, there are probably more," and predicted downturn losses in some NDFI categories will be "worse than other people expected." That's the most consequential tension on the call.
2. **Deposit growth targets are being quietly pushed out, not abandoned.** The 3%/6% CCB deposit growth trajectory laid out at Investor Day is softening — flat this quarter — because of lower savings rates, robust equity markets pulling cash into Wealth Management, and slightly higher-than-forecast yield-seeking flows. Barnum: "the margin, that kind of upward inflection point has been pushed out a little bit," while still calling the long-term trajectory "quite confident."
3. **JPM is deploying excess capital into a ~$1 trillion decade-long lending commitment rather than buybacks.** Framed explicitly as capital that would otherwise go to buybacks at a valuation management "does not love." This is real balance-sheet commitment, not messaging — RWA growth already cost 30bps of CET1 this quarter.
4. **AI expense strategy is deliberately unmeasured.** Rather than mandate provable AI savings ("very hard to do... might result in people scrambling around to use AI in ways that are not efficient"), JPM is using blunt headcount constraints as the actual lever, with AI productivity as an unquantified tailwind behind it. Directly relevant to how a technology-heavy enterprise thinks about AI ROI: they're explicitly rejecting bottom-up savings attribution in favor of top-down cost discipline.
5. **The Tricolor/First Brands moment reframes the credit cycle narrative.** After nearly a decade of near-zero net wholesale charge-offs, management now expects the "normal" charge-off rate to rise structurally as direct lending mix shifts — day-one reserve builds on new direct-lending deals are already showing up in the reserve build.

## Economy & consumer

- **Resilience is real but the labor market signal is ambiguous.** Early-stage delinquencies "stable and slightly better than expected," but Barnum frames the "low hiring, low firing" dynamic as employer uncertainty, not confidence — a fragile equilibrium, not strength.
- **Consumer spending outpacing income.** "Consumer spending remained robust while income was a bit lower" — savings rate compression is funding current spend, a leading indicator worth tracking against deposit-balance softness.
- **Housing is a policy bottleneck, not a rate problem in Dimon's view.** He attributes the 30-40bps mortgage-to-Treasury spread largely to post-GFC regulatory excess ("17 forms, 17 documents") and permitting/zoning constraints on the supply side, not primarily to rates.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **CIB and AWM are the standout engines.** IB fees up sharply on equity underwriting strength; equities markets revenue up sharply with "notable outperformance in Prime"; AWM hit record revenue with $72B of long-term net inflows.
- **Card economics are temporarily distorted by the Sapphire refresh.** Card net interest revenue yield is "currently negative" as rewards costs land ahead of the annual-fee increase — Barnum calls the accounting dynamic "really bad accounting," expecting normalization over "a couple of quarters."
- **Wholesale credit is normalizing upward, not deteriorating — yet.** $170M of the quarter's wholesale charge-offs tied to the Tricolor fraud; management is explicit that low charge-off rates of the last decade were "obviously not reasonable going forward."

## Investing & scarcity

- **~$1 trillion decade-long commitment to security-critical industries**, plus $10B of direct equity investments — an "all of the above" capital deployment answer that prioritizes RWA growth over buybacks at current multiples.
- **Binding constraint is discipline, not capital.** JPM has "a very large excess" of capital and is deliberately not fully deploying it into buybacks; Dimon's stated principle — "putting liquid par assets on the balance sheet and adding leverage is not a thing that actually generates value no matter what the supposed return... is in the spreadsheet" — governs how the excess gets used.
- **Labor cost competition from non-banks is a real binding constraint on comp.** Explicit acknowledgment that hedge funds, law firms, and PE are bidding up talent, forcing JPM to "pay our people competitively" regardless of AI productivity gains.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Believe:** 2026 NII-ex-markets should land near $95B, with market consensus expense estimates ($100B) likely "a little bit low" once seasonality and labor inflation are applied.
- **Act:** Constraining headcount growth as the primary expense lever — not waiting for AI to prove savings before acting.
- **Gap:** Management flags the labor market as ambiguous/risky ("if the labor market deteriorates from here... we are going to see worse consumer credit performance") while simultaneously guiding to accelerating loan growth and a decade-long trillion-dollar lending commitment — a bet that growth outruns the downside scenario they themselves are flagging.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No 2026 expense guidance** — explicitly deferred to Q4 earnings and the February company update, pending completion of the internal budget cycle.
- **No forward buyback or dividend guidance**, despite direct questioning on how the ~$1T lending commitment interacts with dividend-hike capacity.
- **Declined to detail additional NDFI exposures** beyond the disclosed Tricolor number: "with respect to other exposures, I do not really have anything additional to say about that at this point."
- **Sidestepped a direct answer on financial-sponsor activity picking up from rate cuts** — Barnum said he's "not persuaded" fully-priced-in cuts change sophisticated-sponsor behavior, without giving a real forecast.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **NDFI/private-credit exposure dominated the Q&A** — multiple questioners pushed on Tricolor, First Brands, and the broader non-bank lending complex, seeking a framework investors should use to assess risk; Dimon's answer conceded more uncertainty than Barnum's.
- **Deposit growth trajectory versus Investor Day targets** was directly challenged given flat quarter-over-quarter growth against a stated 3%/6% path.
- **AI-driven expense productivity** was probed as a multi-year expense lever; management gave a qualitative, non-quantified answer.
- **Capital deployment logic** (buybacks vs. new $1T lending commitment vs. dividend) came up repeatedly, with management declining specifics each time.
- Compressed worry: **has a decade of benign credit losses hidden underwriting weakness in the non-bank lending complex that surfaces first and worst when the cycle turns?**
