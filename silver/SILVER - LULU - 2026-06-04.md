# LULU — fiscal 2027-Q1 call (2026-06-04)

**The key idea:** A quarter that started strong (Feb-March North America trend beat expectations) fell apart in the final 6-7 weeks on a proxy-contest fight, product-composition controversy, and a yoga-line relaunch that didn't halo the rest of the assortment — forcing a meaningful guidance cut just as the company operates without a permanent CEO. Management's answer is more chase, fewer SKUs, and more marketing spend, but they explicitly refuse to bake any benefit from these actions into guidance, effectively admitting they don't yet trust their own turnaround levers.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The deceleration is real and recent, not baked into a full quarter of bad numbers.** Q1 comps were only -2% and North America outperformed plan (-4% vs. guided low-mid-single-digit decline) through March, then cratered in the last 6-7 weeks — meaning the full-year guide cut (revenue now flat-to-down 1%, EPS cut from $13.26 to $10.95-11.15) is a bet on a very short, very recent trend line, not a slow bleed.
2. **Interim leadership is flying the plane with a new pilot already named but not yet aboard.** CFO Meghan Frank and President André Maestrini are running the company as co-CEOs through a proxy contest and controversy while incoming CEO Heidi O'Neill doesn't start until September — Frank frames the current work explicitly as staging: "trying to set the business up so that Heidi steps in, hits the ground running."
3. **The "new look of yoga" launch is a genuine strategic miss, not a rounding error.** Away-from-body Align/Groove styles got good individual reception but failed to produce the expected halo across the rest of the assortment — a direct signal that the brand's core franchise-refresh playbook didn't translate into broader guest engagement this cycle.
4. **Management self-diagnoses the trend break as two things: brand noise and product misses — and is candid about not knowing the split.** "This is based on our early analysis" is repeated multiple times; they attribute the traffic drop to negative social/media commentary (proxy contest plus mid-April product-composition questions) compounded by underperforming launches, but can't yet cleanly separate the two.
5. **Guidance deliberately excludes upside from every stated action.** Chase, SKU reduction, and marketing increases are all "not assumed" in the range — a tell that management wants to avoid over-promising a turnaround they haven't yet proven works.

## Economy & consumer
- **Category is stable; the brand isn't.** "We're seeing relative stability in the trend of the athletic space" — this is presented as evidence the LULU-specific drop is company-caused, not macro-caused.
- **Broad-based traffic decline across all demographics**, not concentrated in one guest cohort — undercuts any narrative that this is a niche style/generational mismatch rather than a brand-trust issue.
- **Full-price sales are the real health metric they're chasing**: Q1 global reg-price up high-single-digits, but U.S. still slightly negative (albeit improved sequentially from Q4); Q2 guided down mid-single-digits as clearance ramps.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Fast and Free, Swiftly, Metal Vent run franchise refreshes, Daydrift and Define expansions, and China's Chinese-New-Year-driven quarter (+30% reported, +23% constant currency, 13% comp).
- **Not working:** the yoga relaunch's halo effect, and gross margin — down 410bps in Q1 (330bps product margin including 280bps gross tariff hit, 140bps fixed-cost deleverage) with SG&A deleveraging 310bps as store labor, incentive comp, and proxy-contest costs "layered back" after being cut last year.
- **Operating margin cratered**: 11.2% vs. 18.5% a year ago; net income per share $1.69 vs. $2.60.
- **Middle East franchise disrupted by the Iran conflict**, and softer tourism spend in Europe/Japan — called "temporary" but unquantified.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Marketing spend raised 10-15% above the original plan** to 6-6.5% of sales (from 5.6%), funding large-scale brand activations (Great Wall of China yoga event, SeaWheeze, US Open, NYC pop-up, athlete content series) — a direct, funded response to the "brand heat" problem.
- **Enterprise-enablement program continues**: supply-chain network optimization, procurement/indirect-spend reduction, AI-powered automation — framed as multi-year efficiency, not a near-term margin offset (tariffs offset only ~100bps of a 280bps hit in Q1).
- **The real constraint is guest trust/attention, not physical supply** — inventory units are down ~4% by design while the chase model (+20% volume vs. last year) is the tool meant to convert confirmed demand into shelf presence faster; store SKU density cut 15% to sharpen storytelling rather than chase breadth.
- **Store growth trimmed to the low end of the 40-45 unit range** for the year, with some 2026 openings pushed into 2027 — capital discipline creeping in even as new-store ROI is still described as ~1 year payback.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** trend improves modestly through H2, North America stabilizes "generally in line" with Q2's low-double-digit decline, China returns to ~20% underlying growth as negative commentary fades.
- **Action:** faster chase, cut SKUs, ramped marketing, compressed product-development cycle (18-24 months → 15-16, targeting 12-14).
- **The gap:** none of these actions are underwritten in guidance — management states outright that any benefit is pure upside, not a base-case assumption. That is a real hedge dressed as prudence: they're taking the actions but not yet willing to stake the numbers on them working.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No embedded credit for turnaround initiatives** in the second-half or full-year guide — chase, marketing, and SKU work are explicitly upside-only.
- **Declined to break out China's comp vs. new-store contribution** for the full year beyond the Q1 snapshot (13% comp within 30% total growth).
- **No firm timeline for full-price sales turning positive** in the U.S. — only "flat to slightly better" for the full year with vague "progress throughout the year."
- **Would not fully attribute the traffic drop between brand-noise and product-design causes** — repeatedly framed findings as preliminary ("early analysis").
