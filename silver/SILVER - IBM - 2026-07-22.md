# IBM — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-22)

**The key idea:** IBM pre-announced a miss and then spent the whole call arguing it was timing, not damage — clients yanked budget toward CapEx-priced servers/storage ahead of expected price hikes, stranding tens of large mainframe-software deals mid-close. The subtext is a company betting its whole multi-year story (mainframe moat, 80/20 recurring mix, quantum) can absorb one ugly quarter without the market re-rating the growth algorithm.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Lightwell**: a new $1M/year subscription to get AI-remediated open-source packages, launched off the back of "Mythos" accelerating vulnerability discovery — 7,500 package versions patched in two weeks, early adopters reading like the entire top of Wall Street (BofA, Citi, Goldman, JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley, Visa, Wells Fargo). Arvind's pitch is that AI now makes it economical to patch open source you have zero human expertise in — "a factory using AI models to be able to patch open source," with automated test-harness validation. He sizes the TAM at "multiple billions," success measured in "how many hundreds of clients" sign up.
- **Quantum is being treated as a near-term capital commitment, not a research line**: $10B+ over five years, a $1B cash contribution (plus $1B in Commerce Department chips incentives) toward Anderon, "the world's first pure-play quantum foundry," targeting a fault-tolerant machine installed in 2029. IDC ranked IBM #1 of 11 quantum vendors.
- **Go-to-market expansion beyond the Fortune 1000**: IBM is adding "forward-deployed engineers" and expanding coverage to "thousands of additional clients" where Red Hat, HashiCorp, Confluent, watsonx, and Storage have wallet-share headroom — an admission that its historical enterprise-only motion is leaving revenue on the table as AI adoption broadens.
- **Distributed Infrastructure (Power/Storage) as an underrated growth leg**: best quarter of revenue growth on record at 37%, with $500M of backlog — "highest on record" — as clients scramble for AI-era compute and data infrastructure, positioned as a genuine second growth vector alongside mainframe.

## What's changing
- **The z17 mainframe cycle beat prior cycles even while missing IBM's own targets**: nearly 130% program-to-program vs. z16, roughly $1B more mainframe revenue and $3B+ more attached software than at the same point last cycle — yet still described as "below our expectations" this quarter, with transaction-processing revenue down 42% (a 5th-quarter seasonal comp against 70% growth in the launch quarter, per Kavanaugh).
- **Clients openly diverted budget toward CapEx-priced hardware ahead of anticipated price increases**, cannibalizing software attach: transaction processing revenue fell 9% and data software growth slowed to mid-single digits even as Confluent had a strong debut quarter — Arvind's read is data got "hurt because people are buying less of it" in the scramble, not a demand problem with the product itself.
- **The 80/20 recurring-vs-transactional software mix keeps shifting toward recurring** (it "used to be 60/40," now 80/20), which management uses to argue durability, but it also means the remaining transactional 20% — tied to mainframe incumbency — is the volatile tail wagging the quarterly print.
- **Guidance was cut and re-anchored around a "low end as anchor" framing**: full-year revenue growth trimmed to 4%-5% from "5%+," software cut to 6%-8% from prior expectations, infrastructure actually raised to low-single-digit growth on Power/Storage strength — a mixed re-set that leans on Distributed Infrastructure to offset mainframe/TP softness.
- **Productivity actions accelerated ahead of plan**, enough to still deliver 30bp of operating pre-tax margin expansion this quarter despite the revenue miss, and management now guides to 100bp of full-year margin expansion — AI-driven software-development productivity, sales/marketing efficiency, and supply-chain moves are explicitly funding the gap left by weaker revenue.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **The core admission: tens of large CapEx-sensitive software/hardware deals failed to close on schedule** in the final weeks of June, driving the pre-announced miss — and it's telling that Arvind's evidence for "deferred not destroyed" is that roughly a third had closed in the first three weeks of Q3, not that demand was ever independently verified.
- **IBM has held pricing rather than following hardware peers' increases** — "we have actually held our price so far in the month of July... nowhere near as aggressive as some of the alternate infrastructure providers" — a tacit admission that competitors are raising prices in the supply-constrained environment and IBM is choosing (or is unable) to match, which cuts against the bullish "clients will come back once tech debt bites" narrative.
- **Consulting revenue growth is still barely positive (+1%)** despite signings up 6% and GenAI now "over 30% of backlog" — the translation from AI-driven consulting demand into recognized revenue remains slow, undercutting the generative-AI-as-growth-engine story told elsewhere on the call.
- **Free cash flow was flat year-over-year through H1** ($4.8B) despite adjusted EBITDA up high-single-digits — masked by a deliberate inventory buildup (+$600M) to front-run supply constraints, higher cash taxes, and net interest expense. Management frames the inventory build as a coming H2 tailwind, but it's a lever, not organic strength, and worth watching whether it actually unwinds.
- **Notably absent: no mention of IBM's own foundation-model competitiveness** (Granite gets zero airtime) — in a call almost entirely about AI strategy, the actual model layer is conspicuously ceded to others, reinforcing that IBM's bet is exclusively on orchestration/governance rather than competing on model capability.
- **RHEL performance still "constrained by hardware availability"** for a second straight quarter — an unresolved supply bottleneck management hasn't fully explained.

## Street anxiety (compressed)

Underneath it all: is IBM's mainframe-software flywheel — the multiplier where every hardware dollar pulls $3 of software — starting to show real fragility to macro CapEx timing, or was this quarter truly a one-off scheduling accident.
