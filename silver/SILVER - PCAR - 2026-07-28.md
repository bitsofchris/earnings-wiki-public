# PCAR — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** PACCAR posted a sequential jump in net income on strengthening freight fundamentals, but the real story is regulatory: an EPA re-write of the 2027 NOx rule let manufacturers keep selling current-generation engines with a capped per-truck penalty instead of forcing a costlier compliant engine, which management frames as smoothing what would have been a disruptive pre-buy/air-pocket cycle into a longer, steadier upcycle.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Local-for-local manufacturing is now a margin story, not just a tariff hedge.** Truck parts and other gross margin jumped from 13.1% to 14.4% quarter over quarter, and management credited "local-for-local production" for both tariff mitigation and underlying cost control — CEO Preston Feight called out "local-for-local production is benefiting PACCAR" as one of the biggest margin drivers, alongside favorable price-versus-cost.
- **PACCAR Parts hit a record quarter** ($1.75B revenue, $417M pre-tax income, 29.8% gross margin) and management is calling for growth to accelerate into the high end of a 3-5% full-year range as truck utilization recovers — the Fleet Services program (large-fleet parts channel) grew 8% sequentially, an early tell that customers are re-engaging maintenance spend after deferring it.
- **Selling the "current generation" engine through 2027 is the explicit strategy**, not a stopgap — Feight said plainly: "We are planning on selling the current product to our customers. That's the engagement we've had with many, many customers, is that that's their preferred approach, is to ease into this thing," for both PACCAR's own engines and partner Cummins units.
- **Build slots are nearly sold out for the year** ("probably like 90% full for the year"), and PACCAR expects to "sell out of build slots probably in the next month or two," pushing incremental demand into 2027 — a supply-constrained, not demand-constrained, ramp.
- **Financial Services is quietly a strength**, not just a captive-finance afterthought: $124M pre-tax income on "steady finance margins and strengthening used truck markets," suggesting the used-truck glut that pressured fleets for the past few years is clearing.

## What's changing
- **EPA rewrote the 2027 NOx emissions rule mid-year (announced July 9), and it's reshaping the whole industry's product roadmap.** Instead of forcing a hard cutover to 35mg-NOx engines, manufacturers can pay a non-conformance penalty (NCP) — currently proposed at **$6,000–$7,000 per truck** — to keep selling current-generation engines, which are cheaper to build than the fully compliant version (estimated $8K-10K+ premium). This is still a proposed rule with an open comment period, not final.
- **Freight fundamentals flipped positive for customers**: spot rates up roughly 20%, contract rates up about 6.5%, and a "little bit more constrained" driver pool — the underlying reason fleets are finally willing to replace aged trucks rather than defer.
- **Retail demand is backloading hard into H2**: first-half U.S./Canada retail was just 105,000 trucks against a projected ~250,000 full-year, meaning management expects roughly 145,000 units in the second half — a near-40% sequential step-up that leans entirely on the EPA smoothing story and fleets finally deploying capital they'd been sitting on.
- **Tariff exposure has stabilized rather than resolved**: the Section 232 tariff regime is described as "durable" with "no real challenge to that," and PACCAR is positioning its Ohio/Texas local-for-local footprint as the structural offset — a stable-but-permanent cost layer, not a temporary drag that goes away.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **U.S./Canada deliveries actually missed guide this quarter** — management admitted "a few hundred trucks that we didn't even deliver in the U.S." due to "supplier constraints that we're starting to experience as the market ramps up," a live bottleneck as build rates climb, quietly conceded rather than led with.
- **Management repeatedly declined to quantify the EPA-related tariff/refund benefit embedded in this quarter's margin beat**, twice deflecting direct requests to size it ("No, we didn't put that out") — a case of propping up an unusually strong quarter with an item management won't disclose the magnitude of, while insisting the underlying operating strength was "the bigger part."
- **The full 2027 NCP framework is not finalized** — it's a notice of proposed rulemaking with an open comment period and "we probably won't get a final answer till much later in the year" — yet the entire bullish 2027 narrative (steady product cadence, no forced platform disruption, healthy market) is being built on this preliminary number.
- **No discussion of electrification or the earlier-touted hybrid/EV powertrain push beyond a single boilerplate mention** ("industry leading hybrid and electric powertrains") in the scripted remarks — a trucking OEM leaning entirely on clean diesel engine timelines for its 2026-2027 narrative, with zero analyst questions on EV truck adoption or charging infrastructure, is a notable absence for a company that markets itself on multi-powertrain strategy.
- **U.S./Canada retail guidance range (230K-270K) was left unnarrowed with half the year gone**, and when pressed on why, management pointed to inventory as the swing factor rather than committing to a tighter number — an acknowledgment that visibility is worse than the confident 250,000 midpoint implies.
