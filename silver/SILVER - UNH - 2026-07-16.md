# UNH — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-16)

**The key idea:** A year into the post-crisis turnaround, UNH is beating its own reset expectations on Medicare and lifting guidance — but management is now explicit that commercial margin recovery, the segment they promised would hit historic 7%+ margins by 2027, is being pushed out indefinitely by a broken federal arbitration process and aggressive provider billing. The company frames this as "a delay, not a setback," which is exactly the kind of line that deserves skepticism.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **AI as "the operating infrastructure of the future"** — not a bolt-on but a claimed re-architecture of the whole enterprise: ambient documentation now at 70% of employed clinicians (headed to 90% by year-end) with executives citing a **90% reduction in reported clinician cognitive burnout**, AI-summarized nurse case reviews cutting review time 40%, and a digital prior-auth product (Optum Real) that has already processed roughly half a million authorizations externally, saving **69,000 administrative hours**.
- **Committing to eliminate 30% of prior-authorization volume** by year-end, and nearly two-thirds of pediatric prior-auth requirements, alongside a pledge to process **80% of prior authorizations in real time by the end of 2027** — a direct response to years of "delay and deny" criticism.
- **Optum Health is re-centering on integrated value-based care** after last year's retrenchment, with regional/national management restructuring, AI-driven care-transition support cutting hospitalizations by roughly 10% in the West and South, and expansion of rural house-calls/home-based care to nearly 90% of U.S. counties.
- **Optum Rx is converting its whole book to transparent, fee-based pharmacy economics** — more than 95% of clients expected on 100% manufacturer rebate pass-through by year-end, with retention "in the high 90s" and a new monthly per-member-fee model announced in May.
- CEO Stephen Hemsley reiterated the long-term **13%-16% EPS growth algorithm** without hedging: *"I don't ever believe I ever didn't believe in the 13%-16% long-term growth rate."*

## What's changing
- **Medicare Advantage trend is coming in below plan for the first time in this cycle** — full-year MA cost trend now expected below the initial ~10% estimate, driven by benefit redesign, network curation, a milder respiratory season, and favorable prior-period development, prompting a guidance raise (EPS to $19.50–$20, from a materially lower base a year ago) and MA margins now expected above 3%.
- **Full-year Medicare Advantage enrollment is now expected to decline by roughly 1.1 million** — a deliberate shrink-to-improve-margin move following last year's benefit cuts, not organic attrition.
- **Share buybacks doubled** to at least $5 billion for 2026, up from initial guidance of $2.5 billion, alongside a raised annualized dividend ($9.28/share) — capital return accelerating in lockstep with the earnings recovery narrative.
- **Debt-to-capital improved to 41.2%** from 44.1% a year prior, with management targeting ~40% by year-end — deleveraging after last year's crisis-driven balance sheet stress.
- Optum Insight beat expectations in the quarter partly because **client transaction volume pulled forward from H2 into H1** — a timing shift management didn't flag as a reason to be cautious about the back half.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Commercial cost trend is not just elevated but accelerating past already-raised expectations** — running "modestly above" 11% versus a trend that was already the driver of last year's blowup. Management now says commercial margin recovery to the historic 7%+ target will extend **"past 2027"**, quietly abandoning a timeline they defended as recently as last quarter; the reframing as "a delay to margin recovery... not a setback" is spin worth naming as such.
- **The independent dispute resolution (IDR) process under the No Surprises Act is being characterized as broken and gamed**: up to 40% of IDR claims are ruled ineligible, roughly 60% of arbitration cases are brought by just five entities, and arbiters are awarding out-of-network providers payouts averaging **11x Medicare rates, ranging up to 30x**. This is now costing at least 100 basis points of commercial trend, up from 50bps previously — a structural, not cyclical, problem management says "needs to be reformed" by Congress, i.e., outside their control.
- **Medicaid margins stay pressured through 2026** (guided to -1% to -1.7%), with rate increases (~6-7% annualized) still lagging medical trend, and specialty pharmacy, home/community-based services, and behavioral health costs running hot — a slow bleed management is managing to a range, not fixing.
- **Star ratings were conspicuously dodged.** Asked directly about 2026/2027 Star outcomes amid "recent industry lawsuits," the exec response was pure hedge — quality is "critical," they're "restless," but declined to speculate, while noting industry-wide 2026 Star scores hit their **lowest level in about a decade**. No numbers, no commitment, just a pledge to "partner with CMS."
- **Optum Health's restructuring/severance timeline slipped** — a chunk of the Q4-announced restructuring originally expected complete in H1 is now pushed to H2, quietly disclosed only in response to a direct analyst question about why margins didn't seasonally decline as expected.
- Notably absent: no discussion of GLP-1/specialty drug cost trend as a *structural* long-term margin risk beyond a passing mention — striking given how central it is to commercial trend blame.
