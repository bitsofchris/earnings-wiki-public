# UNH — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-27)

**The key idea:** This is a company mid-turnaround admitting the damage plainly and trading membership for margin — UnitedHealthcare intentionally shed millions of members to reprice back to sustainable economics, while OptumHealth took a $1.6B charge and is still being surgically rebuilt after a "back to basics" reset. The unresolved variable overhanging everything is the 2027 CMS Medicare Advantage rate notice, which management called out almost defiantly as disconnected from real-world cost trend — and which they refuse to guide against.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Margin over membership, explicitly.** UHC is guided to lose 2.3-2.8M members in 2026 (including up to 1.4M MA lives) as a deliberate consequence of repricing — CEO Tim Noel: "our strategy in '26 did focus on more so on margin than it did on any specific membership target." This is a company choosing to shrink its way back to health.
2. **OptumHealth's turnaround is real but still bleeding.** Q4 operating income missed the prior guide by roughly a third; management reset the segment's "new baseline" to ~$1.5B and cut risk membership 15%, network size 20%, and EMR platforms from 18 to 3 — extensive restructuring surgery, not incremental tuning.
3. **The 2027 MA rate notice is a live grenade.** Noel called the CMS advance notice "disappointing" and said it "simply doesn't reflect the reality of medical utilization and cost trends," warning of "profoundly negative impact on seniors' benefits and access to care" if finalized as-is — but management would give zero 2027 point estimates on membership or margin.
4. **AI is being deployed as a real cost lever, not vaporware.** Over 80% of member service calls now route through AI tools, feeding a targeted ~$1B 2026 operating cost reduction, alongside a stated $1.5B AI/tech investment commitment for 2026 with more planned for 2027.
5. **A rare admission that trend won't normalize.** Rather than promising medical cost trend will moderate, management guided commercial trend toward 11% and Medicare toward 10% for 2026 — up from 2025's already-elevated levels — framing the current environment as the new baseline, not a transient spike.

## Economy & consumer
- **Medical cost trend is guided up, not down**, across every line of business: Medicare from ~7.5% (2025 actual) to a 10% assumption for 2026, commercial trend orbiting ~11%.
- **Seniors bear the brunt of funding pressure.** UHC frames three straight years of MA funding cuts (cumulative "$130 billion in funding reductions... under the prior administration") as forcing benefit reductions industry-wide, not just at UNH.
- **ACA affordability crisis prompted a rare concession**: UNH "voluntarily pledged to rebate ACA market profits back to our ACA customers this year," and guided exchange margins to roughly 1% (plus or minus) after a decade of the segment never being "a significant contributor of earnings."
- **Medicaid consumers face rate/acuity mismatch**: state rate increases of 6-7% guided against costs management says remain above that pace, with ~565K-715K Medicaid members expected to be lost to eligibility redeterminations and a state exit.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **UHC repricing "played out well" per management** — Medicare Advantage margins expected up ~50bps, group commercial margin gap to historical range more than half-closed, all supporting a targeted ~13% UHC operating earnings growth in 2026.
- **OptumHealth underperformed its own Q4 guide** by a wide margin (~$2.3B actual vs. "just under $3 billion" guided), attributed to restructuring actions and one-time items — CFO-level exec Krista Nelson called results "slightly disappointing to our expectations."
- **OptumRx is the bright spot**, winning over 800 new external customer relationships and passing 100% of rebates through for 95%+ of customers already, positioned as the growth engine offsetting UHC membership losses.
- **A $1.6B net-of-tax charge** blended cyber-attack collection write-offs (~$800M), a portfolio optimization gain (~$440M), and $2.5B of restructuring — including a $625M reserve for "structurally unprofitable" third-party Optum contracts they couldn't exit for 2026, a notable admission of bad legacy dealmaking.

## Investing & scarcity
- **AI investment is being sized and disclosed**: ~$1.5B committed in 2026 (with more in 2027), explicitly tied to both cost reduction (~$1B operating cost savings) and customer experience — 80%+ of member calls now AI-assisted.
- **Optum Financial Services merged into OptumInsight** to combine AI-driven revenue-cycle tooling with payment/financing capabilities, aimed at moving healthcare transactions "from post service reconciliation to real time point of care approval."
- **The real constraint is regulatory rate-setting versus realized cost trend** — not compute, capital, or labor. Both the MA advance notice and Medicaid state rate cycles are described as structurally lagging actual medical cost growth, and that gap is what's driving membership contraction and repricing across every segment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe**: long-term enterprise growth can return to a 13-16% range by 2028, underpinned by AI productivity, value-based care demand, and OptumInsight's platform ambitions.
- **Acting**: concrete 2026 moves are narrower and more defensive — repricing, network trimming, EMR consolidation, and voluntary ACA rebates — actions consistent with margin stabilization, not yet with the stated long-term growth ambition.
- **The gap**: management explicitly declined to bridge 2026 actions to 2027/2028 targets ("we're not gonna really talk to '27"), leaving a real distance between the stated long-term conviction and the guidance actually on the table.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2027 MA membership or margin estimate**, despite direct questioning, citing the unresolved advance notice as the reason it's "too early."
- **Declined to reaffirm a specific 2027/2028 growth glide path** when asked directly whether "low double digit" 2027 and "traditional" 2028 growth still holds — redirected to the vaguer "long term growth rate... 13% to 16%" framing instead of confirming the prior sequencing.
- **OptumHealth's 6-8% long-term margin target was reaffirmed in principle** but with no committed timeline beyond "modest basis point improvement" in 2026 and momentum building "in the back half of the year."

## The street — what analysts asked
