# HON — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-23)

**The key idea:** This is Honeywell's first call as a pure-play automation company post-Aerospace spin, and management is selling a story of portfolio simplification unlocking growth — strong short-cycle orders across every segment, a bolt-on catalyst acquisition bought "at the bottom," and a Middle East business that's oddly thriving amid regional conflict because of decades of local staffing. The tension: guidance raises are being partly fueled by stranded-cost removal and accelerated divestiture timing rather than pure operating momentum, and the AI/data-center growth story is still mostly narrative ("none of that is into our orders even yet").

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Pure-play automation bet, fully executed this quarter**: Aerospace spun off June 29, Quantinuum IPO'd in June (Honeywell keeps 47%, plans on that stake "by early next year"), and the Johnson Matthey Catalyst Technologies (JM CT) acquisition closed July 17 — the CEO called this "the final stage of our portfolio transformation."
- **JM CT is a technology and install-base play, not a cost-synergy deal**: bought at roughly 13x EBITDA "based purely on cost synergies... we haven't acquired the business for cost synergies. We have acquired it to add value through sales synergies," with complementary licensing in hydrogen, methanol, and ammonia that UOP doesn't currently serve.
- **Data centers are becoming a three-pronged opportunity**, not just fire/security: on-site power generation at data centers opens a new Process Automation channel ("automate the utilities and energy storage on location"), plus a longer-term liquid-cooling sensing play — none of which shows up in orders yet.
- **LNG is fully booked out**: "we're sold out LNG for the next three years," with proprietary heat-exchanger equipment (not licensed technology) on 2-3 year delivery windows, meaning 2026 bookings convert as 2028 revenue.
- **Software/ARR as the AI proof point**: Honeywell Forge ARR is guided to ~15% growth this year, crossing "a little over $1 billion," positioned as the company's primary AI monetization vehicle — but the CEO conceded he has no other AI-adoption metric to offer: "I cannot give you any other indicator because they're all wrapped up into other products."
- **M&A pipeline prioritized but deferred**: top priority is Industrial Automation sensing/measurement, second is bolt-ons in eight identified high-growth verticals (semiconductor, microgrid, hospitality), third is tuck-ins in fire/cybersecurity — but 2026 capital is committed to debt paydown, with M&A activity "reviewed more in the lens of 2027 onwards."

## What's changing
- **Guidance raised across the board on one quarter of outperformance**: full-year organic growth raised to 3%-4% (from 2%-3%), segment margin expansion raised to 250-290bps, adjusted EPS midpoint raised to $8.20 (from $8.10) — driven by Q2 beat plus accelerated divestiture timing, even after losing ~$400M of 2026 revenue from those same divestitures closing early.
- **Orders inflection is broad and short-cycle**: total company orders up 16% organically, with every segment posting double-digit short-cycle growth; Process Automation & Technology orders up 24%, Industrial Automation orders up roughly 10% off a low base.
- **Industrial Automation turnaround is tracking ahead of plan**: on-time delivery performance climbing from "mid-40s" to a trend line toward "high 80%," with margin guided to hit 22% in Q4 — a business the CEO says is inflecting from "no growth to low single-digit growth to, at some point... mid-single digit growth."
- **Stranded-cost removal running ahead of schedule**: now expected to end 2026 near $60-65M outstanding versus the ~$85M guided at Investor Day just weeks earlier — a $20M improvement in roughly six weeks.
- **Middle East orders up over 50%**, driven by large process-technology deals, even as the region generates real collection friction — management frames this as conflict-driven diversification of energy investment away from the Middle East into Africa and Asia benefiting Honeywell's backlog.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Middle East collection issues, downplayed but real**: "we observed some collection issues in pockets... modest, they are not material," concentrated in March-April, with a "prudent decision to address the customer set where we have limited risk" — language that stops short of specifics on which customers or how much was written off.
- **Process Automation & Technology segment margin contracted 180bps** to 22.1% this quarter on unfavorable catalyst mix, even as headline segment profit rose — the miss is real, just outrun by other segments' gains.
- **Repositioning costs spiked** for a Q2 footprint consolidation in the process business, pushing full-year repositioning guidance to $100-110M; framed as a "pretty fairly quick payback" but represents cash spent to hit the margin targets being touted.
- **Catalyst business margin volatility is structurally unpredictable within a year**: the CEO admitted the IP-vs-commodity catalyst mix "is not forecastable easily" quarter to quarter, meaning the PA&T margin story requires taking management's word that it "evens out" annually.
- **The renegotiated JM CT price was walked past without real explanation**: "I don't want to dwell a lot upon that" was the CEO's full answer on why the deal price came down from original terms — a red flag left unaddressed.
- **AI-driven physical/industrial demand is still aspirational**: when asked directly whether AI is showing up in Industrial Automation orders, the CEO could only point to Forge software ARR — the physical-AI hardware upside "none of that is into our orders even yet."
