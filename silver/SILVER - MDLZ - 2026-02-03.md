# MDLZ — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-03)

**The key idea:** MDLZ enters 2026 whipsawed by a cocoa market that fell faster than its own hedge book could follow — locking in a ~$1B inventory hit and flat pricing even as spot cocoa crashes toward "historic" levels. The bet: absorb a messy, front-loaded 2026 (European chocolate elasticity misses, US biscuit softness, competitive-reaction risk) in exchange for a real margin and brand-investment reset in 2027, funded by cheaper cocoa that's now locked in for next year but not yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The cocoa hedge caught management flat-footed.** Spot cocoa "declined more than anybody would have expected" in the two weeks before the call, but MDLZ's 2026 pipeline cost is already locked in above the new spot price — forcing a one-time ~$1B inventory revaluation hit, mostly in Q1. CFO Luca Zaramella insists the fundamentals were "clear well before," but that's cold comfort given the company's own book wasn't positioned for it.
2. **Consumers are buying less often, not defecting.** Both in US biscuits and European chocolate, CEO Dirk Van de Put repeats the same diagnosis: "penetration hasn't gone down, but the frequency and the quantity of consumption did." That reframes the fix as an occasions/awareness problem to be solved with A&C spend, not a share-loss problem.
3. **North America chose margin over volume — and is sticking with it.** After an aggressive 2025 promotional push "didn't give us a return on our investment," MDLZ pulled back on deals, took share losses, but improved price realization and P&L. Asked directly about a major peer's snacking price cuts, Van de Put declined to follow: "we don't necessarily think that we need to decrease our prices to the magnitude that I heard from another company."
4. **2026 is explicitly framed as a bridge year to a 2027 payoff.** Management is candid that chocolate margins won't recover until 2027, when the now-locked lower cocoa cost finally flows through pricing that's been held flat through 2026 to protect volume.
5. **GLP-1 risk is being modeled down to near-zero.** Even at a 10-20% ten-year US adoption rate for oral GLP-1s, MDLZ estimates only a 0.5-1.5% cumulative volume hit — treated as background noise rather than a strategic threat.

## Economy & consumer
- **US consumer confidence is "near historic low"** with affordability anxiety, job-security doubt, and a shopping basket that "has not increased for the last two, three years" — spend has rotated toward milk, meat, and bread at snacking's expense.
- **A genuine K-shape, but the bulk sits in value-seeking**: wealthier consumers grow premium/better-for-you and on-the-go categories, while the majority hunt lower unit prices, bulk packs, and shift into club and online channels.
- **European elasticity broke the model in the north.** "In the more northern markets in Europe, Germany, The Nordics, The UK, we saw higher than expected elasticity" versus roughly half of European markets that played out as planned — an admission the 2025 pricing playbook overshot in specific geographies.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US biscuits volume down 4% in the trailing three months, down 3% for full-year 2025** — the category itself is described as "soft," expected to ease but not reverse into growth in 2026.
- **Protein and premium sub-brands are the bright spot**: Perfect Bar, Clif's Builders Bar, and premium biscuit/chocolate lines (dates-based, "you" premium chocolate) are "growing double digits" and getting increased push.
- **Emerging markets remain the resilience story** — India, Brazil, Australia, South Africa cited as strong chocolate markets; Mexico had "a big comeback" into growth territory; only Argentina (deliberately deprioritized to "protect cash" amid economic turmoil) drags LatAm's optics.
- **A North America supply-chain modernization program** spanning three to four years is starting, aimed at efficiency, cost, and network flexibility — a structural, multi-year commitment layered on top of the near-term demand fixes.

## Investing & scarcity
- **A&C spend is swinging hard**: 2025's line was down 25% YoY (mostly nonworking media cuts), and management commits to "more than recover" that pullback in working media between 2024 and 2026 — funded by better 2026 cocoa coverage costs.
- **Cocoa sourcing diversification is a live capital allocation theme**: long-term supply agreements in Brazil (large farms) and Ecuador (smallholder cooperatives), plus early positioning in India and Indonesia, explicitly to de-risk concentration in Ghana/Ivory Coast (currently ~60-65% of global supply).
- **The binding constraint right now is cost-timing mismatch, not raw cocoa scarcity**: cocoa is no longer physically scarce (price is crashing), but MDLZ's own hedge book locked in high costs for 2026, so the real constraint is a self-imposed pricing/margin squeeze for one more year.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: 2027 is the real margin inflection**, once currently-locked high-cost 2026 cocoa laps and lower spot prices flow through. **Action matches belief here** — they're not pricing further into chocolate in 2026 specifically to protect volume and set up 2027, per Zaramella: "at this level, we are not going to price cocoa further."
- **Belief: brand investment restores frequency/quantity, not just penetration.** **Action follows**: the "more than recover" 2024-2026 A&C step-up is a real, funded commitment, not just guidance language.
- **Gap: management wants "significant volume trajectory change" in 2026** while simultaneously guiding organic sales to just 0-2% and admitting North America volume growth isn't even expected — "we are not exactly counting on volume growth in North America." The trajectory-improvement rhetoric is doing more work than the actual guided numbers support.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to match a peer's list-price cuts in North America**, opting instead for PPA and channel expansion — a deliberate bet that price cuts wouldn't pay back, per the failed 2025 promo experiment.
- **No firm commitment on 2027 profit flow-through**: "we are not planning to flow everything to the bottom line" but no split given between reinvestment and EPS growth.
- **No concrete milestones offered for the European chocolate recovery** beyond a vague "potential competitive reaction" — a direct question about what data points to watch got a non-answer.

## The street — what analysts asked

- GLP-1 and lab-grown cocoa were saved for the final question and answered with well-rehearsed, dismissive figures, suggesting management doesn't see them as live risks worth extended discussion.
