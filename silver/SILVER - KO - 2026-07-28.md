# KO — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** A World Cup-and-easy-comp quarter let Coke post its strongest trademark volume growth in 17 years and a record operating margin, but management repeatedly steered analysts back to the two-year average (2% volume) as the real trend line. The underlying story is a bifurcated consumer — lower-income households pressured everywhere from the US to India to Mexico — met with an aggressively deployed revenue-growth-management toolkit rather than genuine demand strength.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Affordability-led expansion into APAC**, especially India and China, is explicitly funded by margin: a third of the region's price/mix drag was called out as deliberate investment in cold-drink equipment and entry price points to "bring more consumers to the base in the right way," with premiumization layered on for later. CEO Henrique Braun framed this as replaying the Latin America playbook a decade behind.
- **The World Cup was used as a data-collection and playbook-building exercise, not just a marketing spend**: over 1 billion Panini stickers distributed across 40+ markets, 25 million new first-party data points, and a claimed record ~80% beverage incidence at venues — explicitly positioned to carry forward into "Coke & Meals" and "POWERADE moments" campaigns in the second half.
- **fairlife capacity is the growth lever management is holding back on purpose**: with production restored after the incident, the priority is keeping core SKUs in stock rather than launching new flavors/formats — "the innovation will come as we have even more flexibility down the line."
- **Coke reclaimed the Marriott contract after 34 years**, explicitly bundling Monster's energy portfolio into the pitch — a first-time joint system play Braun called "we want it all," suggesting more all-portfolio bundling with global accounts ahead.
- **Zero Sugar redesign targeting "evening occasions"** is being scaled from an initial European win to more markets globally — a rare admission that a core SKU wasn't fully covering its addressable occasions.
- **Margin expansion is credited to the asset-light bottling structure**, not brand strength alone — CFO John Murphy named the refranchising model as "one of the primary drivers" of sustained margin gains, with FX now a tailwind after years as a headwind.

## What's changing
- **Volume and price/mix are being deliberately rebalanced toward volume** after years of price-led growth — management called this out as intentional, not incidental, and it shows: 5% quarterly volume vs. 2% price/mix, a reversal of the recent mix.
- **CCBA (Africa bottler) divestiture is now a live 2026 event**, expected to close Q3 or Q4, creating a 2-3 point revenue headwind and ~1 point EPS headwind for the year — margin will get a further structural lift once it's off the books.
- **Guidance raised again**: comparable EPS growth now guided to 9-10% (from an implied lower base), organic revenue toward the high end of prior guidance (~5%), aided by a swing from FX headwind to ~3-point EPS tailwind.
- **Q4 will have six fewer days** than year-ago due to a calendar shift — flagged repeatedly as a reason not to extrapolate H1 momentum linearly into H2.
- **AI and digital tools are being positioned as the mechanism for improving marketing ROI**, with Murphy stressing "quality of investments" over quantity — a subtle signal that ad spend efficiency, not just budget size, is now the internal scorecard.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Lower-income consumers are pressured in every region cited** — US, Mexico, China, parts of Latin America — with management reframing this repeatedly as "it's about value, not only pricing," a soft admission that straight price increases are no longer landing with a meaningful chunk of the customer base.
- **India lost value share** even while volume grew, and management's answer leaned entirely on the affordability-investment narrative rather than addressing competitive dynamics directly — the value-share loss itself was not explained beyond the RGM/investment framing.
- **The IRS transfer-pricing dispute remains unresolved and material** — oral arguments happened in June, a ruling could take "6-12 months," and Murphy would not characterize what a loss actually means for cash beyond pointing to the existing financial-disclosure language. This is a real balance-sheet tail risk being managed by deferral of detail.
- **EMEA profit declined despite revenue and share gains**, attributed to "phasing of investment" — a vague explanation for margin pressure in a region otherwise described as strong.
- **APAC comparable operating income declined** even as volume grew across all units — again attributed to deliberate consumer-base investment, but it means the region's reported profitability got worse this quarter, not just slower-growing.
- **No mention of GLP-1/weight-loss-drug demand impact** on sparkling soft drink consumption — a topic increasingly live across the packaged-food and beverage sector, and its total absence from both prepared remarks and Q&A is conspicuous for a company this size.
