# GS — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-14)

**The key idea:** Goldman posted a blowout quarter — record M&A, record AWM assets, record equities financing — and used the strength to launch two things simultaneously: an aggressive AWM buildout (Industry Ventures, T. The tension of the call is Solomon selling unambiguous optimism on the front end while Coleman spends the Q&A reassuring the street, unprompted and repeatedly, that Goldman has no exposure to the credit blowups making headlines.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **M&A is genuinely back, not just talked up.** Advisory revenue up 60% YoY, over $1T in announced 2025 volume ($220B ahead of the #2 player), and a three-year-high backlog "despite very strong accruals" — Solomon expects 2026 to be stronger still "unless there's some macro disruption."
2. **The credit-risk questions dominated Q&A and got the same answer every time.** Four separate analysts asked about SRTs, NDFI lending, collateral fraud, and buffer sizing; Coleman's answer was consistently "no direct exposure to those names" plus a recitation of underwriting discipline — repetition that reads as reassurance-by-volume rather than new information.
3. **AI framing does double duty.** One Goldman Sachs 3.0 is pitched as pure offense — automation freeing capacity for growth, not cost-cutting — but Solomon also ties the AI narrative directly to his macro optimism: enterprise productivity gains from AI adoption are "one of the reasons why we're optimistic about the forward."
4. **AWM is the deliberate second engine.** Alternatives fundraising guidance jumped from a ~$65B/year historical clip to ~$100B for the year, unrealized incentive fees sit at $4.6B waiting to monetize, and two inorganic moves (Industry Ventures, T. Rowe Price) in one week signal urgency to scale durable fee revenue ahead of the next capital-markets downturn.
5. **Regulatory tailwind is treated as a strategic asset, not just a compliance update.** Solomon explicitly frames looser regulation (SLR relief, G-SIB recalibration, Basel III endgame) as improving Goldman's *competitive position* versus non-bank lenders — regulatory capacity redeployed into growth is presented as a real lever, not a footnote.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** record net revenues ($15.2B), #1 M&A globally in every region, record equities financing ($1.7B, +33% YoY) on record prime balances, record AWM fundraising ($33B quarter, guidance raised to ~$100B/year), 31st consecutive quarter of long-term net inflows.
- **Not working:** equities cash intermediation fell 9% YoY on "difficult comps" against a top-decile prior quarter — management's own framing, but still the one line item moving backward.
- **Redefinition to flag:** AWM's headline 23% pre-tax margin and 10.5% ROE are dragged down by the legacy HPI (historical principal investments) book; excluding it, margin/ROE would be ~150bp/~250bp higher — a real caveat but one management leans on to make the "real" business look better than the reported number.
