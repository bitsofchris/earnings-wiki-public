# TSM — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-16)

**The key idea:** TSMC raised its full-year CapEx guidance for the second time this year and announced another $100B in Arizona, betting that AI demand is still under-supplied through the end of the decade. The tension is unusually candid: CEO C.C. Wei admits he can't actually verify what customers tell him about demand ("all the truths together is not a truth"), yet he's committing tens of billions on the belief anyway — this is a company sizing capacity off aggregated vendor forecasts it doesn't fully trust.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Arizona gets doubled down, not just expanded.** An additional $100B investment — roughly four more fabs, front-end and back-end combined — "to build several more semiconductor logical wafer fab for 2 nm and below technologies, as well as advanced packaging fabs to support the strong multi-year demand from our leading U.S. customers." Total Arizona commitment now TWD 265 billion.
- **CapEx guidance raised again mid-year**, from $52B–$56B in January to $60B–$64B now — the third upward revision, driven by both demand pressure from customers and rising tool/inflation costs.
- **Agentic AI is reviving CPU demand inside data centers**, which Wei frames as pure upside for TSMC regardless of architecture: "no matter what CPU approach is taken, whether it's x86, Arm-based, or RISC-V, they are almost all TSMC's customers."
- **A14 (2nd-gen nanosheet, successor to N2) is ahead of schedule** — customer tape-outs already underway, pre-production 2027, volume 2028 — with A13/A12 derivative nodes extending the family into 2029, explicitly positioned to be "an even larger and longer-lasting node for TSMC than N2."
- **Packaging capacity is the actual bottleneck**, not front-end wafers — Wei says packaging shortage is "limiting my customers' growth," which is why he welcomes Intel's competing EMIB-T packaging technology as relief valve rather than threat: it could free front-end wafer business to flow to TSMC anyway.
- **Full-year 2026 revenue growth guided up to "slightly above 40%"** year-over-year, with management refusing to update the prior mid-to-high-50% five-year AI CAGR number except to say it's "stronger and stronger."

## What's changing
- **HPC now dominates the revenue mix at 66%** of quarterly revenue, up sharply quarter-over-quarter, while smartphone continues to shrink as a share.
- **Gross margin is about to take a real hit from the N2 ramp** — guided down 170bps sequentially to 66%, with 2nm ramp-up alone diluting margin by 3-4 points, only partially offset by pricing power and cost efforts.
- **Mature-node demand is bifurcating sharply**: power management ICs and CMOS image sensors (both AI-data-center-adjacent) are in genuine shortage, while everything else in mature nodes — consumer, commodity — remains soft. This is not a broad-based mature-node recovery, despite some analyst framing.
- **TSMC now openly resents its margin position relative to memory makers** — Wei, on Samsung's memory profits: "I'm jealousy about it... 86% gross margin? About 68%, I would be happy about that." This is a rare admission that foundry economics currently lag the AI supply chain's most scarce component.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **No multi-year CapEx or revenue guidance, despite direct, repeated requests.** Management explicitly declined the ask (twice) for a 3-year CapEx number like they gave during the 2021 upcycle, instead offering only "significantly higher than significantly higher" — a real step back in forward visibility even as the growth story gets stronger.
- **Demand-planning methodology is admittedly unreliable.** Wei's own words: customers each tell him aggressive numbers because "that's a CEO's job... you put all the truths together, it's not the truth" — TSMC is deciding on tens of billions in capacity using inputs it knows are systematically inflated, corrected only by internal "judgment."
- **No answer on how far demand exceeds supply.** Asked directly whether the demand/supply gap for 3nm-and-below is 30-50% or larger, Wei declined entirely: "we don't have a number to share... the gap is very big."
- **High-NA lithography adoption is quietly stalling** — Wei confirms TSMC is still negotiating cost and maturity with ASML rather than committing, implicitly validating the analyst's die-stitching/field-size concern without saying so directly.
- **No mention of yield or execution risk on the Arizona ramp** — despite historical reporting on Arizona yield lagging Taiwan, this call contains zero acknowledgment of execution difficulty; only the acceleration message ("we try to speed it up as fast as possible").

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Customer concentration among a handful of AI hyperscalers growing structurally larger (management waves this off, pointing to new entrants) · whether TSMC will start financing/investing in customers' customers the way some AI compute deals now do (flatly ruled out) · competitive erosion from Samsung's cash-rich memory business and Intel's government-backed foundry push, plus Intel's EMIB-T packaging gaining traction · whether the advanced-packaging CapEx bucket (bundled with testing/mask-making at 10-20% of budget) is being deliberately obscured as packaging becomes strategically more important · High-NA EUV die-stitching limitations potentially capping future node economics · overall worry underneath all of it: **is TSMC's stated conviction in "multi-year AI demand" actually calibrated to real end-demand, or is it sizing capacity off the same aggregated, self-reinforcing hyperscaler CapEx signals everyone else in the AI stack is also over-trusting.**
