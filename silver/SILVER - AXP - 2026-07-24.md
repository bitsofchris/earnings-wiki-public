# AXP — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-24)

**The key idea:** Amex beat its own plan and is choosing to plow the overperformance back into the business rather than drop it to EPS — Steve Squeri said it outright: "We have a choice... we have chosen" reinvestment. The bet is that the Platinum refresh cycle, international product refreshes, dining-platform acquisitions, and early agentic-commerce positioning compound into a more durable growth machine than a bigger buyback would.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **Reinvesting the beat instead of banking it.** Revenue guidance was raised to 10% for the year, but EPS guidance held at $17.30–$17.90 because management chose to redirect the outperformance into acquisition, technology, and the newly announced TheFork deal rather than accelerate buybacks.
- **Building a "closed loop within the closed loop" in dining.** Resy, Tock, and the pending TheFork acquisition (50,000 restaurants, 11 European countries) aren't run as standalone P&Ls — Squeri was explicit they're acquisition and retention tools: "we don't look at the P&L per se." Resy restaurants see 2x card-member spend versus non-members.
- **Betting the data advantage carries into agentic commerce.** Amex's agentic insurance product and closed-loop data (what the customer wanted, what the merchant delivered) is the stated differentiator against fraud and "hallucinations" as agentic purchasing scales. Squeri's framing was notably early-stage: "we're not even in the early innings. We're in preseason here" — relevant context for anyone tracking how payment/trust infrastructure incumbents are positioning against agentic-commerce risk.
- **International running the same playbook with a twist.** ~80% of countries have had a Platinum refresh since 2003, driving 20% FX-adjusted international Platinum spend growth, but management is now differentiating investment by market (leaning hardest into the U.K., Mexico, Canada, Australia, Japan) rather than treating all geographies uniformly.
- **AI is already cutting cycle time, not headcount — yet.** Squeri cited a 30-40% reduction in coding cycle time, reinvested into a large tech backlog, plus a "lack of acceleration in hiring" of customer service and travel reps even as volume grows, with an expectation that headcount "normally decrease[s]... from an attrition perspective" over time.

## What's changing

- **Card fee growth is on a multi-year lag, and it's about to inflect.** Because Platinum members repriced starting in January are amortized over 12 months, the card-fee growth benefit takes roughly two years to fully hit the P&L; management guided fee growth to accelerate in Q3 and exit the year in the high teens, up from 15.4% now.
- **VCE ratio guidance nudged higher** to 44-45% for the full year (from an earlier expectation), reflecting stronger-than-planned spend in categories like airlines where members earn and burn rewards — a direct cost of the Platinum refresh's own success.
- **NII growth mechanically distorted by portfolio sales.** The Lowe's and Amazon small-business co-brand portfolio transfers are stripping roughly 1 point off spend growth and 2.5 points off NII growth by Q4, though management stressed the earnings impact is "negligible" and was baked into original guidance.
- **SME/middle-market softness is being answered with a new product.** Steve Squeri conceded fintech competitors (Ramp, Brex) have pressured the middle market, and Amex just launched "Center," a new expense-management platform, explicitly to defend and win back that segment.
- **Deposits are becoming a quiet second engine.** U.S. consumer/small-business deposit balances grew 9% year-over-year, with only ~10% of U.S. card members currently holding a deposit account — framed as a long runway rather than a mature product.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **The URR (ultimate redemption rate) assumption was quietly lowered**, providing "a small benefit in the quarter" to Membership Rewards cost — management characterized it as accounting "hygiene," but any downward revision to a redemption-rate assumption that benefits current-period expense is worth flagging as a soft earnings tailwind, however small.
- **Middle-market commercial growth admitted to be softer** than the rest of the book, with fintech competitors named directly — a rare specific admission of competitive pressure rather than the usual "strong across the board" framing.
- **No hard numbers were given on Resy/Tock/TheFork economics.** Repeated questions (Mark DeVries) about standalone ROI were answered entirely in qualitative terms ("it's a benefit," "we don't look at it that way") — a genuine data gap investors are being asked to take on faith.
- **Geopolitical/macro risk was raised and largely waved off.** Management acknowledged gas spend and Middle East travel disruption but called it immaterial at the macro level ("no evidence of a general slowdown") — plausible given broad-based 9%+ spend growth, but it's a claim resting on aggregate data that could mask more localized stress.
- **No mention of the broader premium-card competitive response** (no discussion of what Chase Sapphire, Capital One Venture X, or other issuers are doing in response to the Platinum refresh) — a conspicuous absence given how central "premium space leadership" is to the whole thesis.
