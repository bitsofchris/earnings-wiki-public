# DXCM — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-12)

**The key idea:** This is new CEO Jacob Leach's first call, and it doubles as a "line is fixed" narrative — Q4 revenue beat, gross margin snapped back 200+ bps as freight/scrap problems from earlier in 2025 resolved, and G7 15-day is now live everywhere. But the entire 2026 growth algebra still hinges on something DexCom doesn't control: a CMS Medicare coverage decision for non-insulin type 2 diabetics that management is "building for... as if it came tomorrow" without knowing if or when it lands.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Manufacturing crisis is over, and management says so plainly.** CFO Sylvain ties the Q4 gross margin recovery (63.5%, up 200+ bps sequentially) directly to "reestablish[ing] ocean shipping" and improved scrap rates — explicit acknowledgment that 2025's margin drag was self-inflicted supply chain execution, not pricing or demand.
2. **The company is pre-building capacity for a regulatory approval that hasn't happened.** On Medicare non-insulin type 2 coverage: "we are building for it as we speak... to be ready for it as if it came tomorrow." That's real capital risk on an unconfirmed CMS decision, with the RCT readout only promised "in the first half" of 2026.
3. **International is the long game, explicitly pushed past the forecast horizon.** Leach says international could eventually exceed the US market, but Sylvain immediately caveats "it is not going to be in the next five years" — a rare instance of management voluntarily un-selling their own bull case.
4. **G7 15-day margin upside is deferred, not immediate.** Sylvain: contribution "starts to contribute to margins even more in future years" as the installed base converts — 2026 guidance captures only the beginning of that curve.
5. **Utilization-by-cohort answer reads more like guidance protection than data.** Management describes non-insulin type 2 utilization as comparable to basal despite a structurally weaker clinical hook (no hypoglycemia risk), while simultaneously telling analysts to model "mix" rather than declining utilization — worth watching against actual registry data as coverage scales.

## Economy & consumer
- No macro or general-consumer commentary anywhere in the call — DexCom's demand story is entirely a function of insurance/reimbursement policy, not discretionary spending or employment.

- Patient base is growing "almost 20%" exiting the year per Sylvain, the closest thing to a consumer-demand metric on the call.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Q4 revenue +13% (above high end of guidance), international revenue +18% reported / 15% organic with France one of the fastest-growing markets on new type 2 access; free cash flow surpassed $1B for the first time in 2025.
- **Working:** Gross margin recovery — 200+ bps sequential improvement Q4 — tied to resolved freight and scrap issues; "reduction in the sensor deployment issues that we identified earlier last year."
- **Not working / still exposed:** CFO flags a temporary Q4 2026 margin dip when the Ireland facility fully turns on and fixed costs shift from OpEx to COGS against lower initial production volumes — a self-described "geography" issue that complicates YoY comparisons.
- Balance sheet moves: settled $1.2B in expiring convertible notes in cash and repurchased $300M of stock in Q4, while still holding ~$2B cash — capital return alongside heavy capex commitments.

## Investing & scarcity
- Ireland manufacturing facility is the single largest 2026 capital/opex commitment, turning on "likely in the fourth quarter" — costs flow through OpEx during ramp, then move into COGS once live.
- Product investment: G7 15-day (full US rollout since January), a newly cleared adhesive "patch" technology to extend sensor survival, Smart Basal early-access launch this month, Stelo nutrition database/app redesign, and DexCom Direct EHR integration onboarding at 160+ health systems.
- The real scarcity is **regulatory/reimbursement access**, not physical constraint — Sylvain: guidance "assumes the coverage landscape remains predominantly the same as it stands today." Management is chipping away at commercial PBM coverage incrementally (~6.5M of 12.5M target non-insulin type 2 commercial lives) while awaiting the bigger Medicare unlock.

- **Gap:** The RCT that's supposed to help unlock this coverage won't read out until "the first half" of 2026 — meaning capacity investment is running ahead of both the regulatory decision and the supporting clinical data.
- **Belief:** International will eventually be bigger than the US. **Action:** Adding a new international CGM tier/product in 2026 for underserved segments. **Gap:** Sylvain immediately caveats the timeline as "more than five years," undercutting the framing of this as a near-term growth driver.
