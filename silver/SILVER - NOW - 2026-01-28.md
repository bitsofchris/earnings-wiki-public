# NOW — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-28)

**The key idea:** ServiceNow used a blowout beat-and-raise quarter to aggressively rebut two narratives at once — that AI will disintermediate SaaS platforms, and that its recent M&A binge (Moveworks, then Armis and Vesa within days of each other) signals organic growth is running out of runway. The real story underneath the rhetoric: the company is stitching together a security/governance layer (visibility + identity + orchestration) explicitly because enterprises are afraid to let agentic AI run unsupervised, and it's betting a hybrid seat-plus-consumption pricing model captures that anxiety as revenue.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Governance anxiety is the actual product-market fit for agentic AI right now.** The Armis/Vesa acquisitions and the AI Control Tower pitch aren't about flashier automation — they're about answering CISOs' fear of ungoverned agents. Amit Zavery: "CISOs have told us this is the bottleneck preventing AI agent deployment at scale." An analyst's four-part customer worry list (real-time monitoring, kill switches, agent grading, red teaming) was met point-by-point, suggesting these are now table-stakes objections in every enterprise AI sales cycle.
2. **Consumption pricing is arriving but management is deliberately keeping it hybrid, not full metering.** Now Assist NNACV more than doubled year over year to $600M+ ACV, and 70%+ upsell expansion at renewal shows real reload/token-burn dynamics — but Zavery explicitly declined to go "100% consumption," citing customer demand for predictability over pure usage billing.
3. **The M&A messaging was defensive, not offensive.** McDermott spent an unusual amount of airtime insisting Armis and Vesa were not revenue-buying and that the company lost "about $10,000,000,000 in market cap" on deal-timing anxiety — a rare acknowledgment that the market punished the stock for stacking acquisitions, which he's now trying to walk back with a "no more large-scale M&A" pledge.
4. **The buyback signals management sees the pullback as overdone.** A $5B incremental authorization plus an immediate $2B ASR, explicitly framed around "the recent pullback in our stock," is a concrete capital-allocation bet that the M&A-driven selloff mispriced the business.
5. **Public sector demand was undamaged by the government shutdown** — deals simply queued behind the 40-day procurement minimum rather than disappearing, with global government business up 80% year over year, suggesting federal budget conditions were a timing headwind, not a demand headwind.

## Economy & consumer
- **Pipelines are "never been better," per McDermott** — enterprise CEOs are investing without waiting for formal budget cycles when ROI is provable, which he frames as evidence deals no longer require the friction of budget season.
- **Public sector shutdown was purely a procurement bottleneck**, not a demand cut: "What didn't happen in 2025 is only good news for 2026," with state/local and international government business (up 80% YoY) picking up slack.
- No traditional end-consumer read here (NOW sells to enterprises), but industry-level demand was broad: transportation/logistics NNACV grew over 80% YoY, business/consumer services over 70%, financial services over 40%.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broad-based beat**: subscription revenue +19.5% cc (150bps above guide), CRPO +21% cc (200bps above guide), operating margin 31% (100bps above guide), FY25 free cash flow margin 35% (100bps above raised guidance).
- **CRM closed its largest quarter in company history**, and Raptor DB Pro "more than tripled NNACV year on year," including deals over $130M — the platform-consolidation pitch is showing up in the numbers, not just the script.
- **What's not working, or at least what's costing money**: subscription gross margin is guided down to 82% for 2026, driven by the deliberate shift toward hyperscaler infrastructure, which Gina Mastantuono conceded carries "slightly lower gross margins" than internal capacity at this stage.
- **Renewal quality remains a strength**: 98% renewal rate, customers over $20M ACV up over 30% YoY, and one unnamed retailer expanded Now Assist entitlements 13x at renewal — evidence that early AI deployments are compounding rather than plateauing for at least some cohort of customers.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Three acquisitions in rapid succession** (Moveworks closed after a 9-month process, then Armis and Vesa announced within days of each other) assembled what McDermott calls the three layers needed for agentic AI: visibility (Armis, already covering 40%+ of the Fortune 100), identity governance (Vesa's access-graph technology), and orchestration (ServiceNow's existing platform).
- **Explicit ceiling on further large M&A**: "We do not have a large-scale M&A on the roadmap," and post-Armis "we do not see any other large white spaces" in security — a deliberate signal after the market punished deal-stacking with roughly $10B in lost market cap.
- **The binding constraint is gross margin economics of the infrastructure shift**, not compute or talent scarcity per se — moving workload to hyperscalers costs margin now in exchange for capacity and geo-expansion, a tradeoff management says it will make "every single day" because it's offset by opex efficiencies elsewhere.
- **Armis integration financial impact is modest and quantified in advance**: expected ~100bps subscription revenue contribution and up to 50bps operating margin headwind in 2026, closing second half of the year pending regulatory clearance.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Stated belief**: enterprise AI adoption is inevitable and ServiceNow is the "semantic layer" and "AI control tower" that makes it safe and governable — IDC's 2.2B AI agents by 2030 projection is cited as the addressable opportunity.
- **Action behind it**: concrete platform integrations (Microsoft Agent 365, Anthropic Claude for a coding "build agent," OpenAI for voice/speech-to-speech in CRM) that embed specific third-party model capabilities into named product lines rather than staying purely infrastructure-agnostic.
- **Gap worth flagging**: the "AI eats software" rebuttal is stated with total confidence, but management's own guidance shows only a 1-1.5 point acceleration to 20% growth for 2026 versus ~19.5-21% actuals in 2025 — the growth deceleration risk is being managed with commitments (buybacks, integration announcements, security M&A) that are more about defending narrative and stock price than accelerating the underlying growth rate itself.
- **Pricing evolution is directionally consumption but the actual commitment is conservative**: token-reload dynamics are real and growing ("hockey stick," per McDermott), but the company continues selling predictable seat-plus-pack bundles rather than pivoting the core business model.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No commitment to full consumption-based pricing.** Zavery: going "complete 100% consumption may be too early" — hybrid remains the model, explicitly to avoid customers "staying on the sidelines" over unpredictable spend.
- **Declined to give a hard number on the LLM inferencing mix.** Asked directly what percentage of AI workloads would run on ServiceNow's own LLM versus third-party frontier models in 2026, Zavery gave a qualitative answer (frontier models "in the long term" for inferencing, but sovereign/private-data-center needs keeping proprietary models relevant) rather than a figure.
- **Armis financial detail deferred**: Mastantuono declined to give granular Armis financials pre-close, promising more color at the Financial Analyst Day instead.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **M&A rationale and cadence dominated the Q&A** — multiple questions probed whether Armis/Vesa signal a shift away from organic growth, whether more large deals are coming, and what the margin dilution looks like; management answered directly and repeatedly, almost defensively, suggesting real investor anxiety here that the prepared remarks didn't fully neutralize.
- **Gross margin mechanics** (hyperscaler mix shift, LLM inference costs) were probed for permanence versus one-time headwind — answered as structural but offset by opex leverage below the line.
- **Pricing model evolution** (seats vs. consumption) came up from two different analysts, both circling the same question of whether Now Assist success eventually cannibalizes the per-seat model — management held firm on hybrid being the durable answer, not a transitional one.
- **Compressed worry**: is ServiceNow's security-layer land grab (Armis + Vesa + Moveworks in one quarter) actually additive, or is it management moving fast to control the narrative before slowing organic growth becomes undeniable?
