# CRM — fiscal 2027-Q1 call (2026-05-27)

**The key idea:** Salesforce is trying to convert a spectacular but still-unmonetized usage story — 28.6 trillion tokens, 3.8B agentic work units, both roughly doubling quarter-over-quarter — into a credible case for second-half revenue re-acceleration, while cRPO (the actual forward revenue signal) has landed exactly in line with guidance for two quarters running. The gap between the usage fireworks and the bookings reality is the real story, papered over by a record $25B buyback and a heavily stage-managed video call featuring three customer testimonials.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Usage growth is not yet proven revenue growth.** Token volume (+152% QoQ) and agentic work units (+111% QoQ) are eye-popping, but cRPO growth of ~13% in constant currency was, per an analyst, "spot in line with your guidance" for a second consecutive quarter — the company has yet to show usage acceleration flowing through to bookings acceleration.
2. **AI coding tools are substituting for engineering headcount, not adding to it.** Benioff was direct: "we have not been loading up a lot more engineers" with ~15,000 engineers flat for two years, while AI tooling reportedly doubled features/code shipped YoY. Headcount growth is being directed almost entirely into Miguel Milano's sales organization instead.
3. **Capital return, not new investment, is the headline financial move.** The $25B ASR (half the total $50B authorization) cut diluted share count 10% YoY and added $0.23 to non-GAAP EPS — a financial-engineering lever pulled hard in what Benioff twice calls "this unusual time," alongside a new debt issuance that created a 5-point headwind to free cash flow guidance.
4. **Headless/MCP is a real monetization pivot, introduced mid-quarter with no established pricing model yet.** Leadership admits they hadn't even used the word "headless" as of the prior earnings call; now describe it as "a new monetization area" with Srini Tallapragada conceding "we'll figure out the right value" with customers and ISVs — a strategy still being priced in real time.
5. **Commerce Cloud and Tableau are the quiet drag nobody wants to dwell on.** Both cRPO and revenue outperformance were explicitly "partially offset by softness in Commerce Cloud and in Tableau" — mentioned once by Robin Washington and never revisited despite three separate analyst questions about growth durability.

## Economy & consumer

- The closest proxy is customer commentary: PenFed's CEO noted that the credit union industry has consolidated from **18,400 institutions to 8,000** over 25 years, a competitive-pressure signal from financial services but not a macro read from Salesforce itself.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Sales, Service, and Slack collectively drove **more than 60% of Q1 net new AOV**; Agentforce ARR crossed **$1 billion**; combined AI/data ARR (Agentforce + Data 360 + Informatica) reached **$3.4 billion**. A1E/A4X premium SKU bookings grew **nearly 60% YoY**.
- **Working:** Informatica integration is "already unlocking synergies, with revenue growth accelerating since the acquisition" — Milano noted Informatica went from single-digit bookings growth pre-acquisition to bookings that "re-accelerated... off the chart beyond anybody's expectation" in Q2 (though qualified as being helped by on-prem renewal timing).
- **Not working:** Commerce Cloud and Tableau bookings/renewals are explicit, named drags on both revenue and cRPO — flagged as ongoing in the forward guide, not a one-quarter blip.
- **Slack is the standout:** nearly half of the quarter's $1M+ deals involved Slack (**up 80% YoY**), Slack AWUs grew **~350% QoQ**, and the community built **3 million custom apps in Q1, 8x quarter-over-quarter** — Benioff floated Slack becoming "a $10 billion cloud" without formally guiding to it.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The $25B ASR is the dominant capital story:** decreased Q1 share count by 103 million shares (11% of shares outstanding), added $0.23 non-GAAP EPS — described as underscoring "confidence in the future" but functionally a large financial lever amid "this unusual time."
- **Engineering headcount is the binding internal constraint**, held flat near 15,000 for two years by design, with AI coding tools (both OpenAI's Codex and Anthropic's Claude, both vendor-customers of Salesforce) absorbing the productivity gap rather than new hires.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Stated belief:** net-new-AOV growth will keep outpacing AOV growth through H1 FY2027, translating into "organic revenue re-acceleration in the second half of FY2027" and eventually the FY2030 "Rule of 50" framework.
- **Actual action behind it:** Headless/MCP rollout (Coworker, Slack MCP, API monetization) and the Qualified/Piper acquisition are concrete product and M&A moves aimed at that goal — but the pricing/monetization model for Headless is explicitly still being worked out with customers, not locked in.
- **The gap:** cRPO — the metric management itself calls "a leading indicator" — has not beaten guidance for two straight quarters even as usage metrics exploded. The re-acceleration thesis currently rests on anecdote (Adecco, Anthropic usage stories) and confidence language ("we're not magicians... but we were very firm") rather than demonstrated bookings inflection.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Attrition/churn guidance flatly declined:** Benioff pre-empted the question himself — "we're not going to give guidance on attrition on all these things. We never have" — even while Milano volunteered that net-new-AOV (bookings minus attrition) is the one metric the whole org is now compensated on.
- **No direct token-to-revenue conversion given** despite being asked twice; Robin Washington redirected to the AWU-customer-spend multiple (top 10 AWU customers spend 1.5x more) rather than a unit-economics answer.
- **Headless monetization terms left open:** "we'll figure out the right value" — no committed pricing framework for MCP/API consumption yet, months after the strategy's public launch.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered around one core anxiety: **can spectacular usage growth (tokens, AWUs) be trusted to convert into bookings and revenue**, given cRPO has landed exactly in line with guidance rather than beating it for two consecutive quarters.
- A second cluster probed the **Headless/MCP build-vs-buy risk** — whether opening APIs to coding agents lets customers or competitors abstract value out of Salesforce rather than deepen dependence on it; management answered with customer anecdotes (Adecco, Anthropic) rather than retention or revenue data.
- A third question pushed on margin durability under surging token/inference costs; management's answer leaned on flat engineering headcount and AI-driven productivity rather than disclosing inference cost trends.
- **Compressed worry:** the usage numbers are real, but nobody outside the company can yet verify they're worth what management says they're worth.
