# KHC — fiscal 2026 Q1 call (2026-05-06)

**The key idea:** Kraft Heinz is mid-turnaround, using a $600M investment fund (still mostly undeployed) to buy back market share it lost over the prior decade, and the early read — mixed-adjusted share loss cut from 90bps to 30bps in a year — is real, even if management is deliberately not banking Q1's Easter- and weather-flattered strength into guidance. The tension: a consumer that "can only absorb so much price" is colliding with input-cost inflation (energy, resins) reaccelerating due to geopolitical conflict, and management is explicitly choosing to eat more of that cost via productivity rather than price or investment cuts — a bet that could get tested hard in Q3-Q4.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Share losses are decelerating, not just stabilizing.** Mixed-adjusted market share loss went from -90bps a year ago to -30bps year-to-date, with the "Taste Elevation" platform moving from 24% to 87% of items holding or gaining share — a genuine inflection CEO Steve Cahillane attributes to a post-split-pause "maniacal focus on growth and execution."
2. **Management is refusing to take credit for the whole quarter.** Cahillane volunteered that Easter timing and winter-storm pantry-loading flattered results, and Andre Maciel confirmed guidance explicitly excludes "the first quarter overdelivery" — an unusually disciplined stance that adds credibility to the parts of the beat they do claim.
3. **The consumer is bifurcating and the low end is exposed.** SNAP transaction declines are already tracking in line with or worse than plan since February; non-SNAP household strength offset it in Q1 but is not expected to hold, and part of the $600M was specifically steered toward "opening price points" to protect SNAP-exposed shoppers.
4. **Inflation is a second-half story building now.** Energy and resin costs are spiking on conflict-driven volatility; energy is hedged for the year but resin hedges run out mid-Q3, meaning the P&L impact lands in Q3-Q4 — a live risk to the current 20%-price/80%-productivity mix.
5. **Portfolio triage is active, not rhetorical.** Frozen got downgraded from "Win Big" to "Hold" while hydration and cheese moved up — a real reallocation of investment priority, described as ongoing ("we reserve the right to continue to get smarter") rather than a one-time reclassification.

## Economy & consumer
- **SNAP headwind is materializing on schedule.** Maciel: SNAP transactions "already down in line, if not even a little more than expected" since February/March; management still guides a full 100bps SNAP headwind for the year and doesn't know if offsetting non-SNAP strength will hold.
- **Away-from-home is macro-pressured but a stated strategic bet.** Cahillane: away-from-home is "under a fair amount of pressure based on the macroeconomic environment... both in this country and around the world," yet KHC is leaning into Heinz distribution there as "one of the greatest away-from-home brands."
- **Pricing philosophy has flipped from "take price" to "protect the consumer."** Cahillane: the pricing environment is "very rational" post-inflationary-cycle, and the company has actively rolled back some pricing where it "gone a little too far."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Taste Elevation is the standout.** Share performance there jumped from 24% to 87% holding/gaining share in one year — the clearest evidence the investment fund is working where deployed.
- **Meats remain a stated weak spot.** Cahillane, on the leaky-bucket problem: "we don't like leaky buckets, and we're gonna look to plug those at the same time as we lean against our biggest and best opportunities" — an admission meats need turnaround investment separate from the Win Big brands.
- **Gross margin beat is partly one-time.** Maciel disclosed 40-50bps of the quarter's margin gain came from non-recurring items (excess byproduct sales, deferred factory maintenance), with full-year guidance unchanged at a 25-75bps headwind — a candid margin-quality caveat, not just a beat-and-raise.
- **Simplifying the U.S. operating model.** New North America lead Nicolas Amaya is restructuring for "stronger accountabilities, stronger empowerment," paired with added sales/marketing headcount — an organizational bet behind the commercial turnaround.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$600M fund is still mostly unspent** and concentrated on Win Big categories (hydration, cheese, sauces, mac & cheese) with named launches: Power Mac & Cheese (35,000 accounts already after an April launch), a Lunchables renovation next month, Philadelphia Lactose Free later in the year, and Capri Sun's hydration platform.
- **Marketing spend up 37% YoY in Q1**, guided to at least 5.5% of revenue for the full year (≈20% full-year increase) — spend is being reallocated gradually toward the whole portfolio, not just last year's priority brands.
- **The real constraint is consumer price tolerance, not capital.** Maciel confirmed guidance bakes in pricing only 20% of input inflation (versus the historically "normal" ~50/50 split), relying on productivity above 4% of COGS to cover the rest — a deliberate choice to protect the consumer over margin.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **They believe H2 improves on lapping effects, not new demand.** Indonesia alone was a 70bps top-line headwind in Q1 that "should all go away" in H2; combined with European Heinz investment and U.S. away-from-home share gains, Maciel frames H2 as "balanced contribution across those levers" — but explicitly not embedding any of Q1's upside.
- **Action matches belief on brand investment**: real dollars are already flowing to specific SKUs with early distribution wins (35,000 accounts for Power Mac & Cheese pre-sellout-data), showing conviction ahead of proof.
- **Gap: inflation defense is still theoretical.** Management says "first line of defense is productivity" and would "lean into" investment rather than cut it if inflation worsens into 2027, but offered no specific mechanism if resin/energy costs stay elevated past Q3 hedges — a stated belief without a committed action plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Won't bank the Q1 beat.** Cahillane declined to raise guidance despite share and margin outperformance: "we're not really calling for an acceleration... being prudent."
- **Won't say how they'd defend margins in 2027 if inflation stays elevated.** Asked directly whether they'd harvest SG&A/investment to protect the bottom line, Cahillane deflected to "productivity" and "it's unknown what the fourth quarter and 2027 will bring," declining to specify a contingency plan tied to the Middle East conflict's uncertain trajectory.
- **Non-SNAP household strength left unexplained.** Maciel flagged the offsetting Q1 strength in non-SNAP households as real but said "it's hard to predict... if that strength... will hold" — an acknowledged unknown rather than a forecast.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Questions clustered on **quality of the Q1 beat** (Easter/weather noise vs. real share gains), **marketing ROI and allocation** across the $600M and Win Big categories, **gross margin sustainability**, **cash flow and debt paydown mechanics**, and **inflation risk into Q3/2027**.
- The recurring undertone: analysts kept probing whether management would eventually pull back investment to protect margins as inflation reaccelerates — management repeatedly declined to concede that trade-off, insisting productivity absorbs the pressure instead.
- Compressed worry: **is this turnaround's investment spend durable once real (not hedge-protected) input cost inflation hits in Q3, or does KHC eventually have to choose between the consumer and the margin?**
