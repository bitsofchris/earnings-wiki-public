# GILD — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** This is a "good problem" quarter — HIV and liver franchises are outrunning guidance even through a modeled $900M Medicare headwind, buying Gilead room to fund a genuine strategic pivot in cell therapy (autologous CAR-T going ex-growth, capital rotating into in vivo platforms) while the Yeztugo launch is treated as an access-unlock story rather than a demand story. The tension: management's confidence rests almost entirely on coverage/access metrics (75% ahead of schedule) while conceding the actual prescription data used to track real-world uptake is too noisy to trust yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Yeztugo is an access story, not yet a demand story.** Gilead hit "75% coverage goal, nearly 3 months ahead of our target," but Johanna Mercier repeatedly frames Q3's $39M as mostly switch volume from existing PrEP patients (injectables, Descovy, generics), not new-to-market growth — the naive-patient thesis is deferred to "as the market grows... with time."
2. **Kite's cell-therapy business is shrinking and management isn't projecting a turnaround.** Sales fell 11% YoY and QoQ, full-year guide cut to "approximately a 10% decline," and Cindy Perettie's "we anticipate these headwinds to continue in the near future" is a rare direct admission of a franchise in structural decline — the response is not fixing autologous CAR-T but buying into in vivo (Interius, Pregene) as the next platform.
3. **The Part D headwind is being spent as a talking point, not hidden.** O'Day leads with HIV growing "despite" a $900M Part D redesign hit, and Mercier does the analyst's math for them ("if Part D redesign hadn't played out, we would probably be around... 8% to 9%") — an unusually transparent framing that also conveniently makes a strong quarter look even stronger.
4. **A one-time $400M accounting item flattered EPS, and management flagged it themselves.** The $400M IP-royalty recognition (from a 2018 asset sale) added $0.25 to non-GAAP EPS; Dickinson is explicit that core EPS growth was 10% "excluding" it, and O'Day's "even excluding" framing in the open shows they're managing the optics proactively rather than letting analysts discover it.
5. **Regulatory/BD posture is aggressive but non-committal on specifics.** Multiple sBLA/BLA filings (Trodelvy TNBC, bulevirtide HDV) are underway with 2026 launch targets, but on anito-cel filing timing and drug-pricing deal terms, management explicitly declines: "we haven't communicated what our filing dates are nor will we."

## Economy & consumer
- **Drug-pricing negotiation is the macro backdrop, not GDP or rates.** O'Day frames ongoing administration talks as "constructive," citing the PEPFAR/State Department lenacapavir deal as proof of alignment, while declining to give any Gilead-specific numbers on what a broader pricing deal might cost them.
- **PrEP market growth is real and accelerating** — "approximately 14% year-over-year" this quarter, expected to hold at "14% to 15%" into 2026, fueled by awareness campaigns and DTC advertising, not a Gilead-specific catalyst alone.
- **IQVIA script data is explicitly unreliable right now** — Mercier: "the volatility with IQVIA is definitely real right now, and I think it's going to take a little while to settle," an unusually candid admission that the external proxy investors use to track the launch doesn't match internal tracking cleanly.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **HIV core franchise re-accelerating:** full-year HIV growth guidance raised to "approximately 5%," up from 3% last quarter, with Biktarvy hitting a record ~52% US market share and Descovy PrEP at a record >45% share.
- **Livdelzi is the standout non-HIV asset:** "35% sequential growth," crossed $100M quarterly for the first time, now #1 in second-line PBC in the US, partly aided by a competitor's US withdrawal — a tailwind, not pure execution.
- **Kite is the clear underperformer:** sales down 11% YoY/QoQ on "continued competitive headwinds from in and out of class therapies," with management conceding "it's clear that we have more to do before all eligible patients have the opportunity to benefit."
- **Veklury's decline (-60% YoY) is fully attributed to fewer COVID hospitalizations** — a demand-driven wind-down, not a competitive loss, and guided to ~$1B for the full year.

## Investing & scarcity
- **In vivo cell therapy is the new capital priority:** the Interius acquisition and a $120M upfront to Pregene Biopharma signal a bet that off-the-shelf in vivo platforms can leapfrog autologous CAR-T's cost and manufacturing constraints — Dickinson calls it "early days," an honest hedge on a bet sized in the hundreds of millions.
- **BD cadence is disciplined but stated as ongoing:** Dickinson commits to adding "best-in-class therapies" like Livdelzi "every 2 to 3 years at a minimum" across liver, oncology, cell therapy, virology and immunology — a specific enough cadence to hold them to later.
- **The real constraint is logistical/access friction, not capital or supply:** onboarding Yeztugo requires specialty-pharmacy white-bagging (currently "70% to 80%" of volume) transitioning to buy-and-bill as J-codes and formulary integration mature — a multi-quarter operational grind, explicitly not something that "turn[s] on just overnight."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Yeztugo coverage expansion (targeting 90% by mid-2026) will translate into a 2026 revenue ramp. **Action:** full-year 2025 Yeztugo guide held at "around $150 million," a modest number given the access wins — the guidance hasn't yet caught up to the access optimism, which is itself a tell that management is hedging the conversion timeline even while touting the coverage milestone.
- **Belief:** No major LOEs until 2036 means durable growth. **Action:** heavy pipeline investment (ARTISTRY-1/2 for next-gen HIV, anito-cel, Trodelvy line extensions) to replace/extend franchises well before that date — consistent, not just rhetorical.
- **Belief:** In vivo CAR-T could "unlock broad access" beyond autologous limits. **Action:** two inbound deals (Interius, Pregene) but Perettie/Dietmar are careful to call this "early days" with no near-term revenue attached — belief is ahead of committed dollars here, appropriately hedged.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Anito-cel filing timeline:** Cindy Perettie flatly declined: "we haven't communicated what our filing dates are nor will we," while still committing to a second-half-2026 launch target — guidance on outcome, silence on process.
- **BD target specifics:** Dickinson wouldn't confirm NASH or any named indication when asked directly, keeping optionality across "all of our areas of strategic interest."
- **Drug-pricing deal economics:** O'Day gave only structural color (IP location, tax footprint, $32B US investment commitment) and explicitly deferred to "publicly disclosed" information only when asked about deal scale.
