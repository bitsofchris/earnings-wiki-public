# SNPS — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-02-25)

**The key idea:** Synopsys is leaning on Ansys's outsized Q1 (its old December fiscal year-end folding into SNPS's Q1) to mask a genuinely rough patch in Design IP, where growth is "muted" and management openly admits a self-inflicted resourcing/prioritization problem delayed titles for hyperscaler customers. The bet is that AI design-start strength, an NVIDIA GPU-acceleration partnership, and eventual Synopsys+Ansys joint products (not monetizing until FY'27) justify staying the course, while the ARC processor IP divestiture to GlobalFoundries signals a narrowing, not broadening, of IP ambitions.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **IP is the weak link, and management's explanation is a scheduling excuse, not a demand problem — yet.** Design IP revenue fell ~6% YoY, and CEO Sassine Ghazi admits the company "prioritized" resources in a way that delayed specific hyperscaler titles: "it was putting the right resources and priority of the resources to deliver on time and schedule for these titles." The fix is "Q4 weighted," per CFO Shelagh Glaser — meaning the credibility of "sequential improvement" hinges entirely on execution in the back half.
2. **Ansys's Q1 strength is partly a fiscal-calendar artifact, not pure demand signal.** Glaser confirms the historically huge Ansys December quarter landed inside SNPS's Q1 this year, calling it "a real standout," and expects this timing effect to normalize as sales realign to Synopsys's fiscal year — meaning Q1's beat overstates the run-rate.
3. **AI is being framed as a moat, not a threat — a claim SNPS has strong incentive to make.** "AI isn't disrupting our business. It's amplifying our strategic advantage," Ghazi says, arguing deterministic, silicon-proven EDA solvers can't be replicated by probabilistic AI models. This is a direct rebuttal to investor concern about AI disrupting software incumbents broadly.
4. **The NVIDIA partnership is a real monetization lever, not just a press release.** GPU-accelerated versions of EDA/Ansys tools are planned for 2026 with pricing tied to a 15-20x performance uplift — a genuine new pricing axis layered onto the existing CPU-based licenses, plus an Omniverse digital-twin angle for physical AI.
5. **Portfolio-narrowing via divestiture continues:** selling ARC processor IP to GlobalFoundries while doubling down on interconnect/foundation IP (PCIe, HBM, LPDDR, UCIe, 224G SerDes) — a bet that hyperscalers need one-generation-ahead interface IP more than embedded processor cores.

## Economy & consumer
- **Two-speed design economy persists.** "The multibillion-dollar AI infrastructure build-out continues unabated... At the same time, design starts in markets like consumer, automotive and industrial remain subdued despite signals of modest recovery," Ghazi said — a durable bifurcation, not a one-quarter blip.
- **China keeps shrinking for classic Synopsys.** Ex-Ansys, China revenue "declined slightly year-over-year," attributed to entity-list and technology restrictions hitting IP hardest since customers can substitute to domestic foundries; those who still can buy Synopsys "absolutely still prefer" it over domestic alternatives.
- No direct consumer-facing commentary — SNPS sells to enterprise engineering teams, not end consumers, so this key is appropriately thin; Ansys's automotive/industrial exposure (>90% of top 100 auto suppliers) is the closest proxy for downstream demand health, and that exposure was called out as resilient.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: hardware-assisted verification and advanced-node EDA.** A "marquee emulation win versus the incumbent at a leading AI HPC customer," plus Fusion Compiler/PrimeTime hitting "100% usage on critical tape-outs at 2-nanometer and below" — genuine competitive share gains at the leading edge.
- **Working: Ansys cross-sell motion is live, not aspirational.** Sales teams are already "cross-trained" to sell each other's products, and cost-synergy realization ($400M run-rate target by year 3) is being pulled forward into years 1-2 (2026).
- **Not working: Design IP margins compressed alongside revenue.** IP operating margin was 16.2%, versus 47.3% for Design Automation — Glaser flags this stays "muted" through the year since SNPS keeps investment level flat despite depressed revenue, betting on a later payoff.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Balance sheet fully de-levered from the Ansys deal.** "We have repaid the entirety of the $4.3 billion term loans," with the board replenishing a $2B buyback authorization — capital allocation priority is "investing in the business" first, buybacks opportunistic.
- **The real constraint is internal execution bandwidth, not capital or demand.** Management's own words concede a prioritization failure caused missed IP delivery schedules for hyperscalers — a people/scheduling scarcity, self-created, that they say they're now fixing by reallocating "the right resources."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Long-term growth algorithm is unchanged — EDA double-digit, IP mid-teens, simulation/analysis double-digit — and "2026 is the year we begin delivering on the technology promise of Synopsys plus Ansys."
- **Action:** Joint Synopsys+Ansys products won't even monetize until FY'27, per Ghazi: "we are anticipating the monetization of the joint solution to start in FY '27." That's a full year gap between the stated 2026 inflection narrative and any actual revenue proof point — the market is being asked to pre-fund a 2027 outcome on a 2026 story.
- **Belief:** IP business model is shifting toward value/royalty-based monetization given rising customization demand.
- **Action:** Ghazi says SNPS is "in active conversations" and expects some to "close... into an actual business in FY '26" but caveats "you won't see it in terms of that upside until we deliver and the customer tape out" — meaning even successful deal-closing this year produces no near-term revenue.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to size the ARC divestiture.** Asked directly how big ARC revenue is, Glaser said flatly: "No, we haven't provided that" — leaving investors unable to gauge how much of the "sequential IP improvement" story is inflated by a business already being sold off.
- **No specific dollar figure on Ansys cross-sell revenue this year**, only reaffirming the existing $400M run-rate-by-year-4 target without disclosing current-year contribution.
- **Didn't commit to full-year China growth direction** beyond describing puts and takes qualitatively.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **IP credibility was the dominant anxiety**, hit from multiple angles: is the "second-half pickup" believable given milestone-based IP revenue is structurally going away, is the hyperscaler resourcing gap actually fixed, and does late IP delivery risk losing design-in slots to competitors altogether ("is there a risk that Synopsys may miss customer design starts").
- **Ansys forecastability** — analysts probed the ASC 606 revenue-recognition variability inherited from Ansys's old fiscal calendar and whether Q1's beat is repeatable or a timing quirk.
- **Comparisons to Cadence's organic growth** came up directly, with one analyst noting investors are "excited for your organic growth to sort of trend in that direction" — an implicit signal the market sees SNPS underperforming its closest peer on an organic basis.
- Compressed one-sentence worry: **is the IP business's underperformance a temporary, fixable scheduling glitch, or early evidence Synopsys is ceding ground it once dominated?**
