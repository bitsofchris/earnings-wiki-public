# KLAC — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** KLA is riding two structural upgrades at once — process control intensity rising across a broadening leading-edge/DRAM customer base, and a brand-new ~$11B advanced-packaging SAM that barely existed a few years ago — while deliberately withholding hard 2026 numbers and even discontinuing backlog disclosure. The tension: management is *very* constructive in tone ("customers... wanting to make sure they're securing slots") but backs it with qualitative color, not bookings data, right as China exposure shrinks under new export controls.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Advanced packaging went from rounding error to real segment.** KLA's own SAM estimate for advanced packaging is now ~$11B (~6% KLA share, up from ~1% "not much more than a few years ago"), with 2025 packaging revenue guided >$925M, up ~70% YoY — customers *pulled KLA in*, not the reverse: "we did not come to them. It was almost the other way. They said, we want you to provide this capability."
2. **DRAM/HBM has flipped from laggard to leader in process control intensity.** CEO Wallace: in some layers DRAM now sees "higher sensitivity requirements... than we're even seeing in logic," driven by EUV insertion (+~1pt intensity) and HBM's zero-tolerance yield requirement since "the device is only as good as the weakest DRAM."
3. **Management explicitly downplays AI-capex-driven overheating risk** by inverting the usual worry: the constraint isn't demand collapsing, it's that the semi-cap supply chain can't physically build fabs fast enough to match announced AI infrastructure spend — "not enough wafers will be available... it's unlikely to overheat."
4. **A disclosure downgrade removes a key visibility tool right when investors want it most.** RPO/backlog reporting was discontinued this fiscal year (citing cross-industry inconsistency), replaced with vaguer "lead times of 7-9 months" language — convenient timing given several analysts pressed specifically for order/bookings confirmation of the 2026 growth story.
5. **China is being weaned off deliberately and by force.** Mix falls from an elevated 39% in September to a guided high-20s in December and expected mid-20s in 2026, with $300-350M in identified revenue lost to new export controls through calendar 2026 — offset, so far, by leading-edge and DRAM strength elsewhere.

## Economy & consumer
- **No direct consumer read** — KLA sells to fabs, not end consumers; demand signal here is proxy: hyperscaler/AI-lab capex plans and premium mobile device cycles, not household spending.
- **China is normalizing down, not just cyclically but structurally** under "extended export controls from the U.S. government," with an estimated **$300M-$350M** hit to KLA revenue spread across December quarter and calendar 2026.
- Wallace frames 2026 optimism around a **broadening investor base** beyond the single historically dominant leading-edge customer, as second-tier foundries try to catch up on advanced nodes.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** Revenue ($3.21B) and EPS ($8.81 non-GAAP) both beat guidance midpoints; gross margin 62.5% beat by 50bps; record quarterly free cash flow of **$1.066B**; services grew 16% YoY to $745M with management now expecting **12-14% service growth to continue into 2026**, partly from new packaging and HBM service opportunities "I don't think we fully anticipated... a couple of years [ago]."
- **What's not working / soft spots:** China mix compression is real and structural, not just seasonal; tariffs remain a persistent 50-100bps gross margin drag that CFO Higgins hopes becomes "less of a headwind over time" through supply-chain rerouting, not resolution; legacy/non-leading-edge WFE has little growth ("the rest of the legacy market, I'm not so sure there's much growth there").
- **DRAM/foundry-logic mix is genuinely volatile quarter to quarter** — foundry/logic guided down from ~74% to ~59% of semiconductor systems revenue, which one analyst flagged as a $300M+ sequential swing; management attributes it to China normalization plus a DRAM uptick, not demand weakness.

## Investing & scarcity
- KLA itself isn't the capital allocator here so much as the enabler — but its own capital actions are telling: **12% dividend hike (16th consecutive annual increase)** to $7.60/share annualized, plus a new **$5B buyback authorization**, on top of $3.09B total capital returned over the trailing 12 months. This is a company funding shareholder returns aggressively even while declining to commit to hard 2026 revenue numbers.
- **The real scarcity constraint flagged is physical: cleanroom/fab space**, not capital or even orders. Higgins: space constraints "could affect some timing as we move into the second half of next year and into '27," particularly for new memory fab ramps.
- Secondary constraint: **the memory industry underestimated its own capacity needs.** Wallace: memory customers are now realizing "there might be more opportunity in memory than they thought" as capacity announcements accelerated versus guidance given "even a few months ago" — a scarcity narrative shifting in real time from GPUs → packaging → now memory.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** 2026 will be a growth year for WFE and advanced packaging, with 1H26 "roughly flat to modestly up" vs 2H25 and acceleration in 2H26, driven by broadening leading-edge investment and continued DRAM/HBM momentum.
- **Action backing the belief:** OpEx guided *up* to ~$635M for the December quarter specifically "to support expected revenue growth," and management reaffirms its long-run 40-50% incremental operating margin target assuming above-trend growth continues.
- **The gap:** none of this belief is backed by disclosed hard order data — RPO disclosure was dropped this year, and CFO Higgins repeatedly hedges timing ("we'll see how that plays out," "too early to provide precise... guidance"). The optimism is qualitative ("body language from customers," "conversations") rather than quantitative, which is a notably softer evidentiary bar than KLA used to offer investors.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused precise calendar 2026 revenue guidance** outright — "it is still too early" — despite giving directional shape (flat 1H, accelerating 2H).
- **Discontinued RPO/backlog disclosure** entirely this fiscal year, replacing a hard number analysts had used to track order momentum with softer "lead times of 7-9 months" commentary — a meaningful reduction in disclosure granularity, justified as industry-standardization but landing right as bookings visibility becomes most contested.
- **Declined to attribute upside to high-NA EUV adoption** for 2026, clarifying that near-term N2 ramp economics don't materially depend on it: "It isn't influenced by the adoption of high NA in that time frame, certainly not in a material way."

## The street — what analysts asked
- Multiple analysts (Arcuri, Vivek Arya) pushed directly for **backlog/RPO numbers** to sanity-check the "customers securing slots" narrative — management redirected to qualitative lead-time commentary instead, having eliminated the disclosure that would have settled the question.
- Recurring theme: **is the AI-driven WFE story a data-backed acceleration or a hopeful extrapolation from public capex announcements** — see the direct Lam Research $100B/$8B WFE ratio question and the "$0.5 trillion backlog" AI accelerator question, both of which management answered by reframing supply constraints as the real limiter rather than confirming demand math.
- Secondary cluster: **gross margin mix mechanics** (packaging dilutive now, services dilutive on gross but accretive on operating margin, tariffs a persistent drag) — analysts clearly modeling whether 2026's mix shift (more packaging, less China, more DRAM) helps or hurts margins, and getting a mixed, hedged answer rather than a clean trend line.
- Compressed worry: **is KLA's 2026 growth story confirmed in the order book, or is it customer body language and public AI-capex headlines getting ahead of what's actually been booked** — a question sharpened by KLA's own decision to stop disclosing the one number that would answer it.
