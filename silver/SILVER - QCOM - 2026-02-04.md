# QCOM — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-02-04)

**The key idea:** Qualcomm posted an all-time-record quarter and then guided QCT handset revenue down roughly 23% sequentially for one reason only: DRAM. AI data-center HBM demand is pulling memory-fab capacity away from consumer DRAM, and Chinese OEMs in particular are slashing chipset orders and inventory in response — even as Qualcomm insists end-consumer demand for phones has never been better. The call is a clean, real-time case study of AI infrastructure buildout cannibalizing an adjacent hardware market's supply chain.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A record quarter erased by someone else's supply chain.** Revenue, EPS, QCT, handset, and automotive revenue all hit records in fiscal Q1, but the very next quarter's handset guide drops to ~$6B specifically because "memory suppliers redirect manufacturing capacity to HBM to meet AI data center demand." This is the clearest evidence yet of AI infrastructure spend directly starving a downstream consumer hardware category.
2. **Premium tier is the shock absorber.** Amon frames the pandemic-era memory shortage as the playbook: premium and high-tier phones proved more resilient to price increases, OEMs will prioritize scarce memory toward flagship SKUs, and the "dual flagship" strategy lets OEMs protect margin-rich volume while starving the low end.
3. **Automotive is quietly becoming the story management wants told.** A fourth straight record quarter (+15% y/y) is guided to accelerate to >35% y/y in Q2, on real design wins — a Volkswagen Group LOI spanning Audi/Porsche, the Cariad-Bosch ADAS alliance, and the Toyota RAV4 win — explicitly because auto "doesn't move with the industry, we move primarily with our share gains" and is "a little bit less sensitive to memory price increases."
4. **Data center remains a 2027 story, not a Q1 2026 one.** Management leans on the Grok/decode-architecture news cycle to validate its disaggregated-inference thesis but offers no new revenue color beyond reiterating "multibillion revenue opportunity in a couple of years" — this is a roadmap narrative, not a guided business yet.
5. **They can't tell you how long the memory shortage lasts.** Pressed multiple times on 2027/2028 DRAM supply, Amon repeatedly falls back to "we can't really predict," while confirming the fiscal-year handset TAM is now entirely a function of DRAM availability, not their execution.

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro and consumer demand are explicitly decoupled from the guide-down.** "The macroeconomic indicators have been strong... sell-through data also very strong," Amon says, insisting the Q2 cut is "100% sized by the availability of memory," not weakening end demand.
- **Premium-tier mix keeps expanding in an otherwise flat market** — a multi-year trend management expects DRAM scarcity to accelerate, not reverse, as OEMs ration memory toward higher-margin flagship units.
- **China is the epicenter of the pullback.** OEMs there are "taking a cautious approach in reducing their chipset inventory," though management wouldn't quantify exposure directly, only noting their mix skews toward China's higher tiers, softening the raw unit exposure.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **QCT handset ($7.8B, record) craters in guidance to ~$6B**, a roughly 13% y/y decline for Q2 — entirely attributed to OEMs cutting build plans and channel inventory against constrained DRAM supply.
- **Automotive and IoT are the offsetting engines**, with auto growth guided to accelerate past 35% y/y and IoT to low-teens y/y, both explicitly outpacing the run-rate needed to hit long-term diversification targets.
- **QCT margins compress on pure operating leverage, not cost structure** — Akash: gross margin stays "largely in line with December," the EBITDA margin guide-down to 26-28% is simply lower revenue against a similar OpEx base ("just the scale of the revenue coming through").
- **QTL licensing tracks handset units down** — Q2 guided "slightly below" last year, with a "negative bias on units" for the full year contingent entirely on supply, not royalty rate or litigation dynamics.

## Investing & scarcity
- **DRAM is the single named constraint, stated without hedging**: "the resulting industry-wide memory shortage and price increases are likely to define the overall scale of the handset industry through the fiscal year." Not compute, not wafers, not labor — memory alone.
- **Wafer supply for leading nodes is acknowledged as tight but manageable** ("we have great relationships with our suppliers, and so we're confident that we'll have enough wafers"), a secondary constraint dwarfed by the memory story.
- **Capital is flowing into diversification via M&A**: AlphaWave Semi (closed, high-speed connectivity) and Ventana Micro (RISC-V data-center CPU) both closed this quarter, driving the sequential OpEx step-up and reinforcing a two-track data-center silicon roadmap (Orion/ARM plus new RISC-V core).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: handset demand "snaps back" once memory normalizes.** Action: no hedge is offered beyond design-win pipeline talk — they are not guiding, discounting, or restructuring the handset business around a prolonged shortage, betting the dip is transitory.
- **Belief: data center inferencing silicon is a multibillion opportunity starting 2027.** Action: two acquisitions closed and a public roadmap of AI250 plus a new RISC-V CPU — real capital committed years ahead of any guided revenue, a genuine talk-meets-action match here.
- **Belief: robotics/physical AI is the "next frontier" alongside automotive.** Action: a full Dragon Wing IQ10 stack launched and named engagements with seven robotics/humanoid companies (AdventTech, Booster Figure, KUKA, etc.) within "a short period of time" — fast-moving but still pre-revenue, unlike automotive's contracted design wins.
- **Gap worth flagging**: management is confident premium tier will "prove more resilient" to memory-driven price increases, largely by analogy to the pandemic — but they offer no current pricing or elasticity data to support that beyond history repeating.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No guidance beyond fiscal Q2**: "given the uncertainty in the market, we're obviously not guiding beyond the second quarter at this point," despite being pressed twice on June-quarter shape.
- **Huawei licensing: total silence.** Alex Rogers offered zero substance — "really no update on the Huawei discussions... I really can't get into what are confidential discussions" — after being asked directly about sticking points and precedent risk.
- **Declined to size China unit exposure precisely**, offering only a qualitative "less than what you would just see based on units" without a number.
- **No commitment on full-year memory outlook duration**: repeatedly declined to predict whether the DRAM shortage persists into 2027 or 2028, despite direct analyst prompting with DRAM-maker commentary citing shortages "into 2028."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The Q&A was almost entirely one topic wearing different clothes**: how deep is the memory-driven handset cut, does it extend past March into June, what does it do to QTL run-rate and QCT margin drop-through, and is China the whole story or a broader phenomenon (Apple/Samsung's captive memory advantage was raised directly).
- **A secondary cluster probed data center credibility** post-Grok/Nvidia news, testing whether Qualcomm's decode-specialized architecture thesis is gaining real traction or still just "positive feedback" talk.
- **The Huawei license question was asked and flatly dodged** — the one moment management declined engagement entirely rather than reframing.
- **Compressed worry**: is this a one-quarter memory air pocket that reverses cleanly, or the first sign that AI data-center demand for DRAM structurally caps consumer silicon revenue through fiscal 2026 and possibly beyond — and can Qualcomm's automotive/IoT/robotics diversification actually offset it in the meantime.
