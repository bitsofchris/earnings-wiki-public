# VZ — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-27)

**The key idea:** Verizon's turnaround narrative rests almost entirely on churn, not growth — a 13-year first for Q1 postpaid phone net adds and a ~35% cut in acquisition/retention cost, both explicitly framed by CEO Dan Schulman as a rejection of the "free handset" playbook in favor of AI-enabled micro-segmentation. The tension: reported wireless service revenue still declined YoY, and management leans hard on a one-time $2.5B network-outage credit to explain it away while simultaneously claiming record profitability.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A 13-year streak just broke, but the mix matters.** Positive Q1 postpaid phone net adds (55K) for the first time since 2013, with churn improving sequentially through the quarter to below 85bp in March — Schulman says "at least half, if not more than half of our net adds are gonna come from improvements in churn," meaning growth is coming from *keeping* customers, not adding new demand.
2. **Cost discipline is real and structural, management insists.** Cost of acquisition/retention fell ~35% versus Q4 while net adds still improved YoY — a combination Schulman calls "structural changes... not one-time or seasonal," driven by micro-segmentation replacing blanket free-handset retention offers.
3. **The outage credit is doing a lot of narrative work.** An 80bp/~$2.5B hit to wireless service revenue from network-outage customer credits is treated as a clean one-off ("obviously that's a one-time event and doesn't come and follow us through the rest of the year"), which conveniently explains away the revenue miss in an otherwise "record" EBITDA quarter (38.9% margin, highest ever).
4. **AI-native ambitions are aggressive and quantified.** Schulman claims 85% of network issues now auto-resolved, a 1,280bp YoY jump in customer-sat via voice agents (built with Sierra, ElevenLabs, Google), 70%+ reduction in vendor-support costs, and a bill-of-materials cut from ~1 million SKU combinations to ~20 kits — built in partnership with Google and Anthropic, with the full stack "substantially complete" by July.
5. **Fiber over FWA is the broadband pivot.** Only 20% of Verizon's base has broadband despite a 55% wireless attachment rate when they do — management is explicitly shifting capital toward fiber (32M+ passings, targeting 40-50M) because "fiber has inherent advantages over FWA," even as FWA targets (8-9M subs by 2028) are reaffirmed as unchanged.

## Economy & consumer
- **Churn as the macro tell.** Consumer postpaid phone churn fell from 95bp (Q4) to 90bp (Q1) to below 85bp by March — Schulman attributes this to stopping "blunt price increases without corresponding value," not an improving demand environment.
- **Handset hoarding as a soft economic signal.** Management notes customers "because of the economy... are keeping their handsets longer," with upgrade volume growth slowing into Q2 — read alongside a 6% YoY rise in Q1 upgrades, suggesting the deceleration is recent.
- **Competitive intensity "moderating."** Schulman characterizes the industry as pulling back from irrational promotional spend, though this is self-serving context for VZ's own pullback from subsidies.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: postpaid phone net adds and churn**, both showing the largest YoY swings in over a decade (net adds +344K YoY; churn down from prior-year pressure trend).
- **Working: EBITDA and EPS.** Adjusted EBITDA hit $13.4B (+6.7% YoY, 38.9% margin, "highest ever reported"); adjusted EPS of $1.28 (+7.6% YoY) is the best growth rate in four years.
- **Not working: core wireless service revenue.** Down 1% YoY to $20.6B, still absorbing "elevated promotional amortization pressures" and lapping 180bp of prior-year pricing impacts — even before the outage credit drag.
- **Broadband continues to take share:** 341K net adds (214K FWA, 127K fiber) excluding the first 20 days of Frontier ownership, bringing total broadband subs to ~16.8 million.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx held flat at $16-16.5B guidance** despite Frontier absorption, reflecting "sharper prioritization and better utilization" rather than expanded spend.
- **Fiber footprint is the growth vector**, on track for 32M+ passings this year against a medium-term 40-50M ambition; FWA capacity is described as *greater* entering 2026 than entering 2025, so no near-term network scarcity there.
- **The real constraint is behavioral/structural**, not physical: Schulman frames the whole transformation as eliminating "outdated internal policies," legacy cost structures, and an overreliance on free-handset retention — the bottleneck was internal process, not compute, spectrum, or capital.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes:** ARPA and account net adds will keep improving through 2026 into 2027 as promotional-amortization headwinds "flip and become tailwinds." **Doing:** Raised EPS guidance and net-add guidance now, before that revenue inflection has shown up in reported numbers — management is pulling guidance forward on leading indicators (churn, COA/COR, March exit-rate trends) rather than realized revenue growth.
- **Believes:** AI will make Verizon "AI-native," not just AI-first, across cost, customer experience, and hyper-personalization. **Doing:** Recruiting AI talent aggressively, building a four-layer AI stack with Google/Anthropic, and already deploying voice agents (Sierra/ElevenLabs) and Quadcode across the SDLC — a genuinely fast, well-resourced buildout for a company this size ("more in the last three months than... the last three to four years").
- **Believes:** fiber is a structural differentiator against competitors. **Doing:** Explicitly shifting broadband capital mix away from FWA toward fiber, plus bolt-on M&A (Starry) to accelerate multi-dwelling-unit penetration.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No firm 2026 buyback number.** Tony Skiadas repeatedly deflects to "at least $3 billion," declining to raise it despite $2.5B already executed in Q1 alone and explicitly saying "I can't talk about hypotheticals" on going further.
- **No guidance on upgrade-rate trajectory.** Skiadas flags that upgrade growth has slowed into Q2 but stops short of forecasting it: "we don't guide on this... we'll see how it plays out."
- **Vague on headcount/AI job impact.** Schulman pivots a direct question about AI's effect on total headcount growth into product/efficiency talk without giving a number.
