# AXON — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-25)

**The key idea:** Axon used a blowout quarter (bookings +40% for the year, +50%+ in Q4) as the launchpad for a full re-rating pitch: new 2028 targets (~$6B revenue, 28% EBITDA margin) built on the thesis that integrated hardware+software+AI is a moat that pure-software competitors can't replicate as AI commoditizes model access. The tension: management is candid that gross margin is under real pressure (tariffs, low-margin hardware mix) even as they ask investors to underwrite acceleration through 2028 on "just roll it forward" logic.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Bookings, not revenue, is the story.** Full-year bookings crossed $7B (+40%+), with Q4 alone up 50%+ — Isner calls this "the beginning of a trend," and three separate 9-figure deals from the U.S. state-and-local team anchor the claim. This is the number management wants graded on, and they explicitly declined to guide it forward for 2026, which is a tell about how much confidence to place in the acceleration continuing at this rate.
2. **AI monetization is real but early and not decomposable.** The AI Era Plan did ~$750M of bookings (~10% of the total, nearly 3x 2024's new-product bookings) in its first full year, but management repeatedly deflected attempts to quantify penetration ("bottom of the first, top of the second") — Bagley explicitly told an analyst not to divide $750M by 500 Axon Assistant agencies to get a customer count, since it double-counts stand-alone add-ons.
3. **The 911/RTCC land grab via M&A.** Closing Carbyne (this month) on top of Prepared positions Axon to own call intake through dispatch through body-worn evidence — Kunins frames it as "breadth and depth," not consolidation of legacy tech debt. Smith explicitly contrasts this with "buy the mature cash cow" roll-up strategies used elsewhere in the industry.
4. **Margin pressure is being talked through, not hidden.** Adjusted gross margin fell sequentially to 61.1% on tariffs and rising Platform Solutions mix; Bagley flags "quarter-to-quarter volatility from product mix" as the new normal, and the 15% global tariff impact is baked into guidance with no assumption of refunds.
5. **Enterprise and federal are unproven optionality, not booked growth yet.** A second high-volume enterprise customer and new federal leadership are cited as directional wins, but Isner's own framing ("we go from 1 to 4 to 12 customers... it's an exponential curve, not linear") signals this is still pre-scale, and management explicitly won't name the customers.

## Economy & consumer
- **No consumer read applies** — Axon's buyers are law enforcement, corrections, federal agencies, and enterprise security/retail/healthcare departments, not end consumers.
- **Government budget cycles remain the real demand backdrop**, and management describes it as strengthening, not softening — "never been more confident across the board" on demand across all four core markets (Isner).
- **International cloud adoption is described as accelerating for policy, not budget, reasons** — Isner ties on-prem holdouts loosening to customers not wanting to be "signing up for 0 AI tools into the future."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: broad-based bookings acceleration.** Revenue +39% YoY to $797M, Software & Services +40% to $343M, Connected Devices +38% to $454M, TASER +32% to $264M, Platform Solutions +81% to $81M — growth is diversified across product lines, not carried by one segment.
- **Working: net revenue retention expanding to 125%**, evidence existing customers are adopting new products (AI, Fusus, VR, counter-drone) rather than just renewing.
- **Not working / under pressure: gross margin compression.** Adjusted gross margin fell to 61.1% sequentially "due to the impact of tariffs and increased mix from Platform Solutions" — Bagley frames this as temporary but flags it will recur ("we expect to see quarter-to-quarter volatility from product mix").
- **Not working: free cash flow conversion declined YoY** on inventory investment and collection timing; management says 2025 is "a low point" and expects to recover toward the 60% target in 2026 — a claim to watch, not a result yet.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is flowing into hardware innovation despite the software-margin narrative** — Apollo DART (next-gen TASER cartridge), a next-gen TASER platform under a former Apple/iPhone hardware lead (Rubén Caballero), Axon Body Mini, and "at least two new hardware categories" in the pipeline. Smith is explicit that underinvesting in hardware would be a strategic error even though it's lower-margin: "I do want to give a shout out to Brittany... one of the things I worry about is that if we underinvest in continuing to build out the hardware elements."
- **M&A capital is going to the 911/RTCC stack** (Prepared, Carbyne) and community-sensor network (Fusus, Dedrone) rather than horizontal software tuck-ins — "no material M&A is contemplated in the forecast" beyond continued small tuck-ins.
- **The actual scarce resource is enterprise/federal sales capacity and channel maturity**, not capital, compute, or supply — Isner's answer to "what has to happen to scale enterprise" is entirely about team-building and customer success, not product or funding gaps.
- **Manufacturing/inventory is a deliberate hedge, not a constraint**: Isner notes Axon holds inventory upfront because products like TASER ship for "15 or 20 years before discontinued" — a supply-chain choice, not evidence of shortage.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: AI is a "fundamental disruption... not a bubble" that will "break companies that are not ready for it."** Action: building AI natively into hardware sockets (Axon Assistant, Draft One) rather than layering it onto commodity models, plus acquiring 911-layer AI companies (Prepared, Carbyne) to control the data ingestion point.
- **Belief: enterprise/retail/healthcare represent a market as large as core public safety.** Action so far is thin relative to the claim — one named second enterprise customer, a new Axon Body Mini launching this spring, and an explicit refusal to disclose logos, which makes this more narrative than proof point today.
- **Belief: counter-drone regulation will loosen to allow broader state/local and private-sector mitigation.** Action: building the Dedrone sensor/jamming network ahead of legal authorization — "we're building for tomorrow's [regulatory environment]... when the law catches up... Axon will already be there," with Smith personally lobbying Congress for a new less-lethal category exempting TASER 10 from the Firearms Act.
- **Gap worth flagging**: the 2028 target model (~$6B revenue, 28% margin) is presented as requiring investors to "not assume anything differently than what we have just delivered" (Bagley) — but the guide itself only calls for 27-30% growth in 2026, and bookings guidance was explicitly withheld, so the multi-year confidence rests on qualitative momentum more than committed backlog math they're willing to share in detail.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 bookings guidance** — "at this point in time, we probably want to stay away from any bookings guidance" (Isner), despite bookings being called the single most important metric on their own scoreboard.
- **No enterprise customer names** — "I'm not sure that we're going to be announcing logos from us on enterprise deals... I'm not sure that it serves us" (Isner), explicitly for competitive reasons.
- **No confirmation software will outgrow hardware** despite the long-term deck stating that expectation — Bagley walked it back live: "it's hard to really call which will be a bigger contributor," and Smith countered that hardware has "more magic up our sleeve."
- **No precise AI Era Plan penetration rate** — Bagley redirected an analyst's back-of-envelope math (500 agencies / 15,000 total) toward per-officer pricing instead of confirming or denying the implied ~3% penetration figure.
- **No specific Apollo DART revenue timeline** beyond "not going to be a meaningful contributor to revenue this year."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Durability of the bookings acceleration into 2027-2028** dominated the call — multiple analysts pushed on whether the current growth rate is a sustainable base or a one-time step-change from a few outsized deals; management's answer leaned on "future contracted bookings" backlog math rather than new commitments.
- **AI Era Plan penetration and monetization mechanics** drew repeated, increasingly specific attempts to back into a customer count and per-agency revenue figure — management consistently declined to give the clean number.
- **Enterprise go-to-market mechanics** (channel-building, competitive dynamics among retail/healthcare buyers, why a police chief refers a peer but a retailer might not) came up from two separate analysts, suggesting the Street is still trying to underwrite a market Axon has barely proven out.
- **Tariff and margin impact** got a direct, unprompted disclosure from Bagley (15% global tariff baked in, no refund assumption) rather than analyst probing — worth noting as the one place management volunteered a headwind before being asked.
- **The compressed worry**: is Axon's bookings and AI-monetization story durable and diversified enough to sustain a doubling of revenue by 2028, or is it still concentrated in a handful of mega-deals and an unproven enterprise motion dressed up as a platform thesis?
