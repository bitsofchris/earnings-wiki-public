# BKNG — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-28)

**The key idea:** Booking beat across every headline metric and leaned into a confident story that its scale, data, and "Connected Trip" flywheel make it AI-proof — but the Q&A revealed the real test hasn't started yet. Management is simultaneously insisting disintermediation risk from LLM-native travel search is "overblown" and pouring incremental savings into defending exactly that flank, while declining to put any numbers around what an OpenAI- or Google-mediated booking funnel does to their economics.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **U.S. reacceleration is real but attributed to blocking-and-tackling, not a catalyst.** Room night growth hit high-single-digits for a third straight quarter; Fogel repeatedly refused to credit any single driver ("no magic bullet... it's blocking and tackling, day by day"), which reads as authentic but also means the growth isn't easily extrapolated.
2. **The take-rate story is quietly softer than the growth headline.** Revenue as % of gross bookings fell ~30bps YoY on flight mix and rising merchandising contra-revenue — a classic case of top-line beats obscuring mix-shift margin drag, offset this quarter by payments revenue.
3. **Genius loyalty is the actual moat, and the numbers back it up.** Genius Level 2/3 members are now 30%+ of active users and mid-50% of room nights, with materially higher direct-booking rates — this is doing more real defensive work against AI disintermediation than any of the AI product announcements.
5. **Transformation program keeps getting bigger, which is itself a signal.** Run-rate savings target raised for at least the second consecutive quarter (now $500-550M), suggesting either conservative initial sizing or genuine acceleration in efficiency finds — the call doesn't distinguish which.

## Economy & consumer
- **"Two-speed economy" framing, hedged by portfolio breadth.** Fogel: high earners spending well, "the bottom part of the economic strata are suffering a little bit more" — but Booking claims immunity via full-spectrum inventory from economy to luxury.
- **U.S. discretionary caution shows up in the data, not just the talk.** Slightly lower ADRs and shorter length of stay YoY in the U.S. specifically, even as headline growth accelerated — a real (if soft) signal under the optimism.
- **Booking window normalized after ballooning in Q2/Q3**, which management flags as healthy but also explains why Q4 room-night guidance (4-6%) steps down sharply from Q3's 8% actual.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Beat across the board:** room nights +8% (above high end of guide), gross bookings +14%, revenue +13%, adjusted EBITDA +15%, adjusted EPS +19% — all three headline guidance metrics beaten.
- **Connected Trip cross-sell is compounding:** multi-vertical transactions up mid-20s% YoY, now low-double-digit share of Booking.com transactions; flights +32%, attractions +90% (small base) — and cross-vertical bookers return more often, per company data.
- **What's not working as cleanly:** revenue take rate compressed ~30bps YoY, and fixed opex grew 10% YoY (mid-single-digit ex-FX) partly on rising cloud costs — an unglamorous line item worth tracking given the AI buildout narrative.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Transformation program savings raised again**, to $500-550M full run rate, with transformation costs pegged at roughly 1x the run-rate savings — i.e., a payback-in-about-a-year framing.
- **Capital return continued alongside a debt paydown**: ~$700M buybacks, ~$300M dividends, plus $1.5B used to redeem high-coupon 2030 debt early — deleveraging a rate-sensitive balance sheet opportunistically.
- **Stated binding constraint is brand awareness, not capacity or capital** — Fogel names it explicitly for U.S. alternative accommodations and the broader direct-channel push, a soft/marketing constraint rather than a hard supply-side one.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** search/discovery is shifting toward AI-mediated, agentic experiences, and this will become "more hybrid" with traditional search over time — not a replacement event.
- **Action:** early-mover placement in OpenAI's ChatGPT App Store, but Steenbergen's own numbers show LLM-referred leads are "relatively small" — the dollar/product commitment to the new channel is minor relative to how much airtime management gives the topic.
- **The real hedge against the AI-search future isn't AI at all** — it's Genius loyalty and Connected Trip cross-sell, both pre-AI initiatives now being repositioned as the AI-era defense. That's a coherent bet, but it means the flashy Gen AI commentary (chatbots, AI Mode, Smart Messenger) is largely a customer-experience layer, not the actual moat-building capital allocation.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to quantify economic exposure to LLM-mediated booking shift**, calling it bigger than "the old $64,000 question" but offering no model or number.
- **Declined to specify which social media channels are working**, citing competitive sensitivity — a deliberate information withhold even from investors.
- **No specific forward commitment on hotel-partner disintermediation risk** beyond historical analogy to Google-era search — an assertion, not a quantified defense.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Dominant theme: AI/agentic search as existential threat.** Multiple consecutive questions (OpenAI economics, hotel-direct bypass via LLMs, front-end evolution) probed whether Booking's transaction-layer position survives an AI-mediated discovery world; answers leaned on "it's early" and Google-era precedent rather than data.
- **Secondary theme: is U.S. strength company-specific or macro?** Analysts pushed on whether OTA industry share-taking, loyalty, or a bifurcated consumer explains the U.S. reacceleration — Fogel gave qualitative color (brand, service, execution) with no hard competitive share figures.
- **Compressed worry:** if AI agents — not humans — start choosing where to click, does Booking's "get people to come to us direct" flywheel still work, and can management defend that when they can't yet quantify the threat themselves?
