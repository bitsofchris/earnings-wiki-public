# BKNG — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-18)

**The key idea:** Booking closed out 2025 beating every headline metric, funding a bigger 2026 reinvestment push almost entirely from self-generated transformation savings rather than new spend. But the call was dominated by a defensive argument — that the operational complexity of being a two-sided marketplace (4M independent suppliers, 100+ payment methods, dense EU regulation) is what protects Booking from LLM disintermediation, not brand or scale. Management's confidence rests on a bet that AI players will stay "above the funnel" the way Google has for two decades; it's an assertion, not evidence, and Fogel says so explicitly ("that's my opinion").

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Guidance implies a sharp Q4-to-Q1 deceleration.** Room nights grew 9% in Q4 but are guided to only 5-7% in Q1 — a meaningful step-down that wasn't directly addressed despite the strong momentum language ("encouraged by the positive momentum we have seen so far in the new year").
2. **AI savings are actually landing in the P&L, not just the narrative.** Ewout Steenbergen pointedly contrasted Booking with an anonymous peer CFO who said "GenAI is everywhere except for in the P&L" — customer service cost per booking is down ~10% y/y even as bookings grew double digits, a concrete, measurable AI ROI that's rarer than most companies' AI claims.
3. **The merchant-of-record defense is the whole strategic thesis.** Fogel's answer to the LLM-threat question was essentially: payments regulation, currency complexity, and unsophisticated-supplier servicing are too messy for LLM players to want to own, so they'll monetize as a new top-of-funnel referral source instead — mirroring Google's economics. This is unverified — "we don't sit in their boardrooms."
4. **Genius loyalty concentration keeps rising.** Level 2/3 members now drive a high-50% share of room nights (up from mid-50s), and are explicitly tied to higher direct-booking rates — loyalty is doing double duty as a retention tool and a disintermediation defense against both LLMs and OTAs' historic dependence on Google traffic.
5. **Alternative accommodations growth decelerated and got a defensive, almost apologetic answer.** Room night growth there slowed to high-single-digit/9% in Q4 off tougher compares, and it wasn't named among the explicit 2026 investment priorities until an analyst flagged the omission — Fogel then backfilled reassurance ("good thing you asked... it would be a shame").

## Economy & consumer
- **Demand held up despite macro noise:** "Despite volatility in the broader global markets, the underlying fundamentals of our business are solid," with Q4 room nights (+9%) beating guidance by 3 points across all regions.
- **U.S. consumer shows soft edges:** slightly lower ADRs and shorter length of stay versus prior year "may indicate that some consumer segments are continuing to be thoughtful on their discretionary spending" — a hedge phrase for trading down, even as headline growth accelerated.
- **U.S. growth reaccelerated sharply in H2:** from low-single-digit room night growth in H1 2025 to low-double-digit in Q4, credited to brand/performance marketing and B2B momentum rather than macro tailwinds.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broad-based beat:** Q4 gross bookings and revenue both grew 16%, adjusted EBITDA +19%, EPS +17%, all ahead of guidance; full-year EBITDA margin expanded 193bps to 36.9%.
- **Transformation Program is the margin engine:** delivered $550M annual run-rate savings by year-end 2025 (high end of guidance), funding $170M of incremental 2025 reinvestment and set to deliver $500-550M in-year savings in 2026.
- **Payments/fintech is quietly becoming a real business:** merchant gross bookings hit $130B (+25% y/y, ~70% of total), and Steenbergen called fintech "already a significant P&L driver."
- **Alternative accommodations cooling:** full-year growth ~10%, Q4 ~9%, versus double-digit growth in prior periods — management attributes it to "noise" from tough comps but conspicuously left it off the initial list of 2026 investment priorities.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$700M incremental reinvestment for 2026**, expected to generate ~$400M incremental revenue against a net EBITDA drag of ~$300M — spread across GenAI, Connected Trip, Asia/U.S. growth, advertising, OpenTable international expansion, and fintech/loyalty.
- **The scarce resource is supplier-side operational complexity, not capital:** thousands of dedicated partner-services staff manage ~4M largely unsophisticated independent properties, 100+ payment methods and 50+ currencies — Fogel frames this explicitly as the moat: "that requires a lot of things... not as though you just put it up and all of a sudden it works."
- **Capital return stayed aggressive:** $8.2B returned to shareholders in 2025 ($5.9B buybacks, $1.2B dividends), a 9.4% dividend hike, and a 25-for-1 stock split announced for April 2026 — signals confidence but doesn't address the AI-disruption question directly.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Fogel argues LLM players will likely stay "above the funnel" like Google rather than become merchants of record, given the regulatory and servicing complexity of owning the transaction.
- **Action doesn't fully back the confidence:** despite this stated belief, Booking is deliberately deepening partnerships with OpenAI, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon and investing hundreds of millions in "agentic capabilities across our brands" — a hedge against the very outcome management says is unlikely, which is a rational insurance policy but also a tell that the "I don't think so" framing isn't fully trusted internally.
- **Belief:** the Genius/direct-channel loyalty flywheel is the durable defense against funnel disintermediation. **Action:** concrete resourcing behind it — Genius tier expansion into new verticals, and explicit plans to "build a lot of agentic tools at the top of the funnel" to keep customers coming direct.
- **Belief:** alternative accommodations still has "a lot of potential." **Action:** thinner evidence — supply grew 8% to 8.6M listings, but it wasn't in the headline 2026 investment list, suggesting resourcing lags the rhetoric.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No agentic product roadmap or timing:** "I'm not going to talk specifically to which areas or what the rollout times are... we're not going to actually give away the playbook right now."
- **No forward alternative-accommodations growth number:** "We're not going to give a number going forward... this is a number, it's a little bit of noise."
- **No visibility into AI partners' actual strategy:** "We don't sit in their boardrooms nor do we... share their strategies with us in great detail" — the merchant-of-record thesis is explicitly framed as opinion, not inside information.
- **Vague on 2026 marketing discipline:** commits to marketing leverage "unless, of course, we find those really great opportunistic moments" — the identical justification used for Q4's deleverage, leaving the door open to repeat it.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **AI/agentic disintermediation dominated the Q&A** — three separate questions probed where agentic tools create bookings uplift, what OpenAI/Google/Microsoft/Amazon are actually building, and whether LLMs could become merchant of record — all met with confident but evidence-light answers ("that's my opinion").
- **Marketing spend discipline** was questioned directly given the Q4 deleverage, with management attributing it to opportunistic ROI rather than a strategic shift, while declining to rule out repeating it.
- **Alternative accommodations deceleration** drew a pointed question about whether it was quietly deprioritized, forcing management to walk back the omission from its 2026 priority list.
- Compressed worry: **is Booking's supplier-complexity moat actually AI-proof, or is management simply asserting it hasn't been tested yet?**
