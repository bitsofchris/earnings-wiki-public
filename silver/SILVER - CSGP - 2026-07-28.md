# CSGP — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** CoStar swapped its growth-at-all-costs playbook for a margin-first one — adjusted EBITDA more than doubled while bookings growth fell, and management is explicit that this was a choice, not a surprise: they walked back Homes.com's inside sales force, held pricing on Apartments.com against an aggressive discounting rival, and let Ten-X shrink rather than chase revenue. The bet is that discipline now buys credibility for 2028–2030 EBITDA targets, even if it costs a full-year revenue guidance cut.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **Field sales over inside sales, company-wide.** Homes.com cut inside reps from 660 to about 400 while adding a 50-person field team in five metros; per-rep production rose 19% even as headcount fell, and management says field reps run at roughly 2x the productivity of inside reps across Homes, Apartments, and CoStar. "It's also a shift from prioritizing revenue growth as the first priority to committing to hitting our EBITDA goals," the CFO said.
- **Depth advertising is the real Homes.com business model, not subscriptions.** Platinum listings launch in Q3, following the pattern where 70% of Apartments.com customers already buy depth ads and 91% of Domain's revenue comes from it. Management flags this as the mechanism that's supposed to reaccelerate Homes.com revenue after the sales-force reset.
- **Rent Benchmark reframes CoStar's core data asset from asking-rent estimates to actual signed-lease data**, built from 4 million AI-abstracted lease documents, with plans to extend the same approach to Australia and the U.K. This is the clearest case of AI changing what CoStar actually sells, not just how it operates.
- **Geographic expansion continues on multiple fronts** — France launched in Q2 (Business Immo/Brio Local integration), U.K. public-record search went live, Australia CoStar targeted for H2 2026 with 124 researchers already on the ground, though LoopNet Australia got pushed to late 2027 in favor of residential integration first.
- **Zonda acquisition (new-home construction data) still pending regulatory close in H2 2026**, explicitly excluded from guidance — a clean line to watch for the next accretion update.
- **The Richmond headquarters consolidation is being framed as a balance-sheet play, not just real estate.** Andy Florance floated a future sale-leaseback "that could unlock hundreds and hundreds of millions in liquid capital," citing a 145% one-year value gain on the prior D.C. HQ as precedent.

## What's changing

- **Adjusted EBITDA margin jumped roughly 900 basis points year-over-year to 20%,** a full quarter ahead of the company's own target, driven by personnel cuts and "early benefits from AI and other expense initiatives."
- **Token costs are coming in under budget**, and management claims AI-driven labor savings are currently outpacing AI spend — notable as one of the few concrete "AI is net-positive on our P&L today" claims from a non-AI-native company, relevant to anyone tracking real-world LLM unit economics.
- **Net new bookings fell roughly 26% year-over-year** despite EBITDA strength — the guidance cut is a bookings story, not a revenue-recognition story, and roughly a quarter of the full-year guidance revision traces to Ten-X alone.
- **Homes.com's marketing mix is shifting away from celebrity brand campaigns toward SEM and lower-funnel channels**, described as cheaper to produce and more directly tied to leads.
- **Residential segment posted its first-ever positive adjusted EBITDA ($12 million)** since Homes.com launched in Q1 2024 — the segment crossed from cash sink to (barely) profitable.
- **Zillow is under simultaneous FTC/state AG antitrust suit and a CoStar copyright suit** over rental-photo use — CoStar is explicitly framing a competitor's growth as potentially built on legal shortcuts, worth noting given how directly Florance leaned into it ("conduct that may result in significant setbacks for them").

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **Ten-X is being quietly wound down in growth terms** — revenue declined $4 million even as costs were cut $7 million, and it's now being carved out from LoopNet with its own sales/marketing team, language that reads as damage control for a shrinking asset rather than a turnaround plan.
- **Apartments.com's average revenue per property fell ~3.6% year-over-year**, attributed to mix shift toward smaller communities — the company insists this is "price integrity" against a discounting competitor, but it's still declining ARPU on the core segment while claiming to hold pricing firm.
- **Management could not answer a direct bridge question on residential EBITDA guidance** ("I don't have that bridge in front of me, so we'll come back to you") — a live gap in an otherwise tightly rehearsed call.
- **No 2027 growth commentary at all**, despite three separate analyst attempts to get organic growth trajectory heading into next year; CFO repeatedly declined ("nothing to add on that question").
- **The "10-11x ROI" pitch for Homes.com is doing a lot of work to justify a strategy reversal** — the company built a 660-person inside sales team, is now walking it back, and is retroactively explaining the pivot as always-planned optionality rather than a correction of an over-scaled bet.
- **No mention of what happens to the ~260 laid-off Homes.com inside reps** or the cost of the reset itself, despite headcount reduction being cited as a primary lever behind the EBITDA beat.
