# TMUS — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-11)

**The key idea:** This was less an earnings call than a coronation and a reframing exercise: new CEO Srini Gopalan used a "halftime" Capital Markets Day event to raise 2026-2027 guidance, announce the end of subscriber/ARPU reporting in favor of account/ARPA metrics, and signal a coming pullback from industry device-subsidy economics. The bet is that T-Mobile's NPS and network lead can be monetized through relationship depth (accounts, bundling, TLife engagement) rather than raw subscriber counts — a framing shift that conveniently arrives just as churn is normalizing upward off pandemic-era contract-suppressed lows.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The reporting change is a genuine strategy signal, not just optics.** Dropping postpaid phone/ARPU disclosure for accounts/ARPA lets management tell a story where "600,000 postpaid phone net additions" (a rival's number) looks weak next to "only 26,000 postpaid net account additions" for that same rival — Peter Osvaldik used this contrast explicitly to reframe how the whole industry should be judged.
2. **Device subsidies are about to change industry-wide, and TMUS wants to lead it.** Srini was unusually blunt for a public forum: "we're at another such point where we as an industry have gotten over-focused on how free the newest phone is... The Un-carrier will make another move." Combined with Mike Katz's "can't make iPhones any freer than they are today," this reads as a coordinated pre-announcement of a pricing/value repositioning still being finalized — management repeatedly declined specifics.
3. **Fixed wireless access (FWA) is the standout growth engine and structurally undersold in guidance.** 15M FWA customers by 2030 (up from a 12M prior target) is built on zero additional spectrum and zero 6G efficiency gains — pure "fallow capacity" harvesting plus better routers. Combined with fiber JVs, TMUS projects an 18-19M broadband business built from scratch in seven years.
4. **AI is embedded at three layers of the business simultaneously**: cost takeout ($3B run-rate savings by 2027 from TLife self-service, up from 22% to 73% of upgrades self-served in a year), customer experience (AI Live Translate built into the core network, OpenAI partnership for "Intent CX"), and infrastructure strategy (AI RAN with Nvidia/Nokia/Ericsson repurposing base stations as AI compute nodes ahead of 6G).
5. **Churn normalization is being actively reframed as healthy, not concerning.** Both postpaid and prepaid churn rose through 2025, which management attributes to contracts unwinding from artificially suppressed 36-month-contract-era lows — a claim that's plausible but self-serving, and the pivot to account-based churn reporting will make this harder to verify externally going forward.

## Economy & consumer
- **Wireless as recession hedge, but hedged rhetoric**: "We're never the canary in a coal mine" — Srini argues category resilience, but Peter added the caveat that a "flight to value" in a downturn would likely favor TMUS given its value positioning, implicitly acknowledging some macro sensitivity exists.
- **Value narrative shifting from acquisition to retention**: existing customers pay 12-15% less than AT&T/Verizon; new customers get a 20-30% "holistic value" gap including bundled Netflix/Hulu/Apple TV/T Satellite — this is now framed as the moat against subsidy-based competition.
- **December port-in vs port-out value gap (+15%)** offered as real-time evidence that even during an intensely promotional month, TMUS was capturing higher-value switchers, not just more of them.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record account growth, outsized versus peers**: 261K postpaid net account adds in Q4, "10x" the nearest disclosed competitor; full-year organic service revenue +5%, EBITDA organic +4%, free cash flow conversion 25% for the year — called "the best metric for value creation" by Osvaldik.
- **Network leadership now empirically validated**: J.D. Power's #1 network quality ranking ended a incumbent's 35-report, 17-year streak; median download speeds "twice" nearest competitors; 85% faster than one rival on iPhone 17.
- **NPS gap is the causal story management leans on hardest**: NPS parity with rivals in 2023 has opened into a clear lead by 2025, credited as the root cause of outsized financial performance rather than promotions.
- **Retail is being reshaped toward experience, cutting authorized retailers**: in-sourcing more selling/service into company-owned "experience stores" (higher NPS than authorized retail) — a real structural bet, not just messaging.
- **What's not addressed**: no granular color on where FWA/broadband growth is coming at the expense of legacy landline players versus pure incrementality claims beyond assertion ("none of this is an overbuild... all of this is incremental").

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex holds at $10B for 2026**, entirely funneled through the "customer-driven coverage" AI-driven prioritization model now applied across the full existing network base — reallocating away from low-value towers/small cells toward high-value ones.
- **$12B in strategic deals since 2024** (US Cellular acquisition accelerated to a 2-year integration, MetroNet/Lumos fiber JVs, Bliss/Vistar ad-tech, spectrum) funded from a stated $52B+ capital envelope through 2027, of which up to $30B is earmarked for shareholder returns.
- **Explicit build-vs-buy spectrum discipline**: walked away from EchoStar spectrum as "too expensive," reiterating a three-factor test (build economics, portfolio fit, leadership commitment) — spectrum scarcity is real but they're not paying any price to solve it.
- **FWA capacity is the sharpest scarcity constraint on the call**: the "fallow capacity" model reserves wireless peak-hour demand first, sells only the residual — and management stresses the 15M target assumes *no* incremental spectrum or 6G gains, meaning the real ceiling could be materially higher.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes 6G/AI RAN is a multi-trillion-dollar physical-AI platform shift** ("6G is not just the connecting pipe, but really the nervous system for physical AI" — John Saw) but explicitly excludes any AI RAN/6G revenue from 2026-2027 guidance — pure optionality, not yet funded at scale beyond the Nvidia/Nokia/Ericsson innovation-center trials starting field tests in 2026.
- **Says device-subsidy economics need to change industry-wide** but took no concrete action on this call beyond signaling intent — "there's not that much we're going to say about this right now" — leaving a real gap between stated conviction and executed policy.
- **Financial services (T-Mobile Visa with Capital One) talked up as a low-CAC growth vector** leveraging 24M monthly TLife actives, but "very little in the plan" from a guidance perspective — belief well ahead of committed capital.
- **Fiber expansion appetite is real but price-gated**: "open to more assets... they need to be at the right price" — a stated ambition with an explicit refusal to overpay, i.e., action contingent on market conditions rather than committed.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Declined to size or separately disclose financial-services/credit-card or T-Ads economics ("early days," "haven't broken that out separately").
- Would not specify device-subsidy plan changes despite flagging them as imminent — deferred to "the next few weeks and months."
- Would not name a homes-passed number for fiber JV assets, calling it not the metric that matters to them.
- Would not comment on Deutsche Telekom's own motivations for its 2026 no-sell pledge, despite DT being majority owner and board-represented.
- No guidance issued beyond 2027, despite repeated analyst prompts to extrapolate broadband/EBITDA trajectories to 2028-2030.

## The street — what analysts asked

- Broadband penetration math: analysts pushed on whether the 12-15M homes-passed target implies sub-30% terminal penetration; management's answer (still-building cohorts mask true terminal penetration) was somewhat evasive on hard numbers.
- Elevated Q4/FY25 churn drew direct questioning; answer reframed churn as normalization, not degradation, and previewed switching to account-churn-only disclosure going forward — reducing future comparability just as the metric came under scrutiny.
- DT's no-sell announcement and buyback acceleration prompted questions management couldn't fully answer (can't speak for DT's motives) — a minor but real dodge.
