# AMZN — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** This call is Amazon declaring it's back in the AI infrastructure arms race after a stretch of Azure/GCP comparisons — AWS reaccelerated to its fastest growth in 11 quarters, powered by a custom-silicon bet (Trainium) that's fully subscribed and anchored by a single flagship customer, Anthropic, in which Amazon also holds a large and freshly-marked-up equity stake. Underneath the AI story, capex is compounding fast ($125B this year, more next year) and the binding constraint has shifted from chips to power. Layered on top: a headcount reduction management insists is cultural, not financial or AI-driven — a framing analysts didn't push back on.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AWS's reacceleration is real but concentrated.** 20.2% YoY growth (270bps acceleration) on a $132B run rate, with backlog at $200B not even counting October deals that "together or more than our total deal volume for all of Q3." Trainium2 grew 150% quarter-over-quarter but remains "a small number of very large customers" — the broadening thesis rests entirely on unproven Trainium3.
2. **Power, not chips, is the current AWS bottleneck.** Jassy directly named it: "maybe the bottleneck is power... at some point it may move to chips." AWS added 3.8GW in 12 months, more than any competitor, and plans to double 2022-era capacity again by 2027 — capex discipline is being sacrificed to physical capacity race.
3. **The Anthropic relationship is financially circular.** AWS built Project Rainier (500K→1M Trainium2 chips) specifically for Anthropic to train Claude, and the same quarter reported a $9.5B non-operating gain from its Anthropic equity stake driving net income to $21.2B — a vendor and an investment stake intertwined in a way that flatters both segments' optics simultaneously.
4. **Grocery is quietly becoming a retention flywheel, not a side project.** Same-day perishables (now in 1,000 cities, expanding to 2,300 by year-end) are described as changing "this many years tradition of the weekly stock up grocery" — customers who try perishables "came back... twice as often as nonperishable shoppers." This is a stickiness play disguised as a delivery-speed story.
5. **The layoffs framing is a hedge worth flagging.** Jassy explicitly denied the headcount cuts were "financially driven" or "even really AI-driven, not right now, at least" — an oddly specific disclaimer ("not right now") that leaves the door open for AI-driven cuts later while avoiding that admission today.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read offered.** Unlike calls that open with tariff/inflation/rate commentary, Jassy and Olsavsky stuck to company-specific metrics throughout — the only "economy" signal is implicit in strong ad (22% YoY) and retail unit growth (11% YoY worldwide paid units).
- **Grocery behavior change is the clearest consumer signal.** Perishables customers "visiting the site more often and returning twice as often" — a habit-formation data point management is using to justify further same-day grocery buildout.
- **Rufus AI assistant reshaping purchase funnel.** 250 million active customers this year, monthly users up 140% YoY, and users "60% more likely to complete a purchase" when engaging Rufus during a shopping trip — AI-assisted discovery is measurably converting.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **AWS is the story, and it's working.** $33B revenue, 20.2% YoY, $11.4B operating income — the segment's best growth in 11 quarters, explicitly reaccelerating versus prior quarters.
- **North America margin was masked by one-time charges.** Reported 4.5% operating margin was really 6.9% excluding the $2.5B FTC settlement charge — a legal cost large enough to swing headline profitability materially, and one analysts didn't interrogate for follow-on business implications.
- **Advertising accelerated for a third straight quarter** to $17.6-17.7B, 22% YoY, with Jassy citing "every single one of our advertising offerings this quarter grew in a meaningful way" — DSP growth attributed to closing feature gaps versus competitors over "the last 20 months."
- **Severance costs ($1.8B) cut across all three segments**, muddying like-for-like margin comparisons company-wide this quarter.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex is compounding aggressively:** $89.9B spent YTD, full-year guide ~$125B, and Olsavsky flagged it will "increase" again in 2026 — no ceiling articulated.
- **Power is the near-term binding constraint**, not chip supply — a notable admission given how much of the call focused on Trainium chip economics. AWS is "not constrained in any way" on NVIDIA purchasing and expects to "buy more NVIDIA both next year and in the future," undercutting any narrative that Trainium is meant to replace NVIDIA rather than supplement it.
- **Custom silicon economics are the pitch:** Trainium claimed at "30% to 40% more price performance than other options," with Trainium3 expected to be "about 40% better than Trainium2." Majority of Bedrock token usage already running on Trainium — a real substitution underway, not just a slide claim.
- **Robotics investment continues to scale**: over 1 million robots now in the fulfillment network, explicitly tied to safety, productivity, and cost-to-serve gains rather than headcount replacement in Jassy's framing.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: agents are the next big value pool in AI.** Action: shipped AgentCore (1M+ SDK downloads), Strands (open-source agent framework), and Kiro (100K+ developers in days) — real product and adoption numbers behind the thesis, not just talk.
- **Belief: agentic commerce will expand online shopping overall.** Action: still limited to Rufus and "Buy for Me"; third-party agent partnerships are explicitly not yet built — Jassy conceding present-day third-party agent experiences have wrong prices, wrong delivery estimates, and no personalization. Belief is ahead of infrastructure here.
- **Belief: AWS growth clip is sustainable "for a while."** Action: capex acceleration and power buildout (3.8GW added, doubling by 2027) back this conviction with capital, though management stopped short of any multi-year revenue commitment.
- **Belief: grocery habit change is happening now.** Action: committed to expanding same-day perishables from 1,000 to 2,300 cities by year-end — a concrete, dated rollout target, not just aspiration.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Would not attribute layoffs to AI or cost-cutting**, insisting it's "culture," while adding the qualifier "not right now, at least" on AI-driven headcount reduction — leaving room to change that answer later without contradicting today's statement.
- **Would not bless third-party agentic commerce as ready**, citing broken personalization, pricing, and delivery-estimate accuracy — optionality language ("we have to find a way... and have the right exchange value") rather than a partnership commitment.
- **Declined to disaggregate advertising growth** by core vs. DSP vs. Prime Video despite being directly asked — Jassy gave directional color ("every single one... grew") without splitting out numbers.
- **No specific AWS margin guidance given**, with Olsavsky reiterating margins will simply "fluctuate over time" based on investment pace — a standing hedge against being held to any margin trajectory during the heaviest capex ramp in company history.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Nearly the entire Q&A was AWS/AI-infrastructure focused**: capacity constraints, Trainium3 demand outside anchor customers, Project Rainier's architecture and replicability beyond Anthropic, and how much AWS acceleration is core infrastructure versus AI-specific monetization.
- **One question each on grocery/headcount and on agentic commerce** — both answered expansively but without hard commitments (grocery: enthusiasm and city-count targets; agentic commerce: philosophical optimism, no partnership specifics).
- **No one pushed on the FTC settlement's business implications**, the circularity of the Anthropic equity gain sitting alongside the Anthropic chip-supply relationship, or what the "not right now" caveat on AI-driven layoffs might mean going forward.
- **Compressed worry:** the Street is entirely focused on whether Trainium can broaden past Anthropic-scale anchor customers — nobody is yet asking whether the capex, power buildout, and one-time-charge-laden margins are sustainable at this pace.
