# KHC — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-11)

**The key idea:** A brand-new CEO, six weeks into the job, shows up to reverse his own board's signature strategic decision — pausing the Kraft-Heinz separation — in favor of a $600M reinvestment bet on a company he says was starved of marketing, R&D, and sales headcount for a decade. The tension is credibility: is this a genuine self-diagnosed turnaround or a new leader buying time before the numbers force his hand anyway.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The spin is dead in all but name.** Cahillane won't set a re-evaluation date and frames the pause as indefinite: "I wouldn't put an end date on anything." Analysts clearly read this as effectively cancelled, not paused, and pushed hard on the whiplash from a decision made only "four or five weeks" after he endorsed the separation's logic.
2. **$600M is a confession of underinvestment, not a new strategy.** Cahillane frames it explicitly as catching up — "we're really getting back to where we ought to be" — rather than responding to a tougher competitive environment, even though peers have also raised investment and price actions industry-wide.
3. **Last year's pricing playbook failed and they're saying so.** Maciel's admission that 2025 price-investment tactics "did not work the way we anticipated" is a notably direct concession for an earnings call, and the new plan explicitly reworks rather than repeats that approach.
4. **SNAP is a real, quantified drag they're leaning into as opportunity.** With SNAP-exposed revenue over-indexed versus the industry (13% vs 11%) and a 100bps guidance headwind baked in, management is repositioning ~40% of categories around smaller packs and lower entry price points to compete for that budget-squeezed shopper.
5. **The bet has a hard deadline built into the timeline.** Management wants "a change in trend" visible by back-half 2026 and to "exit 2026 with the best trends" of the year — a specific, checkable commitment that gives the Street a clean scorecard for whether this was real investment or a stall tactic.

## Economy & consumer
- **Consumer fatigue from cumulative price hikes.** Cahillane: the industry "busted through four or five levels of price points" during the inflation surge and "the consumer was left very disappointed" — a direct admission that pricing overshot demand tolerance.
- **SNAP cuts are a named, quantified macro headwind.** ~13% of US retail revenue is SNAP-exposed (vs. ~11% industry average), with a 100bps guidance hit already assumed from reduced program funding this year.
- **Opportunity framed alongside the headwind.** Cahillane says SNAP pressure "also presents an opportunity for us to compete for that consumer" via smaller pack sizes and lower opening price points — spin worth noting, since a funding cut is being sold partly as a merchandising opening.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Real, recent share inflection in parts of the portfolio.** Maciel says taste-elevation brands (sauces, cream cheese) flipped to share growth in the trailing 13 weeks, with 70% of that sub-portfolio now gaining share, and total US company share is back to roughly where it was three years ago.
- **Heinz and Philadelphia cream cheese cited as proof points.** Both are highlighted as already showing "meaningful improvement" from investments that began in the back half of last year — used to justify that "brands truly respond to investment."
- **Mac and cheese as the next test case.** A new "17 gram protein super mac" launch is coming, and management says early signs of traction exist, but this is forward-looking, not yet demonstrated.
- **Explicit acknowledgment that ~20% of the portfolio stays challenged.** Cahillane: "there might be a 20% of the portfolio that is more challenged" — the goal is for winners to "far outpace" laggards, not for uniform turnaround, a meaningful hedge on how much of the brand book is salvageable.
- **Emerging markets and Canada framed as steady strength.** Ex-Indonesia emerging markets grew "close to double digits including with volume growth" in 2025, and Canada has delivered growth for three straight years — these are the quieter bright spots subsidizing the US turnaround bet.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$600M investment, roughly split.** About half goes to price/product/packaging (opening price points, promotions, brand marketing); the other half rebuilds a commercial organization management says is deliberately "lean" — hiring sales and marketing headcount that "takes some time," phased more into Q3/Q4.
- **US-concentrated spend.** Cahillane: investment will be "disproportionately against The US," where trend deterioration has been worst, with only selective spend elsewhere.
- **Capital allocation discipline unchanged around the pivot.** Maciel reaffirms the standing priority stack — reinvest in the business first, hold ~3x net leverage, pay down debt with excess cash this year and next — before any other capital deployment (buybacks not prioritized near-term).
- **Constraint is people/capability, not cash.** The repeated "we're lean" framing casts organizational thinness — not balance sheet, not commodities — as the actual bottleneck to executing the turnaround.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** trend change visible in the back half of 2026, organic growth resuming by 2027, with the company exiting 2026 "with the best trends that we've had" during the year.
- **Action:** the $600M spend ramps in Q2, sales/marketing hiring lands mostly Q3–Q4, and base-price/pack actions target ~40% of categories starting in Q2 — a real but slow-build sequencing that leaves most of 2026 without visible proof.
- **The gap:** management is asking the market to underwrite roughly a year of execution risk — spend starts now, but the checkable payoff (share trend, exit-rate momentum) doesn't arrive until the back half, and a full "return to growth" isn't promised until 2027. That's a long runway to fund on faith after last year's pricing tactics already missed.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No long-term growth algorithm.** Cahillane: "we're not prepared, and it's too early to talk about long term algorithms" — deferred to a possible 2027 discussion.
- **No re-evaluation date for the separation.** The pause is open-ended by design, explicitly preserving "optionality" for future portfolio moves without a trigger or timeline.
- **No specifics on which categories get base-price cuts.** Maciel declined to name categories, saying only "we don't wanna talk the specific categories here on this call," despite giving a precise 40%-of-categories figure.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The dominant theme was the spin reversal itself** — multiple analysts asked variations of "what changed in four to six weeks" and pressed on whether "pause" is functionally "cancellation," reflecting real skepticism about the abruptness and the credibility of a strategic 180 this early in a new CEO's tenure.
- **Secondary theme: whether brand investment can actually work** given a widely-perceived quality gap versus competitors and years of underinvestment — one analyst directly asked if some brands simply "won't respond" to more spending.
- **Tertiary theme: mechanics of execution** — phasing of price/pack/promo investment, SNAP exposure, commodity/vertical-integration strategy, and capital allocation/buyback plans, all essentially asking "prove this $600M isn't just another false start."
- **Compressed worry:** a new CEO just unwound the board's headline strategic decision on a six-week timeline and is asking the Street to trust an underinvestment story and an unproven spending plan, with no visible proof until the back half of 2026 at the earliest.
