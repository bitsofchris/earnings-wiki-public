# GEHC — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** GE Healthcare printed a clean beat (4% organic growth, 6% orders, raised EPS floor) that's genuinely obscured by two self-inflicted, self-described-as-temporary items: a PCS product hold that gutted that segment's margin, and a deliberately slow-walked Flyrcado launch. Management's real message is "our multi-year R&D bet (~$3B since 2022) is about to become visible" — RSNA in December is being set up as the proof point, with roughly a dozen new AI-embedded products and Photon Counting CT among them.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth outperformed guidance and management is telegraphing more.** Organic revenue hit 4% against a 2-3% call three months earlier, and CFO Jay Saccaro stated plainly: "our expectation would be to grow faster than 3%" in 2026, backed by record backlog ($21.2B) and 6% trailing-4-quarter order growth — while declining to commit to an actual number ahead of the February call.
2. **Flyrcado's slowdown is a supply and workflow story, not a demand story.** CEO Peter Arduini: "we're going slow to go fast" — the constraint is contract-manufacturing yield ("working to achieve a consistent 95%-plus yield") and customer billing/workflow onboarding, while "our opportunity funnel is actually exceeding what we initially laid out." The CDL/CardioNavix distribution deal (~1/3 of US PET procedures) is the lever to convert that funnel fast in 2026.
3. **PCS masked a decent underlying quarter.** Segment revenue fell 7% (5 points from the product hold alone) and margin dropped 680bp, but management framed it as fixable and temporary, with new leader Jeannette Bankes brought in mid-year to reset commercial execution — a clear "new broom" narrative that still needs to prove out in Q4.
4. **Tariffs are a real, quantified drag turning into a tailwind.** 2025 tariff impact is $265M (~$0.45 EPS), roughly half mitigated already via sourcing/second-sourcing/value engineering/site transfers; management expects net tariff impact to be lower in 2026 versus 2025 under currently enacted policy — an explicit, numbers-backed forward claim rather than vague optimism.
5. **The R&D-to-COGS reclassification is a metrics-honesty tell worth flagging.** As roughly a dozen products near commercialization, engineering costs shift from R&D into cost of goods, mechanically compressing gross margin (60bp/~$30M this quarter) even as EPS is unaffected — CFO Saccaro conceded this "does sort of cloud what I think is a solid overall story," a rare unprompted admission that a headline metric is currently misleading.

## Economy & consumer
- **Capital equipment demand is healthy and broad-based.** Arduini cited "a healthy capital equipment environment" with orders growth across all segments and nearly $0.5B in large system deals signed in the quarter, including a 14-year Care Alliance with UC San Diego Health.
- **China is recovering from a policy-driven trough, not a demand collapse.** "The market has been challenged over the past 2 years due to stimulus as well as the anticorruption campaign... we don't see any structural reason to prevent China market returning from growth." New China leadership is rebuilding market-access and clinical-selling capability country-wide.
- No direct consumer/end-patient demand commentary — GEHC sells to health systems and clinicians, not consumers directly, so this axis is inherently thin; the closest proxy is "procedure volumes," described as healthy and supportive of product revenue growth.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **AVS is the standout, four straight quarters of growth and margin expansion,** driven by AI-embedded product refreshes (Vivid Pioneer, Allia Pulse cath lab, ultrasound Caption AI) that management says win on **both** price and win-rate: "we actually are getting better price and higher capture rate."
- **PDx grew 10% organically** on contrast media and radiopharmaceuticals strength, though segment margin fell 150bp on planned Flyrcado and Nihon Medi-Physics investment spend.
- **Imaging grew 4%,** with management stressing the segment is finally cycling into "a new wave of innovation after 4 years of investment" — implying prior years' Imaging pipeline was comparatively thin.
- **PCS was the clear weak spot** — a product hold (now resolved, shipments resumed) drove most of the 7% revenue decline and margin compression; company expects a "sequential sales step-up" and "meaningful sequential improvement" in Q4, but full recovery bleeds into 2026.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is flowing into commercialization of a multi-year R&D bet:** over $3B in R&D since 2022, now surfacing as ~12 near-commercialization programs to be unveiled at RSNA (Dec 1-2), including Photon Counting CT ("our deep silicon approach... going to make a significant difference") and full-body PET.
- **Tuck-in M&A stays disciplined and thesis-driven:** icometrix acquisition adds digital Alzheimer's-therapy monitoring tools to pair with rising Vizamyl PET-amyloid demand — capital allocation explicitly tied to the D3 (devices/imaging + drugs/PDx + AI) strategy rather than opportunistic scale deals.
- **The binding constraint on Flyrcado is manufacturing yield and customer operational readiness, not clinical demand or reimbursement** — both of which management describes as already solved ("the economics now are clear on the private pay side as well as on the Medicare piece").
- **Tariffs remain a real operational constraint being actively engineered around:** sourcing from lower-cost regions, second-sourcing, value engineering, site transfers, and pricing — roughly half of gross 2025 exposure already mitigated.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** 2026 organic growth "faster than 3%," tariffs a tailwind rather than headwind, Flyrcado "absolutely back on track" toward $500M by 2028 (potentially $1B at just 25% conversion of the PET myocardial-perfusion market). **Action backing it:** the CDL distribution agreement, new PCS leadership, and the RSNA product wave are concrete, funded commitments rather than aspirational targets — this is one of the more credible talk/action alignments on the call.
- **Gap:** management repeatedly asserts confidence in mid-single-digit medium-term growth (from the November 2024 Investor Day) while simultaneously declining to give any 2026 number, citing "a lot of work to be done" in planning — the gap is normal seasonal guidance discipline, not evasion, but it's still a gap between stated confidence and committed numbers.
- **Digital/AI revenue is on track toward the $1.2B→$1.8B by 2028 Investor Day target,** with FDA-authorized AI-enabled devices rising from 85 to 100 year-over-year — a concrete, trackable proof point behind the "AI differentiation" narrative rather than just messaging.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Flyrcado ramp mechanics** drew multiple follow-ups (yield, workflow, CDL distribution reach, weekend shipping capability) — analysts pressing on whether "going slow to go fast" is genuine discipline or a miss being reframed.
- **PCS recovery timeline and Patient Care Solutions margin trajectory** were probed directly, testing whether new leadership's "top priorities" translate into Q4 numbers.
