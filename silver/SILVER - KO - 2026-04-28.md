# KO — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** Coca-Cola printed a "beat" quarter — 3% volume growth, share gains for a 20th straight quarter, EPS up 18% — but nearly all of the guidance raise is financial engineering (a tax-rate cut, FX) rather than operating improvement, and gross margin actually went negative for the first time in years. New CEO Henrique Braun's answer to every hard question was some version of "trust the playbook, it's a balanced algorithm" — a phrase repeated so consistently it reads as a deliberate non-answer to analysts probing whether pricing power and margin are actually softening.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Gross margin went negative — a rare, deliberately downplayed signal.** CFO John Murphy called it "somewhat anomalous," attributing two-thirds of the ~30bps decline to a one-off China juice-inventory phasing item, but the remainder is tea/coffee commodity pressure that "is going to continue somewhat through the year" — the first negative underlying gross-margin quarter management could recall discussing this way in years.
2. **The EPS raise is a tax story, not an operations story.** Full-year comparable EPS guidance rose to 8-9% from 7-8% specifically "due to the lower effective tax rate" (19.9%, down a point); organic revenue guidance of 4-5% didn't move at all.
3. **Supply, not demand, capped two of the hottest brands.** Fairlife and Topo Chico were explicitly capacity-constrained in North America, dragging price/mix — a rare case of Coke leaving demand on the table rather than fighting for it; the Webster fairlife plant comes online in Q2 to relieve this.
4. **APAC margin fell almost 10 points and management's explanation didn't fully hold up.** Braun and Murphy both leaned on the one-off inventory item, but Murphy conceded a second, structural cause — "just a structural headwind given the geographic mix of the markets, Japan versus the more developing part" — meaning some of the compression is durable, not one-time.
5. **Affordability is now explicit strategy for the low end, everywhere.** From U.S. multi-serve packs to Mexico's post-sugar-tax RGM response to Africa/Asia value price points, management is consciously trading mix for volume among "low-income consumers... under pressure today" — a direct read on consumer bifurcation.

## Economy & consumer
- **No unified macro read — it's a patchwork.** Braun: "the external environment differed greatly across our market... many consumers remain resilient, others are under pressure due to persistent inflation, greater macroeconomic uncertainty, and volatility driven by the conflict in the Middle East."
- **Middle East conflict hit real volume in real time.** Volume grew for the quarter but "declined in March after the onset of the conflict" — a live, dated demand shock, not a hypothetical risk.
- **Mexico's sugar tax is a repeat playbook, not a surprise.** Braun noted "it happened in 2014 as well" and the system is using RGM/personalization to "continue to do better than we expected," but volume in Mexico (and Argentina) still declined and had to be offset by Brazil and Central America.
- **Affordability is the low-income consumer's answer.** "The consumers that have pressure today are the low-income consumers, and we are really dialing up our affordability options" — multi-serve and pack-size moves in the U.S., absolute price points across EMEA/Africa.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Volume broad and real: all five segments grew.** 3% global volume, gains in both value AND volume share — the 20th consecutive quarter of value-share gains, a genuinely strong operating signal.
- **Gross margin cracked for the first time in years.** ~30bps decline from commodity pressure (tea/coffee), inventory-cost phasing, and trade-spend timing — CFO calls it manageable but not currently improving.
- **Capacity, not demand, was the drag on North America price/mix.** "Constrained production capacity for Topo Chico and fairlife" directly cited as a driver of soft price/mix — a supply-side own-goal on two growth brands.
- **APAC: volume up, profit down sharply.** Revenue grew across all APAC units but profit declined "driven by commodities, headwinds in tea and coffee, and phasing of inventory costs" — op margin down almost 10 points per analyst math, only partially explained away.
- **McDonald's relationship called "intact" despite Red Bull inroads.** Asked directly about McDonald's reportedly working with Red Bull after a long Coke partnership, Braun didn't address the competitive threat, instead pivoting to Coke's own Sprite flavors inside McDonald's — a dodge on a direct competitive-encroachment question.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Fairlife capacity is the single named capital commitment.** "The Webster capacity, it's gonna start to get online in the Q2, and we're gonna ramp up through the year" — a specific plant, specific quarter, direct response to a demand-outstripping-supply constraint.
- **Distribution and physical footprint scaled hard.** 600,000+ new outlets and 340,000+ cold-drink coolers placed in the past year — capital going into physical reach and impulse-purchase capture, not just marketing.
- **Commodity exposure is concentrated at the bottler, not KO itself.** Murphy: "our bottlers have more exposure, particularly to aluminum and PET on the back of both the oil price impact and just the overall supply disruptions" — Coke's corporate P&L is relatively insulated but the system underneath it is more exposed.
- **Pending CCBA (Coca-Cola Beverages Africa) divestiture is a margin lever, not a growth story.** A lower-margin bottling business exiting the P&L mechanically improves group margin in H2 2026 if the sale closes — margin "improvement" that's structural/portfolio, not operational.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: balanced volume/price algorithm holds all year.** Braun repeatedly promises the mix will vary quarter to quarter but land balanced — "we're managing all the levers to continue to deliver that." Action: no specific new pricing commitment was offered; RGM tools cited are the same ones already in use.
- **Belief: APAC margin recovers, but management wouldn't put a date on it.** Murphy: "we expect us to make progress in the course of the year on overall margin profile, it is a longer term play." Action: the only concrete step disclosed is removing CCBA from consolidated numbers — an accounting fix, not an operational turnaround in China/APAC margins.
- **Belief: commodity cost pressure remains "manageable."** Action: cross-enterprise procurement and market-by-market cost "playbooks" — described as flexible and reactive rather than a fixed hedging or sourcing commitment, explicitly hedged by "next few months are fluid."
- **Gap flagged:** raised full-year EPS guidance is entirely a tax and FX artifact, while the underlying margin/mix picture (negative gross margin, APAC compression) is if anything getting harder — the guidance raise papers over operating softness rather than reflecting it.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No fixed volume/price split for the rest of the year.** "Whether it's gonna be three to two like we have here in the quarter or it's gonna be two to three variable during the different quarters, well, we're gonna see."
- **No timeline for APAC margin recovery** beyond "longer term play" and "more to come on that as we go through the year."
- **No commitment on how commodity inflation resolves** — "it's difficult at this stage to say exactly how it's gonna play out," with an explicit caveat that geopolitical volatility "may cause this outlook to change."
- **Sidestepped the McDonald's/Red Bull competitive question directly**, answering with Sprite's performance inside McDonald's rather than addressing the reported shift.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Price/mix softness, two quarters running, was the dominant thread** — multiple analysts (margin, mix, LatAm specifically) probed whether weaker price/mix is structural (ongoing affordability strategy) or one-off (Easter timing, geographic mix), and management's answer leaned hard on "it varies by quarter, trust the algorithm."
- **Gross and regional margin compression** drew the sharpest, most repeated questioning — Q1 gross margin, APAC op-margin (down ~10 points), and CCBA's mechanical effect on future margin all came up as distinct but related worries.
- **Bottler-level cost exposure** (aluminum, PET, tea, coffee) was probed as a system-wide risk analysts see building faster than the corporate P&L admits.
- **Competitive and channel-shift questions** (McDonald's/Red Bull, new craft-beverage retail formats like Dutch Bros/7 Brew) surfaced a real anxiety about whether Coke's food-service moat is as durable as its "unmatched system reach" pitch — the McDonald's answer notably didn't engage with the Red Bull specifics.
- **Compressed worry:** is the "balanced growth algorithm" a genuine operating strength, or is it obscuring quietly eroding unit economics (margin, mix, bottler cost pressure) that a tax-rate cut is currently masking in the headline EPS number?
