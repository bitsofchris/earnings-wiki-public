# TTD — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-07)

**The key idea:** TTD delivered a "solid" but visibly decelerating quarter (12% YoY revenue growth, guiding Q2 below industry benchmarks) and spent the call reframing every headwind — macro pressure, agency friction with Publicis, a high-profile CSO departure to OpenAI — as evidence the open internet's moment has arrived. The tension: management's rhetoric ("best days ahead," "first inning") is unusually bullish against a backdrop of two straight quarters of deceleration and a CFO explicitly calling 2026 a year of "disciplined reinvestment" rather than growth.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth is decelerating and management knows it.** Q1 revenue grew 12% YoY to $689M and Q2 guide of "at least $750M" implies growth below the 8%+ industry benchmark analysts referenced — the second straight quarter analysts pushed Green to explain a "decel."
2. **The Publicis relationship is genuinely strained.** Green's answer — "there's been a lot said about conflict... I think that's been over-dramatized," followed by "it's probably not prudent for me to say more" — reads as damage control on an active commercial dispute, not routine ambiguity.
3. **Losing the Chief Strategy Officer to OpenAI, on earnings day, is a signal worth weighing.** Samantha Jacobson's departure was broken by trade press hours before the print; Green's response leaned heavily on reassurance ("she'll stay on our board") rather than substance, and pivoted immediately to touting new hires.
4. **JBP momentum is the strongest concrete data point in the quarter.** A record 45 JBP signings in March, total JBP count +55% YoY, and a pharma client won back from Amazon with a 114% YoY spend increase — this is the one area where the "buyer's market favors us" thesis has hard numbers behind it.
5. **The AI-search-ad thesis is a real long-term TAM story but has zero near-term monetization proof points.** Green compares LLMs/chatbots to pre-ad Netflix and argues detailed prompts justify premium (even video) ad formats, but declined to name partners or timelines — pure thesis, no contracted revenue yet.

## Economy & consumer
- **Fortune 500 brands are pulling back selectively, not broadly.** Green: most TTD revenue comes from large brands who "respond differently to macro factors... especially when the headwinds are macro and global in nature" — tariffs, geopolitical instability, and consumer pressure named as the specific drags.
- **CPG and home/garden/food-drink are the visible soft spots**, per Tahnil Davis, tied to "geopolitical uncertainty, consumer softness, and input cost inflation."
- **Auto is "an area of strength overall" but capped by tariffs** — management believes it "could be growing faster absent the impact of increased tariffs on the industry."
- **International is outperforming the US**, both for the ad market broadly and for TTD specifically, attributed to TTD's early multi-market investment — a notable divergence worth tracking as a leading indicator on where ad demand is healthiest.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **CTV and audio are the growth engines.** Video (incl. CTV) is a "low 50s%" of the mix and still growing share; audio is ~6% of revenue but "grew year-over-year at a rate higher than any other channel."
- **Record JBP signings** (45 in March, +55% YoY total count, +40% YoY new-deal spend ex-renewals) is the clearest sign of share gains, including the pharma win-back from Amazon (+114% YoY spend).
- **Margins held but reinvestment is compressing near-term leverage** — 30% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q1, full-year target reaffirmed at "at least 40%... approximately in line with 2025," meaning no margin expansion is being promised despite the growth slowdown.
- **Net income of $40M (6% of revenue)** was well below adjusted net income of $134M, flagging a wide GAAP/non-GAAP gap driven partly by stock-based compensation (opex ex-SBC grew 18% vs. 11% headline opex growth) — worth noting as spin: profitability headlines lean on the non-GAAP figure.

## Investing & scarcity
- **2026 explicitly framed as a "year of disciplined reinvestment"** — both Green and Davis used this phrase almost verbatim, prioritizing platform/AI infrastructure, retail media, measurement, and agentic tooling over margin expansion.
- **Headcount growth deliberately kept below revenue growth** — a lever for margin discipline that also signals TTD isn't chasing growth with headcount the way it might in a stronger environment.
- **The real constraint is data trust, not infrastructure.** TTD's retail data marketplace covers "more than 80% of sales from top U.S. retailers" vs. Amazon's "less than 15% of U.S. retail spend" — the moat is objectivity and data scale, not compute or talent (no mention of GPU/compute costs at all, notable for a company whose AI story is central to its pitch).
- **Buybacks continued despite the growth slowdown** — $164M repurchased in Q1 against a $1.4B cash position, signaling management doesn't see the deceleration as capital-allocation-threatening.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: walled gardens will eventually join the open internet once it "consistently gets the first dollar."** Action: partnerships with Disney, NBCU, Spotify, Netflix on biddable/programmatic integration — real but incremental, not a walled-garden capitulation yet.
- **Belief: agentic AI will be led by TTD, not disrupt it.** Action so far is limited to one named partnership (Stagwell) doing basic campaign creation/editing — Green concedes optimization capability is still to come, a gap between the sweeping rhetoric ("we'll lead the agentic revolution") and the actual product surface area shipped.
- **Belief: LLM/AI-search ad monetization is a coming TAM unlock.** Action: none disclosed — Green explicitly declined to name which AI companies TTD is in discussions with, so this remains thesis without contracted revenue.
- **Belief: the open internet's supply chain inefficiency is "one of the biggest bottlenecks."** Action: OpenPath (plugging into publisher yield-management tech) exists, but Green explicitly ruled out TTD ever doing sell-side yield management directly — a self-imposed limit on how far the "hub" ambition goes.
