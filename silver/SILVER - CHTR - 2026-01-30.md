# CHTR — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-30)

**The key idea:** Charter is trying to convert three years of underlying investment (pricing/packaging, converged mobile, video-with-apps, service guarantees) into an "improved trajectory" for broadband adds, while explicitly declining to promise the adds themselves. The tension: peak capex is over and free cash flow is about to expand meaningfully, but the core subscriber engine — Internet — is still shrinking, and management's own language ("game of inches," net video gains that "can just as easily float back") signals this is a defensive holding action against fiber overbuild, fixed wireless, and moribund housing turnover, not a growth story yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Video's comeback is being talked down by the people who produced it.** +44K video adds (vs. -123K a year ago) sounds like a turnaround, but Winfrey immediately caveats: "Q4 was really no different than Q3... it can just as easily float back into the net loss category." He's explicit the goal was never net adds — it's using video-with-apps as a broadband retention tool, calling positive video growth "a nice side benefit."
2. **Capex is cresting into a multi-year cash unlock.** 2025 marked peak capital intensity ($11.66B); Fischer guides run-rate capex below $8B/year after 2028 and quantifies the swing as "$28 of free cash flow per share based on today's share count" — a concrete, sizeable re-rating case if execution holds.
3. **Management is choosing balance-sheet conservatism over leverage-fueled buybacks.** Charter moved its post-Cox leverage target down to the low end of a new 3.5x-3.75x range (from a 3.5x-4x midpoint originally committed at deal announcement), explicitly citing shareholder preference for less leverage "during a lower growth period" — a rare instance of a leveraged-equity story de-risking rather than re-levering into weakness.
4. **The mobile land-grab framing borrows a competitor's own number as bull case.** Winfrey seizes on a rival telco's claimed 75-80% in-footprint mobile penetration ambition and argues the read-through for Charter/Comcast is "dramatic," given Charter's cost-structure advantage (no macro cell tower/spectrum capex) — an aggressive reframing of someone else's guidance as validation of Charter's own upside.
5. **New guarantees are funded commitments, not messaging.** February launches — Invincible Wi-Fi (battery backup, dual connectivity) and a guaranteed $1,000/year savings claim backed by bill credits — put real economics behind the "best service" pitch, alongside tying 2026 comp incentives to NPS for the first time.

## Economy & consumer
- **Housing, not just fiber, is the macro drag.** Winfrey: "the bigger issue over the past three years is the macro environment in terms of housing, low moves" — ranked above fiber overbuild and fixed wireless as a structural headwind.
- **Consumers are rational bundle shoppers, per management.** "I don't think that customers are ultimately, at the end of the day, fooled" by rival device subsidies — Charter's bet is total-bill transparency wins over promotional theater.
- **Trade-down within growing product attach.** Residential ARPU fell 1.2% y/y as customers shifted to lower-priced video tiers, even as "customers are taking more products per household" — the mix shift, not demand collapse, explains the softness.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Internet churn improved, sales did not.** Internet losses narrowed to 119K (vs. worse a year ago on lower connects, more than offset by lower disconnects tied to last year's ACP cliff) — an improvement built on easier comps, not renewed growth.
- **Video's app-bundling strategy is real but expense-neutral in disguise.** $165M of costs now get allocated to programmer streaming apps and netted within video revenue (vs. $37M prior year) — Fischer flags this GAAP allocation could reach "as much as $1,000,000,000 for the full year 2026," though she stresses it's "ultimately neutral to EBITDA" since an offsetting credit hits programming expense. Worth flagging as an accounting artifact analysts should normalize for.
- **Mobile is scaling but ARPU is compressing.** 428K mobile line adds, but Fischer expects "additional mobile ARPU declines" from higher gig-with-unlimited-Perks mix, before "we've bottomed out" sequentially.
- **Commercial mid-market is a bright spot.** Ex-wholesale mid-market/large business revenue grew 3%, while small business continued to soften — a bifurcation inside the B2B book.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex peaked in 2025 ($11.66B), declining to $11.4B in 2026** and below $8B/year post-2028, once rural build-out (last big year, 450K subsidized rural passings target for 2026) and network evolution wrap.
- **The binding constraint is subscriber acquisition, not network capacity.** Charter is "gig capable everywhere" already; the fight is for households in a low-move, overbuilt, fixed-wireless-substituted market — described flatly as "a game of inches."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management expects an "improved trajectory" in broadband and slight EBITDA growth in 2026. **Action:** they still refuse to project actual net broadband relationship growth for the year — the funded commitments (Invincible Wi-Fi, $1,000 savings guarantee, NPS-linked comp) are real, but they're retention/service tools, not stated top-line targets.
- **Belief:** fiber overbuilders "will [not] reach their ROI goals" in Charter's footprint. **Action:** Charter isn't slowing its own defensive spend in response — capex reduction is scheduled regardless, on the theory that density-driven cost curves will throttle rivals on their own timeline, which Winfrey admits he "doesn't know when" will arrive.
- **Belief:** Silicon Valley developers should build for Charter's ubiquitous multi-gig network (the "fill the pipe tour," Spectrum Front Row with Apple/NBA). **Action:** Charter isn't funding content rights itself ("Do we need to own those rights? No, absolutely not") — it's positioning as infrastructure host, monetization model unspecified.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 broadband growth projection.** "I'm not projecting broadband relationship growth this year" — only "an improved trajectory."
- **EBITDA-growth-ex-political is only "maybe."** Fischer won't confirm Charter clears the roughly 1-point political-advertising tailwind baked into 2026 guidance.
- **Verizon MVNO amendment details dodged.** On the renegotiated Verizon deal, Winfrey deflects: "we'll stay consistent with what Comcast and Verizon have said as well" — declining to characterize new terms independently.
- **No commitment to a specific offload/CBRS acceleration pace** — "opportunistic pace" is repeated language, avoiding a hard target beyond ~20 more markets.

## The street — what analysts asked

- One analyst pressed directly on ROI economics of fiber overbuilders continuing to invest despite Charter's insistence they won't hit return targets — Winfrey's answer effectively concedes rivals may be running on "going concern" logic rather than financial ROI, meaning Charter has no visibility into when overbuild pressure eases.
- Compressed worry: **is this a real inflection in subscriber trends, or a favorable-comp year propped up by political ad dollars and cost discipline while the underlying broadband engine still isn't growing?**
