# APP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-11)

**The key idea:** This call is a rebuttal call. The stock had sold off on fears that AI-driven competition (a "Meta could bid deterministically" scenario) and easier game creation would erode AppLovin's moat, and management spent the entire hour arguing the opposite — that a bigger, AI-improved auction with more bidders makes AppLovin's economics *better*, not worse, because their model captures the highest-value impressions and taxes the rest. The tension: they back this with exceptional current numbers (66% revenue growth, 84% EBITDA margin) but refuse every request to quantify the one thing that would prove out the next leg of growth — e-commerce.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The core defense is a non-zero-sum auction argument, asserted not shown.** CEO Adam Foroughi's central claim — "as bid density goes up, the pie expands... while our share of the auction may shrink, our economics actually grow" — is the load-bearing thesis for the entire competitive narrative (Meta, Unity, Moloco all entering) but is never backed by a disclosed metric; it rests entirely on trust in management's telling.
2. **E-commerce is real but deliberately obscured.** Management confirms a "pretty sizable uplift" in the model just weeks before the call, describes 30-day LTV/CAC breakeven on paid-marketing tests, and cites a single dramatic customer example ($4M→$16M→projected $80M revenue) — but explicitly refuses to disclose e-commerce as a percentage of revenue going forward, arguing vertical breakouts would be "misleading," a framing that conveniently also hides how small or fragile the segment still is.
3. **Margin discipline is being protected ahead of growth investment.** AXON marketing spend for the self-serve funnel is intentionally kept small ("numbers aren't that big... you didn't see anything reflected in our EBITDA margins") — they are gating scale-up on generative-AI creative tooling catching up, not on demand, which pushes any real revenue contribution from paid marketing out past H1 2026.
4. **Capital return is aggressive and fully self-funded.** $2.58B in buybacks for the year, funded entirely from free cash flow ($3.95B, +91% YoY), with $3.28B authorization still unused — a clear signal management sees the stock as mispriced given the sell-off framing this whole call was meant to counter.
5. **Conversion-rate headroom is the real long-term bull case, but is starved of a fix.** Management states conversion could rise from current levels toward a 5% ceiling seen on high-confidence matches, but that the constraint is advertiser/content diversity, not model capability — meaning the growth path depends on succeeding at exactly the e-commerce/self-serve ramp they won't quantify.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read at all.** The call contains zero commentary on broader consumer spending, inflation, or ad-market cyclicality — everything is framed at the auction-mechanics level.
- **Casual gaming audience framed as recession/disruption-resistant.** "This audience skews older and female... very unlikely to stop playing crosswords in 10 years because they're talking to a chatbot" — a specific, testable claim about demand durability that gold-layer synthesis should watch against actual mobile-gaming engagement trends.
- **E-commerce merchants are the new demand-side consumer proxy.** The featured customer story (Israeli cookware company scaling from near-$0 to a projected $80M) is used as a stand-in for "we grow the real economy," contrasted explicitly against large ad-agency brand spend ("if we help Coca-Cola place more dollars... we're not moving the needle").

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Core mobile-gaming/MAX auction is executing at a historic level.** Q4 revenue $1.66B (+66% YoY), adjusted EBITDA $1.4B (+82% YoY, 84% margin, ~95% incremental flow-through) — Foroughi calls it "the strongest operating performance in our history."
- **E-commerce self-service is early and still referral-gated.** Live for 1.5 years overall but opened to self-service (referral-only, not GA) only in Q4; GA still targeted for "first half of this year."
- **Advertiser funnel has meaningful breakage.** Of qualified leads, only 57% of advertisers go live — a 43% breakage rate management attributes mainly to lack of properly formatted video creative, which they're addressing with generative-AI tooling.
- **Video/creative automation is the near-term unlock.** Generative AI interactive-page creation is already live in pilot with 100+ customers; a video-generation model is coming next, explicitly designed to close the "tens of thousands of ads" (gaming) vs. "hundreds of ads" (e-commerce) creative-volume gap.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Scarcity is discovery/matching, not compute or supply.** Foroughi's framed thesis: AI-driven content abundance makes discovery the scarce resource, which is exactly what AppLovin's models are built to solve — a self-serving but coherent argument for why AI commoditizing game creation helps rather than hurts them.
- **Data penetration is the actual e-commerce constraint.** Their pixel is on "thousands" of sites versus Meta's "over 10 million" — the gap they must close before the e-commerce model can approach gaming-model maturity (roughly three years and ~12-14 quarters of continuous improvement, per Foroughi).
- **Capital deployment is buybacks over reinvestment at scale.** $2.58B repurchased for the year, funded entirely from free cash flow, versus deliberately restrained marketing spend on customer acquisition — capital is going to shareholders faster than it's going to growth experiments right now.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** MAX ecosystem will keep growing double-digit through 2026 and e-commerce conversion could triple-plus toward a 5% ceiling. **Action:** marketing spend against that opportunity remains intentionally "controlled" and small — the gap between the stated size of the opportunity and the capital currently committed to capturing it is the most notable divergence on the call.
- **Belief:** generative-AI creative tools will meaningfully raise the 57% advertiser go-live rate. **Action:** only in pilot with 100+ customers for one ad component (interactive pages); video-generation model not yet live — the tooling that unlocks scale is still mid-build, not shipped.
- **Belief:** e-commerce model economics will compound like the gaming model did. **Action:** no disclosed investment ramp, no headcount or budget figures tied to accelerating e-commerce data penetration — the "compounding" is trusted to organic customer ramp, not an explicit acceleration plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refuses any e-commerce/vertical revenue breakout**, framing disclosure itself as harmful to investor understanding rather than simply undisclosed — a notable rhetorical move.
- **Won't build or share a "P times Q" model** for e-commerce advertiser counts, explicitly deferring until the self-serve funnel is "stable," with no timeline given.
- **Declines to reaffirm the previously floated $1M/day AXON marketing spend target**, instead describing current spend as immaterial "testing."
- **Won't speculate on whether Meta could shift to deterministic bidding**, dismissing it as "against Apple's terms" and implausible rather than addressing the technical feasibility question directly asked.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Meta competitive threat dominated the Q&A** — repeated questions (from multiple analysts) on whether Meta's IDFA-based bidding intensification signals a return to Meta's historical dominance, and whether deterministic targeting could bypass AppLovin's edge; management's answer was consistent but circular (competition has always existed, our economics have never decayed from it).
- **E-commerce sizing and self-serve rollout mechanics were the second major cluster** — GMV thresholds, customer mix shift, model maturity versus AXON 2, timeline to GA — all met with anecdote (single customer stories, qualitative "great trends") rather than quantification.
- **The MAX moat question came up directly** — asked point-blank how defensible mediation is against a well-funded new entrant; Foroughi's answer conceded mediation itself is only "a few percentage points better," locating the real moat in bundled advertising-tool stickiness (over 50% of some publishers' total UA spend) rather than the auction technology itself.
- **The compressed worry:** analysts don't doubt Q4's results — they doubt whether the next leg of growth (e-commerce, self-serve, AI-driven creative) is real and sizable, or a narrative substituting for disclosure while the core gaming business's growth inevitably decelerates off a much larger base.
