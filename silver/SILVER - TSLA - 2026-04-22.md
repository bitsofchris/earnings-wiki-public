# TSLA — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-22)

**The key idea:** Tesla is entering a self-described "very big capital investment phase" — $25B+ in 2026 capex, negative free cash flow for the rest of the year — betting that FSD/Robotaxi, Optimus, and in-house chip production (Terafab) will justify it. Meanwhile the near-term business is a mixed bag: real demand resurgence in Europe and a growing FSD subscriber base, propped-up margins from one-time warranty and tariff items, and a battery-pack bottleneck limiting how fast any of it can actually ship.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Capex is exploding well ahead of proven autonomy revenue.** Musk explicitly links the capex ramp to "significantly increased future revenue stream," but Taneja acknowledges Robotaxi revenue "will not be super material this year" — the spending is happening on faith, justified by pointing to what other major tech companies are doing.
2. **Auto margin improvement is partly manufactured.** The headline 17.9%→19.2% ex-credit improvement included a ~$230M warranty write-down release and unspecified tariff relief; underlying cost pressure from tariffs and high rates (subvention costs recognized upfront) is described as ongoing, not resolved.
3. **Hardware 3 owners just got bad news dressed as an upgrade offer.** After previously implying HW3 could reach unsupervised FSD, Tesla now says it flatly cannot due to memory bandwidth, and the fix is a paid trade-in or a physical computer-and-camera swap via new micro-factories — a real cost and credibility hit for early FSD buyers.
4. **The real bottleneck is boring: battery packs, not cells or chips.** Despite all the AI/chip narrative, Taneja and Lars Moravy identify battery pack assembly capacity (Berlin, Reno retooling, China LFP) as what's actually capping vehicle output right now.
5. **Terafab is scarcity-driven, not margin-driven — and organizationally messy.** Musk denies it's about extracting better economics from chip suppliers, framing it purely as "we don't see a path to having enough sufficient quantity of AI chips" — but admits the SpaceX/Tesla split requires independent board reviews and "a lot of complexity" to avoid shareholder conflicts.

## Economy & consumer
- **Europe snapped back hard.** France and Germany deliveries grew "over 150% quarter-over-quarter," with South Korea and Japan also up — Taneja attributes this to product/pricing work, not the tailwind of higher gas prices, which he says arrived after the order-rate improvement began.
- **Backlog at a two-year high for a Q1.** Highest Q1 order backlog in over two years signals real pent-up demand rather than just discounting.
- **FSD engagement is deepening, not just growing.** Subscriber churn is declining and average miles driven per customer is rising — Taneja's anecdote ("I press a button, and it just goes... now I don't even have to park") is offered as proof the product itself, not price, is driving retention.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Berlin hit a production record** — over 61,000 units in Q1 — while the company explicitly says **battery pack capacity, not cell supply,** is the binding constraint on scaling further.
- **Margin gains are partly one-time.** The auto margin improvement to 19.2% ex-credits included roughly $230M from warranty write-downs and unquantified tariff relief; energy storage margin hit a record 39.5% largely because of **more than $250M in one-time tariff recognition benefits**, which Taneja says will normalize downward from here.
- **Energy storage deployment fell sharply sequentially** — 8.8 GWh, a 38% quarter-over-quarter decline — attributed to lumpy customer deployment timing, though full-year 2026 is still expected to exceed 2025.
- **Residential solar is in "a bit of a correction"** after the loss of the homeowner tax credit; Tesla's response is a new lease product designed to let Tesla itself capture the tax credit and pass through competitive pricing.
- **Stock comp and AI spend are structurally elevated for the year** — a full quarter of 2025 CEO compensation plan stock-based comp plus AI5/Cybercab/Semi/Optimus/Megapack development spend, both flagged to continue at "elevated levels" through 2026.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$25B+ capex for 2026**, funding six factories going into operation this year, plus AI infrastructure for Robotaxi and Optimus — management is explicit that free cash flow will stay negative for the rest of the year as a result.
- **The Terafab chip strategy is taking shape but remains organizationally unresolved**: Tesla funds a ~$3B research fab at Giga Texas (mask, logic, memory, and packaging under one roof — Musk claims this combination is unique in the world), SpaceX takes the first phase of the larger production-scale Terafab, and Intel partners on manufacturing using its 14A process. The Tesla/SpaceX split still requires separate board approvals and conflict-of-interest review.
- **Battery pack assembly, not cell chemistry, is the near-term output governor** — in-house 4680 packs ramping in Berlin, Reno retooling after ~10 years on the same line, and China LFP module/pack production all cited as capacity being added "as we speak."
- **AI5 tape-out finished early** via, per Musk, six months of every-weekend work; AI4.1/AI4+ (32GB RAM vs. 16GB, ~10% compute increase, built with Samsung) is targeted for production mid-2027, while AI5 is now planned for Optimus and data centers rather than the car, since AI4 is deemed sufficient for unsupervised driving.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** unsupervised FSD in customer cars by Q4, Robotaxi in roughly a dozen states by year-end, Europe Robotaxi eventually. **Action so far:** Robotaxi live in three cities (Austin, Dallas, Houston) all running the same V14.3-era software, with expansion explicitly gated by "rigorous validation" rather than announced capacity — a real gap between the stated 2026 timeline and what's operationally proven today.
- **Belief:** Optimus will be "Tesla's biggest product ever... probably the biggest product ever." **Action:** production start pushed to "late July, August," with Musk himself refusing to forecast a 2026 output rate, calling the ramp dependent on "10,000+ unique items" all going right — belief far outpaces any committed schedule.
- **Belief:** Optimus needs orchestration/voice intelligence from Grok while running substantial capability locally so it isn't stranded by connectivity loss — a real signal of AI-infrastructure-adjacent thinking (local inference + cloud orchestration split), relevant to anyone tracking edge/cloud inference architecture.
- **Action, not just belief:** the Hardware 3→4 retrofit micro-factories are a concrete, funded commitment, unlike most of the forward-looking Robotaxi/Optimus timeline.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No Optimus production-rate target for 2026**, with Musk stating twice it is "impossible to predict" given the number of new supply-chain dependencies.
- **Optimus V3 reveal deliberately delayed** toward the production date specifically to deny competitors a frame-by-frame copy window — a strategic information hedge, not a technical constraint.
- **No committed Europe Robotaxi timeline** — Musk says outright "I actually don't know" and that Tesla is "at the mercy of the regulators."
- **No confirmation Terafab yield/economics will reach parity** with existing suppliers — the Barclays question on this was answered by reframing Terafab purely as a supply-scarcity hedge, sidestepping the profitability question asked.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered on **FSD-adoption math** (subscriber growth outpacing car sales, churn, engagement), **Robotaxi safety metrics and expansion cadence**, and the **Terafab ownership/funding structure** between Tesla, SpaceX, and Intel.
- A pointed question on **Terafab economics** ("is this really about leverage over chip suppliers?") was met with a scarcity-only framing that didn't directly engage the profitability angle.
- A lingering **NHTSA sun-glare camera filing** question got a partial answer — Musk says the camera hardware issue was already fixed months ago and the filing concerns older vehicles, while Ashok added new software will disable FSD if the camera view is obstructed.
- Compressed worry: **is autonomy revenue going to show up before the negative free cash flow and $25B+ capex bill catches up with the balance sheet.**
