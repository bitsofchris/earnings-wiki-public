# REGN — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-28)

**The key idea:** Regeneron's in-line growth engine (Dupixent, Libtayo, EYLEA HD) is firing on all cylinders, but the quarter is overshadowed by a second consecutive complete response letter on EYLEA HD's prefilled syringe — caused by a third-party filler's (Catalent/Novo Nordisk) failed FDA inspection, not Regeneron's own regulatory function. Underneath the noise, management is making a decade-scale bet: bespoke bispecifics (Lynozyfic, odronextamab) and a broadening complement/siRNA/CD3 pipeline are being pushed aggressively into earlier treatment lines, backed by double-digit R&D growth and a >$7B domestic manufacturing buildout meant to end the company's filler dependency for good.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A third-party supplier is now Regeneron's single biggest near-term risk.** The EYLEA HD prefilled-syringe CRL stems entirely from an FDA "official action indicated" classification at Catalent Indiana (a Novo Nordisk affiliate) — Regeneron has no in-house fill-finish capacity yet and won't until its own plant "come[s] online during the coming year," per Schleifer.
2. **Management drew a hard line distinguishing manufacturing risk from regulatory competence.** Pressed directly on repeated CRLs, Schleifer insisted "the issues that we have had have not been internal regulatory problems... it is, in some respects, a manufacturing issue in terms of getting online our own filling" — a notable, unprompted defense that signals the Street's growing skepticism on this point.
3. **Lynozyfic is being treated as a franchise-scale bet, not a niche late-line launch.** Schleifer disclosed plans for "as many as 10 registrational trials," explicitly targeting Darzalex's $15B run-rate in a "$30 billion market," with early monotherapy data (100% ORR in high-risk smoldering myeloma, 83% ORR in newly diagnosed patients) driving the aggressive earlier-line push.
4. **Patient affordability, not clinical differentiation, is capping EYLEA category growth**, and Regeneron's own goodwill gesture (matching up to $200M in donations) landed at under $1 million matched — a rare admission of a failed corporate initiative.
5. **The drug-pricing negotiation with the Trump administration is being framed as alignment, not adversity** — Schleifer says priorities are "closely aligned" and leans on the $7B+ US manufacturing commitment as leverage, a notably conciliatory posture compared to industry-wide friction over US drug pricing reform.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Dupixent, Libtayo, and EYLEA HD all grew double digits**: Dupixent worldwide sales hit $4.9B (+26% cc), now approved for 8 US indications and treating 1.3 million patients; Libtayo grew 24% cc to $365M with a new adjuvant skin-cancer approval already "off to a great start."
- **EYLEA HD hit an all-time-high $431M** in US sales with 18% sequential unit demand growth, but this came at the direct expense of legacy EYLEA, which fell 10% sequentially — a cannibalization story, not incremental market growth, and management expects the pattern to continue into Q4.
- **A second CRL landed on EYLEA HD's prefilled syringe**, this time solely due to unresolved FDA inspection findings at supplier Catalent — Regeneron is racing parallel paths (an alternate syringe filler application due January 2026, and an alternate vial filler with a PDUFA date in "late December") to salvage approval.
- **Lynozyfic's fifth-line myeloma launch is tracking normal early indicators** (formulary listings, payer coverage, REMS completion) but management is candid that revenue contribution in this heavily pretreated population will be "modest" — the real value is proof-of-concept for earlier lines.

## Investing & scarcity
- **R&D spend guided up mid-teens% for 2026**, explicitly to fund an expanding registrational slate across myeloma, lymphoma, anticoagulation, and obesity — a clear acceleration in capital commitment, not steady-state spending.
- **The binding constraint is fill-finish manufacturing, not capital, people, or regulatory relationships.** Schleifer was unusually direct: qualifying a backup filler requires the FDA to review "literally what equipment it's going to touch" plus full stability and quality testing — a multi-year process that COVID-era supply chain disruption delayed further.
- **The $7B+ domestic manufacturing buildout (NY and NC) is the structural fix** — framed as both a competitive necessity and a bargaining chip in the government pricing negotiation ("investing in U.S. manufacturing is not only vital for creating jobs... but for safeguarding national security").

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: EYLEA HD's real commercial unlock requires label enhancements (4-week dosing, RVO indication, prefilled syringe) still pending FDA action — management projects only "moderate to high single digits" sequential growth until those land.
- **Action**: Two parallel filler-approval tracks are in motion (alternate syringe filler by January 2026, alternate vial filler with a December PDUFA date) specifically to unblock this — a real, dated commitment rather than a wait-and-see posture.
- **Belief**: Bispecifics like Lynozyfic and odronextamab have "unprecedented" efficacy that should carry into earlier treatment lines.
- **Action**: Up to 10 registrational trials committed for Lynozyfic alone, including a head-to-head Phase III against Darzalex — money and trial infrastructure are already behind the belief, not just guidance language.
- **Gap worth flagging**: management is confident glaucoma and uveitis represent new "EYLEA-scale" ophthalmology franchises via CD3 and internally discovered genetic targets, but the actual clinical programs are only "rolling out... over the next couple of months" — the rhetoric (each could be "the EYLEA for uveitis... the EYLEA for glaucoma") is well ahead of any committed trial data.
