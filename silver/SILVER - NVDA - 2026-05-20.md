# NVDA — fiscal 2027-Q1 call (2026-05-20)

**The key idea:** NVIDIA reported a blowout quarter and reframed its own business — splitting Data Center into "Hyperscale" and a new "ACIE" (AI clouds/industrial/enterprise) bucket — specifically to argue it should grow *faster* than hyperscaler capex, not track it. The subtext: as investor anxiety builds about a hyperscaler capex plateau, Jensen is pointing at a second, fragmented, harder-to-model demand pool (AI natives, sovereigns, enterprise/industrial) that he says is now nearly as large as hyperscale and growing faster — the thesis the whole segmentation change exists to sell.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The segmentation change is a narrative move, not just an accounting one.** Colette Kress introduced Hyperscale vs. ACIE mid-call, and every management answer thereafter routed back to it — Jensen explicitly used it to argue NVIDIA "should be growing faster than hyperscale CapEx" because ACIE ($37B, +31% QoQ) is now nearly the size of Hyperscale ($38B, +12% QoQ) and, in his telling, has more runway.
2. **Vera CPU is a genuinely new, uncontested TAM claim.** The $200B CPU market and ~$20B of visibility this year sit entirely outside the prior $1T Blackwell/Rubin 2025-2027 number — this is incremental by design, and Jensen leans hard on an "agents need CPUs for orchestration and tool use" argument ("billions of agents... every one of those agents are going to spin off sub-agents") to justify it.
3. **Anthropic is now a headline logo, described as recently as "largely zero" coverage.** Jensen frames the expanded Anthropic partnership (capacity across AWS, Azure, CoreWeave, SpaceX AI) as a material share gain in frontier AI and inference, a noticeably fast reversal from having "largely zero" exposure previously.
4. **China remains fully zeroed out despite approved licenses** — H200 export approval hasn't converted to a single dollar of guided revenue, and management won't predict if it ever will this cycle.
5. **Capital returns spiked sharply** — dividend raised 5x (from $0.01 to $0.25/share) and an $80B buyback added on top of $39B remaining, with management targeting ~50% of free cash flow returned to shareholders this year, even as supply commitments also jumped to $145B.

## Economy & consumer
- No direct consumer-spending commentary — Edge Computing (gaming/PC) saw "consumer demand fell modestly due to higher memory and system prices," a rare admission of price-driven softness in an otherwise uniformly bullish call.
- The dominant "economy" framing is B2B/enterprise capex: hyperscale capex forecast to exceed $1T in 2027, with AI infrastructure spend broadly seen reaching **$3T-$4T annually by decade's end**.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Data center revenue $75B, +92% YoY, +21% QoQ** — Blackwell GB300/NVL72 demand described as the "fastest product ramp in our company's history," with Spectrum-X now "larger than all ethernet network peers combined."
- **Gross margin flat at ~75%** as Blackwell dominates the mix; full-year guide still mid-70s, so no major margin expansion or compression signal.
- **What's not working:** Edge/consumer PC demand softened on pricing; China compute revenue remains a complete blank despite license approval — management still can't say whether product will even clear customs.
- **DSO dipped to 45 days** on "favorable timing of collections," expected to normalize to mid-50s next quarter — a one-quarter working-capital tailwind, not a trend.

## Investing & scarcity
- Total supply commitments (inventory + purchase commitments + prepaids) jumped to **$145B**, and Jensen states directly the company expects to be "**supply constrained throughout the entire life of Vera Rubin**."
- Capital priority order is explicit: R&D and ecosystem investment first, then shareholder returns — record $20B returned in the quarter, dividend raised 5x, new $80B buyback.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** ACIE/enterprise-industrial-sovereign demand will outgrow hyperscale over time, eventually addressing "$50 trillion-$80 trillion of the world's economy." **Action so far:** ACIE grew 31% QoQ this quarter vs. 12% for Hyperscale — one data point, not yet a trend, but management is already restructuring disclosure around the bet.
- **Belief:** Vera Rubin will be "even more successful than Grace Blackwell." **Action:** production ships start Q3, ramping into Q4, with Kress declining to commit to ramp *slope* — the belief is stated with high confidence, the operational commitment is deliberately vague.
- **Belief:** LPX/SRAM-based inference accelerators could reach ~20% share of premium token services "someday." **Action:** none of substance yet — Jensen calls it a "niche product for some time to come" today, an honest hedge against his own upside framing elsewhere.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **China revenue:** despite approved H200 export licenses, "we have yet to generate any revenue" and management is "**uncertain whether any imports will be allowed**" — zero dollars baked into guidance again, with no timeline offered.
- **Vera Rubin ramp cadence:** Kress explicitly declined to compare the ramp slope to GB200's record pace — "hard to say at this point," "a little early to say."
- **LPX share ceiling:** Jensen gave a wide, noncommittal range ("whether it's 20% or 10%, it just depends on where we are in the development of AI") rather than a forecast.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Multiple questions probed the new Hyperscale/ACIE segmentation — why now, and what it implies competitively — signaling analysts suspect the reporting change is strategically timed to blunt hyperscaler-capex-deceleration fears.
- Inference market share against custom silicon (LPX, CPX) came up repeatedly, with the underlying worry being whether NVIDIA's platform lock-in holds as specialized low-latency chips proliferate.
- The standalone Vera CPU $20B figure prompted direct questions on incrementality vs. cannibalization — analysts wanted to know if this is new revenue or repackaged existing GPU-attach spend; Jensen's answer (four distinct Vera use cases, all incremental to the prior $1T number) directly addressed it rather than dodging.
- Compressed worry: **is NVIDIA's growth truly decoupling from hyperscaler capex, or is the new segmentation just a more favorable way to describe the same demand pool as hyperscale capex growth cools?**
