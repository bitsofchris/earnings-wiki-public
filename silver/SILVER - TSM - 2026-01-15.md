# TSM — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-15)

**The key idea:** TSMC guided a dramatic step-up in 2026 CapEx ($52-56B, up ~30% from 2025) and raised its 5-year AI CAGR to mid-to-high 50s%, all while the CEO openly admitted he'd spent months personally interrogating hyperscaler customers to check the demand was real before committing the capital. The tension of the call is that TSMC is the physical chokepoint for the entire AI buildout — wafer supply, not power or capital, is what's binding — yet management repeatedly declines to say how long the cycle lasts or to translate its guidance into token/gigawatt terms analysts wanted.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **CEO did his own diligence before betting $52-56B.** CC Wei: "I'm also very nervous about it... I talked to those cloud service providers, all of them... I also double check their financial status. They are very rich." This is a foundry CEO stress-testing hyperscaler balance sheets before green-lighting CapEx — a notable admission that even the entity with the best visibility into AI demand doesn't take it on faith.
2. **Wafer supply is the actual bottleneck, not power.** Asked directly about gigawatt/power constraints on AI data centers, CC relayed that customers told him "silicon from TSMC is a bottleneck and ask me not to pay attention to all others because they have to solve the silicon bottleneck first." TSMC now evaluates power, turbines, racks, and cooling supply chains as inputs to its own planning, but says none of those are the binding constraint today.
3. **Guidance raised, but management won't commit to a cycle length.** Full-year 2026 revenue guided to "close to 30%" growth, AI CAGR raised to mid-to-high 50s%, yet CC directly declined to forecast cycle duration: "can the semiconductor industry to be good for 3, 4, 5 years in a row, I'd tell you the truth, I don't know."
4. **Arizona expansion is accelerating in response to customer pull, not proactive planning.** Fab 2 tool-in moved up, fab 3 construction started, fab 4 and the first advanced packaging fab are in permitting, and a second land parcel was purchased — CC: "due to the strong demand from our customers, we are also putting forward the production schedule."
5. **Gross margin story is mostly productivity and utilization, not pricing.** CFO Huang was explicit that in recent years "the pricing benefits to the profitability was just enough to cover the inflation cost" — the margin upside comes from utilization rate, manufacturing excellence, and cross-node capacity optimization, undercutting the read that TSMC is aggressively re-pricing its way to higher margins.

## Economy & consumer
- **Memory-price inflation is squeezing the low end, not TSMC's book.** CC: memory costs are pushing up consumer electronics prices, but "the high-end smartphone is less sensitive to the memory price," and TSMC's customer base skews to that high end — "for TSMC, no impact."
- **Non-AI segments still get pulled along by AI infrastructure.** Networking/server growth is framed as AI-adjacent ("the networking processor, you still need to have AI data to scale up or scale out"), suggesting little of the portfolio is truly AI-decoupled.
- **Tariff and component-price risk flagged for 2026 but not quantified.** CC names tariff policy and rising component prices as sources of "uncertainties and risk" driving a more "prudent" planning posture, without giving a magnitude.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Margins beat guidance on cost control and FX, not volume surprise.** Q4 gross margin of 62.3% beat the high end of prior guidance by 130bp, driven by "better-than-expected cost improvement efforts" and a more favorable exchange rate than assumed.
- **N2 ramp is healthy and fast.** N2 hit high-volume manufacturing in Q4 "with good yield" at both Hsinchu and Kaohsiung, with N2P and A16 following in H2 2026 — CC expects the N2 family to be "another large and long-lasting node."
- **Mature-node capacity is being quietly rationalized.** CC confirmed 8-inch and 6-inch wafer capacity is being reduced/optimized, while insisting existing customers "will not let them down" — a real capacity reallocation dressed as customer-first messaging.
- **2026 overseas dilution is now quantified and rising.** Overseas fab ramp-up dilutes gross margin 2-3 points early, widening to 3-4 points later; N2 ramp itself will dilute margin 2-3 points in H2 2026 — margin headwinds are getting bigger, not smaller, even as utilization improves.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx nearly doubling versus three years ago, explicitly tied to a supply gap that won't close soon.** 2026 budget of $52-56B compares to $40.9B in 2025 and $29.8B in 2024; the prior 3-year total was $101B, and management says the next 3 years will be "significantly higher" without giving a number.
- **Cost per unit of capacity is structurally rising.** CFO: "the CapEx dollar required to build 1,000 wafer per month capacity of N2 is substantially higher than 1,000 wafer per month capacity for N3. The CapEx per k cost for A14 will be even higher" — a straightforward admission that Moore's-Law-era capacity economics are getting more capital-intensive, not less.
- **New capacity won't relieve the bottleneck until 2028-2029.** CC: fabs take 2-3 years to build, so 2026-2027 CapEx dollars "almost none" affect near-term supply — the near-term fix is pure productivity ("1% or 2% productivity improvement, that is free to TSMC"), with real relief targeted for "2028, 2029."
- **Long-term margin target reaffirmed despite rising costs.** Management still targets "56% and higher through the cycle" gross margin and "high 20s percent" ROE, positioned as achievable via pricing discipline, cost improvement, and cross-node optimization rather than pricing power alone.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes AI demand is durable ("endless") — and is funding that belief with concrete capital.** The CapEx raise to $52-56B, accelerated Arizona construction, and a second land purchase are the funded proof points behind the "megatrend" language — this is one of the few calls where talk and action are well aligned.
- **Believes near-term supply/demand gap won't close until 2028-2029 — and is explicitly choosing productivity over new construction as the 2026-2027 lever.** CC frames this as deliberate sequencing, not a shortfall: focus on output-per-fab now, new fab volume later.
- **Gap: raised long-term AI CAGR guidance (mid-to-high 50s%) without being able to translate it into the terms analysts wanted (tokens, gigawatts).** CC admitted "I lose the track to be frank with you" on token consumption — the guidance is a top-line number without a stated underlying demand model management will defend in detail.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No cycle-length forecast.** CC explicitly declined to predict how many years the AI-driven upcycle can continue.
- **No confirmation that ~20% ASP increases are "the new normal."** CFO redirected to a 6-factor profitability framework, crediting utilization and productivity over pricing as the dominant profit driver.
- **No gigawatt or token-consumption figure behind the AI CAGR guide.** When pressed for the compute/power assumptions underlying the mid-to-high 50s% AI CAGR, CC gave a qualitative answer about GPU generational leaps (Hopper→Blackwell→Rubin) rather than a number.
- **No specific figure for post-2026 CapEx.** CFO confirmed the next 3 years will be "significantly higher" than the prior $101B but declined to give a number.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **AI-bubble skepticism was the dominant thread**, opened directly ("whether the AI demand is real or not") and revisited via the token-consumption/gigawatt line of questioning later in the call.
- **U.S./Arizona expansion pace** — timeline to reach the previously floated 20-30% of 2-nanometer capacity in the U.S. — got a process answer (permits, land, speed) rather than a firm timetable.
- **Competitive threat from Intel Foundry**, prompted by U.S. political commentary and reported NVIDIA/Apple interest, was waved off by CC on the grounds of 2-3 year technology qualification lead times.
- **Power/electricity availability as an AI infrastructure constraint** was raised and effectively closed off — TSMC's answer, sourced from hyperscaler feedback, is that silicon (not power) is the bottleneck.
- **Mature-node (8-inch) exit rumors and pricing sustainability** were both addressed with reassurance short on hard commitments.
- The compressed worry across the desk: **is the unprecedented CapEx step-up backed by demand TSMC can actually quantify, or is management guiding off qualitative customer reassurance it can't fully verify itself?**
