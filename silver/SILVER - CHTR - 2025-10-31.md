# CHTR — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-31)

**The key idea:** Charter's mobile and video businesses are working — mobile scaling past 20% line growth, video losses cut to a quarter of last year's — but the core broadband engine is stalling as gross adds turned negative year-over-year for the first time in 2025, squeezed between AT&T's fixed-wireless expansion, continued fiber overbuild, and a housing market that isn't moving. Management's answer is to bundle harder (mobile, video app inclusion, Xumo) and cut costs with AI while waiting for macro or competitive tailwinds it admits it cannot time. Underneath the "we'll grow Internet again" confidence sits a same-quarter EBITDA guidance reversal, from "Q4 better than Q3" in September to "Q4 pressured at least as much" now, driven by mispriced promotional offers management had to pull from the market.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Internet gross adds went negative YoY for the first time this year.** CEO Winfrey: "Q1 and Q2, our gross adds were actually higher year-over-year. Q3, it was lower year-over-year," with the low-income segment hit hardest — a segment shift he flagged but couldn't explain, calling it "not an excuse" but "pretty notable."
2. **A guidance miss inside one quarter.** In September, CFO Fischer signaled Q4 EBITDA decline would be milder than Q3's; by this call she's reversing that — new promotional offers "impacted ARPU a bit more than we had anticipated without driving the additional sales that we expected," and are being pulled from the market as of November.
3. **Bundling is the entire defensive strategy.** 21% of Internet customers are now converged (mobile + Internet), and video-app activation is driving a "significant" churn reduction by tenure cohort — the clearest lever management has while broadband net adds shrink.
4. **AI cost-reduction is real today, agentic AI is a 2026 bet.** Existing ML/AI tools already cut cost-to-serve across an $8B service cost base; Winfrey admits he "kind of rolled my eyes" a few months ago on agentic AI but now sees "real and material" impact — though explicitly 12-18 months out and unsized.
5. **Capex is allegedly peaking, but with a footnote.** 2025 capex guided down to ~$11.5B from $12B, with management calling 2025 the peak "even if by a small margin" — but the reduction is because network-evolution spend got pushed into 2026, not eliminated, muddying the "peak" claim.

## Economy & consumer
- **Housing and mobility are the macro drag.** Winfrey: "We have a really muted housing environment. There's slow household formation and low move rates" — directly compressing the number of addressable broadband switchers.
- **Mobile substitution for home broadband continues.** Cited alongside fixed-wireless competition as a structural, not cyclical, pressure on gross adds.
- **Low-income segment pulled back sharply.** "The impact there was most pronounced in the low-income segment" — a consumer-health signal management couldn't fully attribute to a specific competitor action.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Mobile is the standout.** ~493K lines added in the quarter, 2M over 12 months (20%+ growth), with the majority from higher-value Unlimited Plus plans and 88% of device traffic now on Charter's own network rather than costly macro towers.
- **Video losses cut to a quarter of prior year's.** 70,000 video losses vs. 294,000 a year ago, driven by new pricing/packaging and app-inclusion churn reduction — though CFO Fischer was blunt that the real goal is "bringing stability" to a shrinking, margin-pressured category, not growth.
- **Internet is the weak link.** 109,000 Internet losses, in line with last year nominally, but gross adds fell YoY for the first time this year — "small changes in sales or churn have an outsized impact on Internet net gains" in the current low-volume environment.
- **Revenue basically flat ex-noise.** Consolidated revenue down 0.9% YoY, but +0.4% excluding advertising and streaming-app cost reallocation — the underlying business isn't shrinking, but growth is thin.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex guided down, but the "peak" claim has an asterisk.** 2025 capex now ~$11.5B (from $12B guide), with the cut explained by network-evolution capital pushed into 2026 — not reduced scope, just deferred timing.
- **BEAD/rural build-out is a small, defined commitment.** ~84,000 subsidized passings awarded across 20 states, ~$230M of Charter's own capital net of subsidies over several years — a contained, low-optionality investment relative to the core network.
- **The real scarcity is addressable gross-add volume, not capacity.** Charter has "the best network... fully capable" per Winfrey, but the binding constraint is a shrinking pool of switchable customers being split among more competitors (AT&T fixed wireless, fiber overbuilders).
- **B2B network monetization (Amazon, Nexar deals) is exploratory, not material.** Winfrey: "There's nothing here that I can sit here and tell you is material today" — real optionality, explicitly not yet revenue.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: Internet growth returns once macro/competitive noise clears.** Winfrey lists multiple potential catalysts (household formation, mobile substitution steadying, fiber overbuild finishing) but says explicitly "it's just unclear... very difficult to predict, frankly, the timing" — a stated belief with no committed timeline.
- **Action: pull bad offers, lean into bundling.** The only concrete near-term action against the Internet slowdown is withdrawing the ARPU-diluting Q3 promotional offers and doubling down on mobile/video bundling to protect churn — a defensive, not growth-restoring, move.
- **Belief: agentic AI could be "significant" for the $8B cost-to-serve base.** Action so far is limited to "a few key partners" for 2026 integration — real spend commitment is still ahead, not behind, this statement.
- **Gap flagged directly:** management is confident net adds inflect eventually but is not betting capital or guidance on when — free cash flow framing ("mechanical" step-down from capex) is being used to reassure investors independent of whether Internet growth ever returns.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No size on AI/agentic cost savings:** "We don't know yet the exact size" of the $8B cost-to-serve opportunity, despite calling it potentially "significant."
- **No material claim on new B2B network monetization** (edge CDN, financial-transaction low-latency services, EV offload) — all described as exploratory conversations, not revenue plans.
- **Declined to abandon national pricing/packaging framing even while running test offers that had to be pulled** — insisted "our national pricing and packaging hasn't changed" while acknowledging a specific pricing experiment failed and was yanked.
- **No new leverage target announced** despite a direct question about paying down debt given lower growth — Fischer reaffirmed existing 3.5x-4.0x post-Cox target rather than revising it downward for a weaker growth environment.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Why should broadband get better from here?** The dominant question, probing whether churn improvement (bundling, video-app activation) can offset gross-add erosion, or whether this is structural share loss to AT&T fixed wireless and fiber overbuilders.
- **The EBITDA guidance reversal** — analysts caught the flip from "Q4 better than Q3" to "Q4 worse than Q3" within one quarter and pressed on what changed (answer: mispriced promo offers).
- **Competitive pricing discipline** — whether Comcast/Verizon pulling back on rate increases signals reduced broadband pricing power industry-wide; Winfrey argued Charter's lower relative ARPU gives it more room to pass through costs than peers.
- **Balance-sheet risk** if broadband growth doesn't return — Fischer defended current leverage targets on the strength of projected free cash flow rather than offering a contingency plan.
- **Compressed worry:** is Charter's broadband net-add decline a temporary cyclical trough, or the start of permanent share erosion to fixed wireless and fiber that bundling and AI cost-cuts can only cushion, not reverse?
