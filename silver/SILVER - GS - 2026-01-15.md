# GS — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-15)

**The key idea:** Goldman delivered a record year (EPS +27%, ROE 15%) and is explicitly telling investors "2021 is not the ceiling" for the 2026 investment banking cycle — backlog at a four-year high, deregulation freeing capital and expense burden, and AI (One GS 3.0) positioned as the next efficiency lever. The tension: analysts keep asking management to commit to a higher permanent return target, and management keeps declining, reaffirming mid-teens as the through-cycle number while conceding near-term results could exceed it.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The Apple Card exit is now complete and reshaped Q4 optics.** Goldman announced a deal to transition the Apple Card portfolio (following GM's card exit last August), finishing its retreat from consumer lending; the transition added $0.46 to Q4 EPS and 50bps to ROE via a $2.5B reserve release that outweighed a $2.3B revenue hit, but it also makes the reported Q4 efficiency ratio look worse YoY — an accounting artifact management asks to be read on a full-year basis.
2. **Backlog and management's own language point to a bigger 2026 than 2025.** IB backlog rose for a seventh consecutive quarter to its highest in four years; Solomon: "I don't think we've yet [been] in the middle of the potential for a full-on M&A and sponsor cycle," pointing to $1T of sponsor dry powder and $4T of portfolio-company value still needing to be monetized.
3. **Deregulation is being framed as a double capital unlock — not just capital ratios.** Beyond the 320bp CCAR stress-buffer improvement, Solomon argues the last five years' compliance/consulting cost burden is itself being freed up for "more productive growth," though neither he nor Coleman would quantify the dollar amount.
4. **AWM getting a formal upgrade, but wealth remains adviser/headcount-constrained by design.** New 30% pretax margin target (up from 25% actual in 2025) and a new 5% long-term fee-based net-inflow target were introduced; Solomon admits GS "would like to do more" adviser hiring but is "constrained because we're also trying to balance and deliver returns" — a direct trade-off between near-term payout and wealth-platform investment.

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro read is constructive but explicitly fragile.** Coleman: "the operating environment can shift quickly. Economic growth, policy uncertainty, geopolitical developments, and market volatility are factors we continue to monitor closely," citing April 2025's tariff-driven slowdown as precedent for how fast sentiment can reverse.
- **No direct retail-consumer commentary** beyond the Apple Card/Marcus wind-down — Goldman has now fully exited the consumer-lending business it once tried to build, leaving no consumer read in this note; NIM compression in the Marcus deposit book was flagged as a lingering drag.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record results across nearly every franchise:** #1 M&A adviser for a 23rd consecutive year, record equities revenue ($16.5B, +$3B YoY), record FICC/equities financing ($11.4B), record AUS ($3.6T), record alternatives fundraising ($115B).
- **Investment banking fees +25% YoY in Q4**, with backlog growth "primarily driven by advisory" — management stresses the advisory flywheel pulls through financing, hedging, and secondary activity elsewhere in the firm.
- **Q4 YoY efficiency ratio looked worse, but it's an Apple Card artifact**, not a comp-discipline breakdown; full-year operating leverage was strong (revenue +9%, pretax +19%, EPS +27%).
- **Private banking and lending flagged for 2026 NIM compression** as rates come down, even as lending penetration to UHNW wealth clients keeps growing.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital deployment priorities, in order:** fund client franchise (acquisition financing, equities/fixed financing, UHNW lending) first, then dividend growth, then buybacks — dividend just raised 50% YoY to $4.50/quarter, with $32B of buyback capacity remaining.
- **Alternatives is the clearest AWM growth bet:** raising $75-100B/year "sustainably" targeted going forward, fee-paying alternative AUS targeted at $750B by 2030, underpinning the existing $1B incentive-fee target.
- **Bolt-on M&A only, high bar for anything transformational:** T. Rowe Price partnership plus Industry Ventures and Innovator acquisitions "fill in gaps" and "accelerate the journey," but Solomon repeated multiple times that transformational AWM M&A remains very unlikely ("the bar has to be high... there are very few really, really great large businesses").
- **The real constraint is self-imposed capital discipline, not compute/people/supply**: wealth-adviser hiring pace is being throttled to protect near-term returns, not because talent or capital is unavailable — Goldman had 1.1M experienced-hire applications in 2025 (+33% YoY), so labor supply is not the binding constraint.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: 2026 could rival or exceed 2021 IB activity levels.** Action: reaffirmed only mid-teens through-cycle ROE targets rather than raising them — a clear gap between the optimistic verbal framing ("2021 is not the ceiling," "highly constructive setup for 2026") and the conservative formal target, which management frames as deliberate ("we're not going to set targets until we're very comfortable").
- **Belief: AI/One GS 3.0 will meaningfully lift productivity and free capacity for growth investment.** Action: six specific workstreams identified and underway since fall 2025, but zero quantified targets disclosed — the biggest talk/action gap on the call, with Solomon acknowledging he'd hoped to say more but couldn't.
- **Belief: tokenization, stablecoins and prediction markets are real emerging opportunities.** Action: no product commitments — described as "a big team of people spending a lot of time" evaluating, with regulatory clarity (the Clarity Act) still stalled in Washington; Solomon met personally with two prediction-market companies but made no capital commitment.
