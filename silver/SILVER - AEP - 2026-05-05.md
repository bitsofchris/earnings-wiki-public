# AEP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-05)

**The key idea:** AEP is compounding an already extraordinary load-growth story — 56 GW to 63 GW of contracted demand in a single quarter, an $78B capital plan — while its CEO openly floats reducing reliance on PJM (and possibly SPP) because the RTOs can't connect generation to load fast enough. This is a company that has solved its supply-chain and equipment problem (turbines, transformers, 765 kV steel, labor via Quanta) and is now fighting a market-structure problem it doesn't fully control.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Load growth is compounding, not just growing.** Contracted load rose from 28 GW (last fall) to 56 GW (last quarter) to 63 GW now, with ERCOT alone jumping from 36 GW to 41 GW in three months — nearly 90% of the total is data centers/hyperscalers.
2. **AEP is quietly threatening to walk from PJM.** Fehrman: "the current state of PJM's performance and stakeholder approval process does not give me great confidence... if something is not done now, I expect we could still be having these same conversations in 10 years." AEP is "assessing all of our options" including alternative structures, and running a parallel review of SPP membership (though SPP gets a notably better grade — "more aggressive in getting after these issues").
3. **The affordability pitch is explicit and self-serving but real.** Up to $16B in cost offsets for existing customers is engineered directly from fixed-cost allocation to large-load contracts — a mechanism to defend rate cases politically as much as to help ratepayers, but it's also driving genuinely improving ROE outcomes (Ohio 9.7%→9.84%, Arkansas 9.5%→9.65%, West Virginia 9.25%→9.75%).
4. **Capital is expanding but back-loaded and conservatively financed.** The $6B increase is only 18% equity-funded (vs. a typical industry 30-40%), and management stresses forecasted $47B of five-year operating cash flow — signaling confidence they can grow without heavy dilution, at least for the disclosed plan.
5. **A further $10B+ pipeline is being teed up, not committed.** Piketon transmission, Wyoming fuel cells, and unspecified generation projects are flagged now but deliberately excluded from guidance until the Q3 call — a "conservative" positioning move aimed at managing Street expectations upward incrementally.

## Economy & consumer
- **No traditional residential-consumer read** — AEP is a regulated utility; its "customer" story here is corporates (hyperscalers, industrials), not consumers, though affordability for residential ratepayers is repeatedly emphasized as a political/regulatory priority.
- **Broad-based industrial demand alongside data centers**: Fehrman frames the growth as "not only driven by data centers, but also broader economic development," and states get generation policy support because "transmission forms the backbone for economic development."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Transmission execution is the core strength**: 2,100+ miles of 765 kV lines, six decades of experience, a strategic labor partnership with Quanta Services, and three newly awarded 765 kV projects (SPP, PJM, MISO) totaling roughly $3.5B in near-term additions.
- **Regulatory outcomes are running AEP's way**: "we have not ended up with a reduced ROE in any recent rate case outcome" — a striking claim given the broader utility sector's regulatory friction.
- **What's not working: interconnection speed.** AEP has equipment, engineering and contractors lined up but says the RTOs themselves — PJM specifically — are the bottleneck, a problem entirely outside AEP's direct control.
- **Segment softness**: Transmission Holdco earnings were pressured this quarter by storm restoration and higher property taxes, though management expects that segment to turn favorable year-over-year by year-end.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Generation capital up to $24B**, driven by I&M's planned Sycamore/Big Sandy gas acquisitions and 10+ GW of secured turbine slots (Mitsubishi, GE) — pricing under NDA, but access itself is treated as the competitive moat: "we are a preferred supplier for these customers."
- **Nuclear stays exploratory only**: "any nuclear investment will require strong capital protection, disciplined balance sheet safeguards... no projects will move forward if they place undue risk on our business or our shareholders" — an explicit hedge against the industry's nuclear enthusiasm.
- **The real scarcity is queue/interconnection throughput, not hardware.** AEP has securitized its supply chain years out; what it cannot buy its way out of is RTO stakeholder-approval velocity.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: demand is durable and understated — 190 GW sit in AEP's interconnection queue against only 63 GW contracted, and management calls the $78B plan and even the incremental $10B "conservative."
- **Action matching belief**: capital plan raised twice in six months (72B→78B), turbine capacity locked years out, DOE loan guarantees pursued aggressively ($1.6B closed, more filed).
- **Gap**: despite bullish demand belief, AEP is simultaneously hedging its RTO relationships — a company that believes fully in the demand is nonetheless preparing contingency plans against the market structure meant to deliver it, and won't commit financing detail on the next $10B until Q3, i.e., talk (unprecedented opportunity) is running ahead of committed capital by design.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No PJM exit commitment, no timeline**: "we're not saying we're exiting PJM... we're in the very early stages of the evaluation phase."
- **No detail on financing the $10B+ incremental pipeline** until the Q3 call, despite naming specific projects (Piketon, Wyoming) today.
- **Flatly declined to discuss M&A or asset sales** even when asked directly what could be divested to ease equity needs: "I would just leave it at that."
- **No turbine pricing disclosed** — commercially confidential.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered hard on **PJM exit mechanics** — pushed multiple times (three separate analysts) for timeline, backstop-bidding intentions, and GenCo-structure alternatives (e.g., West Virginia), with management repeatedly declining specifics beyond "early stages."
- Recurring probes on **financing the growth**: equity content, ATM pacing, hybrid instruments, and whether asset sales could offset dilution — management held the line on no divestitures and modest (18%) equity content.
- Questions on **Wyoming fuel-cell contract risk** got unusually precise answers (June 30 deadline, six-month extension, 110%-of-cost put-back option) — a sign analysts are worried about counterparty/customer follow-through, not just AEP's own execution.
- Compressed worry: **is the 63 GW of "contracted" demand actually going to get connected on the growth trajectory AEP is promising, or will RTO/interconnection dysfunction — a risk AEP itself flagged unprompted — cap realized earnings growth regardless of how much steel and turbine capacity AEP has locked up?**
