# GOOGL — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-29)

**The key idea:** Alphabet posted its strongest quarter in years — Cloud through $20B for the first time, backlog nearly doubling to $462B, Search accelerating to 19% growth despite the "AI will cannibalize search" narrative — while simultaneously admitting it can't build compute fast enough and is raising CapEx again, with 2027 flagged as a much bigger step-up still to be quantified. The tension underneath: a company that says it's compute-constrained is choosing to sell some of that scarce compute (TPU hardware) directly to outside customers rather than pouring 100% of it into its own unmet Cloud demand, a choice justified only in ROIC-framework language, never numbers.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Cloud has crossed an inflection point.** Revenue grew 63% to over $20B for the first time, operating margin nearly doubled year-over-year to 32.9%, and backlog nearly doubled sequentially to $462B — Anat Ashkenazi attributed the margin jump to top-line leverage plus "scientific process innovation" in data center operations, not pricing.
2. **AI-native products are now the growth engine, not a side bet.** Cloud revenue from GenAI-model-based products grew nearly 800% year-over-year, Gemini Enterprise paid MAUs grew 40% quarter-over-quarter, and existing customers "outpaced their initial commitments by 45%" — a sign of real usage expansion, not just logo-count growth.
3. **CapEx guidance keeps ratcheting up mid-year.** FY26 CapEx raised to $180-190B, and Ashkenazi pre-announced that 2027 CapEx "will significantly increase" versus 2026 — disclosed as a directional certainty months before any number is attached, a pattern that should make investors expect further raises.
4. **Selling TPUs to outside customers while claiming to be compute-constrained.** Google will begin shipping TPU hardware into customer-owned data centers (capital markets firms, AI labs like Thinking Machines Lab), with Pichai admitting in the same breath that "our cloud revenue would have been higher if we were able to meet the demand" — an unresolved allocation tension.
5. **The "20% ad coverage" ceiling is explicitly under attack.** Philipp Schindler said Gemini's improved intent-understanding creates "upside" to Google's historical ~20% rate of monetizable queries, extending ads into longer, previously-unmonetizable conversational searches — a structural lever, not just a cyclical one.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read given** — growth was attributed entirely to product/AI-driven share gains in retail, finance, and health verticals, with FX providing a 3-point tailwind in Q1 that's expected to shrink to ~1 point in Q2.
- **Consumer AI adoption is accelerating fast**, with Google One/Gemini subscriptions hitting 350M and their strongest-ever quarter, driven by demand for premium Gemini model access.
- **Search usage is expanding, not contracting**, despite AI Overviews/AI Mode substituting for some traditional queries — Pichai and Schindler both emphasized queries are "at an all-time high."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Cloud is the standout**: 63% revenue growth, operating income tripled year-over-year, margin near-doubled to 32.9%.
- **Search accelerated to 19% growth**, driven by ads-quality improvements from Gemini deployed "across our entire Ads infrastructure" and 20+ bid-strategy improvements over the past year.
- **YouTube Network advertising (third-party inventory) declined 4%** — the one shrinking major line item, even as YouTube's owned advertising (direct response, brand) grew 11% and subscriptions posted their best quarter since 2018.
- **Wiz acquisition (closed March) is "exceeding expectations,"** per Ashkenazi, though it brings a disclosed low-single-digit-point headwind to Cloud operating margin for the rest of 2026.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx raised again**: FY26 guide now $180-190B (from $175-185B), driven by the Intersect acquisition; ~60% of Q1 technical infrastructure spend went to servers, 40% to data centers/networking.
- **Compute is the binding constraint** — Pichai stated directly that Cloud revenue growth was capped by supply, not demand, and described an internal "ROIC framework" for rationing capacity across GDM research, Search, YouTube, and Cloud.
- **Supply chain inflation acknowledged but not quantified** — Pichai said the company is "factoring that into any commentary we give" on CapEx, while claiming scale gives them deeper, more favorable supplier partnerships than smaller rivals.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: Alphabet says the agentic/AI-search era is "expansionary," not cannibalistic, for both organic Search and ads monetization, with real upside to the historical 20% query-coverage ceiling.
- **Action behind it**: heavy, uncapped CapEx increases now with 2027 flagged as a bigger step yet to be quantified, plus direct TPU hardware sales into external data centers and the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) launched with Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, Salesforce, Stripe, Shopify, Etsy, Target, and Wayfair now on board.
- **Gap**: management repeatedly declined to say how much bigger 2027 CapEx will be, and declined to detail margin economics on the very large ($100B-scale) GenAI cloud deals investors are most worried about — the confidence in the narrative isn't matched by disclosed numbers.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Ashkenazi would not size 2027 CapEx, promising only "more clarity in future earnings calls."
- Pichai twice deflected questions on TPU-sale and mega-deal pricing/margins to a generic "ROIC framework," declining to give any specifics on unit economics.
- Schindler repeatedly stressed Gemini-app advertising is "not rushing anything" — an open-ended non-commitment on monetizing the fastest-growing consumer AI surface.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Compute allocation dominated**: multiple analysts pressed on how Alphabet rations scarce capacity across internal R&D, Search, Cloud, and now external TPU sales — Pichai's answer stayed at the framework level ("ROIC," "long-range planning") without hard allocation percentages.
- **Cloud margin sustainability** was probed directly, given the market thesis that AI infrastructure carries structurally lower margins than legacy Cloud — Ashkenazi's answer leaned on efficiency gains and top-line leverage rather than conceding any margin dilution risk.
- **Agentic commerce and ad-pricing mechanics** came up twice (UCP's implications, and how agentic shopping affects volume/price mix for core AdWords) — Schindler kept redirecting to "user experience first," never engaging with the pricing-mechanics part of the question.
- Compressed worry: **can Alphabet keep raising CapEx at an accelerating rate while proving that AI-driven Cloud and ad revenue converts to margin as cleanly as the legacy business did — and is it already quietly under-serving its own Cloud demand to free up TPUs for outside sale?**
