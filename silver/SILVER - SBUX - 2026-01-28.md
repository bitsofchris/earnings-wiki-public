# SBUX — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-01-28)

**The key idea:** Starbucks says its turnaround has hit the inflection it promised — top-line first, earnings to follow — with US transaction growth positive for the first time in two years and both rewards and non-rewards customers growing together for the first time since 2022. But the margin math tells a less tidy story: North America operating margin contracted 420bps as labor, tariff, and coffee-cost investments outran the recovery, leaving management asking investors to trust a "not linear" path and a wide EPS range while the real earnings payoff is pushed to the back half of the year and beyond.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The transaction inflection is real and broad-based.** US company-operated transaction comps grew YoY for the first time in eight quarters, and this was "the first quarter we grew both rewards and non-rewards transactions since 2022." Niccol was explicit that non-rewards customer decline had been "never healthy," making broad-brand relevance (not couponing) the deliberate fix.
2. **Margin is the bill coming due for the turnaround, and it's bigger than modeled optimism suggests.** North America operating margin fell ~420bps YoY, roughly a third of it from tariffs and coffee-price inflation, the rest from Green Apron labor investment annualizing. Consolidated EPS dropped 19% even as revenue grew 5% — proof the "top line first, earnings follow" sequencing is running behind on the earnings half.
3. **China is being partially exited, not doubled down on.** Starbucks is selling up to 60% of its China retail operations to Boyu Capital, deconsolidating ~8,011 company-operated stores into a licensed JV structure — even as China posted its strongest comps of the three quarters shown (7%, third straight quarter of growth). This is capital discipline overriding a genuinely strong operating story.
4. **People, not real estate or demand, is gating growth.** Niccol said flatly there's "no barrier on unit growth" from a site-availability standpoint — thousands of identified locations in the US alone — but new openings were destabilizing existing store staff, so a new "coffee house coach" (assistant manager) pipeline had to be built before the growth engine restarts.
5. **A $2B, multi-year cost program is running in parallel with growth investment**, spread across procurement, G&A, and technology efficiency, explicitly framed as "not broad-based cost-cutting" but distributed ownership ("a list of projects with people's names next to it").

## Economy & consumer
- **Visitation increased across every age and income cohort** in the quarter, per Niccol, cited as behavioral (not just survey) evidence that broad marketing and menu relevance — not price — is driving the recovery.
- **Value perception held strong alongside average ticket growth (+1%)**, which management framed as delivering value "through menu innovation and customer connection, not through discounts" — a claim worth some skepticism given the parallel move of "repurposing" discount/coupon spend into marketing dollars (CFO Smith), meaning some price-based demand generation was cut, not eliminated.
- **No inflation-driven consumer pullback mentioned**; the only inflation discussion was on the cost side (coffee and tariffs), not demand elasticity.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: throughput and service.** Café and drive-through service times are both under the 4-minute target company-wide during peak, even with transaction growth; the 650-store Green Apron pilot cohort is outperforming the fleet by ~200bps in comp.
- **Working: brand and marketing.** Holiday launch week was a record-revenue week for US company-operated stores; brand affinity, connection, and convenience scores all improved.
- **Not working / still broken: licensed and grocery/retail channel.** "Our U.S. licensed store portfolio revenue declined in Q1, primarily due to ongoing trends within the grocery and retail channels" — a segment management didn't dwell on or explain further.
- **Not working: labor model still needs retuning.** Niccol flagged ongoing work "to strengthen our supply chain and reevaluate menu offerings" and "fine-tune" the labor model by store format — an admission the current model isn't yet right-sized.

## Investing & scarcity
- **New store format ("Ristretto") with multiple footprint sizes** is designed to lower build cost and unlock faster, more flexible unit growth once people-capability constraints ease.
- **Digital menu boards rolling out system-wide** to enable AM/PM daypart-specific menus (ritual morning vs. "reset" afternoon) — a genuine operational lever tied to the afternoon-daypart growth thesis.
- **The binding constraint is explicitly people, not capital or real estate**: "there's no barrier on unit growth... it really is areas that I think drive a lot of opportunity" — but disruption to existing staff during past openings forced a new manager-pipeline program before growth resumes at scale.
- **China capital reallocation**: proceeds from the Boyu JV are earmarked for debt reduction and balance-sheet flexibility, not reinvestment in China itself — a signal of capital discipline over growth-chasing in an already-strong market.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: margin recovers in H2 as costs anniversary and sales leverage builds.** Action backing it: Green Apron investment anniversaries in Q4, $2B cost program actively being executed, coffee/tariff costs expected to abate in Q3/Q4 — concrete mechanisms exist, but each depends on external commodity/tariff timing management doesn't control, a real gap between conviction and control.
- **Belief: unit growth reaccelerates meaningfully.** Action: new store format, new manager pipeline, but FY26 guidance of only 150-175 net new US company-operated stores is modest relative to the "thousands of sites" rhetoric — the stated opportunity is far larger than the near-term commitment, with the bigger numbers deferred to Investor Day messaging the next day.
- **Belief: the afternoon daypart is a major untapped opportunity.** Action: digital menu boards, an energy/protein platform pipeline — real product and infrastructure investment, aligned with stated belief.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to decompose the comp drivers.** Repeatedly asked to size how much of the 4% comp came from service vs. marketing vs. innovation, Niccol answered "it's hard to distill it" and instead argued the components are inseparably synergistic — a plausible but also convenient dodge that avoids accountability for any single lever.
- **CFO wouldn't rule out additional mid-year investment spend** beyond what's already annualizing, framing the wide EPS guidance band ($2.15-$2.40) as intentional "flexibility" rather than committing to a tighter number.
- **No specific total dollar or percentage sizing given for the protein/health platform's revenue contribution** — Niccol cited "low awareness but high repeat" without quantifying mix.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered around three anxieties: **whether the comp strength is durable and attributable to something repeatable** (repeated asks to isolate service vs. marketing vs. menu), **whether margin recovery is real or aspirational** (multiple questions on the pace and composition of the 420bps NA contraction and the wide EPS range), and **unit growth credibility** (whether "thousands of sites" translates into a believable buildout given past disruption to store operations).
- The compressed worry: *is Starbucks buying comp growth with margin it may not get back on the timeline promised?*
