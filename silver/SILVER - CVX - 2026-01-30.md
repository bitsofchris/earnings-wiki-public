# CVX — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-30)

**The key idea:** Chevron closed a record year — production, refinery throughput, free cash flow all up — while absorbing two unplanned operational hits (a TCO power-system outage, a drone-damaged CPC mooring berth) that it insists don't change the 2026 plan. Underneath the "bigger, stronger, more resilient" framing is a company actively re-rating geopolitical risk: it's more bullish on growing in Venezuela and courting Libya/Iraq than on its home California refining base, betting fiscal terms and capital discipline matter more than jurisdiction.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Two operational hits, one story: "unchanged" guidance.** A TCO power-distribution failure forced a proactive production suspension, and a Black Sea drone strike knocked out a CPC loading berth for over a month — yet Chevron reiterated its $6B TCO free-cash-flow guidance at $70 Brent unchanged, attributing 2026 resilience to "maintenance optimization" absorbing the lost time.
2. **Venezuela is becoming a real portfolio lever, not a sideshow.** Gross Venezuela production is up over 200,000 b/d since 2022 to ~250,000 b/d, self-funded entirely from venture cash, with Wirth flagging "the potential for up to an incremental 50% production growth over the next eighteen to twenty-four months" pending US authorizations — a scale-up big enough to matter to the total portfolio.
3. **Cost program keeps beating its own target and got raised.** The structural cost reduction program delivered $1.5B in 2025 (>$2B run-rate) and the target was expanded to $3-4B by 2026, with Bonner crediting the October reorg and citing AI use in supply-chain negotiations as a new, tangible lever.
4. **Portfolio "high-grading" is explicit strategy, and Middle East underweight is by design — until now.** Wirth says Chevron avoided Iraq entirely and stayed out of Libya for a decade because service-type contract terms didn't compete, but a post-Trump-visit "notable uptick in inbound inquiries" and improving fiscal terms have reopened both, plus a fresh Libya MOU.
5. **California framed as a cautionary tale, not a growth market.** Wirth calls competitor refinery closures in California a tailwind for Chevron's remaining scale advantage, but uses the moment to indict state policy: "decades of... poor energy policy making that has made it more difficult to invest," pointedly contrasting it with more-investable Venezuela.

## Economy & consumer
- **Demand growth is structurally slow, and that's the whole strategic premise.** Wirth: "the demand for our products isn't growing at an enormous rate... arguably 1% a year, give or take. Gas is growing a bit more strongly than that" — this single line underwrites the entire pivot from volume growth to cash-flow/margin growth.
- **No direct consumer-health commentary** — Chevron doesn't touch retail fuel demand elasticity or consumer spending; the closest proxy is California's isolated, policy-driven high fuel prices, framed as a market-structure artifact rather than a demand signal.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record production and refining throughput carried the year.** 2025 was "the highest full-year worldwide and US production in Chevron's history" and "the highest US refinery throughput in two decades," with adjusted free cash flow up over 35% YoY despite Brent down ~15% — genuine operating leverage, not a pricing tailwind.
- **Permian capital efficiency is outperforming its own plan.** Bonner: holding the Permian flat at 1M b/d for three quarters while CapEx has already fallen to $3.5B, with drilling rig efficiency "more than doubled" since 2022 — a plateau strategy delivering more cash than modeled.
- **Two supply-chain/operational failures dented the quarter.** The TCO power outage forced a full recycle-mode shutdown, and the CPC single-mooring-berth bottleneck (from a December drone strike) throttled Kazakhstan exports for 30-45 days; Wirth declined to detail root cause on the power issue beyond calling it "a mechanical issue."
- **Chemicals and refining margins under quiet pressure.** Sequential adjusted earnings fell ~$600M quarter-over-quarter, driven partly by "lower Chemicals earnings, and Refining volumes" — a soft spot management didn't dwell on.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Eastern Mediterranean is getting real capital committed, not just interest.** Leviathan just reached FID to expand gross capacity toward ~2.1 Bcf/d by decade-end, Tamar's optimization project is starting up (~1.6 Bcf/d), and Aphrodite entered FEED — Wirth expects the combined projects to raise production ~25% and "double earnings and cash flow" by 2030.
- **The binding constraint is explicitly people and capital, allocated by return, not availability of opportunity.** Wirth: "in a world of limited human resources and limited capital resources, we need to deploy these to what we believe are the highest return opportunities" — used to explain why Iraq and Libya were passed on for a decade and why LNG expansion is selective.
- **Chemical-recovery technology is being scaled as a capital-efficient lever inside existing wells.** Bonner cites 20% ten-month cumulative recovery improvement from proprietary surfactant treatments in the Permian (treatment coverage rising from ~40% to ~85% of new wells, targeting 100% by 2027), plus 5-8% declines reduced in ~300 existing-well retreatments — cheap incremental barrels versus new drilling.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: Middle East and North Africa terms are finally becoming competitive → Action: active discussions, not yet capital committed.** Chevron signed a Libya MOU and is "engaged in discussions" in Iraq, but Wirth is explicit these remain conversations pending "improvements in fiscal terms" — a real signal with money not yet behind it.
- **Belief: Venezuela could grow up to 50% more within two years → Action: currently limited to venture-funded reinvestment, no new outside capital committed.** All current Venezuela activity is funded from venture cash flow covering "tax and royalties," debt repayment, and basic maintenance — Chevron hasn't yet put fresh CapEx in, waiting on "stability... confidence in the fiscal regime" and a newly passed hydrocarbon law still under review.
- **Belief: shale/tight cross-learning will double recovery → Action: org consolidation already executed, technology scale-up still in pilot stage.** The Permian/DJ/Bakken/Argentina businesses were merged into one organization and surfactant treatments are already near-ubiquitous in the Permian, but Bakken/DJ results are only "some treatments... in the fourth quarter" with results still pending — the org move is real, the technology payoff isn't proven outside the Permian yet.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No timeline commitment on Venezuela's longer-term portfolio size.** Wirth: "I think it's a little early to say what our longer-term outlook is" — despite quantifying near-term growth potential, he wouldn't extend that into a strategic sizing statement.
- **Declined to speculate on Kazakhstan's OPEC+ compensation-cut mechanics** tied to TCO's unplanned downtime, calling it "a matter for the Republic... which we're not privy to."
- **No root-cause detail on the TCO power outage** beyond ruling out sabotage or cyberattack — "I think it's a mechanical issue, I can say that. But beyond that, I don't want to say anything more."
- **No quantitative view on how much Venezuelan heavy crude the US market can absorb** without disrupting heavy-light spreads or backing out Canadian heavy: "I don't have a simple rule of thumb I can give you."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **TCO reliability and debottlenecking dominated early questions**, probing both the power outage details and whether new plant capacity upgrades (retrayed columns) will show through once fully ramped — management confirmed the upside is plausible but undemonstrated: "we've actually not run that at full capacity long enough."
- **Venezuela scale and structure drew the most strategic curiosity** — how big it could become in the portfolio, and whether Chevron is shifting to a self-funding model; Wirth's answer stayed carefully bounded to near-term mechanics rather than a portfolio-weight commitment.
- **Portfolio concentration versus diversification** came up directly, with Wirth defending scale-over-spread as a safety and management-bandwidth argument rather than disputing the concentration itself.
- **M&A financing structure (cash vs. equity) and the shift to a debt-to-cash-flow metric** suggest analysts are probing whether Chevron is bracing for further inorganic deals, possibly in Kazakhstan, funded differently than the Hess transaction.
- **Compressed worry: is Chevron's operational reliability (TCO, CPC) keeping pace with the geopolitical and portfolio ambition it's signaling in Venezuela, Libya, and Iraq?**
