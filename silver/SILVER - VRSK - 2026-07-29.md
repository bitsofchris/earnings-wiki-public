# VRSK — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Verisk is trying to prove that in a softening insurance pricing cycle, AI-enhanced data products can lift renewal pricing and usage enough to offset cyclical pressure rather than just ride it out — the Anthropic MCP launch, XactAI adoption, and Premium Audit AI renewal lift are the early evidence. Management is candid that monetization is still small and unquantifiable, but treats client engagement intensity as the leading indicator.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **First insurance data provider with MCP connectors for Claude**, built with Anthropic on what Lee Shavel called an "expedited timeline from initial discussion to production and public launch" — one connector for underwriting, one for claims, with a top-10 carrier already live and usage "building along with" interest.
- Actively **developing additional connectors with Anthropic and other Frontier Model companies**, plus a next-generation, digitally native **agentic underwriting platform** for a global insurance firm, and early-stage agentic capabilities layered onto the "Ask Max" life-solutions assistant.
- **XactAI adoption has accelerated sharply**: user count is up roughly tenfold since its Q4 2025 launch, now approaching 7,000 licensees, automating claim summarization, photo labeling, document extraction, and estimating recommendations.
- Launched **Verisk Synergy Studio**, a cloud-native catastrophe modeling platform, alongside a re-engineered U.S. Tropical Cyclone Model — both delivered "on-time and on-budget," with first clients already onboarded and a "robust pipeline" queued to migrate through year-end.
- Closed the **tuck-in acquisition of McKenzie Intelligence Services**, a geospatial/real-time catastrophe-and-conflict event-response company, to pair with existing cat models and claims data for more complete event response.
- Laid out three monetization paths for AI: **richer subscription renewal pricing** (already happening, small scale), **transactional pricing for early AI experimentation**, and **licensing fees for agentic platforms** — explicitly framing subscription capture as the durable end state, transactional as a bridge.

## What's changing
- Combined ratios and industry profitability remain strong, catastrophe losses are **tracking below $100 billion globally for the first time since 2020**, and carriers have pivoted from rate hikes to **growth-driven price competition**, most pronounced in commercial property.
- **Federal government contract work stoppage has ended**, resuming for a one-year term at the start of Q3 — removes one of the three drags cited on this and the prior call.
- Client conversations on AI have shifted from experimentation to **accuracy, efficiency, repeatability, and ROI** — Verisk is responding by investing in RAG and a "semantic layer" that structures data for LLMs rather than building foundation models itself.
- Premium Audit AI is "beginning to show up commercially" in its **first renewal cycle post-launch**, an early but real proof point that embedded AI supports pricing power, not just efficiency claims.
- Clients building their own in-house LLMs was reframed by Shavel as **net-positive rather than disintermediation risk** — proprietary cleansed data and the semantic/RAG layer stay relevant regardless of whose model sits on top.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **AI's contribution to organic growth still can't be quantified** — asked directly whether it's tens of basis points or more, Shavel could only say he's "more confident this is additive," a notably soft answer after two quarters of AI messaging.
- **Sales cycles remain elongated**, per Mann, driven by client-side legal and data-rights negotiations that "remain consistent" quarter over quarter — not resolving as fast as the AI narrative suggests.
- **Transactional revenue declined** on tougher prior-year comps, softer commercial property volumes expected to "persist... in the second half," lower international travel volume tied to **Middle East travel disruptions**, and a slow hurricane start — the ILS strength that flattered Q2 explicitly "will not repeat" in Q3.
- Net income and GAAP EPS **fell year-over-year**, pressured by a prior divestiture, a higher tax rate, higher interest expense, and **higher legal fees tied to ongoing litigation** — largely absent from the prepared remarks and surfaced only in the financial detail.
- CIO Nick Defond is departing after two decades, covering the mainframe-to-cloud migration, with only an interim replacement (the CTO) named — no permanent search timeline given, notable given the company is mid-AI-platform buildout.
- Guidance still assumes an "average year" of hurricane activity despite management itself flagging June-July as unusually quiet — the base case hasn't moved even as the input has.
