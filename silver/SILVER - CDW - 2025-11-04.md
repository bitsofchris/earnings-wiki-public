# CDW — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-04)

**The key idea:** CDW's quarter is a study in a business coasting on breadth while its two biggest end markets — public sector and enterprise data-center refresh — stall out under policy and macro uncertainty. Small business and Services are quietly becoming the growth engine, and management is candid that 2025 is a "period of transition" back toward normalized operating leverage after two years of pandemic-distorted comp accruals flattered 2024's expense base.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Services is the real growth story, not hardware.** Services revenue grew 9% (managed/professional services specifically up 14%) and now makes up 9% of net sales, nearly double its 5% share in 2020, delivering "nearly 1/3 of our total gross profit growth" this quarter alone — CFO Al Miralles flagged it as the practice most likely to be an "outlier" in future growth contribution.
3. **Small business is outperforming and CDW won't extrapolate from it.** SMB grew top line and gross profit in double digits, accelerating from Q2, driven by cloud and client-device demand plus early uptake of "AI workstations" as local inference boxes — but when asked if this predicts broader-market recovery, Leahy demurred: "unclear... we need to be a little cautious about that right now."
4. **Enterprise solutions spending is stalling on decision paralysis, not budget.** Storage and larger data-center projects were "uneven," with Miralles attributing it more to macro/geopolitical hesitation ("is this the time to get on with the spend, or could we kick the can a bit more?") than to a genuine AI-driven reallocation, though he wouldn't fully rule AI decision-making in as a factor.
5. **Margin recovery is a 2026 story management won't yet size.** SG&A-to-gross-profit efficiency ratio improved to 57.7% from a worse Q2 level but remains above the "sweet spot" of 55-56%; getting back there requires sustained gross-profit growth plus operating leverage — Miralles offered the qualitative recipe but declined to give a number.

## Economy & consumer
- **Volatility, not any single shock, is the operative word.** Pressed on why she'd called the environment "complex" for four straight quarters, Leahy answered directly: "If I had to boil it down to one thing, I would say volatility... that has had technology buyers, business owners, schools, all institutions adjusting to the volatility and, therefore, not having a certainty and predictability to invest."
- **Institutions appear to be adapting to the unpredictability.** Leahy noted leaders are "getting used to the unpredictability... and just starting to really pick up and move forward with mission-critical needs" for fear of falling behind — a soft signal of pent-up demand normalizing rather than a fresh macro win.
- **Small business is the standout consumer signal**, described as showing a "slight shift" toward leaning more heavily into technology over the past 12-18 months to "level the playing field" against larger competitors.
- **Federal government (a major indirect "customer" via funding) is actively constrained** by the shutdown, with health care (Medicare-dependent cash flows up to 30% for some systems) and education flagged as at risk of second-order funding effects.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Public sector is bifurcated.** Government net sales rose 8% as state and local delivered double-digit growth that "more than offset" an anticipated federal decline; federal itself is described as navigating a "post-DOGE landscape" with "strategic value" being rebuilt for future growth.
- **Education contracted outright** — total net sales down 9% — as K-12 reverts from pandemic-era federal subsidy funding back to typical state/local funding sources, while higher ed grew on institutions competing for enrollment.
- **Health care has been "on fire" for seven-to-eight quarters** on clinical continuity and security spend, gross profit outgrowing its 7% top-line growth, though management is explicitly "watching for signs of customer hesitancy" tied to Medicare funding shifts.
- **U.K. and Canada ("Other") were standout performers**, up 9% net sales with profitability growing faster than sales in both markets — called out as "executing extremely well under unsettled conditions."
- **Client devices remain a steady tailwind (up 7% consolidated)**, driven by Windows 10 end-of-life replacement and a "heightened focus on GenAI productivity initiatives," partially offset by declines in K-12 and Federal device spend.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital return is running well ahead of plan**: $747 million returned to shareholders through Q3, or 112% of adjusted free cash flow, against a stated 2025 target of just 50-75% — management now expects to "meaningfully surpass" that target for the year via dividends and opportunistic buybacks.
- **M&A is explicitly aimed at Services scale**, not general diversification — Leahy tied future deal activity directly to "high growth, high relevance offerings" in professional/managed services and industry-specific expertise.
- **The actual constraint is customer commitment, not CDW's own capacity** — coworker count was down both year-over-year and quarter-over-quarter (to ~14,900), reflecting a deliberate push for efficiency rather than a hiring bottleneck.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: device refresh cycle has a "couple more quarters" left**, per Leahy's inning metaphor (sixth, "rounding to the seventh-inning stretch"). **Action:** no specific new investment disclosed beyond continued sales focus — this is treated as tailwind to ride, not a lever to pull.
- **Belief: shutdown losses are timing, not permanent.** **Action:** Q4 guidance nonetheless models the shutdown persisting the entire quarter — the stated belief and the modeled action openly diverge, with management framing the gap as prudence rather than pessimism.
- **Belief: Services will be an outsized growth contributor going forward.** **Action:** new services leadership installed, explicit "very, very focused on refining exactly where we play," and M&A capacity earmarked for the category — this is the one place where stated belief and visible investment are fully aligned.
- **Belief: margins should mix upward as client devices become a smaller share and services grow.** **Action:** Miralles confirmed the direction but declined to quantify it, deferring detail to the year-end 2026 outlook call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 guidance of any kind** — repeatedly deferred to the year-end call, standard practice but notable given how many forward questions (federal recovery pace, margin trajectory, device cycle length) touched on it.
- **Declined to call small-business strength a leading indicator** for the rest of the business, despite it being the best-performing segment on the call.
- **Would not attribute enterprise solutions softness cleanly to AI decision-making versus general macro caution** — Miralles called AI "probably a variable" but led with macro/geopolitical uncertainty instead.
- **No specific quantification of margin upside from mix shift** — "modestly, you could see some tick up," with detail reserved for 2026.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Federal shutdown mechanics dominated the Q&A**: how much is baked into Q4 guidance, whether lost sales come back, and how health care funding risk compares to shutdown risk — CDW answered with specifics on guidance conservatism but stayed vague on the pace of any federal "catch-up."
- **Expense growth and operating leverage** drew repeated follow-ups (SG&A acceleration, stock-based comp as % of gross profit) — Miralles's consistent answer was that 2024's comp-accrual reversals are distorting the comparison and 2025 is a "year of transition" back to normalized parity between expense and gross-profit growth.
- **Services scale and potential M&A** came up directly, testing whether CDW would pursue larger acquisitions to build out the practice — Leahy kept the answer to "close eye on M&A opportunities" without committing to deal size or timing.
- The compressed worry: **is CDW's growth increasingly dependent on segments (small business, Services, international) that offset — rather than reflect a real recovery in — its historically larger public-sector and enterprise hardware businesses?**
