# MMM — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-21)

**The key idea:** 3M's two-year turnaround story is compounding faster than management itself expected — commercial execution and a reengineered R&D pipeline are now producing above-macro growth simultaneously with margin expansion, something the company explicitly could not do a few years ago. The new subplot is an optical-connector bet on hyperscaler data centers, positioned as a genuine technology moat rather than a cyclical industrial story.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **Expanded Beam Optical (EBO) is 3M's live pivot into AI infrastructure.** A patented fiber-optic connector technology (**~100 patents granted, 50 pending**) just qualified with Microsoft as the first hyperscaler deploying it in Azure data centers, cutting circuit install time by roughly 85%. Current revenue is tiny ($40-50M) against a **TAM management expects to roughly double from $1B this year to $2B by 2028**, with 3M explicitly planning to be one of many suppliers in a 44-member multi-supplier ecosystem rather than sole-source.
- **Holding company → operating company transition, phase two.** After two years of "commercial excellence" and innovation-engine rebuilding, the next phase is "transformation": consolidating finance, HR, and customer service into a single global shared-services model run with an external partner using automation and AI, plus factory/distribution network rationalization.
- **Innovation output is nearly tripling versus three years ago**, with a stated goal of **1,000+ new product launches by 2027** and development cycle time down ~20%. The mix is shifting from incremental "Class 3" launches (~75% today) toward "Class 4/5" new-market products, targeted at 40%+ within industrial segments — CEO Bill Brown: "the machine churning faster on innovation."
- **Portfolio reshaping via bolt-on M&A**, not big acquisitions: the Madison Fire & Rescue deal folded into a majority-owned Scott SCBA joint venture, netting $700M cash while keeping an $800M, high-single-digit-growth, above-average-margin business inside the tent.
- **China is being run as a quasi-standalone unit** (5,000+ people, six factories, localized NPI) rather than through global business groups — double-digit growth for two straight quarters, split roughly 50/50 domestic vs. export, which Brown called out as a deliberate structural choice, not a macro tailwind.

## What's changing

- **Growth-above-macro is re-rating upward mid-year**: full-year outgrowth guidance moved from an original $1B (2025-2027) framing to **~$450M this year, up from ~$340-350M guided just last quarter** — management attributes this almost entirely to internal execution, not improving end markets ("It's not really macro tailwind... mostly just internal performance").
- **Customer attrition/churn is visibly improving** — down roughly 200 basis points, concentrated in the Safety & Industrial segment — after two years of deliberate sales-force and account-management investment.
- **Gross margin is finally moving off its post-spin floor**, now "close to mid-40s" versus the low-40s baseline the turnaround started from, with management reiterating a path back to high-40s as transformation-stage cost work (SG&A, IT, procurement) layers on top of factory productivity.
- **Pricing is stepping up to fully offset a worsening input-cost picture**: oil-linked inflation guidance was raised again mid-year, from $125M to **$150-175M**, which management says will be offset dollar-for-dollar by price actions already taken, holding margin-rate drag to a controlled 20bps.
- **Capital return is running ahead of plan** — $3B of buybacks in the first half against an original $2.5B full-year plan, average price ~$153, with management committing only to staying "opportunistic" rather than raising the target.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **Consumer channel destocking hit late in the quarter and wasn't fully explained.** Point-of-sale growth was healthy (~2.5%, positive in 18 of 26 weeks vs. 7 all last year), but "several key retailers" cut inventory weeks of supply in late June — management frames this as normalizing but named no retailer and gave only a one-week magnitude.
- **Consumer electronics and auto remain structurally weak, and getting worse into the back half.** Memory chip shortages/pricing are cited as the direct cause of a **device production forecast swinging to down high-teens** in H2 versus a milder first half; auto build rates stay negative and auto aftermarket repair claims are expected down — none of this is new, but the deterioration is.
- **Madison Fire & Rescue was conspicuously excluded from raised guidance despite already having closed** — CFO Anurag Maheshwari's stated reason ("apples-to-apples" comparability) is plausible but also conveniently keeps a completed, cash-generative deal out of the numbers investors are being asked to extrapolate from.
- **Utilization is still only ~63-64% company-wide**, meaning the "record production" anecdote at the New Ulm electrical-connector plant is a story about pockets of localized bottleneck, not broad-based capacity tightness — the transformation/footprint-rationalization thesis still depends on unlocking that slack, which hasn't happened yet.
- **No mention of PFAS liability, litigation reserves, or the earlier-referenced "exit of certain PFAS manufacturing assets"** beyond a single throwaway line about its GAAP EPS impact — a topic that has dominated 3M's narrative for years got zero analyst follow-up and no management elaboration.

## Street anxiety (compressed)

Whether 2x-macro growth is a sustainable steady-state algorithm or a catch-up sugar high from a low 2023 base · how much of the new-product acceleration is genuinely incremental TAM versus cannibalizing existing SKUs (management concedes ~75% of launches are still low-differentiation "Class 3") · whether EBO can scale past a single anchor hyperscaler and small current revenue base into the multi-billion-dollar TAM being pitched · whether gross margin can actually reach high-40s given tariff/oil cost persistence, or whether 500bps of margin gain so far is mostly SG&A cost-out that has a ceiling · the underlying worry is that 3M's re-rating story rests on execution metrics (attrition, cycle time, cross-sell) that are hard for outsiders to verify and easy for management to describe favorably quarter after quarter.
