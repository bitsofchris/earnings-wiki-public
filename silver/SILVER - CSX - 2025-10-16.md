# CSX — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-16)

**The key idea:** This call is less an earnings report than a coronation — new CEO Steve Angel's first appearance, arriving mid-industry-consolidation-wave (Union Pacific's pending Norfolk Southern merger) with an operationally strong quarter to lean on. The tension: every analyst wants to know if CSX is a merger target/acquirer-in-waiting or a standalone operator, and Angel answers every version of that question the same careful way — "run the business well, keep options open, decide later."

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Best operating quarter in years, but it's covering for real revenue softness.** Fastest train velocity since 2021, lowest dwell since 2023, and completion of two multi-year infrastructure projects (Howard Street Tunnel, Blue Ridge) — all while revenue fell 1% and coal revenue dropped 11%. Operational excellence is the story management wants told; underlying freight demand is not.
2. **A $164M non-cash impairment on Quality Carriers quietly ate the quarter's headline EPS.** GAAP EPS was $0.37 including the goodwill write-down tied to the trucking subsidiary; CFO Sean Pelkey pivoted immediately to adjusted figures. Quality Carriers is explicitly being run for "efficiency" now rather than growth, an admission the trucking bet hasn't paid off in this cycle.
3. **The infrastructure overhang is gone, and that's the real 2026 story.** With Howard Street and Blue Ridge complete, CSX has "full network access" for the first time in years and about $100M of non-repeating disruption costs falling out of the base heading into 2026 — a clean cost tailwind independent of any demand recovery.
4. **New CEO deliberately will not commit to a number.** Angel repeatedly says he's "still working on it" on margin targets, framing his first quarter as diagnosis, not action — a stark contrast to the operational specificity coming from COO Mike Cory and CCO Kevin Boone.
5. **Cross-railroad cooperation is being sold as a genuine structural shift, not opportunism.** Boone and Angel both point to intermodal truck-to-rail conversion work with BNSF/CN as evidence the industry is "in a better position to grow" — convenient timing given the antitrust spotlight on UP-NSC.

## Economy & consumer
- **"Business conditions are mixed"** — Boone's framing captures trade-policy uncertainty, weak global commodity prices, unsupportive interest rates, and "a persistently soft trucking market," the longest such down-cycle "than all of us have seen in a long, long time."
- Forest products and chemicals are tariff casualties: customers "rationalized production," volume down 7%, with pricing gains only partially offsetting the decline.
- No direct consumer read — CSX's exposure is industrial/agricultural, though Boone flags "general consumer demand" as a watch item for international intermodal and notes recent volume softening looks like normal seasonality, not a demand break.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Minerals (aggregates/cement, +8%/+12% volume/revenue), fertilizer (+7%, phosphate producer restart), metals and equipment (+5%, new capacity plus scrap/EAF share gains), intermodal (+4% revenue, +5% volume despite soft trucking pricing).
- **Not working:** Coal revenue down 11% (export tonnage down 11% on mine fires, though a key export mine just reopened); steel/industrial coal tonnage down 15%; forest products/chemicals down 7% on tariffs and plant closures; ag and food down 7% as a strong Southeastern crop displaced rail-hauled feed grain.
- **Cost discipline is real, not just optics:** headcount down while volume grew, PS&O efficiency gains "broad based," fuel productivity and car-cycle times improved — Pelkey frames this as "structural cost improvement," though $60M of the quarter's expense increase was one-time severance/disruption cost.

## Investing & scarcity
- Full-year core CapEx guidance held at $2.5B (ex-Blue Ridge); Blue Ridge itself will run "in excess of $500 million before insurance recoveries" — a cost overrun CSX is explicitly not folding into core capital metrics.
- **The scarcity that bound them all year was physical network capacity**, not capital or people: the Howard Street/Blue Ridge outages forced CSX to reroute volume through alternate corridors that "could have handled about 40%" of what the closed routes normally carried at just 20% capacity utilization elsewhere (Cory). That constraint is now resolved.
- 2026 CapEx has "nothing anywhere close to that scale" of Blue Ridge/Howard Street on the horizon — capital shifts back toward safety/reliability spend and shareholder returns (Pelkey).
- Double-stack clearance through Baltimore — the payoff of the Howard Street project — doesn't monetize until "bid season" in 2026 with revenue capture more like Q2 2026, a full commercial cycle behind the capital spend.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Angel believes CSX can reach "best in class" margins via a combination of price/yield, volume growth, and continuous improvement — but the gap between belief and action is explicit: **he has "not put a number on it yet."** Talk of a vision precedes any committed target.
- **Belief:** Boone expects domestic intermodal strength to continue and coal to benefit from data-center power demand — **action:** CSX is actively marketing the new Baltimore double-stack service into bid season and reinvesting in Blue Ridge to "get a return on that reinvestment."
- **Gap:** On industry consolidation, management repeatedly frames CSX as "positioning from a position of strength" for a "downstream" strategic opportunity, yet takes no concrete action toward a deal — Angel: "it is really too early to address" and there's "a lot that has not been determined yet." The talk of readiness is not backed by any disclosed action beyond running the existing business well.
- **Gap:** On Quality Carriers, the belief that trucking will "recover" and the unit will "fully capture the upside" is stated, but the committed action is cost-cutting ("aggressively identify additional efficiency opportunities"), not investment — a hedge against a downturn they don't control, not a bet on recovery.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Angel declines any margin-improvement number: **"I am still working on it. I have not got there yet."**
- On whether CSX would pursue a merger of its own: **"It is kinda hard to really say standing here today how all this is gonna shake out because there is a lot of it that has not been determined yet."**
- Asked directly whether other railroads' customers have reached out with competitive positioning color, Angel gives a flat, unelaborated no: **"I would say, you know, since I have been here, we have not had that kind of discussion."**
- Boone declines to forecast the industrial-market cycle: **"I am not here to call the cycle."**
- 2026 cash-priority specifics (debt paydown targets vs. buybacks) were deferred entirely — Pelkey: "we are working through our plans for next year as we speak."

## The street — what analysts asked
- The dominant theme, asked in some form by at least six different analysts, was **Steve Angel's read on industry consolidation** — whether CSX is a target, an acquirer, or a bystander to the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern deal, and whether his M&A history at Praxair/Linde signals intent. Angel deflected every version toward "run the standalone business well first."
- A second cluster pushed for **hard 2026 cost and cash-flow numbers** now that Blue Ridge/Howard Street spending ends — Pelkey gave sequential expense bridges but declined firm 2026 guidance.
- A smaller thread asked about **BNSF/CN partnership mechanics and contract duration** amid consolidation uncertainty, which Boone answered constructively but without new commitments.
- The compressed worry: **is this operational turnaround a foundation CSX is building for itself, or just a stronger negotiating position for whatever comes next in industry consolidation?**
