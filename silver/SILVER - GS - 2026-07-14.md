# GS — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-14)

**The key idea:** Goldman posted record results across nearly every line and is explicit that this is an AI-capex-driven super-cycle — financing demand for infrastructure, energy, and data centers is now "outstripping" what the firm is willing to supply, not what it's able to supply. Management's real message is durability: they're framing today's numbers as a diversified, multi-year base rather than a cyclical peak, while quietly acknowledging the whole thing could recalibrate hard.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **The "One GS flywheel" is the explicit operating thesis**: advisory relationships are treated as the entry point that cascades into financing, capital markets, and wealth management — CEO David Solomon cited **nearly 900 referrals from investment banking to wealth management since the start of 2025** and said the firm is "finding ourselves in these discussions earlier and alone and without other banks."
- **Asia equities financing was a deliberate, multi-year bet that just paid off**: CFO Denis Coleman described identifying a "competitive shortcoming" in Asia after regulatory capital relief, then staffing and funding it ahead of demand — equity financing revenue and average prime balances **hit records**, with **equity underwriting revenue up 130% year-over-year**.
- **Capital Solutions Group is becoming a distribution engine, not just a balance-sheet function**: Goldman now originates fixed-income and event-driven financing and routes it three ways — onto its own balance sheet, into Asset & Wealth Management for client co-investment, or distributed to institutional buyers — explicitly because client demand for financing **exceeds what Goldman is willing to hold**.
- **OCIO/solutions platform scaling via large pension mandates**: Goldman was named manager for **Verizon's and Lockheed Martin's retirement plans, a combined $70 billion** in assets under supervision, positioned as a template for future corporate pension wins.
- **Alternatives fundraising target was raised mid-year**: full-year gross fundraising guidance moved to **"exceed $125 billion,"** up from a stated $75–100B annual target, after **$59 billion raised in Q2 alone** (record) including **$31 billion in private credit**.
- **AI inside the firm is framed as leverage, not headcount replacement**: Coleman linked declining sequential headcount to AI-driven productivity — engineers hired specifically to build internal tools atop the firm's "enormous amount of highly differentiated data," explicitly not a structural workforce cut.

## What's changing
- **Investment banking backlog hit a 5-year high — even after record Q2 revenue was recognized out of it**, with a record advisory backlog; management called this the strongest read on forward momentum.
- **Financing has become nearly as important as trading itself**: combined FICC + equities financing revenue was **$4.5 billion, up 62% year-over-year, now 37% of total FICC/equities revenue** — a structural mix shift toward balance-sheet-driven income.
- **Wallet share gains, not just market beta, are doing real work**: Solomon quantified the multi-year push — Goldman moved from being top-3 with **44 of the top 100 accounts six years ago to nearly 80 of the top 100 today** (and top-3 with 127 of the top 150) — offered as the underlying explanation for outsized trading results versus peers.
- **SLR is now the binding constraint, not CET1**: supplementary leverage ratio fell **40 basis points to 4.3%**, flagged as the firm's lowest among peers, a direct consequence of leaning into low-RWA, leverage-intensive prime and financing growth.
- **Sponsor/PE deal activity remains the missing leg of the recovery** — still "subdued versus historical averages" — which management repeatedly frames as unrealized upside rather than a demand problem.
- **Incentive fees are set to jump sharply in H2**, tied to specific already-known transactions/events, not just AUM appreciation — a rare instance of forward revenue guidance on a call otherwise light on numeric guidance.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Sponsor/PE volumes still haven't recovered**, and management can't say when — "it's going to come at some point" is the extent of the visibility offered, after multiple quarters of the same line.
- **Leveraged finance strength is partly refinancing/recapitalization activity, not new deal formation** — Solomon conceded some of the DCM strength is sponsors "recapping" existing holdings to extract capital, not fresh LBO issuance.
- **David Solomon declined to quantify the "multiplier effect" of advisory-led cross-sell** when asked directly for a number, retreating to qualitative language ("a meaningful part," "a very, very virtuous flywheel") — a case of answering a different question than the one asked about a metric the firm otherwise touts constantly.
- **No mention of credit quality deterioration despite loan book growth**: loans grew **3% sequentially to $261 billion** and provisions ticked up (**$102 million**, tied to wholesale loan impairments) with essentially no discussion of underlying credit stress — a conspicuous absence given how much balance sheet is being deployed into deal financing.
- **Solomon explicitly hedged the AI-cycle durability question**, comparing today's buildout to dot-com and 2021-pandemic cycles and stating plainly that "there will be a recalibration, a reset, a drawdown" — notable candor that the current run is not assumed to be linear, even as the firm leans further into financing it.
- **No detail offered on token-cost economics or enterprise AI monetization** — Solomon referenced "a lot of talk about token spend and the cost of the technology" as an open uncertainty the market hasn't resolved, without offering Goldman's own view — a telling non-answer for a bank this deep in financing the buildout.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Analysts pressed repeatedly on whether the quarter's strength is durable or a one-time confluence: equities wallet-share sustainability and Asia concentration risk (are a handful of big clients driving the gain?) · SLR now the lowest among peers, raising the question of how much more balance-sheet-heavy financing growth is even possible · AI capex "bubble" framing versus Solomon's own "more greed than fear" comment made earlier in the quarter · headcount down 2% sequentially despite record revenue, probed as either efficiency discipline or early AI displacement · alternatives fundraising-to-fee-recognition lag, since a large chunk of raised capital isn't yet fee-paying · whether prime/financing pricing power is emerging now that capacity is scarce. The underlying worry: is Goldman's record quarter a genuine structural share/franchise gain, or a leveraged bet on an AI investment cycle that management itself admits will "recalibrate" at some unknown point?
