# PAYX — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-03-25)

**The key idea:** A year after closing the disruptive Paycor acquisition, Paychex says the hard integration work is done — expense synergies beat plan, bookings and broker referrals are back to pre-deal levels — and the story now shifts to replaying its PEO playbook (buy distribution, then out-grow the category for years) in the enterprise HR-outsourcing market, with AI cast as the scale advantage that lets a "do it for you" HR department compete against tech-only rivals.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Organic growth is genuinely accelerating, but off an easy compare.** CFO Schrader was unusually candid that the jump from ~4% (1H) to ~6% (2H) organic growth is "a couple drivers... one, to be fair, is the easier compare on the PEO business" from last year's Florida MPP enrollment headwind, alongside real operating strength — a rare moment of self-skepticism about the company's own acceleration narrative.
2. **The PEO business is the proof-of-concept for the Paycor bet.** Gibson explicitly draws the parallel: the 2018 Oasis acquisition took PEO to double-digit worksite-employee CAGR outpacing all competitors, and "I think we are well positioned to duplicate the story that we did in PEO in the enterprise space" with Paycor — this is the thesis investors are underwriting.
3. **AI is framed as a moat extender, not a disruptor of Paychex's advisory model.** Gibson's pushback on the "AI-proof" advisory question is pointed: compliance work like tracking "the changes in Akron, Ohio" requires a human who "has to go onto Akron's website... watch what is going on in Ohio courts" — AI augments that pipeline but the regulatory tracking itself is judged not automatable, at least by their own admission "in the short term."
4. **Cross-sell, not cost cuts, is now the whole Paycor value story.** Schrader stated plainly the expense synergies "probably justified the purchase price, but really the value creation opportunity longer term with this deal is the cross-sell" — Paycor's larger average client size gives access to ASO/PEO/retirement products that legacy Paychex reps couldn't previously sell upmarket.
5. **Land size per client hasn't recovered.** A rare unprompted admission: "we probably had higher expectations going into the year about the number of modules that we would be able to add, and I would say that did not change much in Q3 selling season" — smaller initial deal sizes are a persistent, not one-time, phenomenon.

## Economy & consumer
- **Labor market: stable but tight at the bottom, hesitant at the top.** Gibson: "a low-fire and a low-hire type of environment right now," with small businesses (<50 employees) facing "continued inability to find qualified people" while larger employers show "a degree of potential hesitancy to add in this uncertain environment."
- **No recession signal in their own data.** "We have not seen a significant change in this fiscal year in terms of the small business index that we report" and "nothing that would indicate that we would change what we are thinking in terms of pays."
- **Health-cost inflation is reshaping benefit choices.** PEO medical enrollment ran high-single to near-double digits, which Gibson partly credits to AI-assisted plan selection nudging employees toward "the middle plan choice versus the lower plan choice" — a subtle margin tailwind dressed as a "better outcome."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Revenue +20%, adjusted operating income +22%,** with Paycor contributing ~19 points of Management Solutions' 23% growth; adjusted operating margin expanded ~80bps to 47.7% even while funding AI investment.
- **PEO is the standout segment:** 9% revenue growth (up from 6% last quarter), high-single-digit worksite-employee growth, "record retention rates," and larger-than-expected broker-referred deals — Schrader confirmed net-new bookings, not installed-base headcount growth, are "the entire driver."
- **The insurance/agency side remains a drag,** though improving: "the agencies were certainly still a drag in the quarter to the segment. But we saw sequential improvement," attributed to soft workers' comp and health-care market conditions requiring new strategies.
- **Management Solutions organic growth is stuck at 4%** for two consecutive quarters — flat, not accelerating, ahead of the Paycor anniversary in Q4.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Paycor expense synergies raised to ~$100M** (from an initial $80–90M range) and now largely realized — "we are kind of moving into BAU" — with further procurement-related upside still being chased.
- **Capital return stepped up sharply:** new $1B buyback authorization, $463M repurchased this quarter and $1.5B+ YTD, alongside repaying the first $400M Oasis debt tranche; CFO left the door open to using leverage for buybacks given the stock's "disconnect between the underlying fundamentals of the business and the valuation."
- **The binding constraint is sales/distribution capacity, not capital or compute** — Paychex is actively adding enterprise headcount to chase demand it says already exists, and Gibson calls out "plenty of opportunities for investment" they don't want to defer into next fiscal year.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** organic growth keeps accelerating sequentially into Q4 and FY27, aligning with Street consensus, on the back of Paycor cross-sell and easier comps. **Action:** headcount adds in enterprise sales, AI agent rollout across service/sales, Perks expansion into Paycor's base, and continued M&A-integration discipline — the actions largely match the stated belief, an unusually well-aligned talk/action pairing for this call.
- **Gap worth flagging:** management touts AI's "significant productivity improvements" and a "flywheel effect" from proprietary data, yet Gibson himself says quantifying AI's real revenue/margin impact for FY27 is "a big debate right now" internally — the confident public framing outruns their own internal certainty.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No FY2027 guidance:** "We are in the early stages... of our operating plan and are going to finalize that over the next six to eight weeks," preferring their historical practice of guiding at Q4 rather than mid-year color as they did coming out of COVID.
- **No comment on FY2027 pricing:** Gibson declined directly — "I am not going to make any comments on how we are going to set pricing going into next year at this time. So I do not want to give anybody a heads up" — a explicit competitive-information hedge.
- **EPS guidance left unchanged despite raised interest-income outlook,** which the CFO attributed to "a combination of maybe a little conservatism and where we may potentially want to take advantage and make some investments as we end the year" — optionality preserved rather than flowed through.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Decomposing the beat:** multiple analysts pushed on how much of the Q3 acceleration and Q3-vs-Q4 organic growth "step up" was PEO/insurance timing (carrier bonuses, SUI revenue timing) versus durable operating improvement — management conceded some of it was timing.
- **Paycor bookings-to-revenue lag:** analysts probed how long larger Paycor clients take to convert from booking to revenue; Gibson admitted "a couple-quarter lag... much longer than ours where you could sell them and implement them in the same day," though cross-sell/attach converts faster.
- **Margin durability and AI attribution:** repeated questions on how much margin expansion is AI-driven versus labor arbitrage or scale, with management unable to cleanly separate the drivers, admitting the quantification question is still being worked out internally.
- **The compressed worry:** is Paychex's accelerating growth real underlying demand strength, or comp-driven optics dressed up ahead of a Q4 guide that's already a point below Street?
