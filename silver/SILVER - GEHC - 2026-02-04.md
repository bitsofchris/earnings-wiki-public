# GEHC — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-04)

**The key idea:** GEHC is threading a story of record backlog and a loaded new-product pipeline (nine RSNA launches, each pitched as $100M+) against a 2026 guide that, stripped of spin, is flat with 2025's growth rate — a gap the CFO had to defend live on the call. The near-term swing factors are entirely execution: whether Flurcato's CMO-constrained dose supply scales fast enough, whether China stabilizes or keeps sliding, and whether regulatory approvals land in time to convert backlog into 2026 (rather than 2027) revenue.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Tariffs were the whole story in 2025, and management is claiming victory over them.** ~$245M tariff hit to EBIT and 43¢ to EPS for the year was largely offset via manufacturing relocation (PET-CT and surgery lines moved to the US) and Heartbeat-driven cost productivity; ex-tariff EBIT margin would have been up 20bps and EPS growth 12% instead of 2.2%.
2. **Flurcato (Vorcado) is demand-gated by supply, not adoption — a deliberate 'go slow to go fast.'** CMOs are now hitting ~95% on-time-delivery and 220 weekly doses as of Jan 23, and management is only now "opening up the throttle" on new customer onboarding — a genuinely constrained rollout rather than a soft-launch euphemism.
3. **2026 guide quietly walks back prior guidance.** CFO Jay Saccaro had told investors on the last call to expect >3% growth in 2026; the actual guide (3-4%, 3.5% midpoint) matches 2025's realized rate exactly, and an analyst called this out directly — the response leaned on China caution rather than disputing the optics.
4. **China is guided down for a second consecutive year**, with Q4 down double digits and management explicitly "budgeting China down" in the 3-4% total guide despite citing improving VBP tender win rates as a leading indicator of eventual recovery — the caution is deliberate, not a data-driven call.
5. **Recurring revenue mix-shift is the real strategic thread.** IntelliRed (SaaS/cloud imaging, low-double-digit growth, >30% EBITDA margin), rising service capture rates tied to more sophisticated new products, and enterprise deals with embedded multiyear service components are all pointed at the same goal: less lumpy capital-cycle dependence.

- **EMEA orders recovering over multiple quarters**, per CFO Saccaro — a genuine tailwind rather than a hedge.
- **China guided down again**, the second straight year of decline, despite management citing "improved commercial execution" and better VBP tender win rates as early proof points not yet reflected in guidance.
- No consumer-facing signal — GEHC sells capital equipment/consumables to health systems; the closest proxy is procedure-volume growth, described as "pretty healthy."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Pharmaceutical diagnostics is the standout segment**, +12.7% organic in Q4 on contrast media growth, pricing, and radiopharmaceutical NPI adoption — though segment margin fell 330bps year-over-year on NPI investment and the Nehan Metaphysics acquisition.
- **Imaging (+5.3%) and Advanced Visualization Solutions (+4.2%) both grew mid-single-digits**, aided by new products like Vivid Pioneer in cardiovascular ultrasound.
- **Patient Care Solutions remains the problem child**: organic revenue down 1.1% year-over-year on a life-support decline, even after recovering sequentially from Q3's shipment hold; EBIT margin down 380bps year-over-year on mix and tariffs.
- **Tariffs cost ~$100M of Q4 EBIT** (200bps of margin) and 17¢ of Q4 EPS — the dominant driver of reported margin compression across nearly every segment.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$1.7B+ innovation spend in 2025**, prioritized toward "programs that strengthen our competitiveness" — a targeted rather than broad-based R&D ramp.
- **IntelliRed acquisition (~$270M revenue, low-double-digit growth, >30% EBITDA margin) to close 1H26**, adding cloud/AI SaaS tools across radiology and cardiology and extending into the outpatient network — framed as accretive to both top and bottom line.
- **The binding constraint is manufacturing/logistics reliability for Flurcato**, not clinical demand: CMO dose consistency (95% on-time-delivery threshold) is gating how fast new hospital customers can be onboarded, and management is deliberately capping ramp speed to protect the clinical experience — "if that doesn't show up that morning, that's a missed patient."
- **Tariffs functioned as a forced scarcity/reallocation event**: manufacturing lines physically relocated (PET-CT from Middle East, surgery from Asia, both to the US) purely in response to tariff exposure, not demand or cost efficiency in the ordinary sense.
