# NOW — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** ServiceNow posted a broad beat-and-raise quarter and is leaning entirely into an "AI control tower / agentic workflow" narrative — positioning itself as the governance and orchestration layer sitting above hyperscalers and foundation models rather than competing with them. The tension: nearly every AI metric cited (55x consumption growth, $0.5B ACV) is off a small base and is still almost entirely subscription revenue, not usage — management is explicit that real consumption monetization hasn't started yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A beat-and-raise quarter with no real cracks.** Subscription revenue, cRPO, and operating margin all beat guidance by wide margins (cRPO by 250bps, op margin by 300bps), and full-year subscription, margin, and free-cash-flow guidance were all raised again — the fourth consecutive raise cycle referenced on the call.
2. **AI revenue is still almost entirely subscription, not usage.** CFO Gina Mastantuono was direct: of the ~$0.5B Now Assist ACV expected this year, "consumption won't materially start impacting for a little while... that $500 million at this point is just the subscription piece" — the 55x consumption growth touted by Bill McDermott is a leading indicator of future monetization, not current revenue.
3. **Federal timing risk is real and explicitly reflected in guidance.** The Q4 subscription growth guide (17.5-18% cc) sits below the cRPO trend (19% cc) specifically because of "the ongoing government [shutdown that] may impact deal timing" in U.S. federal — management pulled forward renewal cohort activity from Q4 into Q3 to hedge this.
4. **MoveWorks' rationale looks softer under scrutiny.** The CFO undercut the acquisition's urgency by noting the blowout Now Assist results happened "all on our own without MoveWorks" — the deal (expected to close near end of Q4) is framed as talent/roadmap acceleration, not a gap-filler.
5. **A 5-for-1 stock split was announced alongside earnings**, framed as broadening retail accessibility and employee equity flexibility — a signal of management's confidence in sustained high absolute share price, not an operational datapoint.

## Economy & consumer
- **No direct consumer read** — ServiceNow is enterprise/B2B; the closest proxy is enterprise IT and public-sector budget behavior.
- **Enterprise demand described as broad-based and accelerating**, with transportation/logistics net-new ACV up over 90% y/y and retail/hospitality and education both over 50%, alongside "healthy" energy/utilities demand.
- **Federal demand strong but supply-constrained by shutdown mechanics**, not budget appetite: net-new ACV in U.S. federal grew over 30% y/y even as the government was partially shut down during the quarter, per CFO commentary.
- **Anecdotal evidence of AI-project fatigue among enterprise buyers**: McDermott relayed that CEOs are killing sprawling proof-of-concept programs ("One had 900 proof of concepts going on in the company... and said it was uncontrollable and they killed them all") — a signal that enterprise AI spending is consolidating toward fewer, platform-level vendors rather than expanding experimentation.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Core workflow business remains "rock-solid"**: 103 deals over $1M net-new ACV, security/risk crossed $1B ACV (ServiceNow's fifth billion-dollar product line), renewal rate held at 97% (98% ex. one large federal agency closure).
- **Now Assist significantly outperformed internal plan**: 12 deals over $1M (one over $10M), AI Control Tower deal volume "more than quadrupled quarter-over-quarter," and Now Assist price uplift over 30%.
- **What's not working / softer**: Q4 subscription revenue guide undershoots cRPO growth trend due to federal-timing prudence and "on-prem" mix headwind (CFO flagged but didn't quantify); CapEx is declining as workloads shift to hyperscalers, which reads as good capital discipline but also means less owned infrastructure leverage over time.

## Investing & scarcity
- **MoveWorks acquisition** expected to close near end of Q4 — framed as an AI talent and roadmap accelerant rather than filling a current product gap.
- **Forward-deployed engineering investment**: Amit Zavery described standing up "AI black belt" forward-deployed engineers to handle the customer-specific integration work that generic AI deployments can't solve alone — this is the actual bottleneck they're spending against, not compute or model access.
- **Capital allocation**: buybacks up ~70% q/q (644K shares) explicitly to offset dilution, not to signal undervaluation; $2B of authorization remained. The 5-for-1 split is a distribution/accessibility move, not a growth investment.
- **Scarcity is organizational/trust-based, not infrastructural**: the repeated framing (McDermott, Zavery) is that enterprises lack integration expertise and cross-system trust, not that they lack GPUs or models — ServiceNow is positioning its prepackaged "100-plus" agentic workflows as the solution to that specific constraint.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: management is confident Now Assist consumption is an imminent "hockey stick" — agentic use cases require far more token volume per interaction (Zavery: "10x, 5, 12x depending on each of the calls") than prior summarization-style usage, and this will convert into consumption revenue "in future quarters."
- **Action gap**: despite this stated confidence, the CFO explicitly declined to raise the $1B 2026 Now Assist ACV target or give any 2026 margin specifics, even while tracking ahead of plan — a clear instance of talk outrunning committed numbers.
- **Belief**: AI Control Tower will be a major standalone growth vector as enterprises need governance across heterogeneous AI stacks.
- **Action**: this is backed by real product investment — integrated visibility across "different pieces of technologies," 4x sequential growth in AI Control Tower business — making this one belief that does have committed resourcing behind it, unlike the consumption revenue talk.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **2026 margin guidance**: CFO repeatedly declined to confirm or update prior "+100bps operating, +50bps free cash flow" 2026 targets despite raising 2025 numbers twice this year — "I'm not going to update guidance yet... I'll share more specifics on 2026... next year."
- **The $1B 2026 Now Assist ACV target**: despite tracking ahead of plan and record consumption growth, CFO declined to raise the number: "I'm not going to up the guide at this point."
- **Government shutdown timing**: CFO explicitly disclaimed forecasting ability here — "I would like to say I know how to forecast when the government is going to reopen, I just don't" — and built prudence into Q4 guidance rather than committing to a specific recovery timeline.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Questions clustered heavily on AI monetization mechanics: what's driving the 55x consumption spike, how much of the $500M Now Assist ACV is subscription vs. usage, and whether pricing/packaging changes are working as an "unlock."
- A second cluster probed the Q4 guidance bridge — why subscription revenue guide sits below cRPO growth trend, pressing for how much is federal-shutdown-specific versus a broader signal.
- MoveWorks got one direct question, and management's answer (strong standalone results "without MoveWorks") was more revealing than reassuring about deal rationale.
- The compressed worry: **is the eye-popping AI usage growth a real, monetizable trend, or a low-base statistic being run hard while the actual dollars — and even next year's specific targets — stay conspicuously unconfirmed?**
