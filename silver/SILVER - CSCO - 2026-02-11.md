# CSCO — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-02-11)

**The key idea:** Cisco delivered a record, guidance-beating quarter riding two multiyear waves at once — an accelerating AI infrastructure build-out (hyperscaler orders up from $1.3B to $2.1B sequentially) and an early-innings campus refresh cycle — while a memory price shock is actively eating into gross margin. Management's framing is confident but the guide is conspicuously conservative relative to the CapEx headlines analysts kept citing, and several forward numbers (AI orders/revenue) explicitly exclude newly announced flagship products.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI infrastructure orders are accelerating hard but guidance still lags the narrative.** Hyperscaler AI orders hit $2.1B in Q2 alone — matching all of FY'25 — yet the raised FY'26 guide of "in excess of $5B" orders / "over $3B" revenue explicitly excludes G300, P200, and the new optics just announced, which analysts flagged as oddly conservative given the CapEx wave.
2. **Memory is the real story under the hood.** Product gross margin fell 130bps YoY "primarily driven by... higher memory costs," and Cisco is responding with price hikes, renegotiated partner/customer contracts, and a 73% YoY jump in advance purchase commitments — Chuck Robbins framed this explicitly as a game Cisco believes it can "manage... better than our peers."
3. **Campus refresh is real and reaccelerating, not just marketing.** Robbins: "we're in the top of the first inning" — enterprise switching, routing, wireless, and industrial IoT are all ramping faster than prior product transitions, backed by tens of billions in installed base nearing end-of-support, though management downplays pull-forward risk from the price increases.
4. **Security's headline numbers are misleading.** The -4% Security revenue is almost entirely a Splunk accounting artifact (cloud subscription mix shift vs. on-prem), masking that new/refreshed security products (AI Defense, Hypershield, Secure Access) grew new-customer adoption 100% sequentially and firewall units grew double digits for a third straight quarter — management expects the organic security portfolio near double-digit growth by Q4 exit.
5. **NVIDIA and hyperscaler engagement is inflecting from a small base.** Field engagements with NVIDIA grew 70% sequentially, and Cisco is positioning as "the network standard" alongside NVIDIA GPU deployments in enterprise AI factories — still early, but a relationship Cisco is visibly leaning into (Jensen Huang appeared at Cisco's AI Summit).

## Economy & consumer
- **Broad-based, not concentrated demand.** Product orders up 18% YoY, and even stripping out hyperscalers, up 10% — enterprise (+8%), public sector (+11%, double digits across all geos), and service provider/cloud (+65%, hyperscaler-driven) all grew, with all three geographies (Americas +23%, EMEA +11%, APJC +15%) accelerating from the prior quarter.
- **No consumer read** — Cisco's customer base is enterprise, government, telco, and hyperscaler, not retail end-consumers.
- **Customers are absorbing price increases without pushback so far.** Robbins recounted a lunch with "one of our absolute biggest customers" who "completely understood" the memory-driven pricing pressure, though he added the caveat "while they may not like it."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Networking is carrying the company.** Revenue up 21% YoY, with data center switching posting double-digit growth in 6 of the last 8 quarters and Wi-Fi 7 up 80% sequentially — the strongest segment by a wide margin.
- **Security's headline decline is a Splunk accounting story, not a demand problem.** New/refreshed products (excluding refreshed firewalls) added over 1,000 new customers in Q2 alone (100% growth quarter-over-quarter, ~4,000 cumulative since launch), but this is "offset by a decline in our prior generation portfolio," and Splunk's on-prem-to-cloud shift is an intentional but revenue-suppressing transition management says will continue through H2 FY'26.
- **Gross margin is the visible casualty.** Non-GAAP product gross margin fell to 66.4% (down 130bps), "primarily driven by negative impacts from mix and higher memory costs" — CFO called part of this "timing" that should improve, but declined to call an explicit trough.
- **Collaboration is a quiet, steady grower** — up 6%, led by double-digit device growth, unremarkable but not a drag.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Memory is the binding constraint today**, forcing Cisco to raise prices, renegotiate channel/customer contracts, and dramatically pre-buy — advance purchase commitments up $1.8B in 90 days, up ~73% YoY, "a big chunk... around memory."
- **Silicon capacity is also constrained on the high end**: Robbins said flatly on the 51.2/100T switch portfolio, "we're selling as much as we can build at this point... we just need to continue to build capacity" — demand, not orders, is the limiter.
- **Capital is flowing into AI infrastructure build-out and joint ventures**: the AMD/HUMAIN JV targets up to 1 gigawatt of AI infrastructure by 2030 (100MW Phase 1 in Saudi Arabia, starting this calendar year) — a sovereign/Middle East bet layered on top of the hyperscaler business.
- **Shareholder capital return continued uninterrupted** despite the investment ramp — $3B returned in Q2 ($6.6B YTD), dividend raised, $10.8B still authorized for buybacks — signaling confidence that the AI capex doesn't crowd out capital discipline.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: campus refresh is a multiyear, multibillion-dollar opportunity still in early innings.** Action behind it: new AI-native Catalyst-generation products, security woven into the fabric, and explicit reliance on tens of billions in aging installed base nearing end-of-support — a real, funded pipeline, not just talk.
- **Belief: neocloud/sovereign AI is a large incremental opportunity.** Action is notably hedged — Robbins said sovereign has "no real need nor expectation for meaningful impact in FY'26... purely upside," and neocloud ramp is expected to "begin in the second half, but really be FY'27." The pipeline number ($2.5B+) is real, but the JV and go-to-market motion are early-stage relative to the rhetoric.
- **Belief: scale-up networking is a future revenue stream.** Action: none disclosed — "we haven't made any announcements on scale up... stay tuned" — a clear talk/action gap, an acknowledged opportunity with zero committed product or revenue plan yet.
- **Belief: memory cost pressure is manageable and temporary.** Action: price increases, contract renegotiation, and heavy pre-buying are already executed, suggesting management is treating this as a sustained fight, not a one-quarter blip, despite calling gross margin pressure "timing."

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No scale-up product or revenue commitment** — explicitly declined to give a timeline: "we do plan to participate in it, and we expect in the future to have products and revenue... but haven't announced anything."
- **AI order guidance kept deliberately soft relative to visible CapEx momentum** — Robbins: "we're giving you that number based on the pipeline we see," emphasizing the customer base is "less than a handful" of major accounts and orders are "quite lumpy" — an explicit refusal to extrapolate from the accelerating trend.
- **No FY'27 guidance** — CFO repeatedly deferred gross margin and software-growth improvement questions to "as you get into FY'27... we're not here to talk about FY'27."
- **CPO (co-packaged optics) roadmap left vague** — "we absolutely believe it's going to happen. We don't believe it's actually imminent right now," preserving optionality rather than committing to a timeline while today's pluggable-optics products are pitched as offering customers "choice."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Gross margin trajectory dominated the Q&A** — at least four separate analysts pressed on whether memory-driven compression is a trough, structural, or masking an underlying EBIT improvement; management's answer leaned on "timing" and pricing/contract levers without committing to a specific inflection quarter.
- **AI order/revenue guidance conservatism relative to CapEx headlines** was a recurring skeptical thread — multiple analysts implied the $5B/$3B numbers look low given hyperscaler announcements, and Cisco's answer (pipeline-based, lumpy, excludes new products) reads as an intentional expectations-management move rather than a true ceiling.
- **Pull-forward buying ahead of price increases** was probed directly, invoking memories of prior supply-chain-driven order pull-ins; CFO's rebuttal (linearity, pipeline checks, software activations) was thorough but the concern wasn't fully dismissed given management's own "perhaps" hedge on campus.
- **Scale-up networking and CPO roadmap questions got the least substantive answers of the call** — both were met with "stay tuned" / "not imminent" framing.
- **Compressed worry:** is Cisco quietly capping its own AI upside guidance because supply (memory, capacity) — not demand — is the real ceiling, and is gross margin pressure from that same constraint here to stay through the AI build-out?
