# NKE — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2025-12-18)

**The key idea:** Nike's turnaround has bifurcated cleanly: North America is working (wholesale +24%, brand momentum, margin resilient despite tariffs), while Greater China is getting worse before it gets better (revenue -16%, EBIT -49%, unplanned inventory write-offs). Management leans hard on a baseball metaphor — "middle innings" — to buy time without giving investors a date, while tariffs layer a structural ~$1.5B cost the turnaround has to out-earn.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **North America is the actual proof point, not marketing spin.** Wholesale grew 24%, gross margin declined only 330bps despite a 520bps tariff hit, and Nike.com had its "best Black Friday ever" on the back of the Jordan AJ4 Black Cat launch — evidence that premium, full-price, story-led product is working when Nike executes it.

2. **China deteriorated in ways management didn't plan for.** CFO Matt Friend admits "inventory obsolescence in Greater China... was not contemplated ninety days ago" — a real miss versus their own guidance, not just a tough market. Hill's diagnosis is candid: Nike "become a lifestyle brand competing on price" in China after cutting store investment and field headcount.

3. **Tariffs are now a structural cost line, not a one-time hit.** $1.5B annualized incremental product cost equals ~320bps of gross margin; Nike has only clawed back to a net ~120bps impact so far, meaning tariff mitigation itself has become an ongoing management project layered on top of the marketplace-cleanup turnaround.

4. **Leadership reorganized around speed of decision-making.** All geography GMs now report directly to Hill (collapsing a layer), and a new COO role was created specifically to unify "create, plan, make, deliver, sell" — both moves aimed at closing the gap between what North America's playbook proved and how slowly it's propagating to EMEA/APLA/China.

5. **The classics wind-down is intentional and large.** Classic footwear franchises are being cut ~$4B from peak by fiscal year-end, deliberately subtracting ~$550M of quarterly revenue — a rare example of a company choosing to shrink a profitable-looking segment because it's viewed as brand-damaging (over-promoted, over-distributed).

## Economy & consumer

- **No independent macro read given.** Management never characterizes the broader consumer spending environment; all framing runs through Nike-specific brand health and tariff cost, an absence notable for a bellwether consumer name.
- **North America consumer rewards story and scarcity.** "Kids lining up for sneakers again" (AJ11 Gamma) and the Black Cat Black Friday record suggest a consumer still willing to pay full price for genuinely differentiated drops.
- **China consumer has been conditioned into discount-seeking.** Hill: Nike's own promotional habits and thin store investment created "a cycle" of "soft demand leading to consistent promotions and then impacting profitability" — a self-inflicted demand problem, not a purely macro one.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Working: running, up over 20% for a second straight quarter**, "up double digits in every channel, including NIKE Direct" — the single cleanest growth data point on the call.
- **Working: wholesale overall, +8% globally, +24% in North America**, with the order book for spring/summer "up," reversing years of wholesale contraction.
- **Not working: NIKE Direct/Digital, down 9%/14% company-wide**, as Nike deliberately pulls back promotional intensity — margin-healthy but growth-negative near term.
- **Not working: Greater China across every line** — revenue -16%, Digital -36%, EBIT -49% — with "higher levels of aged inventory," "higher obsolescence charges," and wholesale discounts compounding.
- **Not working (yet): Converse and APLA**, both flagged as continued headwinds through Q3 with no turnaround signal given.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Structural reorg to unify execution speed:** geography GMs now report directly to Hill; new COO role (Venkatesh Alagirasami) created to own the end-to-end create-to-sell pipeline — an admission the prior structure was too slow to propagate what worked in North America.
- **Concentrated product/marketing bets:** World Cup football investment (booking units ~40% above 2022 World Cup, 100+ owned and 1,400 partner doors refreshed), new platforms (Nike Mind, Aerofit "air conditioning for the body"), Winter Olympics apparel debut — all back-half-weighted.
- **The real scarcity is margin headroom, not demand.** Tariffs ($1.5B annualized) and the cost of unwinding years of over-promotion (obsolescence write-offs, wholesale discounting) are the binding constraints; CFO Friend is explicit that supply-chain cost leverage "will help us" only once volume returns — margin recovery is gated by growth, which is gated by marketplace repair, which is gated by time.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief: "we see the path back to double-digit... EBIT margins."** Action: no dollar figure, no date, and only a 90-day guidance cadence — the commitment is verbal, not quantified.
- **Belief: China "continues to stand out as one of the most powerful long-term opportunities in sport."** Action: concrete but modest — one store pilot, reduced Singles Day promotions (-35%), inventory cuts, reduced forward buys — Hill himself concedes "it's not happening at the pace we need."
- **Belief: North America's playbook can propagate to other geos.** Action: EMEA only "activated their sport offense on December 1" — barely two weeks old at call time — while APLA and China lag further, showing the propagation is real but slow, not yet proven repeatable.
- **Gap flagged directly:** management's confidence language ("increasingly confident," "clear path") consistently outruns the specificity of their commitments — every hard question about dates got a "ninety days at a time" answer.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No date for China's turnaround:** "it gets tough to put an exact date on it" — repeated across two separate analyst questions.
- **No timeline for double-digit EBIT margins**, despite calling it "a top priority" twice in the prepared remarks.
- **No commitment to raise demand-creation spend above ~10% of revenue**, even though wholesale growth is creating cost leverage Friend concedes could otherwise fund it.
- **Declined multi-year targets outright:** Friend states plainly they need "flexibility to make the right decisions every week," explicitly rejecting the analyst's ask for 2-3 year guideposts.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Dominant theme: timeline pressure.** Nearly every question, reframed multiple ways, asked when "middle innings" ends and when investors get real visibility — on China specifically, on EBIT margin recovery, and on the overall turnaround cadence.
- **Secondary theme: is North America's growth durable or liquidation-fueled?** Analysts probed whether the 24% wholesale growth was clean, prompting Friend to concede Q3 guidance bakes in "much less" liquidation benefit than Q2 delivered.
- **Tertiary theme: product-pipeline conviction outside running** — whether Nike has the same confidence in other categories (basketball, training, football) that it had in running a year ago before it inflected.
- **What got dodged:** every direct ask for a date or multi-year number was redirected to "90 days at a time" language — management's underlying message is "trust the direction, not a deadline." The compressed worry: is this a turnaround on a knowable clock, or an open-ended repair job that keeps re-guiding itself one quarter at a time?
