# CSGP — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-28)

**The key idea:** This call is less an earnings report than a prosecution brief against Zillow, delivered by a CEO who spent a meaningful fraction of prepared remarks and Q&A time laying out five lawsuits (antitrust, copyright, consumer deception, FTC/state AG action) targeting his chief rival, while the underlying business — Homes.com bookings up 53% QoQ, Matterport and Domain freshly integrated — actually did have strong organic news that got somewhat buried under the litigation narrative. The tension: is this genuine competitive vindication or a CEO using legal drama to distract from the fact that Homes.com, after 7 quarters, still holds only 6% share of active listings.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Zillow litigation dominates the narrative by design.** Florance spent an extended, almost gleeful monologue cataloging five lawsuits against Zillow (Compass, CoStar's own IP suit, a consumer class action, FTC v. Zillow/Redfin, and a multistate AG suit) and even interjected it unprompted into an analyst's bookings question: "And Ryan, did I mention that the FTC was suing our competitor?" This is unusually aggressive framing for an earnings call and should be read with real skepticism about how much of the CRE/residential portal competitive dynamic is actually shifting versus how much is rhetorical.
2. **Homes.com's growth is real but the reallocation of investment is a tell.** The "50% of software development into AI" is entirely a reallocation of existing budget, not incremental spend — Florance was explicit that total spend is not increasing, which reframes the "AI investment" story as internal prioritization rather than new commitment.
3. **Sales headcount is scaling faster than it can be absorbed.** Management openly admitted that hiring classes of 100+ reps at Homes.com are hurting per-rep productivity and that they've deliberately capped hiring growth — an unusually candid admission that growth-by-headcount has diminishing returns.
4. **Domain acquisition ($1.9B) shows an aggressive playbook: cut noncore products, redirect resources to the core.** CoStar found that under prior ownership, most of Domain's software resources were spent on products generating under 20% of revenue — a familiar CoStar pattern of buying underinvested assets and reallocating focus.
5. **The margin/EBITDA beat was expense discipline, not just revenue.** Q3 adjusted EBITDA of $115M beat the high end of guidance ($75-85M) on "greater-than-expected headcount savings" — worth noting this came from cost control as much as top-line strength.

## Economy & consumer
- **No standalone macro or consumer commentary was offered.** This is a B2B/B2B2C real estate data and marketplace business — the closest proxy to "the consumer" is agent/subscriber behavior (retention, NPS), and CFO Lown's only macro reference was "continued volatility in the commercial real estate sector" as context for CoStar's core product reacceleration to 8% growth.
- **Homes.com traffic and engagement metrics suggest healthy underlying consumer housing search demand:** 115M unique monthly visitors, unaided brand awareness up from ~4% to 42%, and average session duration up 93% YoY — but these are CoStar's own marketing/product wins more than a read on housing market health.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: Homes.com bookings acceleration.** Net new annualized bookings rose to $16M in Q3, up 53% quarter-over-quarter, with subscriber retention climbing to 86% from 62% a year prior — Florance called this "amazing" progress on a metric that took legacy CoStar roughly a decade to achieve.
- **Working: CoStar (core product) reaccelerated to 8% YoY growth**, "ahead of our guidance," with per-rep productivity at its highest since Q3 2023 and renewal rate at 93.3%, the highest since 2023 — a genuine inflection in the legacy business.
- **Not working / still small: Homes.com's absolute share remains thin.** Despite the growth rate, Homes.com covers only 130,000 active listings against 2.2 million active U.S. properties for sale — roughly 6% penetration — a reminder that percentage growth off a small base is not yet market dominance.
- **Working but capacity-constrained: sales force scaling.** Apartments.com crossed 500 reps for the first time; Homes.com has 500 in production plus 150 in preproduction — but management conceded new reps take time to ramp and productivity dips during rapid hiring waves.

## Investing & scarcity
- **AI investment is a reallocation, not new spend.** "That -- the 50% of our software development going into AI features in Homes.com is an allocation of the existing resources. It does not reflect an increase in total spend," Florance said directly in response to a direct question — worth flagging since headline language ("single biggest commitment by far") could otherwise be read as incremental investment.
- **Domain ($1.9B, closed August 27) and Matterport sales-force buildout (30 → 200+ reps by end of 2026) are the two concrete capital/people commitments this quarter**, alongside continued buyback activity ($165M of $500M authorization used YTD).
- **The binding constraint is onboarding capacity, not capital or demand.** CoStar has $2B cash and is actively buying back stock, so capital is not scarce; the actual limiter management named was the ability to train and ramp large hiring classes without hurting productivity.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: GEO/AEO (AI-engine-driven search traffic) will become a significant future top-of-funnel channel**, even though today it's under 1% of traffic for real estate portals broadly (0.45% for one major U.S. portal, 0.15% for an Australian one). **Action:** CoStar has already shipped AI Smart Search on Homes.com and reallocated half of Homes.com's dev budget toward AI features — an actual product commitment that matches the stated belief, not just talk.
- **Belief: vertical-specialized AI will beat general-purpose AI in real estate**, per Florance's history lesson that AOL, Yahoo, eBay, Microsoft, and Google all tried and failed to dominate real estate portals. **Action:** doubling down on proprietary data and specialized models (buyer personalization, valuation, digital twins) rather than adopting off-the-shelf generative AI wholesale — consistent with stated thesis.
- **Belief: residential portal margins can reach 40%+ EBITDA long-term**, citing precedents like Rightmove and REA Group at 50-75% margins. **Action/gap:** no roadmap or timeline was given — when asked directly, the CFO admitted "I don't have a specific date for that," so the belief significantly outruns any committed plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No date for the 40% residential margin target** — Florance answered a direct analyst question on timeline with "I don't have a specific date for that," offering only qualitative confidence ("I like the progression... I think they're making good progress").
- **No specific 2026 spend number for Homes.com or AI investment** — guidance was limited to "same or lower spend," a soft directional comment rather than a figure, and the CFO simply deferred to the CEO ("You're the CEO. I agree with whatever you say, Andy") rather than adding independent detail.
- **No commitment on Zillow litigation outcomes or timing** — Florance acknowledged "these lawsuits will take years to resolve" and that "the full extent of Zillow's conduct... is yet to be seen," appropriately hedging on outcomes he can't control while still using the litigation aggressively as a narrative weapon.
