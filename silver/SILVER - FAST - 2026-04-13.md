# FAST — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-13)

**The key idea:** Fastenal delivered its third straight double-digit sales quarter with broad, genuinely diversified growth — construction up 17%, non-manufacturing accounts growing faster than manufacturing — even as US manufacturing PMI just barely crossed back above 50. But the quarter's real story is a self-inflicted margin miss: pricing actions lagged tariff- and branded-supplier-driven cost increases by 40bps versus FAST's own target, the second time in a year this has happened, and management openly admits it over-promised on the pace of recovery back in January.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth is real and broadening, not a comp illusion.** 12.4% daily sales growth came with construction swinging from negative mid-single-digits in 2023-24 to +17% this quarter, and non-manufacturing $50K+ sites growing 25% versus 16% company-wide — Dan Florness: "a great supply chain partner is relevant to every industry... all we have to do is understand that end market."
2. **Pricing execution, not demand, is the actual problem.** Gross margin came in ~40bps below FAST's own Q1 target because "tariff-related costs moved through the P&L faster than our pricing." This is explicitly the second consecutive miss on this exact issue.
3. **Management admits its own guidance was wrong.** Florness: "we probably over-answered in January a little bit and underestimated what it would be like to push a string through Q1. It's been a slog." That's an unusually direct self-correction for a company that normally sounds buttoned-up.
4. **Structural SG&A leverage is doing the real work on margins.** SG&A fell to 24.3% of sales from 25%, absorbing a full incentive-comp reload and still expanding operating margin 20bps — the efficiency story (technology, fewer heads per dollar of growth) is outrunning the pricing story.
5. **Branded suppliers, not tariffs per se, are the sharper edge of cost inflation.** Florness singles out branded suppliers using tariff cover to push "aggressive" price hikes, plus a petroleum-linked nitrile glove cost spike "going through the roof" from Middle East disruption — a reminder that non-tariff commodity shocks are layering on top of trade-policy noise.

## Economy & consumer
- **PMI just turned positive after years of red.** "If you look at the last decade or so, you don't see much red on it until the last three years... We've had three months now where we're above 50," Florness said, calling this a genuine inflection rather than noise.
- **Customers are treating tariffs as a planning problem, not a demand problem.** Max Tunnicliff: "most customers are viewing this uncertainty primarily as a cost and planning issue rather than a demand issue" — activity stayed healthy through the quarter despite the confusion.
- **No consumer-facing exposure** — FAST sells B2B industrial/MRO supply, so no direct read on end-consumer health; omitted from frontmatter accordingly is not applicable here since the read comes entirely through industrial customers.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Key-account strategy is compounding.** Contract count up ~8% YoY to 3,600+, $50K+/month sites up 16.3% to ~2,900 and now over half of total sales, average monthly spend per site up $5,700 — "75% of our Q1 sales came from these customers."
- **Digital and FMI adoption keep outrunning company growth.** Digital Footprint sales +13.6%, FMI now ~45% of sales (up 150bps YoY), ~7,000 new FMI device agreements signed in the quarter — a real stickiness moat, not just a reporting metric.
- **Fastener margins are fine; branded-product margins are the leak.** Florness was explicit: "the struggles we have right now are not in fasteners... where some of the struggles are is you start looking at some of the areas of the business that maybe have a little bit more of a branded presence" — safety and cutting-tool categories specifically.
- **Customer mix is a structural gross-margin drag but an operating-margin tailwind.** Growth skews toward larger, lower-gross-margin accounts, but "these accounts are positive to operating margin due to strong fixed cost leverage" — a trade-off management says it's comfortable making repeatedly.
- **International accelerating from a small base.** March international sales (Europe/Asia) grew ~24%; still a small share of revenue but flagged as strategically important.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex held at the high end of FAST's historical range.** FY26 net capex guided to ~$320M (~3.5% of sales), versus a 5-year average of ~2.5% and a prior-decade average of ~4% — funding hub/automation capacity, FMI hardware, and IT infrastructure.
- **Buybacks resumed but only to offset dilution.** $296M returned via dividends plus "a small amount of share repurchases" — Tunnicliff: "we just felt as a management team that we wanted to start to offset dilution... we'll remain opportunistic."
- **The real scarcity is pricing bandwidth, not physical supply.** Branded suppliers pushing "aggressive" cost increases and volatile petroleum-linked commodity costs (nitrile gloves) are the binding constraints — FAST's four-months-of-inventory visibility on fasteners cushions this, but branded/FIFO categories don't have that lag protection.
- **ROIC keeps climbing** — up 180bps trailing-12-month to 31%, reinforcing that capital discipline, not capital scarcity, defines the investment posture.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: price-cost neutrality arrives "mid-year-ish."** Action: regional leaders are running SKU-level pricing triage with the analytics team, and reps are arming customers with documented evidence of specific brand-by-brand cost increases to justify price asks — a real operational lift, not just a forecast.
- **Belief: growth momentum continues (April data reportedly still expanding).** Action: no headcount ramp to match — management is explicit that efficiency gains (tech, FMI, customer mix) have decoupled headcount growth from revenue growth versus prior cycles, meaning they're betting on productivity rather than staffing up ahead of demand.
- **Gap flagged directly by management itself:** they concede Q2 will still be "challenging" on price-cost even while touting a positive PMI signal and cautious optimism on growth — the belief in eventual plateau is not yet backed by a quarter of clean execution.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No Q2 pricing guidance.** Tunnicliff: "we're not guiding toward a Q2... we would do everything in our power to not see the same sequential move from Q1 to Q2" — a stated aspiration, not a forecast.
- **No firm date for price-cost neutrality** beyond a soft "mid-year-ish" — and Florness caveats even that: "we still don't understand some other aspects and what other type of challenges there will be to tariffs coming down the road."
- **No commitment on FY growth trajectory.** Jeff Watts: "it's hard to say what the rest of the year is going to look like, but right now we're... cautiously optimistic" — deliberately non-committal after getting burned by January's over-confidence.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Tariff mechanics got granular scrutiny** — IEEPA vs. Section 122 vs. Section 232, refund exposure, and whether competitors were cheating on tariff pass-through (management said no on all counts, calling IEEPA "a much smaller portion of the total tariff landscape").
- **Capital allocation and incremental margins got confirmatory, not adversarial, questions** — management reaffirmed prior high-20s incremental margin guidance and framed the resumed buyback as a modest, formulaic dilution offset rather than a new capital-return regime.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts aren't questioning demand — they're questioning whether Fastenal's pricing discipline, the thing that's historically defined its margin durability, has genuinely stalled or is merely lagging a temporary tariff-driven cost shock.
