# CDNS — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-27)

**The key idea:** Cadence posted a record quarter (record backlog, 19% revenue growth, best-ever hardware quarter) and is betting that agentic AI expands EDA consumption rather than commoditizing it — CEO Devgan's framing is that AI agents "run more of the base tools" by exploring far more design variations than a human ever would, turning automation into a tailwind instead of a threat. The tension: management is confident enough to raise full-year guidance to 17% growth and target "Rule of 60" for the first time, yet pointedly refuses to build any AI monetization step-function into that same guide.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Agentic AI is being sold as a consumption multiplier, not a threat.** Devgan's core defense against "AI will write better EDA tools" fears is that agents invoke the physically-accurate simulation/verification engines far more heavily than human engineers do — "AI does a lot more exploration than a human would do... it by nature runs more of the base tool."
2. **A new pricing category is opening up.** New agentic "super agents" (ChipStack, ViraStack, InnoStack under the AgentStack framework) are being sold subscription-plus-consumption, similar to other leading AI tools, layered on top of — not replacing — the base-tool subscription model.
3. **The Hexagon D&E deal is explicitly dilutive this year.** $160M of 2026 revenue but ~$0.28 EPS dilution, mostly from lost interest income on the cash portion of the deal — management frames 2026 as a pure integration year with accretion expected in 2027, echoing the Beta acquisition's 2024→2025 margin recovery pattern.
4. **IP business strength broadened beyond the usual suspects.** Record deal with a "leading global foundry" (explicitly not Intel) plus continued momentum with Intel on 14A, alongside newer foundries (Rapidus, Samsung) adding incremental IP demand as design activity fragments across more manufacturing nodes.
5. **Guidance discipline despite a blowout quarter.** Management raised full-year revenue guide but kept second-half quarterly run-rate below Q2 levels, and explicitly said they "wanted to wait until July" to update the back half — a deliberately conservative posture layered on genuinely strong bookings.

## Economy & consumer
- **Broad health across the semiconductor stack.** "Some of the hyperscalers and AI semi companies were all already doing well last year, but now... the memory companies are doing well, even... analog mixed-signal companies are doing well," per Devgan — a widening, not narrowing, of strength.
- **Chip shortages are a design-activity tailwind, not a brake.** Customers are pursuing multi-foundry/multi-node sourcing strategies to secure capacity, which "directly lead[s] to more design activity" rather than delaying it.
- **No end-consumer exposure** — CDNS's customer base is chip/systems designers, so this note has no separate consumer read; the closest proxy is customer R&D spend health, which is strong.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Across-the-board double-digit growth**: IP +22% YoY, core EDA +18% YoY, SDA +18% YoY, hardware "best quarter ever" — no soft segment flagged.
- **Record $8B backlog "ahead of plan"** underpins the raised 17% full-year growth outlook and first-time Rule of 60 target.
- **Hexagon integration is a near-term margin drag by design**: operating margin guide came down primarily from Hexagon dilution ($0.28 EPS, 5-10% margin on the acquired $160M revenue), financed 30% stock / 70% cash.
- **China steady at ~13% of revenue**, described as "broadly consistent" with expectations, though CFO flagged the YoY comp looks generous because China was weak in Q1 2025.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital and headcount going into agentic tooling and physical AI**: continued build-out of AgentStack/ChipStack/ViraStack/InnoStack, expanded Nvidia physical-AI partnership, and the completed Hexagon D&E acquisition to round out a "principal simulation and optimization" layer for physical AI.
- **The real constraint is customer engineering headcount, not CDNS's own capacity.** One large customer reportedly needs "2x more engineers" per new design — "unrealizable headcount growth" — which is precisely the gap agentic tools are pitched to close.
- **Hardware capacity is not yet a binding constraint**: Palladium Z3 can handle "1 trillion transistor systems" versus today's largest designs at 100-200B transistors, so Devgan explicitly declined to rush a Z4/Z5 refresh, only committing to "a Z4 system before 2030."
- **Go-to-market muscle, not just tech, was a stated gap** — Hexagon's sales team and acquired reseller channel are called out as filling a historical SDA go-to-market weakness.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: EDA's share of customer R&D spend has already risen from 7% to 11% and management expects it to keep climbing as agentic tools prove out. **Action**: still described as directional ("how much it'll go up, we will see") with no committed monetization figure in guidance — belief is ahead of contracted revenue.
- **Belief**: physical AI "will be bigger than data center AI by a long shot." **Action**: backed by the completed Hexagon acquisition and Nvidia partnership, but Devgan's own historical framing ("I've said this for five years now") signals the payoff has been a persistent forecast, not yet a realized inflection — worth watching whether this cycle differs.
- **Belief**: agentic monetization could land sooner than the traditional "two contract cycles." **Action**: explicitly NOT reflected in 2026 guidance — CFO Wall: "We're not assuming a sudden step function in AI monetization in the guide."

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No AI monetization step-function baked into guidance**, despite management's own optimism about faster-than-usual (sub-two-contract-cycle) agentic monetization.
- **Declined to give a Palladium Z4/Protium X4 launch date**, only a soft "before 2030" commitment, despite a direct analyst ask about refresh cadence.
- **Second-half guide deliberately not raised** even though Q1 bookings strength implied ~$100M of extra operating cash flow versus the $65M revenue raise actually taken — management called this "appropriate prudence."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Moat durability**: whether AI could enable competitors/startups to replicate CDNS's base tools — management's rebuttal leaned on R&D scale (15,000 employees, ~10,000 in R&D, 1,000+ PhDs) rather than a technical argument.
- **Monetization mechanics**: repeated, pointed questions on how agentic tools are actually priced (subscription-plus-consumption vs. renewal-embedded) and whether CDNS can capture incremental value beyond base-tool licenses.
- **Margin/guide reconciliation**: multiple questions dissecting the Hexagon dilution math and why the raised full-year guide didn't flow fully into a higher second-half run-rate — a mild skepticism that the strong Q1 fully justifies the muted back-half assumptions.
- **Portfolio strategy**: SDA/physical-AI roadmap post-Hexagon, directly invoking Synopsys-Ansys as the competitive comparison point.
- Compressed worry: **is this an AI-driven structural re-rating of EDA demand, or a strong-but-ordinary bookings quarter that management is being unusually cautious about extrapolating?**
