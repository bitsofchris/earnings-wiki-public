# MDLZ — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-28)

**The key idea:** Mondelez pushed cocoa-driven pricing further than the consumer (and some private competitors) would bear, and got caught on two continents simultaneously — European chocolate elasticity nearly doubled versus plan and the US biscuit category decelerated into the government-shutdown quarter. Management's answer is not to retreat into discounting (which they say already failed) but to surgically re-engineer price points and pack sizes while banking on 2026 cocoa deflation to fund a "significant improvement" — a bet that's more hope-plus-mechanics than demonstrated recovery.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Europe's pricing overshot the market.** A ~30% cocoa-driven price increase produced 0.7-0.8 elasticity versus an expected 0.4-0.5, compounded by competitors (mostly private companies) declining to match and retailers quietly grabbing extra margin. Dirk Van de Put: "we passed 2 key price points, and that was probably a little bit more than the consumer can accept at the moment."
2. **US biscuits decelerated sharply into Q3**, down 4% volume versus a 2.8% year-to-date average, with management explicitly linking the softness to consumer anxiety and flagging the government shutdown as a fresh headwind — a rare instance of a CPG company naming a specific Washington event as a demand risk in real time.
3. **The "protect the profit pool" pivot is really a failed-experiment admission.** Van de Put conceded the early-2025 promotional/value push "wasn't giving us the volume effect we were hoping for" and now argues broad value plays don't work because "even if the biscuits are a very cheap price, it doesn't necessarily mean that they will buy them" — consumers are rationing baskets around essentials, not chasing discounts.
4. **Guidance for 2026 is EPS-only, not top-line** — CFO Luca Zaramella committed to high-single-digit EPS growth on the strength of cocoa turning deflationary, but declined multiple times to frame organic revenue growth, citing unresolved cocoa-level assumptions for next year.
5. **Emerging markets are quietly doing the heavy lifting**: Brazil double-digit growth, India mid-single-digit despite a deliberate downsizing-over-pricing strategy, and China only recently turning slightly negative for the first time in the disclosed period — the portfolio's ballast while the two developed anchors wobble.

## Economy & consumer
- **US consumer squeezed on baskets, not just prices**: "the basket size of the consumer is really not increasing over the last 3 years," per Van de Put, with snacking treated as discretionary and shifting toward club/value/e-commerce and multipacks over food-and-mass channels.
- **Government shutdown named as a live confidence risk** — an unusually direct call-out of US political dysfunction as a demand driver, not just a supply one.
- **Europe's heatwave and downsizing both dented volume** in the UK and Germany independent of the pricing issue, muddying how much of the miss is elasticity versus one-off weather/portfolio effects.
- **Argentina hyperinflation and Mexican job anxiety** are cited as macro drags on emerging-market volume, alongside a hopeful note that Argentina's "recent elections" could improve the backdrop.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **North America OI negative largely on cocoa pass-through into non-chocolate SKUs** — Oreo, Chips Ahoy, and Tate's all carry meaningful chocolate content, so the cocoa spike bled into products investors might not associate with chocolate cost exposure.
- **Protein and premium are genuine bright spots**: Perfect Bar and Builders (Clif) growing 20%+, with Tate's, belVita, and Hu (vegan chocolate) also cited as connecting with consumers even as core biscuits struggle.
- **China turned slightly negative in Q3** for the first time referenced on this call, though still positive year-to-date; management attributes it to consumer confidence at a "low for the last 20 years" while expecting gradual recovery.
- **Brazil and India outperforming**: Brazil posted double-digit Q3 growth on "excellent execution" across biscuits, chocolate, and gum/candy; India ran mid-single-digit growth in Q3 by leaning on downsizing over price increases.

## Investing & scarcity
- **New multiyear North America supply chain program** announced: automating US bakery lines to relieve capacity constraints and cut cost, plus consolidating the DSD network's distribution centers and branches — savings flagged as visible "most likely as of 2027," inside existing cash-flow targets.
- **A&C (advertising & consumer) spend set to step up materially in 2026** after being managed down in 2025, funded by expected cocoa deflation; non-working media and overhead stay controlled except for incentive-plan normalization.
- **PPA (price-pack architecture) is the real 2026 investment thesis** — deliberately building products at $3-4 price points (down from ~70% of the range above $4 roughly 1.5 years ago) while preserving margin, rather than a blanket value play.
- **The binding constraint is consumer price tolerance, not input availability** — cocoa is described as "well protected and covered" with upside optionality if prices fall further, so the real scarce resource is how much price the consumer will absorb.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Europe chocolate volume returns to growth in 2026 as elasticity works in reverse on cocoa deflation. **Action:** repricing/downsizing specific bar formats (potential 300g→250g) and stepping up seasonal/promo-effectiveness work — a targeted, not company-wide, fix.
- **Belief:** US returns to "positive growth" in 2026. **Action:** channel-share investment in club/value/e-commerce, route-to-market spend, multipack push for on-the-go occasions, and incremental pricing — notably, management is NOT projecting the underlying US category itself to improve, only their share/mix within it.
- **Gap flagged explicitly:** the EPS algorithm (high-single-digit growth) is a firm 2026 commitment, but the top-line path that's supposed to fund it is described as literally not yet modelable — a divergence between the confidence embedded in the bottom-line target and the acknowledged uncertainty on volume/price recovery.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 revenue guidance** — Zaramella: "it is a little bit premature to put all the pieces together for you at this point in time," pending clarity on cocoa levels.
- **Declined to promise elasticity will normalize** in Europe — framed adjustments as targeted to specific overpriced SKUs (e.g., the 300g bar), not a signal that broader elasticity assumptions are being revised down.
- **No commitment to a value/discounting strategy** in the US despite competitor and analyst pressure to follow that path — management explicitly distanced itself from that approach after calling their own earlier promotional push ineffective.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Heavy, repeated drilling on Europe elasticity mechanics** — multiple analysts pushed for granularity on whether the 0.7-0.8 reading was structural or would mean-revert, forcing management to distinguish seasonal-versus-everyday elasticity.
- **Skepticism about 2026 EPS confidence without top-line visibility** — one analyst asked directly for algorithm-level revenue guidance and was rebuffed with the cocoa-uncertainty explanation.
- **Questions on whether the North America pull-back from promotion was really "protecting the profit pool"** versus a tacit admission the earlier value push failed — management conceded the latter more than they defended the former framing.
- **Compressed worry:** did Mondelez lose control of price elasticity on two continents at once, and is a 2026 EPS target credible when the company itself can't yet model the revenue path that's supposed to deliver it?
