# FTNT — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-05)

**The key idea:** Fortinet posted a headline-beating quarter — 14% billings/revenue growth, record Q3 operating margin, product revenue up 18% against a 12% street estimate — but spent most of the Q&A parrying a single, unresolved question: why does strong product growth keep failing to show up in service revenue, which just decelerated for the ninth consecutive quarter? Management's answer is a 29-month attach-rate lag story that pushes the payoff to "second half of 2026," a horizon convenient enough that several analysts pushed back on it directly.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Product beat, service lagged — again.** Product revenue grew 18% (vs. a 12% street estimate) while service revenue growth slowed to 13%, its ninth straight quarter of deceleration; management's explanation is that last year's negative product billings are still working through customers' ~29-month service terms, and this year's product strength should flip service growth up in "the second half of 2026."
2. **SASE growth is real but the underlying mix raises questions.** FortiSASE billings grew over 100% and unified SASE ARR grew 13%, but Ken Xie admitted much of SASE growth rides on customers upgrading existing firewall/SD-WAN installs rather than net-new wins, and a CFO clarification revealed the "8-figure" city police force deal was "less SASE and more SD-WAN" — i.e., product, not cloud-delivered subscription.
3. **The end-of-service refresh narrative got walked back live on air.** Ken Xie downplayed the widely-anticipated 2026 hardware end-of-support cohort as not a real growth driver ("I just think that was some example pretty funny... probably will stop tracking that"), directly contradicting a narrative the sell-side has built models around — and COO John Whittle had to walk it partway back by noting the new ASIC/OS launch timing "lines up well" with that same refresh cycle.
4. **AI security framed as the next leg, but still unquantified.** The new Secure AI Data Center product leverages Fortinet's ASIC performance/power advantage and has landed early wins (a Fortune 150 e-commerce logistics firm), but Ken Xie conceded it's "still in the early stage... difficult to estimate" — enthusiasm without a number attached.
5. **Capital return dwarfs incremental infrastructure spend.** $1.83B went to buybacks in a single quarter (cutting share count ~3%) against just $88M in infrastructure capex — Fortinet is prioritizing shareholder return over aggressive infrastructure build-out even while touting AI-datacenter opportunity.

## Economy & consumer
- **Geographic softness dismissed as noise, not signal.** North America "lagged" growth this quarter; CFO attributed it purely to lumpy large-deal timing versus Q2 strength, explicitly denying any share loss: "They performed well, no share loss."
- **Not a consumer-facing business** — no end-customer commentary applies.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** Billings +14% to $1.81B, record Q3 operating margin of 36.9% (up 80bps), gross margin 81.6% "better than expected... driven by strong execution and cost control," and $1M+ deal count up 26% with total dollar value up over 30% — large enterprise momentum is broad and real.
- **What's not:** Service revenue deceleration is now a nine-quarter trend, and the CFO's own explanation implies Fortinet's billings-to-revenue conversion is becoming less predictable quarter-to-quarter — she told analysts to "look at it more on a longer-term basis and not just quarter-to-quarter," an implicit admission that near-term service billings are noisy and hard to forecast.

## Investing & scarcity
- **New silicon and OS cadence anchors the roadmap:** FortiOS 8.0 launches March 2026 (in beta now), alongside a new ASIC (MPA) promising "a few times faster" performance at "almost the same cost" — this is the core mechanism management leans on to justify durable 10-15% product growth without relying on refresh-cycle timing.
- **Infrastructure spend is modest and self-funded:** $88M Q3 infra investment (up $51M YoY), full-year guided at $380-430M, dwarfed by $1.63B year-to-date free cash flow — Fortinet is not capital-constrained; it is choosing where to allocate, not scrambling for funding.
- **No constraint cited on compute, talent, or supply** — the closest thing to a "scarcity" statement was framed as an embarrassment of riches: too many growth vectors to chase all at once, per John Whittle.
- **OT/critical infrastructure security is a standout, underappreciated growth line:** billings up over 30%, described as a market where Fortinet is "the only leader" per a 3-year Westland report, built on 10+ years of investment competitors haven't matched.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Stated belief:** product revenue growth normalizes to 10-15% "for the next 5 to 10 years," service revenue reaccelerates in 2H 2026, and Fortinet becomes the #1 SASE vendor "within a few years."
- **Action behind SASE belief:** real — FortiSASE billings +100%, Gartner Magic Quadrant leadership claimed, expanding sovereign SASE deals with governments; the product investment (single-OS architecture, ASIC-accelerated) is concrete and differentiated.
- **Gap:** on the service-revenue reacceleration thesis, there is no new investment or commitment described beyond "wait for the attach cycle to catch up" — it's a passive, time-based bet on math working itself out, not an active initiative. Management also declined to actually commit to 2026 guidance even while implicitly leaning on 2026 timing to defend the narrative — a soft contradiction between the confidence expressed and the guidance withheld.
- **AI datacenter belief vs. action:** stated as a "massive growth opportunity," but action so far is limited to a handful of early customer engagements and one flagship logistics-company case study — investment framing is aspirational, not yet resourced at scale.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to size the AI security opportunity:** "It's still in the early stage. It's difficult to estimate" (Ken Xie) — enthusiasm without commitment.
- **Avoided quantifying the SD-WAN-to-SASE conversion opportunity** when asked directly what percentage of the SD-WAN base remains available to convert — pointed back to the same slide rather than giving a number.
- **Sidestepped the end-of-service cohort question's real intent** — dismissed its importance rather than providing the transparency analysts wanted on its actual contribution.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Dominant theme: the product-vs-service revenue disconnect.** Multiple analysts (BofA, Citi, Barclays) pushed on why 18% product growth isn't translating into service acceleration, and openly questioned whether Q4 guidance being "slightly below the street" makes sense given the product strength story.
- **Second theme: skepticism about the 2026 refresh "super-cycle."** At least two analysts (Scotiabank, Truist proxy via Piper) noted management's own commentary conspicuously omitted the widely-anticipated refresh cycle as a 2026/2027 driver, forcing Ken Xie into a defensive, almost dismissive answer that read as underprepared for the question.
- **Third theme: SASE unit economics and competitive dynamics** — deal composition (hardware vs. cloud-delivered), pricing pressure, and win-rate visibility, none of which management would quantify precisely.
- **What got dodged:** any forward numerical commitment — 2026 guidance, AI security TAM, SD-WAN conversion opportunity size — all met with directional confidence but no figures.
- **Compressed worry:** is Fortinet's product strength this year real forward momentum, or a billings-timing artifact being explained away with a conveniently long attach-rate lag story that pushes accountability out to late 2026?
