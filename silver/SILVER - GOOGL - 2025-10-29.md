# GOOGL — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** Alphabet crossed $100B in quarterly revenue while explicitly reframing AI as a demand *expander* rather than a cannibalization risk to Search — and backed that story with a second consecutive CapEx raise (to $91-93B) plus a warning that 2026 capacity will still trail demand. The tension the whole call dances around: management has strong proof AI is driving query and cloud-revenue growth, but essentially no hard proof yet that AI Mode itself monetizes as well as legacy Search, and every analyst question aimed at that gap got an engagement-metrics answer instead.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **First $100B quarter, doubled in five years.** Pichai's framing — "5 years ago, our quarterly revenue was at $50 billion... we are firmly in the generative AI era" — sets up AI as the growth driver of record, not a cost center, even as depreciation from that same AI buildout grew 41% YoY.
2. **Cloud backlog exploded, not just revenue.** Backlog jumped 46% sequentially and 82% YoY to $155B — a $49B sequential increase — with more $1B+ deals signed in nine months of 2025 than the prior two years combined. This is a leading indicator well ahead of the 34% reported Cloud growth.
3. **CapEx raised twice in one year, and 2026 will be worse.** Guidance moved from $85B to $91-93B, and Anat pre-announced "a significant increase" for 2026 without a number, meaning the market is being asked to underwrite another leg up before seeing the figure.
4. **AI Mode monetization is still an open question the company won't close.** Despite 75M U.S. daily actives and queries doubling in the quarter, Schindler's answer on ad testing was blunt: "It's really too early to tell." Compare to AI Overviews, where they do have a number: monetizing at "approximately the same rate" as classic Search.
5. **Waymo momentum is real but integration with Gemini is not yet planned.** Multiple city expansions (London, Tokyo, Dallas, Nashville, Denver, Seattle) signal scaling confidence, but Pichai deferred the obvious next question — personal-data-powered pre-scheduling — to a future internal review rather than describing a roadmap.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read offered.** The call contains zero commentary on consumer spending health, employment, or broader economic conditions — all "demand" language is AI-product-specific (Search queries, Cloud deals, subscriptions).
- **Election-year ad comp flagged as a headwind, not tailwind.** Anat warns Q4 YoY ad comparisons, especially YouTube, "will be negatively impacted by the strong spend on U.S. elections in the fourth quarter of 2024" — the only place the call acknowledges a comp-driven distortion rather than organic strength.
- **Engagement, not spending, is the consumer signal given.** AI Mode's 75M U.S. daily actives and doubled quarterly queries, plus AI Overviews reaching "2 billion users," are the closest things to consumer data points, and they measure usage, not wallet.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Every segment grew double digits.** Search +15% to $56.6B, YouTube ads +15% to $10.3B, subscriptions +21% to $12.9B, Cloud +34% to $15.2B — genuinely broad-based, not one segment carrying the quarter.
- **Cloud margin expansion is the standout.** Operating margin went from 17.1% to 23.7% YoY even while absorbing higher depreciation and energy costs — Anat attributes it to "strong revenue performance and continued efficiencies," a rare case of scale beating cost inflation.
- **Network (third-party) advertising keeps shrinking.** Down 3% to $7.4B, the one line moving the wrong direction, consistent with continued disintermediation of publisher-network ad inventory.
- **The EC fine muddies the headline margin.** A $3.5B European Commission charge cut Google Services margin and made reported operating income growth (9%) look far worse than underlying growth (22% ex-fine) — a legitimate distinction Anat discloses cleanly, but headline readers who don't back it out will misread margin trajectory.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx raised again, mix disclosed.** $91-93B for 2025 (from $85B), with Q3's $24B split ~60% servers / 40% data centers and networking — a capital-intensity ratio worth tracking against depreciation growth.
- **Depreciation is now a real headwind, stated as such.** Up 41% YoY to $5.6B in Q3 alone, and Anat explicitly guides it "to accelerate slightly in Q4" — management naming its own margin drag rather than burying it.
- **Compute is the binding constraint, admitted directly.** "We still expect to remain in a tight demand-supply environment in Q4 and 2026" despite improved server-deployment and data-center-construction pace — demand is outrunning even accelerated capacity builds.
- **Custom silicon as the differentiation thesis.** Pichai frames TPUs (Ironwood, 7th-gen) plus NVIDIA GPU access as the reason Google Cloud can expand margin while growing — "the only company providing a wide range of both" — with Anthropic's disclosed plan to access up to 1 million TPUs as the proof point of external validation.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** AI Mode and AI Overviews will keep expanding query volume into commercial territory over time. **Action:** AI Max ad product already rolled out globally in September and used by "hundreds of thousands of advertisers" — monetization infrastructure is moving faster than the stated uncertainty about AI Mode ads themselves, which are still just "testing."
- **Belief:** 2026 will be a big CapEx step-up and a big Waymo expansion year. **Action:** Guidance for the CapEx number itself is withheld until Q4 earnings — the belief is stated with conviction, the commitment (the actual dollar figure) is not yet given.
- **Belief:** Agentic commerce and checkout are the future of Search monetization. **Action:** Concrete but early — PayPal partnership for agentic commerce, new "agent-to-agent" open protocols — real deals exist, but Schindler frames the whole space as "all early," matching the tentative belief to a correspondingly tentative build-out.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **AI Mode ad economics:** "It's really too early to tell" — no timeline, no metric, despite being asked about it in some form by nearly every analyst.
- **Waymo x Gemini integration:** Pichai declines to describe specifics, instead noting he's "scheduled to meet with the team to do a review on it in a few weeks" — a review that hasn't happened yet, offered in place of an answer.
- **AI Mode ad expansion pace:** Schindler repeats "we'll continue to test and learn before we expand this any further" twice across different questions — a consistent, deliberate non-commitment on when or how ads scale inside AI Mode.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The dominant cluster: does agentic/AI-mode search monetize as well as classic Search, and how fast will it get there.** Framed variously around agentic e-commerce rates, AI Mode query-length effects on ROAS, and the addressable market for AI-driven commercial queries — asked by nearly every questioner in a different wrapper, and answered each time with usage stats (DAUs, query-doubling) rather than a monetization number for AI Mode specifically.
- **A secondary cluster on CapEx discipline and ROI proof**, including a direct question to the CFO on how she evaluates returns on the AI infrastructure buildout — answered with a "rigorous framework" description rather than disclosed hurdle rates or payback periods.
- **Dodged outright:** AI Mode ad monetization details and the Waymo/Gemini personal-data integration roadmap — both explicitly deferred to future testing or a future internal review.
- **Compressed worry:** Alphabet is spending at an accelerating rate on a bet that AI-driven Search stays as monetizable as the search it's replacing, and the Street can't yet get a straight answer on whether that bet is paying off in AI Mode itself — only that it's paying off elsewhere (Cloud, AI Overviews).
