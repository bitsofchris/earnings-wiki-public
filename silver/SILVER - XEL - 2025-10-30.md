# XEL — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** Xcel used its annual Q3 five-year-plan reset to lean hard into the AI/data-center capital supercycle — a $60B plan with 11% rate base growth and a raised 9% EPS growth objective — while simultaneously closing the book on its Marshall wildfire liability via settlement. The tension analysts kept circling: management is confident enough to flag $10B+ of *additional* unbooked upside, yet won't raise its stated long-term growth range past "6-8%+," and insists 11% rate base growth won't compress ROEs even as earnings grow slower than the asset base.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth is diversified, not just AI hype.** Of the 5% sales growth assumption for 2026-2030, only 3 points are data centers; 1.5 points come from Permian Basin oil-and-gas electrification (SPS running 8% growth) and 0.5 from residential electrification — a hedge against any AI capex air pocket that peers more concentrated in hyperscaler load lack.
2. **Wildfire liability closed on Marshall, still open on Smokehouse Creek.** The $290M Marshall settlement (excluded from ongoing EPS) let management pivot the call to growth, but the smaller Smokehouse Creek claims process is still live, with the estimated low-end liability raised to $410M against $360M already committed and $500M of insurance — a reminder wildfire risk is structural, not one-time, for a utility building a wildfire mitigation plan in every state.
3. **Supply chain is the real constraint, and Xcel claims a scale advantage.** CEO Bob Frenzel: turbines are "call it, 4 years out" and 345kV transformers are similarly tight; Xcel says its response was to order 19 turbines ahead of need and "backward integrate" vendors into its full capital plan for pricing and equipment certainty — a bet that scale buys queue position competitors without comparable capital plans won't get.
4. **The 9% vs. "6-8%+" framing is a hedge, not a typo.** CFO Brian Van Abel explicitly declined to raise the stated long-term growth objective to match the 9% five-year number, saying 6-8%+ reflects "balancing affordability" beyond 2030 — management is telling investors to bank the 9% for now but not assume it's the new steady state.
5. **Data centers are self-funding on interconnection but not fully on system upgrades.** Frenzel: large-load customers "usually [are] paying for that 100%" on dedicated lateral transmission, but broader regional transmission tied to MISO/SPP buildouts is cost-allocated regionally, "not necessarily coming directly onto our customers" — a distinction that matters for who actually bears the AI infrastructure buildout cost.

## Economy & consumer
- **Sales growth beat plan and stayed diversified.** Weather-normalized electric sales rose 2.5% YTD versus a 3% full-year guide, with "strong residential sales growth across all OpCos and increased C&I load in SPS and PSCo."
- **Bills remain the core value proposition.** Residential electric and gas bills sit 28%/12% below the national average over five years, and are 14%/20% lower than 2014 in inflation-adjusted terms — management leans on this explicitly to defend the case for continued heavy capex without political backlash.
- **Assistance program scaling.** Since 2024, Xcel connected over 200,000 customers with nearly $300M in financial assistance, with further proposals pending in Minnesota, Wisconsin, and an upcoming Colorado rate case.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Guidance discipline intact.** Reaffirmed 2025 ongoing EPS of $3.75-$3.85, on pace for a 21st consecutive year of hitting guidance — management repeatedly leans on this track record as its credibility anchor.
- **O&M creeping up.** Full-year O&M now forecast to rise 5%, driven partly by a $37M quarterly increase, $25M of it from higher health and benefit costs — a cost pressure not fully offset by rate case recovery timing.
- **Wildfire liability is a moving target.** Marshall is settled and excluded from ongoing earnings, but Smokehouse Creek's low-end liability estimate was revised up to $410M, with $360M already committed in settlements against $500M of insurance coverage — the tail risk isn't closed.
- **Financing costs are the earnings drag.** Higher financing costs cut EPS by $0.15 and higher D&A cut another $0.09 this quarter, both direct consequences of the accelerating capital plan — the growth story has an earnings-quality cost baked in during the build-out years.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The plan is now $60B over five years**, up from a prior comparison base, delivering 11% annualized rate base growth — 7,500 MW renewables, 3,000 MW gas, ~2,000 MW storage, 1,500 transmission miles, and ~$5B in distribution/transmission resiliency.
- **A large incremental pipeline sits outside the base plan.** Van Abel: RFP-driven upside is "a range of 6,000 to 9,000 total megawatts" plus transmission, with Xcel "winning about half of that plus that transmission," sizing incremental opportunity at "$10 billion-plus," some spilling past 2030.
- **Equipment scarcity is being managed through scale and lock-in.** 19 gas turbines already on order provide over 4 GW of future generation; management describes "backward integrating" vendors into the full five-year plan for pricing and supply certainty, plus safe-harboring renewable and storage projects ahead of the 2030 tax credit cliff.
- **Financing mix: ~40% equity / 60% debt on incremental capital**, with $7B of equity content planned for 2026-2030 (on top of ~$3B already issued or contracted in 2025), targeting a return to 17% FFO-to-debt credit metrics only in the back half of the forecast period — an acknowledged near-term credit metric squeeze from front-loaded capex.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** 9% average EPS growth through 2030, no ROE compression despite 11% rate base growth, and confidence that Colorado generation RFPs plus unannounced MISO/SPP transmission tranches will "fill in" the back half of the plan.
- **Act:** Already locked $3B of 2025 equity financing, placed 19 turbines on order years ahead of need, filed multiple state RFPs (Colorado 4,500 MW near-term procurement, SPS 870 MW capacity) with decisions due within months, and is filing a Minnesota gas rate case the day after the call requesting a $63M revenue increase.
- **The gap:** Management is confident enough to publicly size $10B+ of unbooked upside, yet deliberately keeps the *stated* long-term growth objective at "6-8%+" rather than lifting it to track the 9% they're actually modeling — telling investors "trust the plan for five years" without committing to the growth rate persisting beyond it.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Won't raise the long-term growth objective to match plan-period math.** Van Abel kept 6-8%+ as the stated long-run range even after guiding to 9% through 2030, citing affordability trade-offs that could reassert once the current capex wave passes.
- **Won't count the $10B+ incremental pipeline as booked.** Explicitly separated "base plan" from "additional pipeline" sourced from unresolved RFPs (Colorado Just Transition Solicitation still awaiting adjudication) and un-awarded future MISO/SPP transmission tranches.
- **Wouldn't quantify lateral-transmission-specific customer economics.** Asked to scale how much data-center-specific lateral transmission spend versus regionally allocated transmission, Van Abel said those are "really customer-specific system impact studies... hard to put a number on it in a general sense."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Growth-rate mechanics dominated.** Multiple questions dug into why 2029-2030 capital dips versus peers ramping up, and why management won't reconcile the "9%" five-year number with the "6-8%+" long-term objective — the implicit worry being that growth mechanically decelerates once this specific capital wave and tax-credit window close.
- **ROE compression math got direct pushback.** One analyst laid out that 11% rate base growth against a lower EPS growth rate implies "pretty significant compression in earned ROEs," pressing management on whether conservatism is embedded — Van Abel's answer (a ~200bp financing-lag gap, not structural erosion) didn't fully settle the tension.
- **Supply chain, credit metrics, and cost allocation rounded out the Q&A** — equipment queue lengths for turbines/transformers, comfort with FFO-to-debt only reaching 17% in the plan's later years, and how much transmission spend gets shifted onto data-center customers versus socialized regionally.
- **Compressed worry:** is Xcel's growth story durable past 2030, or is it a five-year sugar high built on a tax-credit cliff and a capital wave that has to eventually taper?
