# ROST — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-20)

**The key idea:** Ross posted a 500bps sequential comp acceleration to +7% under a CEO barely a year into the job, and spent the entire call refusing to take a victory lap — crediting inherited merchant talent, hedging on how much is macro tailwind, and explicitly declining to increase investment despite clearly positive early ROI on marketing and store refresh. This is a company choosing to prove the inflection is durable before it spends against it.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A genuine, broad-based inflection, not a single-category story.** Every major merchandise category and every major geography was positive, and CEO Jim Conroy repeatedly used "broad-based" to underscore this wasn't one hot category masking weakness elsewhere — a meaningfully different picture than Q1's flat comp.
2. **The branded strategy is now visibly paying off in the P&L, not just the narrative.** Ladies apparel, the category most touched by the branded pivot, went from a comp drag a year ago to "comp enhancing" this quarter — management said the multi-quarter merchandise-margin investment "seems to be paying off now," with three more quarters of anniversary-driven upside expected.
3. **Tariffs are being fully absorbed via mix and opportunism, not price alone.** A modest, deliberate AUR increase (kept "under the umbrella" of traditional retailers) combined with vendor cost concessions and abundant closeout inventory turned what was a ~$0.05/share Q3 tariff drag into a guided-negligible Q4 impact — described as "somewhat neutral" heading into 2026 barring policy changes.
4. **Deliberate under-investment despite proof points working.** Self-checkout, store refresh, and the new marketing agency are all showing positive signals, yet management explicitly kept spend within the pre-existing financial model, framing further investment as a decision for "three to six months" from now — a conscious choice to validate before scaling.
5. **CEO transition risk is being managed through humility, not confidence.** Conroy repeatedly credited "the best merchants in the world" already in place before he arrived and flagged that his own contribution was "amplifying" marketing/stores — an unusually self-effacing frame for a CEO delivering a beat-and-raise quarter, and one that reads as calibrated expectation-setting ahead of a tougher compare.

## Economy & consumer
- **No income-cohort trade-down signal.** "The 7% comp was very broad based across all income levels" — management explicitly checked and found no distinction between lower- and higher-income trade areas, a notable contrast to off-price's usual counter-cyclical narrative.
- **Traffic-led comp, not just AUR.** "Traffic or transactions were the biggest" driver of the comp, with UPT and AUR also both up — evidence of real demand rather than pure pricing/mix engineering.
- **Macro read stays cautious even amid strength.** Conroy: "there's a whole bunch of other macro uncertainties that have probably left consumers a little bit uneasy in their shopping" — strength coexists with an unsettled underlying consumer, per management's own framing.
- **SNAP-delay question drew a clean denial.** Asked directly about early-November SNAP benefit delays hitting DD's (Ross's more value-sensitive banner), Hartshorn said trends were "very consistent" across the quarter with nothing to call out.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Cosmetics, shoes, and ladies led; home lagged then recovered.** Home was "slightly below the chain average" for a second consecutive quarter before turning "nicely positive" late in Q3, which management attributes to sequential merchandising improvement rather than assortment pullback.
- **Merchandise margin declined slightly but beat expectations** — down 10bps, better than planned on "less ticketing and stronger shrink results," even as distribution costs deleveraged 60bps from a new DC ramp and tariff processing costs.
- **Operating margin compressed 35bps to 11.6%**, "mainly due to the impact of tariffs," with the CEO transition itself adding an SG&A headwind that was still absorbed within flat SG&A costs.
- **Self-checkout took years to get right.** After multiple failed shrink models, an 80-store prototype now shows both lower shrink and higher sales — a rare case of an operational tech initiative actually clearing its own hurdle before scaling.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Inventory build pulled forward, not overbuilt.** Average store inventory was up 15% (total consolidated up 9%) as the holiday sales floor was set in October, earlier than last year — described as intentional, with packaway share actually down (36% vs 38% last year).
- **The scarce resource was pricing precision, not goods.** "Exceptional product availability in the marketplace" let merchants chase demand with closeouts; the binding constraint was navigating tariff-driven cost/price decisions correctly across categories, not sourcing supply.
- **Capital return continued on autopilot.** $262M repurchased in Q3, on track for $1.05B for the year — no signal of accelerating buybacks despite the earnings beat.
- **New DC capacity is now the long lead-time investment.** The next distribution center is "two to three years away," meaning current capacity will be levered through that window with a "slight headwind" from the newest DC easing into 2026.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: tariffs go from a real cost to a non-issue.** Stated expectation is Q4 impact negligible and 2026 "somewhat neutral" barring policy shifts — a clean, quantified forward view ($0.15/share full-year 2025 cost, versus near-zero going forward).
- **Action gap: no corresponding spend increase to capture the opening.** Despite calling tariff pressure a fading headwind and marketing/store initiatives a clear tailwind, management kept marketing spend flat as % of sales and capex within the "financial and operating model" inherited pre-CEO-transition — a explicit choice to wait for more data rather than lean in now.
- **Belief: the branded strategy in ladies has further room to run** ("a fair amount" of opportunity, three more quarters before anniversary). **Action:** continuing the existing brand-sourcing investment, but no incremental dollars announced beyond the existing multi-quarter plan.
- **Belief: Northeast/Puerto Rico expansion works** — new stores "outperformed our plan," Brooklyn store called an outlier standout. **Action:** roughly 30% of new stores go to newer markets today, described as a pace expected to "continue," but no acceleration commitment — that's explicitly deferred to the 2026 guide.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No forward marketing/capex commitment.** Conroy: "I'd like to let some more time go before we come out and say we're gonna over-invest betting on the come for future results" — a direct refusal to extrapolate from clearly positive early signals.
- **No 2026 guidance of any kind offered** on comp trajectory, DD's growth pace, or store count acceleration — repeatedly deferred to "the 2026 preview."
- **No loyalty program commitment.** CEO's answer was noncommittal ("I'm not sure about the loyalty program... who knows?") despite having a multi-million-address email database already in hand.
- **No confirmation on how much of the 3-4% Q4 comp guide reflects genuine confidence vs. conservative sandbagging** — Hartshorn insisted it's simply "our internal plan," declining to engage with the analyst's implied question about deliberately under-promising.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Attribution obsession.** Multiple questions pressed on how much of the 500bp acceleration is CEO/company-specific change (marketing, store refresh, branded strategy) versus macro tailwind — management repeatedly declined to fully quantify the split.
- **Durability of the marketing pivot.** Analysts wanted to know what's "sticky" enough to sustain multi-year growth; Conroy's answer leaned on brand equity generally rather than any specific hard metric of retention.
- **Tariff mechanics and margin structure**, including whether merch margin has structurally reset post-branded-strategy and whether distribution deleverage from the new DC persists into 2026 — both got fairly direct, quantified answers.
- **Guidance philosophy** — one analyst explicitly asked whether guiding to 3-4% comp (unusually high in the post-COVID retail context) signals a change in how conservatively Ross plans; management flatly denied any methodology change.
- **Compressed worry:** is this a durable, self-reinforcing inflection from a new management team, or a favorable macro setup and an easy prior-year compare dressed up as company-specific execution — and will management's own caution about spending against it become self-fulfilling if the tailwind fades?
