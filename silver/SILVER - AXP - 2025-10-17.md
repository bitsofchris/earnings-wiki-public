# AXP — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-17)

**The key idea:** This call is entirely about the U.S. Platinum refresh as a self-funded, self-timed margin trade — American Express is deliberately eating an immediate expense hit (benefits cost, marketing) against a card-fee benefit that won't fully show up in earnings for roughly two years, betting that scale (a $530B-a-year franchise) and demographic momentum (Millennials/Gen Z now 36% of spend) justify running the P&L "with our eyes wide open" through the trough.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The refresh is going better than plotted, but the accounting lag is the whole story.** New Platinum account acquisitions are running at "twice the level before the refresh" in the first three weeks, with 500,000+ requests for the new metal card — but Le Caillec is explicit that "it takes roughly two years to fully lap the impact of the refresh on card fees," so near-term optics (decelerating card-fee growth, rising VCE ratio) will look worse before they look better.
2. **Management preemptively defused their own headline number.** Rather than let analysts discover it, Le Caillec volunteered that ~5 points of the 17% "service fees and other revenue" growth came from an $80M one-time gain tied to the Global Business Travel/Carlson Wagonlit merger, then noted growth would still be double-digit without it — an unusually self-auditing disclosure for a company whose bull case rests on organic momentum.
3. **They are directly rebutting the consumer-credit-panic narrative with peer data, not just their own.** Squeri didn't just point to Amex's own stable delinquencies (1.3%) and improving write-offs (1.9%, down sequentially) — he cited that competitor banks' write-offs and delinquencies "all got better" this quarter, using it to push back on "a little bit of noise out there... about defaults."
4. **Retention offers are explicitly being de-emphasized as a lever.** Asked how the company will manage fee-increase pushback, Squeri says retention offers already "play a very [small role]... and I would argue they may play even a smaller role than they have in the past" — the strategy is to make the value proposition self-evidently worth keeping rather than to buy retention.
5. **Younger cohorts are the explicit long-term investment thesis, not a side note.** Millennial/Gen Z card members transact "25% more" than older cohorts and now match Gen X's share of total spend; management's framing is that acquiring them younger means capturing a longer, growing spend curve — the entire marketing and product-refresh cadence is built around this bet.

## Economy & consumer
- **"Relatively stable" is the operative phrase**, repeated across 6-7 quarters, with retail spend up 12% and airline swinging from flat (Q2) to a solid rebound this quarter — Squeri frames this as encouraging but not a signal of forward acceleration: "are we going to see a big accelerant from here? It's not what we're expecting, but we're not also expecting a deceleration."
- **The card base is explicitly non-representative of the broader economy** — Squeri repeats his standard caveat that Amex's premium, high-income cardholder skew means its read on "the consumer" doesn't generalize to the mass market.
- **Credit metrics show no deterioration**: delinquencies flat, write-offs down sequentially to 1.9%, with management noting a "huge gap" versus competitor issuers on write-off rates.
- **Government shutdown impact: none observed** — "this is not our first rodeo," citing a prior 35-day shutdown with no material effect, with hardship-relief programs on standby for affected cardholders.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record revenue, broad-based**: $18.4B, up 11%, EPS up 19% to $4.14, ROE 36% — the strongest headline quarter cited.
- **Platinum refresh demand is exceeding internal expectations**: "the strongest start we've seen for a US platinum Card refresh," with FICO scores of applicants up 15 points post-refresh alongside 2x acquisition volume — better credit quality *and* better volume simultaneously.
- **What's not working: card-fee growth is intentionally moderating.** Net card fees up 17% FX-adjusted, but growth is "moderating as we expected" ahead of an anticipated 2026 inflection — a rare case of a company forecasting its own metric to get worse before better.
- **Small business is inflecting positive but not fully explained**: organic growth "starting to turn around... especially at the small end," attributed partly to lapping large Covid-era transaction cohorts rolling off, with fintech competition acknowledged but not quantified.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The refresh cost structure is inherently front-loaded by design**: benefits are expensed immediately upon customer engagement while card-fee revenue amortizes over 12 months and phases in only at renewal anniversaries — VCE (variable customer engagement expense) already stepped up this quarter on "strong early engagement with... quarterly credits."
- **Marketing spend (~$6B annualized) is governed by ROI discipline, not top-down budget-setting**: Le Caillec describes it as tension between "let's do more" and "let's make sure the dollars work really hard," with investments cut when they don't meet profitability criteria — notably, no dollar figure was given for the incremental marketing behind Platinum specifically.
- **Constraint isn't external (compute, supply, labor) — it's a deliberately engineered timing mismatch** between cost recognition and revenue recognition that management is choosing to run through, backed by confidence in the underlying unit economics ($530B franchise, 37 million merchants, best-in-class spend-per-card multiples).
- **International merchant coverage expansion continues as a capital priority**: nearly 5x merchant growth since 2017, with an explicit "75% live coverage" city-level target and continued Europe focus cited as unfinished work.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: card-fee growth troughs now and inflects upward in 2026 as the refresh lapping completes; VCE ratio rises structurally over time due to premium mix shift. **Action matches belief here** — guidance was raised (9-10% revenue, EPS $15.20-$15.50) precisely because management is confident enough in the multi-year math to eat near-term margin pressure.
- **Belief**: younger cohorts will "grow with them as their careers grow" into higher lifetime spend. **Action**: heavier marketing dollar allocation toward fee-paying, premium products indexed to younger acquirers — a real, funded bet, not just rhetoric, though management explicitly declined to share vintage-level data that would let outsiders verify the payback curve.
- **Gap flagged by management itself**: they say the small-business organic rebound "will stabilize" but offer no concrete driver beyond Covid-cohort lapping — a belief stated with less operational specificity than the Platinum narrative, suggesting less conviction or less data maturity on that segment.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused 2027 expense-pacing guidance**: "predicting what's going to happen in 27 is going to be like really hard" — the two-year lapping story was given in detail up to 2026 but pointedly not extended further.
- **Declined to disclose spend-by-acquisition-vintage data** that would let analysts independently verify the younger-cohort payback thesis, despite being asked twice (Moshe Orenbuch, similar framing from Erika Najarian) — management redirected to qualitative "philosophical" reasoning instead of numbers.
- **Noncommittal on a new card tier between Platinum and Centurion**: Squeri's answer — "if you have any ideas, we're open to those ideas" — treats a direct product-roadmap question as an open floor rather than giving a real answer either way.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The dominant cluster: force management to quantify the Platinum refresh's P&L mechanics** — card-fee timing curves, VCE step-up pacing into 2026/2027, and whether mid-teens EPS aspirations survive the investment period. Management answered qualitatively each time and declined precise multi-year modeling detail.
- **Second cluster: consumer and small-business credit health**, prompted directly by "seemingly one-off headlines" in the market about defaults — management met this head-on with peer-bank data rather than deflecting.
- **Third cluster: competitive/product positioning** — takeaway share from other premium issuers, gold-vs-platinum cannibalization, and fintech competition in small business — all met with "too early to tell" or qualitative confidence rather than hard numbers.
- **Compressed worry**: analysts are trying to determine whether the Platinum refresh's cost is truly front-loaded-but-safe as management insists, or whether the two-year payback promise is a story that can't be checked until the fee increases actually land — and management is choosing not to give them the vintage data that would resolve it now.
