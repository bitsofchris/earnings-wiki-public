# PEP — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-09)

**The key idea:** PepsiCo's affordability push finally turned U.S. salty-snack volume positive after a long slide — but management spent most of the call explaining why the win isn't showing up in profit yet, blaming a gas-price spike for softening convenience-channel traffic. The subtext: international (now near $40B and margin-accretive) is quietly doing the heavy lifting while North America gets "optimized," not reset.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Three-pillar North America playbook** — affordability investment, portfolio transformation (permissible snacks, portion control), and away-from-home expansion — credited with turning salty snacks from a share-losing, volume-negative category back to **share-gaining and volume-positive**, called the "first strategic intent" of the year.
- **International is becoming the real growth engine**: crossing **$40 billion** this year, with international beverage volume now two-thirds of total company volume and international food volume over half. Q2 operating margin there **grew a full point**, and management explicitly denies "starving" it to fund the U.S.: *"We are not starving the international business to fund the U.S. business."*
- **Logistics convergence in Texoma testing**: combined mixing centers, shared fleet, and combined delivery across the foods and beverage businesses — described as scaling with "positive return so far," aimed at funding U.S. growth investment without cutting from international.
- **Bolt-on brand acquisitions as portfolio strategy**: Siete and poppi both had integration hiccups (poppi's distributor-system transition, Siete's ingredient issue) that are now resolved and "growing again at a good pace" — plus the Celsius/Alani Nu-style partnership model as a lower-risk alternative to outright M&A for entering new occasions.
- **World Cup sponsorship** cited as a real demand lever in Europe and Latin America, explicitly credited with accelerating category activation this quarter.

## What's changing
- **"Optimizing ROI" on affordability spend** — corporate-speak for tightening which price investments actually convert to volume, channel by channel, customer by customer, high-low vs. everyday-low-price. CEO Laguarta: *"The system is getting much [more] knowledgeable and much more intelligent in how we get the best return from those investments."*
- **Tariff refund claims** on last year's tariffs will contribute roughly **one full point of EPS growth** for the year — an explicit, acknowledged prop being used to offset new commodity inflation, not a sign of underlying operating improvement.
- **North America advertising spend rising** in the second half year-over-year, framed as "not making decisions that hurt the top line" even as EPS guidance skews to the low end.
- **PBNA margin compressed ~90 basis points**, about half of it from the Alani Nu commercial arrangement, the rest from convenience-channel softness and mix — margin recovery pinned on gas prices coming down, not on company action.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Convenience/gas-channel traffic conversion is broken** and this was explicitly unplanned: management ties it directly to gasoline prices (Iran war impact cited) suppressing impulse purchases, and admits they're only now building bundles/incentives to fix it — a real-time admission the affordability strategy didn't anticipate this failure mode.
- **Volume growth "not as much as we expected"** in Q2 despite the affordability push — attributed partly to a **weaker-than-modeled consumer** and partly to **execution delays** in rolling out price investments with certain customers "for multiple commercial reasons" (unspecified).
- **Away-from-home expansion slowed in Q2** due to supply chain and customer-execution issues — one of the three core growth pillars underdelivering, though management claims it's "accelerating in Q3."
- **No acknowledgment of demand elasticity risk from the reported 10-20% price increase on Frito-Lay single-serve/convenience bags** — when asked directly, Laguarta pivoted to bundling/incentives language rather than addressing whether this contradicts the affordability narrative for the same channel that's already underperforming.
- **Guidance reaffirmed but shaded toward the low end**, propped by tariff refunds and heavier Q4-loaded productivity — a "trust the back half" setup where the improvement is back-loaded and dependent on gas prices normalizing, a factor entirely outside company control.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Flat PFNA volume despite stepped-up affordability and shelf-space investment · North America margin pressure from Alani Nu deal terms and weak convenience/gas channel · whether the U.S. needs a full spending "reset" rather than incremental tweaks · skepticism about whether test-market enthusiasm from last fall is translating to current in-market results · questions on whether PepsiCo is overinvesting in a mature U.S. market versus underinvesting in higher-growth international · the ROI-optimization language read as vague enough to mean either "spend smarter" or "quietly retreat" · underlying worry: that the North America turnaround story is being carried by macro tailwinds (gas prices, tariff refunds) that could reverse, not by durable structural improvement.
