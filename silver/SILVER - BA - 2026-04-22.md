# BA — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-22)

**The key idea:** Boeing is threading a recovery narrative — stabilized 737 production, improving 787 factory metrics, a defense business catching a tailwind from rising budgets and live conflicts — against a backdrop of persistent execution friction (wiring rework, seat certifications, engine shortages, a 777X engine fix requiring retrofit of dozens of built aircraft). The tension: management insists nothing has changed in full-year delivery or cash flow guidance, but nearly every operational update this quarter comes with an asterisk about a supplier or certification delay that "we're working through."

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **737 is genuinely stabilizing, but the next rate break is structurally harder.** Rate held at 42/month with a 20% cut in rework hours; the 42→47 move this summer is described as similar to prior increases, but 47→52 requires standing up an entirely new production line (Everett North) with a new workforce and fresh FAA production-certificate conformity — a different kind of risk than simply running the existing lines faster.
2. **787 deliveries are being held back by paperwork, not the factory.** CEO Ortberg: seat certifications are "getting the pig through the python" — airplanes are built and sitting, waiting on cabin-configuration approval, while the factory itself shows a 25% rework improvement. Full-year 90-100 delivery guidance is unchanged but "I wish it was a little more linear than what it is."
3. **The 777X engine issue is being managed, not solved.** GE has identified root cause on the durability/mid-seal issue and is finalizing a fix, but ~30 already-built aircraft will need a multi-year "change incorporation" retrofit before delivery — a program history that has burned Boeing before, and CFO Malave's careful language ("you're never done until you are done") signals this isn't fully de-risked.
4. **Defense budget growth is treated as low-risk upside because it's mostly volume, not new development.** CEO cites $5B for F-47, $4B for KC-46, $3B for F-15EX, $2B for enhanced SATCOM in the new budget — framed explicitly as funding "additional production of existing systems," the safest kind of growth for a company still recovering credibility on new-program execution.
5. **The $10B long-term free cash flow target is being reframed as a floor, not a ceiling** — CFO explicitly says "significant growth beyond that into the next decade," built on BCA rate increases compounding with higher-priced backlog and cost absorption, BDS's march to high-single-digit margins, and BGS's continued double-digit performance.

## Economy & consumer
- **No demand erosion attributed to the Middle East conflict** — Ortberg: "we have seen no impact so far. No customers are requesting changes in their deliveries," with 4 deliveries completed into the region since the conflict began.
- **Fuel price, not the war directly, is the risk transmission channel** to watch, specifically for commercial aftermarket (MRO) demand — flight-hour activity is called out as the leading indicator to monitor.
- **Defense op-tempo is rising in real time and offsetting concern elsewhere** — Apache, PAC-3, and other platforms are actively supporting live operations, which management frames as a partial hedge against any commercial MRO softness.
- No traditional end-consumer read applies to Boeing; the closest analog (airline flight hours / booking behavior) is not yet showing stress per management.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** 737 production genuinely stable at 42/month with material quality gains (30% defect reduction on wing tip via a shop-floor process fix); BDS delivered 3.1% operating margin (up 60bp) with a record $86B backlog and $9B in quarterly orders; BGS posted double-digit margins in both commercial and government with record $33B backlog and 1.6x book-to-bill.
- **Not working:** BCA operating margin remains negative (-6.1%), and Malave concedes the improvement was "largely due to this onetime benefit" rather than underlying trend; 787 engine supplier deliveries "fell behind a little bit" this quarter, requiring Boeing to forward-deploy its own resources into supplier factories.
- **Spirit AeroSystems integration is going to plan** but still needs "some improvements" — contributed $150M of BDS sales and is credited with helping 737/787 quality gains via biweekly integration reviews.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Everett North line is the marquee capital commitment** — construction complete, tooling in place, hiring and training underway, explicitly designed to enable rate increases beyond 47/month toward 52.
- **787's constraint is supply chain, not factory capacity** — CFO: "we don't enjoy the same buffer we have on 737," so Boeing is forward-deploying its own people into supplier operations (engines, interiors) to force a recovery.
- **777X's constraint is a single supplier fix (GE)** on engine durability, cascading into a multi-year, dedicated-team retrofit ("change incorporation") of already-built airplanes — described as standard practice but scaled to ~30 aircraft this cycle.
- **PAC-3 seeker capacity was directly expanded** via a new framework agreement in Huntsville, a rare case of proactive capacity investment ahead of confirmed demand growth in defense.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** long-term FCF can grow "significantly" beyond the $10B target on BCA rate ramps, BDS margin recovery, and BGS growth. **Action matching it:** concrete rate-increase plans (42→47→52), Everett North build-out, and BDS's "3 Ps" (performance, process, price discipline) underwriting discipline — this is one of the more credible talk/action alignments on the call.
- **Belief:** China orders are "a meaningful opportunity" and "a big number" once a US-China summit-level agreement lands. **Action matching it:** none disclosed — this is pure optionality contingent on geopolitics outside Boeing's control, and CEO declines to give any size or timeline.
- **Belief:** the Middle East conflict poses limited risk to OE deliveries given backlog flexibility. **Action matching it:** management can point to zero deferral requests and completed deliveries, but the "flexibility" claim rests on 2/3 of Middle East backlog delivering in 2030+ — a resequencing capability that hasn't actually been tested by a real disruption yet.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **CEO refuses to size the China order:** "I'm not going to give you the number of airplanes, but it's a big number" — pure directional signaling with no commitment.
- **CFO won't commit to Q2 breakeven on free cash flow,** only characterizing it as an outflow "in the range of, say, low hundreds of millions of dollars," still negative but improved.
- **On BDS margin trajectory, CFO hedges with a range rather than a number:** "it could be a little bit better, kind of think about 3.5% for the year" — directional, not a hard target.
- **Middle East conflict duration is explicitly disclaimed:** "the big question is how long does the war last. And I can't answer that."
