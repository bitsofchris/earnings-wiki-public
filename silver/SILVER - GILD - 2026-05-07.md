# GILD — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-07)

**The key idea:** Gilead spent $11.5B across three acquisitions (Arcellx, Ouro Medicines, Tubulis) in a single quarter to rebuild its pipeline for a post-2036 world, while its current portfolio — led by a surprisingly hot PrEP franchise — is strong enough to absorb the entire cost without denting per-share earnings guidance. The tension: nearly every growth driver being celebrated (YEZTUGO, BIC/LEN, anito-cel) is still pre-revenue-scale or has a 2027+ payoff, while the near-term print is flattered by launches, not by any macro or demand tailwind — this is a company converting a strong balance sheet into optionality against its own patent cliff.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **PrEP is the real story, and it's outrunning management's own guidance.** YEZTUGO sales grew 72% sequentially and 2026 guidance was raised from $800M to $1B mid-quarter, with 95% U.S. coverage at $0 copay and "early signs of growing momentum" in naive (not just switch) patients — a genuine demand story, not a pricing one.
2. **The M&A binge is being financed by the base business, not new debt-fueled growth.** CFO: excluding the $9.50/share hit from acquisition upfronts and financing, "we are effectively maintaining our start of the year non-GAAP EPS guidance" — the $400M revenue beat is functionally paying for the entire Arcellx/Ouro/Tubulis spend this year.
3. **Cell therapy is Gilead's one visibly struggling segment.** Kite sales fell 12% y/y and 11% sequentially from "ongoing in and out of class competition," and the fix (anito-cel) won't generate revenue until "early 2027" even with a December 2026 approval decision — a full-year gap between approval and cash.
4. **Persistency data for the flagship new drugs is thinner than the confidence suggests.** On YEZTUGO second-dose return rates, Johanna Mercier directly conceded "it's not perfect data, I'll be honest with you. I don't have the level of detail" — a rare admission under a barrage of analyst questions on the same topic.
5. **The pipeline reload is explicitly a pre-emptive move against the 2036 BIKTARVY cliff**, even though management repeatedly stresses "no major LOEs until 2036" — the acquisitions read as insurance bought a decade early while the balance sheet allows it.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macroeconomic framing offered at all** — growth and headwinds discussed are entirely policy (Medicaid pricing agreement, ACA changes cutting ~2% off 2026 HIV growth) and access/reimbursement mechanics, not consumer spending or broader economic conditions.
- **PrEP market itself is expanding independent of Gilead's execution** — U.S. PrEP market grew ~14% y/y, providing a rising tide beneath YEZTUGO/DESCOVY performance.
- **Geographic consumer patterns are visible in PrEP uptake**: fastest uptake in already-PrEP-penetrated markets (San Francisco, LA, New York, Florida) via switching, while naive-patient growth is concentrated in high-incidence, under-served areas like the "rural South."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **HIV franchise remains dominant and durable**: $5B in sales, +10% y/y, BIKTARVY holding >52% U.S. share with "year-over-year gains in every quarter since launch."
- **TRODELVY (oncology ADC) accelerating on label expansion momentum**: +37% y/y, driven by NCCN Category 1 recommendations ahead of formal first-line TNBC approval — "spontaneous use" already building ahead of the regulatory decision.
- **LIVDELZI (PBC) more than tripled y/y** and holds >50% U.S. second-line market share, though sequential softness (-11%) reflects a one-time competitor-discontinuation bolus normalizing out.
- **Cell therapy (Kite) is the clear laggard**, down double digits both y/y and sequentially from competitive pressure — the only segment management didn't attempt to spin positively.
- **Product gross margin ticked up to 87%**, aided by the structural expiration of a "long-standing TAF-related royalty obligation," not organic execution.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Three acquisitions in one quarter** ($11.5B combined upfront): Arcellx (closed) for CAR-T/anito-cel and its D-domain binder platform; Tubulis (pending) for ADC technology (TUB-040 in ovarian cancer, next-gen conjugation platform); Ouro Medicines (pending) for a BCMAxCD3 T-cell engager in autoimmune disease.
- **R&D spend rising only "mid-single-digit" percentage** despite absorbing three new clinical programs — management frames this explicitly as "modest and manageable," with room found by "wrapping up a number of phase III trials across our entire portfolio."
- **The binding constraint is regulatory/operational timeline, not capital**: anito-cel needs site activation lag even post-approval; BIC/LEN needs switch-market access build-out before it can move revenue; α4β7 and GS-3242 both show data-readiness (not funding) as the pacing item.
- **Capital returns continued in parallel** — >$1.4B returned to shareholders in Q1 (incl. $400M+ buybacks), ~60% of free cash flow — signaling the M&A spree wasn't cash-constrained.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief**: BIC/LEN can meaningfully capture the ~20% of HIV patients who switch regimens annually. **Action**: guidance explicitly tempers this — "it'll be modest revenues in 2026 with a nice ramp in 2027" — talk and near-term financial commitment are consistent here, unlike anito-cel.
- **Belief**: GS-3242 could deliver twice-yearly HIV dosing. **Action**: still in dose-escalation phase I, with the 2031-2033 launch window called a deliberately "conservative" placeholder — the confident public rhetoric outpaces the disclosed trial design maturity.
