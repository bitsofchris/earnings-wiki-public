# ROST — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-03-03)

**The key idea:** Ross just posted its best quarterly comp in years — 9%, all traffic-driven, dead broad across every category and region — while insisting to analysts, almost defensively, that it didn't spend a dollar more on marketing or store labor to get there. The tension of the call is whether that's a genuinely durable flywheel (new customers, word of mouth, self-funding reinvestment) or a lapping effect against two historically weak quarters that management is being careful not to over-promise into full-year guidance.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Comps inflected hard, not incrementally.** 9% comp growth in Q4, up from low-single-digits over the trailing four quarters, driven by transactions not basket, broad across every merchandise category and region — "it was really broad-based across essentially all merchandise categories, all regions of the country," per CEO Jim Conroy.
2. **Home fully recovered from tariff damage.** The category that was "most under attack with tariffs" and comp-eroding early in the year turned into a real contributor to the Q4 beat — a genuine operational turnaround story, not just a comp lap.
3. **Management insists the growth was self-funded, then contradicts itself on why.** Conroy repeatedly says marketing and store labor stayed flat as a rate of sales ("we don't intend to sort of buy the comp"), yet also credits the new marketing campaign and in-store operational changes as drivers of the inflection — those two claims sit in tension.
4. **Guidance embeds real conservatism.** Full-year comp guide of 3-4% is a steep step-down from the 7-8% Q1 guide, and management explicitly declined to bake in tax-refund tailwinds ("still early... we'll have to wait and see") despite refunds running 7% ahead of last year.
5. **Store growth is reaccelerating opportunistically.** dd's openings more than double (10→25) partly riding a Rite Aid bankruptcy real-estate pickup, and new-store productivity is running unusually high — "one of our best years in a while in terms of new store productivity" — which is now feeding back into a higher long-term unit-growth appetite (2,900 Ross / 700 dd's targets reaffirmed).

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro red flags, but explicit macro caution remains in the guide language.** Conroy cites "a lot going on in the macro environment right now" as a reason to stay conservative on H2, without specifying what.
- **Tax refunds are a wildcard management won't lean on.** Refunds are running "up about 7%" early in the season per Treasury data, but with two-thirds still to flow, management built nothing into guidance.
- **Customer base broadened, not shifted.** Growth spans income levels, age cohorts (including 18-34), and ethnicities; no rotation toward branded-seeking or value-seeking behavior detected — "an overarching rising tide... for essentially all customers."
- **Share is coming from mainstream retail, not off-price peers.** "The bigger forfeiture of market share is coming from mainstream retail... department stores;" a competitor's own strong quarter argues against meaningfully cannibalizing off-price rivals.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Every major category positive; center-core (shoes, cosmetics) led.** "Cosmetics and shoes... very nicely growing businesses for us in both Q3 and Q4."
- **Home's turnaround is the standout recovery story.** Started the year "kind of difficult" under tariff pressure, ended the year contributing to the Q4 comp beat; still "slightly comp eroding" per COO Hartshorn but no longer a drag.
- **New store productivity running well above plan.** Guidance embeds 70-75% first-year productivity for new stores, feeding an above-normal 3-4% growth contribution to the long-term algorithm.
- **Packaway inventory ratio declined (41%→37% of total).** Consolidated inventory up 8%, below sales growth — management deliberately pushed more inventory to stores versus DCs to "chase sales" through the holiday, calling this an opportunity for further shift, not a one-off.
- **Q1 margin will contract year-over-year** (11.8-12.1% vs. 12.2% prior year) despite the strongest comp guide in years — driven by unlapped new-DC costs, packaway expense timing, and a higher incentive-comp base after a weak prior-year Q1.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Store growth reaccelerating across both banners.** 110 new stores (5% growth) planned for 2026 vs. ~90 in 2025; dd's specifically more than doubles its opening pace (10→25), aided by a Rite Aid bankruptcy real-estate pipeline.
- **Capex ($1.1B) split roughly evenly across growth and maintenance.** About a third each to distribution centers and new stores, 25% to store maintenance, the balance to technology/merchandising tools — no single line item dominates.
- **No binding resource constraint identified.** The recurring message is that outsized comp growth arrived without needing "artificial" or "unnecessary" investment in marketing, labor, or capex — the constraint, if any, is self-imposed guidance discipline rather than compute/labor/capital scarcity.
- **Repurchase program expanded 21%** ($2.55B over two years vs. prior $2.1B) plus a 10% dividend increase — capital return scaling with confidence, not funding growth trade-offs.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Stated belief: this could be a genuine flywheel.** Conroy explicitly floats that new customers convert, return, spread word of mouth, and "you can start spinning that proverbial flywheel" — a structurally higher growth story, not just a comp lap.
- **Actual commitment: full-year guide assumes deceleration to 3-4%.** Management is not underwriting the flywheel thesis with the balance sheet or opex plan — this is the clearest talk/action gap on the call. As Conroy admits, "it's just so early... we've had 2 nice quarters, but the 2-quarter part that weren't so great."
- **Small, reversible experiments only.** Slight marketing spend increase, expanded self-checkout pilot, targeted store-labor test-and-learn — all framed as optional levers to pull later "if we can find ROI," not scaled bets.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to embed tax-refund tailwind in guidance** despite favorable early data, citing insufficient visibility with two-thirds of the season remaining.
- **Would not disclose dd's category mix** (kids vs. home vs. other) — "we tend to not want to disclose sort of with such specificity the size of each of our businesses."
- **Could not (or would not) characterize new-customer composition** — new vs. lapsed, same customer on a different card — citing genuinely ambiguous card-level data, not just reticence.
- **Reasserted "conservative" framing repeatedly** even while delivering the strongest comp guide in years, deflecting the implication that confidence has structurally increased.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Sustainability of the comp inflection dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pushed on whether 7-8% Q1 guidance reflects genuine share capture or easy two-year-stack comparisons; management's answer split the difference (acknowledged easy compares, but personally leaned toward the "virtuous cycle" narrative).
- **Margin flow-through skepticism** — given the best comp guide in years, why isn't operating margin expanding more in Q1 (11.8-12.1% vs. 12.2% prior year)? Answer: unlapped DC costs, packaway timing, and incentive-comp base effects, "pretty evenly split."
- **Market-share source** (off-price peers vs. mainstream retail) got a direct, non-dodged answer: mainstream/department stores, not other off-price chains.
- **AUR and pricing power** — asked whether the branded strategy allows retail-price increases; CEO conceded "we probably have gained some confidence" to nudge AUR up selectively, a notable tell for a company whose brand is built on value positioning.
- The compressed worry underneath all of it: **is this a new, higher-growth Ross, or the same conservative Ross benefiting from an unusually easy comparison base that reverts once lapped?**
