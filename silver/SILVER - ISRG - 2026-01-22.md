# ISRG — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-22)

**The key idea:** A blowout 2025 (21% revenue growth, 37% operating margin, third straight year of 20%+ EPS growth) gets capped with 2026 guidance for meaningfully slower procedure growth (13-15% vs. 18%), built on a list of risks management says they haven't actually observed yet — ACA subsidy changes, Medicaid cuts, GLP-1 drugs, Japan and Europe capital constraints. The tension: is this prudent guardrails from a company that's earned credibility, or an early tell that the growth machine is decelerating structurally as it scales past 11,100 installed systems and 20 million lifetime patients.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Guidance steps down deliberately, not reactively.** 2026 procedure growth guided to 13-15% versus 2025's actual 18%, and CFO Jamie Samath was explicit that Q4 showed "no evidence either way" of the ACA/Medicaid risks baked into that lower range — this is precautionary guidance, not a response to observed weakness.
2. **da Vinci 5 is still margin-dilutive.** Gross margin fell 170bps y/y to 67.8% in Q4 as the newer, more capable platform hasn't reached target product cost, compounding with 95bps of tariff drag and rising facility depreciation from recent capacity expansion.
3. **Cardiac surgery is the next big disease-state bet, quietly sized at ~160K procedures/year TAM** in currently-cleared geographies (U.S., Korea) versus a global cardiac base of just 17,000 procedures today on older Si/Xi platforms — a "multiyear" build requiring new instrumentation, Force Feedback expansion, and surgeon training pathways from scratch.
4. **The ASC and refurbished-hardware pivot is a real strategic shift.** Intuitive is deliberately routing trade-in Xi systems into a new XiR refurbished line specifically to chase the ambulatory surgery center opportunity, acknowledging ASCs run lower reimbursement and likely lower ASPs — a self-cannibalizing move to defend share as procedures migrate out of hospitals.
5. **China has turned from opportunity to fight.** Local robotic competitors — many architecturally similar to Xi — are winning share on price in provincial tenders; ISRG's Q4 win ratio dropped even as they're manufacturing locally to compete.

## Economy & consumer

- **No observed macro hit yet, but guidance hedges hard.** CFO: "we haven't heard that from customers at all" regarding ACA subsidy or Medicaid effects through Q4, yet 2026 guidance explicitly discounts for exactly those risks alongside European macro capital pressure and "shifting governmental priorities."
- **GLP-1/obesity drugs named as a live risk for the first time this prominently** in guidance language, even as bariatric procedure mix has already been a drag on I&A revenue per procedure for several quarters.
- **Patient demand shows no slowdown**: 3.1 million patients treated in 2025, "line of sight" addressable procedures grew from 7M (2024) to 9M (2026) — expanding faster than the installed base itself.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Multiplatform growth is broad and real**: da Vinci procedures +18% globally, SP +87%, Ion +51%, combined total procedure growth of 19% for the year — not reliant on one platform.
- **U.S. acute-care penetration deepening**: after-hours procedures (a proxy for acute/emergency use) grew 35% in Q4, "primarily driven by cholecystectomy and appendectomy."
- **Gross margin under real pressure**: fell to 67.8% from 69.5% a year ago, driven by tariffs (95bps), facility cost inflation, and mix shift toward lower-margin da Vinci 5 and Ion revenue — only partly offset by cost-reduction programs.
- **Japan and China are the two soft spots**: Japan capital placements have lagged "over the last several quarters" amid government budget constraints (with a pending reimbursement decision due June 2026), while China's win ratio fell in Q4 as local competitors undercut on price.
- **da Vinci I&A revenue per procedure actually declined** slightly ($1,850 vs. $1,860 y/y) — a rare metric moving the wrong direction, attributed to "customer ordering patterns" and a mix shift toward lower-acuity cholecystectomy over higher-value bariatric cases.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Cardiac surgery is the marquee new bet**: FDA clearance for non-Force-Feedback cardiac instruments on da Vinci 5 this month, with Force Feedback cardiac instruments, training pathways, and surgical-society partnerships all described as "multiyear efforts" — a slow, deliberate build rather than a quick monetization play.
- **XiR refurbished systems as a second product line**: 42 units placed in 2025, explicitly positioned to expand access in ASCs and international markets at a lower price point than new Xi — "we believe there is a sizable long-term opportunity."
- **Going direct in Italy, Spain, Portugal** by end of Q1 2026, absorbing ~250 employees — a distribution-control investment that adds to OpEx growth.
- **Binding constraint isn't demand, it's cost structure and competitive pricing.** da Vinci 5 isn't at target product cost yet, tariffs are rising to 120bps of 2026 revenue (from 65bps in 2025), and China pricing intensity is compressing win rates — all cost-side or competitive constraints, not capacity or demand shortfalls.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief**: management repeats "early stages of this journey" despite 20M+ lifetime patients and a maturing installed base of 11,100+ systems. **Action**: capital and R&D are flowing into genuinely new territory (cardiac, advanced imaging, ASC-specific hardware) rather than just scaling the existing playbook — the belief and the spend are actually aligned here, unlike a typical hedge.
- **Belief**: ACA subsidy changes and Medicaid cuts could pressure U.S. procedure volumes. **Action**: no concrete mitigation described beyond building it into the guidance range — this is a risk they're pricing in, not one they're actively defending against.
- **Belief**: ASCs are a "multiyear effort" and a real structural shift in where procedures happen. **Action**: matched with real infrastructure — the XiR refurbished pipeline, economic programs tailored to ASC reimbursement levels, and explicit targeting of the ~70% of ASC opportunity affiliated with existing IDN customers.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No 2026 utilization or system ASP guidance.** Jamie Samath explicitly declined: "I'm not going to predict what the overall '26 direction will be," citing offsetting mix dynamics between da Vinci 5, XiR, and trade-ins that they'll "let you model."
- **No January trend commentary** despite a direct question about early-quarter dynamics — management gave Q4 color but stayed silent on anything more current.
- **No capital expenditure guidance for 2026** at all — they simply stopped providing it, citing an expectation that "capital expenditures will return to more normalized levels."
- **No XiR price point disclosed** — acknowledged it will be "quite a bit below" new Xi pricing but declined to give a number.
