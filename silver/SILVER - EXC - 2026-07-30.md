# EXC — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-30)

**The key idea:** Exelon's operating story is boring and clean — top-quartile reliability, in-line EPS, reaffirmed guidance — but the call is really about a regional power system running out of headroom. PJM's July heat event, a record 168 GW peak, and a third straight capacity auction clearing at the FERC price cap are used to argue that "cost discipline alone" can't fix affordability; only new supply can. The unresolved tension: does Exelon's shrinking data-center pipeline (43 GW to 36 GW) reflect disciplined demand-vetting, as management insists, or the first sign that PJM's dysfunction is costing them real growth?

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The grid is the story, not the balance sheet.** Calvin Butler spent most of prepared remarks on PJM stress — a tenfold price spike to $800/MWh, a record 168 GW peak, and a capacity auction shortfall of 6.8 GW (worse than the prior year's 6.5 GW) — arguing this "is not a one-off event." PJM's own simulation shows prices would have cleared near $777/MWh-day in ComEd absent the FERC price cap, implying scarcity is worse than headline numbers show.
2. **Data-center pipeline shrank 11 GW and management insists that's good news.** The data-center growth slide dropped from 43 GW to 36 GW; Jones frames this entirely as Transmission Security Agreements "weeding out speculative projects" and says the $41B capital plan is "unchanged due to this update because we have not put in speculative projects." Of the 11 GW increment, only 4 GW carry signed TSAs backed by $1B of collateral — the rest is pre-TSA-era projects management is "comfortable" will proceed.
3. **Storage is being positioned as the fast answer to a slow-moving supply problem.** The 500-MW ACE battery project in New Jersey (~$1B investment, largest in PJM) is pitched as deployable in ~3 years versus 5-10+ for traditional generation, with over $700 million in projected net customer benefits and no bill impact until at least 2035 — a specific, quantified counter to the "affordability requires new supply" argument.
4. **Affordability is now an explicit tradeoff being negotiated with regulators, not an abstraction.** BGE delayed its rate filing and deferred select projects to manage the sticker shock of a $156.1M revenue-requirement ask; Butler warns bluntly that deferred maintenance "customers ultimately pay the price through more outages, more costly repairs, and higher long-term costs" — a rare admission that the affordability optics are being actively managed.
5. **Pennsylvania regulatory risk lingers unresolved.** The PECO rate case was withdrawn months ago and has not been refiled; management describes "constructive" conversations with Governor Shapiro's office but offered no concrete refiling trigger, leaning on the DISC mechanism and RISE grants as interim affordability levers instead.

## Economy & consumer
- **PJM ran out of margin in July.** Record demand of 168 GW forced emergency procedures and demand-response activation, with prices surging tenfold from ~$80 to ~$800/MWh — offered as the clearest real-time evidence that regional power demand (data centers, electrification, weather) is outrunning supply.
- **Third straight price-capped capacity auction.** The reliability shortfall grew to ~6.8 GW (from 6.5 GW), "the equivalent of roughly seven nuclear reactors of missing supply," with only ~525 MW of new generation/uprates clearing even at the price cap — a signal that current market prices, however high, aren't attracting new supply.
- **Affordability is the explicit political frame**, not consumer spending — Exelon ties customer protection to structuring large-load agreements with "real financial commitments" so growth doesn't burden existing ratepayers, and frames BGE's deferred filing as a deliberate affordability accommodation.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Reliability metrics remain genuinely strong**: all utilities projecting top-quartile reliability, ComEd and PHI in the top decile, and an estimated $1 billion in avoided outage costs for customers in 2025 from top-quartile performance.
- **Weather stress is escalating operationally**: ComEd logged 16 major weather events this year — "more than it's seen in over two decades" — and a single Monday storm affected ~530,000 customers, with 90% restored within 48 hours.
- **EPS in line, guidance reaffirmed**: $0.43/share vs $0.39 a year ago, full-year $2.81-$2.91 guidance unchanged, with Q3 expected at ~27% of full-year midpoint (weighed down by weather, storms, and a PECO employee strike in early July).
- **Financing is de-risked for the year**: ~86% of 2026 debt needs completed and ~37% of equity needs through 2029 already priced via forward contracts, insulating the plan from near-term rate volatility.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The stated binding constraint is generation supply, not capital or people**: Butler is explicit that "affordability cannot be solved through cost discipline alone. It also requires new supply" — an unusual admission from a regulated utility that the fix is largely outside its direct control.
- **Capital is being redirected toward speed, not just scale**: the 500-MW ACE battery project (~3-year build) is pitched against transmission and generation alternatives that take "5 to 10 years or longer," with ~10 GW of PJM solar/wind currently going unused daily that storage could capture.
- **Transmission remains the largest lever**: the $12-17B "offset opportunity" spans five themes (existing infrastructure, data-center-driven new business, generation retirements/additions, and competitive transmission), with two new MISO Tranche 2.1 bids filed in Iowa alongside Invenergy, decision expected Q4.
- **The $41B four-year capital plan is held flat** despite the data-center pipeline shrinking by 11 GW — management's framing is that this proves discipline (no speculative capital was ever included), though it also means no incremental upside was captured from the "high probability" 7 GW of legacy pipeline projects.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: PJM's capacity-market fixes (the walked-back EDC proposal) "may help address near-term reliability concerns, but they're unlikely to resolve any long-term affordability challenges" — management believes only new generation, much of it utility-owned, actually solves the problem.
- **Action**: Exelon isn't waiting on PJM/FERC — it's filing state-level generation and storage proposals (ACE battery, BGE/Pepco Maryland storage solicitation bids, ComEd VPP program) and pushing states toward independent resource-adequacy planning (Illinois IRP process, Pennsylvania's independent consultant study, Maryland procurement studies).
- **Gap flagged explicitly by an analyst**: is the data-center demand really "real and durable" as Jones insists, or is the 11 GW pipeline reduction partly PJM-dysfunction-driven attrition? Management's answer stayed entirely in the "we filtered for quality" register and didn't directly engage the attrition hypothesis.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No refiling timeline for the PECO rate case** — months after withdrawal, management describes only "constructive" dialogue with the Governor's office and PUC, with no stated trigger or date for resubmission.
- **No commitment on competitive-transmission "win rate"** — when asked directly about win-rate expectations for upcoming MISO/PJM windows, management pointed to track record and 765kV expertise rather than giving a number.
- **Declined to validate or refute the attrition read on the data-center pipeline** — reframed every question about the 43→36 GW drop as evidence of disciplined vetting, without addressing whether PJM dysfunction is actually slowing conversions.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **PJM capacity-market reform and whether Exelon will escalate to FERC** — the market wants to know if Exelon sees the recent PJM proposal as sufficient or a stopgap; management called it a "step in the right direction" but insufficient for long-term affordability, implying continued advocacy/intervention.
- **The data-center pipeline haircut (43 GW to 36 GW)** — the most pointed line of questioning, explicitly asking whether this is reclassification or attrition tied to PJM's dysfunction; management held firm that it's disciplined filtering, not demand loss.
- **Pennsylvania regulatory path and capex composition post-PECO-withdrawal** — analysts wanted specifics on whether future PECO spend skews toward pure safety/reliability or also economic-development-linked investment; answer stayed general ("customer value," "transparency on ROE").
- **Overall plan conservatism given multiple adverse regulatory "paper cuts"** — Butler pushed back hard on the framing itself, arguing platform diversification (not conservatism) is what protects the plan from any single jurisdiction's outcome.
- Compressed worry: **is Exelon's "we only build what's real" story hiding early cracks in AI-driven demand, or is it genuinely insulating the plan from a slower-growing pipeline?**
