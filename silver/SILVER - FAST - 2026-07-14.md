# FAST — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-14)

**The key idea:** Dan Florness's final call as CEO after 30 years, handed off amid genuinely strong execution — double-digit growth, share gains, record ROIC — but the quarter's real tension is a gross margin fight the company is candidly still losing: a 40bps net price-cost headwind is eating incremental margins that management insists should be mid-20s and instead printed in the low-20s.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Key account strategy is the whole growth story now.** Sites spending $50K+/month grew faster in revenue (26%+) than in count (16.5%), and contract count rose 7% — Fastenal is explicitly optimizing for fewer, bigger, deeper customers even though those accounts carry structurally lower gross margin.
- **FMI (managed inventory devices) is the labor-efficiency engine.** 109 weighted devices signed per day, now 44.6% of total sales; leadership frames every device install as "a deposit into next quarter's sales" and next year's retention.
- **International expansion is shifting from build to buy.** Incoming CEO Jeff Watts wants to compress the "10 years to build a supply chain" timeline to "two to three, four maybe" via M&A, aiming to give global manufacturers the same FMI/digital platform in Romania or Italy that they get in Chicago — a capability Fastenal says no competitor currently matches globally.
- **AI is being used quietly for internal productivity, not marketed.** Florness: "A lot of companies are talking about AI. We don't talk a lot about it. We just do a bunch of things behind the scenes." Faster quoting tools are explicitly credited for turning on large accounts faster and holding headcount growth well below sales growth — framed as a 5-10% productivity question weighed against a $1.6B annual labor cost base.
- **Dividend raised to signal confidence, not to set a new payout trajectory.** Florness pushed the payout to $26.25 to reach a full $1/share for the year, calling it a two-time-lucky ask of the CFO and explicitly warning: "Don't read anything more into it than that."

## What's changing
- **Leadership transition, but explicitly not a strategy transition.** Watts insists the three pillars are unchanged and reframes the "Florness era" as a "Blue Team effort" rather than a personality-driven one — a deliberate continuity signal to the Street.
- **Digital Footprint's share of sales is growing slower than plotted, but for a good reason.** 2026 guide cut to 63-64% of sales from an original 66% target — not because digital adoption slowed, but because non-digital (larger, newer) customer sites are being added even faster, diluting the denominator.
- **June "shocked" the company with one-off orders it doesn't fully understand yet.** Watts admits June sales growth accelerated to 20% partly from one-time orders won only because internal tooling let them turn on new business faster than planned — a positive surprise, but one management can't yet distinguish from a step-change versus a pull-forward.
- **Price-cost negative position narrowed only 10bps, from -50bps to -40bps.** Pricing realized 2.9% in-quarter (4.5% stacked), but management is explicit that further tariff/inflation pass-through will take multiple quarters more to close.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Incremental margins missed the target management set for itself.** Florness volunteered, unprompted, "I would have felt a hell of a lot better about the quarter if our incremental margin would have been 24%" — it came in at 21.5%, well under the mid-20s the company has repeatedly called its "commitment."
- **Gross margin contracted ~75bps year-over-year**, with price-cost (~40bps), customer mix, transportation cost, and rebates all working against it simultaneously — management's own framing is "fighting and clawing our way back" rather than resolved.
- **No firm date for reaching price-cost neutrality.** Repeatedly asked, management would only commit to "chipping away" and explicitly declined to promise resolution even by Q4: "Give us some time... it'll be small on this net price cost position."
- **Discretionary consumer-linked end markets continued to lag** while manufacturing and construction carried the quarter — a bifurcation management acknowledged but didn't quantify.
- **SG&A "de-leverage" concerns were really just bonus math**, but the confusion itself is notable: analysts had to press twice before management clarified SG&A actually levered 90bps, with fuel and bonus payouts being separate, smaller headwinds — a sign the messaging wasn't clean the first time through.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Whether 15%+ growth can coexist with 24%+ incrementals or whether that promise keeps sliding a quarter at a time · pressure on management to explain SG&A behavior line-by-line (fuel vs. bonus vs. travel) rather than accept the aggregate leverage number · repeated attempts to pin a date on price-cost neutrality, rebuffed each time · curiosity about whether Fastenal should just prioritize price over volume given clear demand strength, which management deflected with a "we love growth, but incrementals matter" answer rather than a real trade-off. The underlying worry: is this margin drag a temporary lapping issue that clears in a couple quarters, or a structural cost of the large-account strategy the company will keep re-explaining every call.
