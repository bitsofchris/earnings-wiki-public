# ROP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-27)

**The key idea:** Roper beat on EPS and margin but missed organic growth for the second straight quarter, and management's answer is a maximally conservative 2026 guide that assumes zero improvement in any of the three problem areas (Deltek GovCon, DAT freight, Neptune) plus a fresh $500M+ buyback because they think the stock overreacted. The tension: is this three unrelated, genuinely fixable execution misses (their story), or early evidence the growth algorithm itself is decelerating (the question every analyst circled back to)?

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Two misses in a row, and management owns it without deflecting.** "Organic growth this past year was below our expectations for 2025, and we own that," Neil Hunn said upfront — full-year organic came in near 5.5% against a higher initial target, with the miss traced almost entirely to three names: Deltek (GovCon shutdown), Neptune (tariff/copper surcharge fallout), and ProCare (implementation bottleneck).
2. **The buyback is a valuation call, not an M&A-thesis retreat.** Roper bought 1.1M shares for $500M in Q4 at ~$446, and by the call the stock was trading ~15% below that print — Hunn called the pipeline-is-weakening read "absurd" and reaffirmed Roper as "the preferred buyer" for PE-owned vertical software assets facing mounting LP liquidity pressure.
3. **Refusing to AI-wash revenue is a deliberate positioning choice.** "We're not gonna say x dollars R&D's, therefore, y dollars of revenue is AI-related," Hunn said, contrasting Roper's stance with peers — instead they hired a two-person AI accelerator team (Shane Luke, Eddie Raphael) to coach 21 software businesses and expect AI to show up first in bookings, not a disclosed metric.
4. **ProCare's problem is self-diagnosed as sequencing, not competition.** "We're winning a majority of the jump balls versus the primary competitor... we're slow to the software, which means we're slow to implement the payments" — a solvable operational lag, in Hunn's framing, versus Deltek's exposure to genuinely exogenous GovCon policy disruption.
5. **2026 guidance is engineered to be beatable.** CFO Jason Conley laid out the bridge explicitly: no Deltek recovery, no DAT market recovery, an assumed Neptune decline, with growth acceleration coming almost entirely from Central Reach and SubSplash turning organic in H2 — "optically, it looks like an acceleration through the year, but... it's actually pretty steady."

## Economy & consumer
- **GovCon disruption was real and specific.** The extended government shutdown delayed signature-stage deals ("two large contractor government contractor deals... slipped" at the finish line) and depressed Deltek's Q4 perpetual-license revenue, though management says commercial pipeline build "has actually been encouraging" throughout.
- **Freight recession persisted all of 2025** at DAT with no assumed recovery baked into 2026 guidance — DAT's growth strategy is explicitly built to work regardless of the freight cycle via ARPU expansion and network automation rather than volume recovery.
- **No traditional consumer exposure** — Roper's end markets are enterprise buyers (legal, insurance, healthcare ABA, utilities, freight, GovCon), so no read-through on household spending.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Recurring revenue is holding up broadly** — Application Software recurring grew 6% and Network Software recurring grew 6% organically, both steady vs. prior quarters, even as nonrecurring/perpetual revenue dragged the headline lower.
- **Deltek's perpetual-license mix is the single biggest swing factor** — nonrecurring revenue in Application Software fell 8% in the quarter almost entirely on Deltek, whose SaaS bookings were actually strong even as perpetual collapsed; full-year Deltek growth fell to "the lower end of mid-single digits" versus its decade-long mid-single-digit-plus trend.
- **ProCare's implementation backlog is explicitly called out as fixable, not competitive** — leadership was fully replaced and payments infrastructure rebuilt in 2025; the remaining gap is speed from deal-win to software/payments go-live.
- **NDI and Verathon are genuine bright spots** in Test & Measurement — NDI benefiting from cardiac ablation demand and OEM program ramps, Verathon taking share as US market leader in single-use bronchoscopes.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$3.3B in 2025 M&A** anchored by Central Reach and SubSplash, plus DAT bolt-ons aimed at automating spot-freight workflow — Central Reach is "off to an outstanding start... ahead of our deal model," while SubSplash is tracking to plan.
- **$6B+ capacity for 2026**, split between M&A and opportunistic buybacks, with net leverage at a comfortable 2.9x and $2.7B of revolver availability.
- **The real constraint is AI talent depth inside the business units, not capital.** "We gotta build these people. It's not something that we're gonna be able to hire en masse," Hunn said of scaling AI development capability across the portfolio — a meaningfully different scarcity story than the compute/power binds dominating hyperscaler calls.
- **60% of the bolt-on pipeline is now proprietary/founder-driven**, a motion Hunn says "would not have been the case three years ago," reflecting three years of deliberate relationship-building investment in the tuck-in acquisition function.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: long-term portfolio-entitled growth remains north of 8%, unchanged.** Action: 2026 guidance is set at 5-6% organic with explicit non-improvement assumptions baked in for the three problem businesses — the gap between the long-term belief and the near-term guide is deliberate, not a reflection of eroded confidence.
- **Belief: AI is a TAM expander that will show up first in bookings, then recurring revenue.** Action: centralized AI accelerator team hired and already delivered a constructive readout to the board, but the guide "does not assume a meaningful revenue uplift from our AI development work" — the money (a small central team, not a large investment) doesn't yet match the stated magnitude of the opportunity.
- **Belief: the stock's valuation dislocation is "silly."** Action: $500M actually deployed to buybacks in Q4, with more capacity reserved — this is the one place talk and action are fully aligned, since the buyback size directly reflects the conviction stated.
- **Belief: DAT's automated load-matching technology "unambiguously... does the job."** Action: still described as "early days," with the real 2026 work being network-side integration (TMS partnerships, carrier trust/fraud tooling) rather than scaling the automation itself — the tech claim is ahead of the commercial rollout.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No disclosed AI revenue metric, on principle.** Management explicitly declined to adopt an "AI SKU revenue" disclosure standard used by peers, calling it revenue allocation gaming they won't do — even though they concede investors want the number.
- **No 2026 M&A dollar target.** "It's hard to set an expectation that says we're gonna get x dollars deployed" — capital deployment is described as opportunistic and quality-gated, not budgeted.
- **Declined to quantify guidance upside magnitude** when directly asked for "guardrails" on how much better 2026 could look if Deltek/DAT/Neptune all improve — Hunn said only that guidance "tilts more conservative" than in past years without a number.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Deltek GovCon exposure and shutdown risk protection** dominated early questions — analysts wanted specifics on which appropriations bills matter most and whether December/January order volumes showed early recovery signs.
- **The two-consecutive-quarter miss and guidance credibility** was raised directly and pointedly ("this is the second quarter in a row you've missed expectations... help us understand where the incremental conservatism is"), forcing management to walk through the Deltek/Neptune/ProCare reconciliation in detail.
- **AI monetization quantification** came up from multiple analysts wanting a path to an attributable revenue number, and again probing whether the new AI leadership hires had surfaced any negative findings about parts of the portfolio being weaker than assumed (management said no material market-opportunity concerns surfaced, only a talent-depth gap).
- **Capital allocation mix and timing** (M&A vs. buyback, dollar amounts, valuation dislocation reasoning) recurred across at least three separate questioners, suggesting the buyback introduction genuinely unsettled the M&A-compounder narrative among investors despite management's pushback.
- Compressed worry: *are Deltek, DAT, and Neptune really three isolated, fixable problems — or the first visible cracks in Roper's compounding algorithm?*
