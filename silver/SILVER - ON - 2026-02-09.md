# ON — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-09)

**The key idea:** ON is threading a message of "stabilization plus a genuine AI data center growth leg" against a backdrop where core automotive/industrial demand still isn't restocking and roughly $300M of 2026 revenue is being deliberately walked away from. The tension: management insists the underlying (ex-exits) business is growing above seasonal and gaining structural margin, but nearly every forward number offered — AI mix, margin recovery timing, restocking — comes wrapped in "when, not if" language rather than commitments.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI data center is real but still small and un-guided.** $250M+ in 2025 from "almost nothing," Q1 guided up high-teens sequentially, and a new slide shows content-per-rack roughly doubling by 2030 vs. the prior deck — yet Hassane El-Khoury refuses to give a 2026 number or a 10-15%-of-revenue timeline, repeatedly falling back to "it's a matter of the when, not the if."
2. **Margin expansion is being manufactured through cost actions, not demand.** Thad Trent lays out an explicit 700bps underutilization bridge (needs ~25% more revenue at current mix to fully absorb), plus 200bps from further FabRite, 200bps from prior fab divestitures, and 200bps+ from new higher-margin products — a bottoms-up path to the long-stated 53% gross margin target that depends on utilization climbing from ~68-70% into the low 90s.
3. **Automotive restocking is conspicuously absent.** Despite inventory data points (Experian, "DVR four") that would typically catalyze a tier-1 restock, El-Khoury says flatly "we're not seeing the restocking" and attributes it to tier-1s being too thin-margin to fund the capital — a dynamic he calls a setup for "a risky ramp when the demand does pick up."
4. **The exits are inflating the growth story's optics.** Q1 revenue guide is "in line with seasonality," but excluding ~$50M of Q1 non-core exits (ramping toward ~$300M for the year) the company claims to be running above seasonal — a framing analysts pushed back on twice, wanting to know if the underlying number is durable.
5. **GaN strategy is now fully articulated as a two-track, partnered model.** Lateral GaN devices are outsourced to Innoscience and GlobalFoundries and paired with ON's in-house Treo driver/control silicon to sell as an ON-branded "smart switch," while vertical GaN (GaN-on-GaN, built in ON's own US fab) is reserved for the highest-voltage, highest-margin applications — including a named EV drive-system collaboration with GM, with first revenue not until 2027.

## Economy & consumer
- **Stabilization, not recovery, is the operative word.** "We are seeing seasonal patterns and are encouraged by improving order trends" — automotive inventory digestion "largely behind us," industrial has "seen the bottom" per global PMI signals, but neither market is actually restocking.
- **Book-to-bill and expedites are directionally improving.** El-Khoury cites rising book-to-bill, fewer turns needed entering the quarter, and more customer expedites than 90 days ago as the real-time KPIs behind the improved-visibility claim — but explicitly caveats "we're not seeing the replenishment yet."
- **No direct consumer read** — ON is two steps removed from end demand (auto OEMs/tier-1s, industrial equipment makers), so no commentary on consumer spending strength or weakness surfaces on this call.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Industrial's first YoY growth in eight quarters (+6%) is a real inflection**, driven by traditional industrial and factory automation, alongside a 9% sequential jump in the Intelligent Sensing Group.
- **Aerospace, defense, and security revenue jumped 70% YoY**, driven by North America and Europe, including a solid-state circuit breaker design win using SiC JFET — a genuinely new growth vector, small in absolute terms but notably strong.
- **PSG and AMG (the two largest business units) both fell double digits YoY** (PSG -11%, AMG -9%), showing the "stabilization" story is uneven — legacy power and analog businesses are still shrinking even as new franchises (Treo, AI data center, aerospace) grow.
- **The "Other" segment, which houses AI data center, fell 14% sequentially** in Q4 due to seasonality and non-AI softness — a reminder that AI growth is currently riding inside a bucket that's still net-declining.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex keeps shrinking even as margin investment continues** — Q1 2026 guided to $35-45M (vs. $69M in Q4, 4.5% of revenue), reflecting that "large capacity investments are behind us"; capital is now directed at product development (30+ new GaN parts, VCORE integration) rather than fab expansion.
- **Explicit refusal to build new capacity for AI-driven silicon carbide substrate demand** — asked about converting furnaces to 300mm for SiC substrates, El-Khoury said flatly "we are not gonna be converting furnaces to 300 millimeter... you're not gonna see a CapEx cycle going to the furnaces or substrate manufacturing."
- **The binding constraint is demand/capital on the customer side, not ON's supply** — tier-1 automotive customers can't fund restocking; ON itself says "we have the capacity," so scarcity here is external and financial, not physical.
- **Free cash flow is being fully recycled to shareholders**, not reinvested — record 24% FCF margin in 2025, ~100% of FCF returned via buybacks ($1.4B in 2025, new $6B authorization), signaling confidence in the balance sheet but also that management sees better use of cash in buybacks than in organic capacity right now.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes:** AI data center will keep growing "at a pretty good rate," margins will expand through 2026, and 2027 will bring a return to above-market core growth once the ~$300M of exits are lapped.
- **Doing:** Backing the AI belief with real product investment — SiC JFET, dual VCORE, TLVR two-phase modules, 30+ GaN devices — and design wins already landed (SunGro, Delta, Lite-On, Great Wall, a "leading US power supply supplier"). This is the clearest talk-matches-action thread on the call.
- **Gap:** On margin, the belief (53% gross margin, "within sight") is backed mostly by *not spending* (capex cut, fab divestitures) and matching utilization to whatever demand materializes — i.e., margin expansion is largely contingent on a market recovery ON isn't driving, not a commitment being funded independent of demand.
- **Gap on restocking:** Management believes tier-1 restocking is coming eventually and even frames it as a coiled-spring risk ("risky ramp when the demand does pick up"), but there is no stated action to prepare for or hedge that ramp beyond generic capacity readiness.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 or longer-term AI data center revenue number**, despite being asked three separate times by three different analysts; answer stayed at "it's a matter of the when, not the if."
- **No specific year for hitting the 53%/40% long-term margin targets** — Thad Trent walked the bridge (utilization + FabRite + divestitures + product mix ≈ getting there) but tied all of it to "assuming that the market continues to recover," not a date.
- **Declined to frame M&A as fab-filling or urgent**, distancing from a competitor's rationale for buying Silicon Labs; called ON's own M&A approach "strategically driven" and opportunistic on valuation, not compelled by the market currently existing.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Credibility of the "above seasonal" claim.** Multiple analysts (Ross Seymore, Vivek Arya) pushed on whether reported seasonal-level revenue is disguising real above-seasonal core growth once ~$300M of 2026 exits are excluded — a framing question about whether the beat narrative is real.
- **AI data center sizing and timeline**, asked from at least three angles (Seymore, Curtis, Rakesh) — all converged on wanting a number (10%? 15% of revenue? what year?) that management would not provide.
- **Margin bridge mechanics** — several questions (Buchalter, Bolton, Moore, Kumar) drilled into the utilization lag, FabRite timing, and depreciation math behind the Q1 gross margin guide, suggesting skepticism that a 30bps sequential improvement squares with the underutilization headwind described.
- **Automotive restocking absence** (Moore) — direct challenge on why lean tier-1 inventory hasn't triggered a restock despite favorable indicators, met with a candid tier-1-capital-constraint answer rather than a deflection.
- **Compressed worry:** is ON's "stabilization" story a genuine inflection into 2026-2027 growth, or is it seasonal noise being flattered by one-time exits and an AI narrative that management still won't quantify?
