# COST — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-05-28)

**The key idea:** A record-breaking gas quarter, driven by Middle East-driven price spikes, both flattered and complicated the print: it drove all-time volume records and new first-time gas customers, but it also diluted core-on-core merchandising margins as Costco deliberately widened price gaps on gas and staple items (eggs, beef) to defend its value promise. Underneath the gas noise, the core business — comps ex-gas steady at 6.6%, membership normalizing to 4-5% — looks like a maturing, still-healthy machine that is leaning hard into AI (search visibility, retail media, checkout tech) as the next growth lever rather than new physical footprint.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Gas volatility became a marketing tool, not just a cost pass-through.** Record volumes and "top five volume weeks ever" came from consumer price sensitivity driving members to Costco's gas stations, including "many members to use our gas stations for the very first time." Management is explicit that this is a loyalty play: gas customers "typically spend more with us in the warehouse" and renew at a higher rate.
2. **Margin give-back was a strategic choice enabled by an easy comp, not a panic move.** Core-on-core margin fell 9bps on lower fresh/food-and-sundries margins from investing in eggs and beef prices, but Gary Millerchip framed this as opportunistic: "we saw it as an opportunity, recognizing that our members were dealing with higher gas prices, to really invest in increasing value" — funded by lapping a large prior-year LIFO charge, not from margin structurally deteriorating.
3. **Membership growth deceleration (4.1%) is being reframed as normalization, not weakness.** Management attributes the slowdown to the absence of new-market openings (Japan/China-style step-changes) and says "4%-5% is a more normal rate of growth" absent a special event — a reasonable explanation, but also exactly what you'd say if growth truly were structurally slowing.
4. **AI search is early but already showing outsized conversion.** Triple-digit growth in AI-search-driven traffic in Q3, with "the highest conversion rate of all traffic coming to our site" — still low volume, but management is actively rewriting product pages to be more legible to LLMs, and gave a concrete example (all-in tire/appliance pricing including install/haul-away) of value that shows up better through AI summarization than traditional search.
5. **Capital discipline persists despite a swelling cash pile (~$45/share).** Rather than commit to a special dividend, management reiterated the priority stack (warehouses, remodels, depots, digital) and said cash would need to grow further to match the yield of the last special dividend at today's stock price — leaving the timing entirely open.

## Economy & consumer
- **Gas price spike from Middle East supply disruption drove the quarter's headline numbers**, with gas comps up "positive high 20s" and record-setting volumes across all three fiscal periods.
- **Underlying inflation stayed mixed**: fresh and food/sundries were net deflationary (produce, eggs, dairy), while non-foods saw rising costs from memory chips (majors) and anticipated resin/oil-linked cost increases in plastic/polyester/cotton items.
- **Consumers are trading up selectively, not broadly retrenching** — near-50% growth in saunas and massage chairs alongside heavy demand for value proteins (ground beef, poultry) shows a barbell: everyday value plus occasional premium splurge.
- **Traffic normalized rather than weakened**: two-year basket-vs-traffic mix has "leveled out" from a period of flat-to-negative basket/high traffic growth to steadier, more balanced growth in both metrics.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: pharmacy and e-commerce.** Ancillary comps up mid-20s, led by pharmacy gaining "significant market share" on GLP-1 demand (Wegovy/Ozempic inclusion, Medicare D flex cards); digitally-enabled comps up 21.5%, with personalized recommendation carousels driving 3x conversion and "just under half a billion dollars" of e-commerce sales.
- **Not working / pressured: core-on-core margin and SG&A leverage.** Core-on-core margin down 9bps on deliberate price investment; SG&A deleveraged 3bps ex-gas due to higher healthcare costs and one-time legal settlements/reserves in Central — management called these episodic, but it's a real miss against the historical 4-5% comp-to-leverage relationship.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$6.5B full-year capex** funds warehouse pipeline growth (26 net new in FY26, two units slipped to FY27), remodels/relocations to relieve capacity-constrained high-volume clubs, depot network expansion, and digital/member-experience tech.
- **The binding constraint is physical capacity, not capital or demand** — relocations exist specifically because legacy Northeast clubs built on old Price Club footprints (500 parking spots) hit volume thresholds that choke traffic; infill strategy is designed to "relieve the pressure" at over-capacity locations.
- **International expansion pipeline is loaded toward Asia (China, Korea, Japan) and Europe (Spain, UK momentum, France still young)** as the next multi-year growth vector, alongside a multi-year Canada buildout.
- **Manufacturing capacity investment is narrow and demand-driven**: expanding hot dog and coffee roasting capacity to support KS growth — small in scale but signals confidence in private-label velocity.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: AI search will become a meaningful sales channel.** Action so far is limited to product-page optimization for LLM legibility — real but early-stage, not yet a resourced initiative with disclosed spend.
- **Belief: gas engagement builds durable loyalty.** Action matches — deliberately widened gas price gaps to acquire first-time gas customers, betting on the historical pattern that gas customers spend more and renew more. This is a real financial commitment (margin given up) behind a stated belief, not just talk.
- **Belief: tariff refunds will be returned to members "in some form."** Action lags — refund claims are only now being submitted to CBP, with no committed amount, timeline, or mechanism, explicitly contingent on litigation and how much money actually comes back. Talk here is running well ahead of action.
- **Belief: retail media is a "significant milestone" opportunity.** Action is real but capped by design — the Google Commerce Media/YouTube partnership just launched, and management explicitly says 80-90% of retail media value gets reinvested back into member pricing rather than flowing to margin, so the monetization upside is deliberately throttled.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No special dividend timeline or amount**, despite ~$45/share in cash — management said the board will "continue to review options," with cash needing to grow further before it makes sense at current valuation.
- **No commitment to building owned first-party delivery infrastructure** to match Walmart/Amazon delivery-speed competition; content to keep evaluating third-party partnerships (currently <45 minutes average, 4.8/5 satisfaction) rather than vertically integrate, unlike the 2020 big-and-bulky delivery acquisition precedent.
- **Declined to quantify tariff refund pass-through** — explicitly contingent on unresolved variables (claim approval pace, lawsuit outcome).
- **Declined to give forward same-store-sales guidance** when pressed directly on membership deceleration risk, repeatedly redirecting to "we don't provide guidance."

## The street — what analysts asked
- The dominant anxiety cluster was **membership growth deceleration and its read-through to future comps** — multiple analysts pushed on the 4.1% paid-membership growth rate as the lowest in some time and asked whether comp expectations should be tempered; management resisted extrapolating and reframed the number as a return to a "normal" non-event growth rate.
- A second cluster probed **margin sustainability and competitive rationality** — whether the core-on-core margin give-back signals a more aggressive, structural share-gain strategy or a one-quarter opportunistic move; management insisted the market remains "rational" and framed the investment as self-competition, not a response to peer behavior.
- A third cluster centered on **capital allocation optionality** (special dividend, tariff refund deployment) — management kept every door open without committing to specifics.
- Compressed worry: **is Costco's demand engine (membership, traffic) quietly decelerating toward a lower structural growth rate, and is management's margin generosity this quarter a sign they're bracing for that by buying goodwill with price investment?**
