# WBD — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-06)

**The key idea:** This call is a victory lap that happens entirely in the shadow of the pending $31/share Paramount Skydance acquisition, which management was explicitly barred from discussing. The operative tension: WBD is reporting HBO Max's best-ever subscriber and engagement numbers just as it's about to hand the asset to a new owner, and every answer about "the future" is implicitly an answer about what Paramount inherits, not what WBD will build.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **HBO Max blew past its own guide right before the finish line.** Total subscribers exceeded the "over 140 million" target after simultaneous UK/Germany/Italy/Ireland launches, with management now guiding to "more than 150 million subscribers globally" by year-end — a beat announced on the eve of an ownership change, which reads as much as a valuation defense as an operating update.
2. **Sports-in-streaming remains explicitly unsolved.** JB Perrette was unusually candid that WBD runs five different sports-monetization models across markets (simulcast in the US, a la carte in the UK, bundled in Brazil/Mexico, standalone in Chile/Argentina) because "we know it can be acquisitive... but we are more wanting to prove out the ability to do sports profitably" — a direct contrast to Disney's same-day framing that owning ESPN inside the bundle is strategically necessary.
3. **The bundling logic contradicts itself in real time.** Zaslav praised splitting sports and entertainment into separate UK products as smarter than aggregation, then in the same breath framed the Paramount combination as valuable specifically because "putting that scale all in one place" creates a "more robust and compelling consumer experience" — the same executive arguing both that disaggregation and aggregation are the right move, depending on which deal he's defending.
4. **Studio profit quality is being propped by an accounting choice.** Gunnar Wiedenfels defended fair-market-value internal content licensing (versus Paramount's different approach) by acknowledging WBD has "created value in our company profits that are eliminated and sitting on the balance sheet" from years of shifting content internal-only — profit that is real but was deliberately timed to "bleed back" into consolidated results now rather than earlier.
5. **Separation and deal costs are a real, ongoing cash drag** — Wiedenfels flagged ~$100M of negative free-cash-flow impact from separation/sale-process costs in Q1 alone, with 2026 tracking "pretty close" to 2025's negative impact, layering advisory fees, bridge financing interest, and tax leakage on top of an otherwise improving operating story.

## Economy & consumer
- No macro or ad-market commentary was offered; the call stayed entirely inside company-specific execution and deal context.
- **Engagement/churn metrics are "the best we've seen in the 4 years"** per JB Perrette, attributed to slate strength (The Pitt at 20M+ viewers/episode, A Knight of the Seven Kingdoms at 36M/episode) rather than any stated shift in consumer spending behavior.
- Zaslav frames the broader consumer problem as **app fragmentation fatigue** — "three people on a couch googling where it is" — as the demand-side rationale for industry bundling.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** HBO Max turned from "losing over $2 billion" to "made $1.4 billion last year," with Perrette citing accelerating operating leverage as growth "kick[s] in."
- **Working:** Studio side had an "absolutely outstanding" 2025, capped by 11 Oscars including Warner Bros.' first Best Picture win in over a decade.
- **Working:** International linear/CNN strength — CNN minutes up 30% YoY, general entertainment delivery up 16% sequentially versus Q4, Olympics streaming hours more than doubled versus Beijing 2022.
- **Not working / capped:** Linear networks EBITDA guidance was explicitly withheld — Wiedenfels wants to "stay away from giving very specific guidance" on the segment's revenue-to-EBITDA trajectory, an implicit admission the structural decline isn't fully offset anymore ("not to the extent anymore as in the earlier years... where we were able to offset very significant [percentage] of the revenue declines").

## Investing & scarcity
- Capital keeps flowing to **HBO Max's international expansion** (four major European launches in one quarter) and a heavy **theatrical slate** (14 films in 2026, up to 18 in 2027 including Batman and Man of Tomorrow), alongside a newer bet on **experiences/consumer products** (Potter Tour Tokyo live, Shanghai in development) that Wiedenfels says will "increasingly contribute to our profits."
- The binding constraint is **sports profitability in streaming**, not capital or content supply — management is running parallel pricing/bundling experiments market-by-market specifically because they haven't found a model that pairs sports' engagement value with acceptable margins.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** subscriber-revenue growth will "pick up real pace" in Q2 and beyond. **Doing:** already completed the European launch wave that unlocks that growth, plus moving wholesale subs to higher-LTV retail — a case where stated belief and funded action line up cleanly.
- **Believe:** bundling is the structural future of the industry, evidenced by Disney bundle churn improvements. **Doing:** live bundle deals with Disney (US), RTL+ (Germany), Viu (Southeast Asia), and unnamed LATAM partners — again a case of belief matched with signed deals, though the largest bundling bet (Paramount) is the one thing they're not allowed to discuss.
- **Believe:** studio EBITDA can hold near 2025 levels despite a "slightly smaller slate." **Doing:** leaning on experiences/consumer products and internal-content-utilization accounting to backfill the gap — a belief resting partly on non-operating profit timing rather than new revenue.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No forward EBITDA/revenue trajectory for linear networks** — Wiedenfels declined twice to frame where the segment's margin structure is headed long-term.
- **No comment whatsoever on the Paramount Skydance transaction**, imposed as a blanket rule by IR before Q&A even opened — the single largest event in the company's near-term future was explicitly off-limits.
- **No commitment to a single sports-streaming model** — framed as deliberate ongoing "experimentation" rather than a strategy the company is ready to scale.
