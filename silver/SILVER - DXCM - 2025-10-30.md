# DXCM — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** DexCom delivered 20% organic growth and record EPS while simultaneously defending itself against a public narrative — visible across nearly every analyst question — that G7 sensor quality problems dented new patient starts and could bleed into 2026. Management's core bet is that this is a temporary manufacturing-quality blip (fixable in the factory) layered on top of a durable, multi-year coverage-expansion growth story (type 2 non-insulin, basal, international), and that both a 15-day sensor launch and a company-run RCT will reaccelerate growth once the current noise clears.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Quality issue admitted, but framed as solved, not systemic.** CEO Jake Leach directly addressed media scrutiny of G7: "the quality of the sensors coming off our lines today is exceptional," while CFO Jereme Sylvain conceded "there's likely been a bit of an impact on new starts" in Q3 — a real acknowledgment of commercial damage, immediately paired with reassurance.
2. **Gross margin cut is a self-inflicted, temporary hit.** Margin guidance was lowered to ~61% specifically because DexCom chose to add scrap/scrutiny on components rather than ship defective sensors — a deliberate cost-for-quality trade the company is absorbing rather than passing through.
3. **Type 2 non-insulin coverage is the multi-year growth lever, and it's a "when not if."** ~6 million type 2 non-insulin lives now covered (about half the addressable commercial population), with a company-sponsored RCT readout coming early 2026 designed to be the evidence cornerstone for the remaining ~25 million Americans.
4. **2026 base case deliberately excludes upside.** Leach: the top of DexCom's internal 2026 range "probably comes in just under where the Street is today" — and that base case assumes zero new coverage wins, meaning any CMS or payer expansion is pure optionality not yet priced into their own plan.
5. **15-day G7 rollout is fully cleared on reimbursement but margin-neutral in the near term.** Contracts are finalized with Medicare, all major commercial payers, and DME partners, but CFO says the product will have "a pretty nominal contribution" to 2025 revenue/margin — the real economic payoff (2 sensors vs. 3, per patient) lands in 2026 and beyond.

## Economy & consumer
- No macro or discretionary-spending commentary at all — growth here is a function of payer coverage decisions and clinical evidence, not consumer sentiment.
- Utilization data by cohort is the clearest read on genuine customer value: AID/intensive insulin users run >90% utilization, basal users 80-85%, and non-insulin type 2 users (largely uncovered, often paying via Stelo) still hit ~75% — real usage even without full reimbursement support.
- Management's own words on the quality-driven demand hit: "there's likely been a bit of an impact on new starts here over the course of the third quarter," though they call the underlying complaint rate "stable" — a claim this note flags as spin (see Contradictions).

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** 22% reported / 20% organic revenue growth, record quarterly EPS ($0.61), international accelerating for a third straight quarter (France and Canada singled out as standouts off recent coverage wins), Stelo surpassing $100M in revenue in its first 12 months.
- **Not working:** Gross margin compressed to 61.3% from 63.0% on elevated scrap rates from a sensor-deployment defect plus lingering expedited-freight costs; management is now guiding full-year gross margin down to ~61%, offset only partially by OpEx leverage.

## Investing & scarcity
- Capital and R&D are going toward: Smart Basal (FDA/CE under review), Stelo international expansion, a type 2 RCT, and the 15-day G7 broad rollout — all coverage/evidence/product moves, not new categories.
- The real scarcity is manufacturing quality control, not compute, capital, or people: CFO explicitly chose to scrap more supplied components "even if this results in higher costs in the near term" rather than risk field defects — a deliberate trade of near-term margin for long-term trust.
- Balance sheet is not a constraint: >$3.3B cash, plans to cash-settle a $1.2B convert and keep repurchasing shares, with "plenty of cash on hand" left over for further capital allocation.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Type 2 non-insulin coverage expansion is inevitable — "it's a matter of when, not if" — backed by a growing real-world evidence base and a company-run RCT due early 2026.
- **Action:** They're not waiting on coverage to invest — building Smart Basal now, taking Stelo abroad now, and fully clearing 15-day reimbursement contracts now — all ahead of the coverage actually landing.
- **Gap:** Despite calling coverage expansion near-certain, their own 2026 base-case guidance explicitly assumes *no* new coverage wins — a conservative-guidance / bullish-narrative split that management itself flagged as deliberate.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- No 2026 numeric guidance — deferred to a board meeting in December and an official guide "in the next few months."
- Wouldn't quantify G8/multi-analyte platform timing beyond confirming it's "an incredibly important part" of the roadmap; pointed instead to a first-half-2026 investor event at the Mesa plant for more detail.
- Declined to commit to a pace for the 10-day-to-15-day product transition, citing multi-year precedent from prior sensor-generation switches (G5→G6, G6→G7).
- Repeatedly declined to build coverage-expansion upside into any stated 2026 range, treating it purely as optionality.

## The street — what analysts asked
- The dominant cluster was 2026 modeling: multiple analysts pressed for numeric detail on growth, pricing/volume mix, and gross margin bridges, and were consistently redirected to "wait for official guidance."
- A second heavy cluster interrogated the G7 quality narrative directly — whether complaint rates were truly stable, whether new-start deceleration was quality-driven or seasonal, and whether the issue is now behind them; management's answers leaned on anecdote ("very encouraged... anecdotally") rather than hard September data, explicitly citing a 45-day data lag.
- A smaller cluster asked about competitive dynamics (Abbott's dual-analyte product) and long-run penetration ceilings (basal ~60%, non-insulin hypo-risk still under 5% today) — met with directional but non-committal answers.
- Compressed worry: *is the deceleration in new patient starts a seasonal artifact, or a demand scar from a manufacturing quality problem that hasn't fully shown up in the data yet?*
