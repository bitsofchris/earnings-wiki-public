# SBUX — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** Starbucks posted its first simultaneous top- and bottom-line growth quarter in over two years, and management is framing it as proof that the "Back to Starbucks" operational turnaround (Green Apron staffing, coffeehouse uplifts, GROW Scorecard) is compounding into real demand — not discounting. The China deconsolidation (Boyu JV) closes the loop on a multi-year distraction, but the CFO's refusal to touch the FY2028 EPS target, even after a beat-and-raise, signals real caution about how repeatable this is.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Transactions, not price, drove the comp.** U.S. comps hit 7.1% with transactions up over 4% — the first transaction strength "in three years" — and management explicitly ties it to service execution (GROW Scorecard, four-shot stores up 30+ points) rather than promotions.
2. **Green Apron's cost is now visible and still not fully annualized.** North America operating margin fell ~170bps even amid a comp beat, split between staffing investment and ~190bps of product/tariff/coffee cost inflation — the labor investment doesn't fully anniversary until August.
3. **China exits as a reported segment, not as a growth story to trust yet.** The Boyu JV converts China to a license model and pulls ~$3.1B cash out, but the rebased China business is still early in "stepping back into growth," targeting 1,000 to 1,500 county-level cities over three years — a plan, not yet a result.
4. **Loyalty relaunch outperformed expectations.** The rebuilt Rewards program (60-star tier, weekly card loads, tiered merch access) grew membership through what's normally a seasonal dip quarter, with management calling this a genuine surprise rather than a forecasted outcome.
5. **Management is deliberately not extending the credibility of this quarter to 2028.** Asked directly whether the FY2026 raise should flow through to the prior ~$4 EPS long-term target, the CFO declined to update it, wanting "another quarter behind us" first — a notably conservative posture given the headline beat.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro bleed-through yet, but management is watching for it.** "We haven't seen a lot of the macro effects trickle into consumer behavior," Brian Niccol said, while flagging gas prices, utilities, and fuel costs as the channels that could eventually hit.
- **All income cohorts grew transactions**, a result QSR peers have largely failed to replicate even with heavy discounting — Niccol attributes it to experience quality ("a little touch of luxury") rather than price.
- **Refreshers' customizable-caffeine feature is cross-cutting demand**, pulling lower-income customers into higher-caffeine morning occasions and higher-income customers into lower-caffeine afternoon occasions on the same $2B+ platform.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: throughput and service consistency.** GROW Scorecard "four-shot" store share rose over 30 percentage points since October; ~80% of stores now hit the 4-4-12 service-time targets (café/drive-thru/mobile).
- **Working: delivery as incremental revenue**, up over 30% year-to-date across U.S. company-operated stores, contributing to both ticket and transaction growth.
- **Not working yet: 40% of stores still below the four-shot standard**, and coffeehouse "uplifts" — the more dramatic Third Place remodels driving the biggest transaction lift — have reached only ~300 of a planned 8,000+ store base.
- **Not working yet: North America margin still contracting** (-170bps) even in a strong comp quarter, with "greater than anticipated legal accruals" cited alongside cost inflation as a drag CFO did not fully quantify.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is going to labor deployment technology and hardware**, not headcount cuts — a new Mastrena espresso machine (four shots in under 30 seconds) and "Smart Queue" order-sequencing are framed as throughput multipliers rather than labor reduction tools: "we don't see our way forward with cutting."
- **Coffee commodity cost is the clearest binding constraint on margin right now** — CFO cited roughly a $1/lb year-over-year increase plus tariff pass-through through inventory, both expected to ease only in the back half of the fiscal year.
- **$2B cost-savings program reaffirmed but its P&L visibility this year is mostly in G&A** (down 5.5% in the quarter); product/distribution and OpEx savings are described as still being built out through fiscal 2028.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: comp momentum continues near-term** ("positive comp trends have continued through April") — backed by concrete action: guidance raised to 5%+ comps and EPS of $2.25–2.45, plus accelerated uplift rollout (1,000+ by fiscal year-end).
- **Belief: China is a durable long-term growth engine** — but the acting move here is structural risk transfer (deconsolidation to a JV, cash extraction, debt paydown) rather than direct capital commitment to China growth; Starbucks is now a licensor watching Boyu execute against a 1,000→1,500-city target.
- **Gap: FY2028 algorithm not updated despite a strong beat.** Management talks up "durable, repeatable" results but explicitly declines to move the multi-year EPS target forward, wanting more evidence before committing numbers to it — talk of confidence outpacing the guidance action.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to reaffirm or raise the FY2028 ~$4 EPS long-term target** despite raising FY2026 guidance at both ends: "we're really not changing our FY 2028 targets at this time... at the right time, we'll come back with some different guidance."
- **Would not commit to reducing total labor hours**, even while investing in throughput technology — CFO framed it as reallocating "non-covered hours," not shrinking the labor base, leaving the actual multi-year cost trajectory of labor unquantified.
- **No specific quantification of the "greater than anticipated legal accruals"** that hit North America margin — mentioned once, not broken out or explained.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Cluster 2: margin flow-through skepticism.** Multiple questions pressed on why the EPS raise wasn't larger given the comp beat, probing COGS/tariff/coffee cost detail — CFO's answer conceded caution given "the macro backdrop" rather than promising more upside.
- **Cluster 3: does FY2026 strength extend the FY2028 long-term algorithm?** Directly asked and directly dodged — the CFO's non-answer is itself the most informative moment on the call.
