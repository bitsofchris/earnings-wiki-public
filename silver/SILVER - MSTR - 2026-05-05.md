# MSTR — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-05)

**The key idea:** MSTR reported a $12.8B net loss driven by a non-cash Bitcoin markdown, but the call was almost entirely about a structural pivot: the company has stopped thinking of itself as "buy Bitcoin with stock and debt" and started thinking of itself as a Bitcoin-backed financial engineering shop, using a new preferred instrument (Stretch/STRC) to strip volatility out of Bitcoin and sell "digital credit" to yield-hungry retail and institutional buyers. The tension underneath: management now explicitly says it will sell Bitcoin itself when convenient — a reversal of the "never sell" religion that built the stock's cult following — and is betting the market rewards this as sophistication rather than punishes it as capitulation.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The business model has quietly become a preferred-stock arbitrage, not a HODL story.** Stretch (STRC) grew to $8.5B outstanding in nine months — per Saylor, faster capital inflow than the iPhone or Google Ads — and is now 83% of new issuance vs. 17% common equity, a full reversal from January. The stated logic: sell low-volatility credit at 11.5%, buy 40-vol Bitcoin, capture the spread as BTC-per-share growth.
2. **Management now explicitly reserves the right to sell Bitcoin, reversing years of "never sell" messaging.** Saylor: "we'll probably sell some Bitcoin to fund a dividend just to inoculate the market... if you're a short seller and your thesis is the company's got to sell equity in order to fund the dividends, I would like nothing better than to... rip your wings off." This is a direct response to a prior incident where a "Strategy is selling Bitcoin" rumor hit the stock — they're choosing to control that narrative by doing it deliberately rather than letting it be discovered.
3. **The accretion math depends on a moving mNAV threshold, and they said so plainly.** Selling MSTR stock to buy Bitcoin is dilutive below 1.22x mNAV (up from a historical 1.0x as preferred/debt load has grown) — "there is a misconception that the break-even point is 1.0x." Below that line, the accretive trade flips to selling Bitcoin or credit instead.
4. **Retail, not institutions, is carrying Stretch.** 80% of STRC shares are retail-held, spread across ~120,000 accounts and traced to roughly 3 million households, distributed through Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood — and notably Vanguard, which won't let clients buy Bitcoin directly but does distribute Stretch. That's a meaningfully different (and more fragile-in-a-panic) buyer base than the institutional credit market management says it's chasing next.
5. **They're framing dividend obligations as self-funding forever, contingent on a 2.3% Bitcoin ARR floor.** The "BTC breakeven ARR" of 2.27% is the number they say determines whether dividends can be funded from BTC appreciation alone in perpetuity, without touching equity markets — a genuinely low bar if Bitcoin performs anywhere near its historical trend, but one that assumes continuous access to Bitcoin liquidity in a drawdown.

## Economy & consumer
- **No traditional consumer read** — MSTR has no operating business; omit as not applicable to this note.
- **Macro sensitivity is explicit and self-described as leveraged**: Saylor's own framing is Bitcoin as "risk assets squared," MSTR as "risk assets cubed" — a restrictive Fed is described as a direct multiplied headwind, not just a discount-rate effect.
- **Regulatory tailwind treated as sentiment, not necessity**: Saylor says the pending Clarity Act and Basel Bitcoin-collateral treatment "isn't necessary... we could be a multi-trillion dollar company and sell $400 billion of STRC and not have that fixed" — but would accelerate institutional (vs. retail) adoption of the credit instruments.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working**: Stretch liquidity has scaled fast — 30-day average trading volume is cited as 25x the next-largest preferred (Wells Fargo's), and daily liquidity grew from $54M in January to $360M in April. Sharpe ratio (2.53) is presented as beating hedge fund strategies and the Magnificent Seven.
- **What's not working / what's being spun**: the $12.8B net loss is a mark-to-market artifact, not an operating result — standard for MSTR, but worth flagging every quarter: this is not an operating business generating cash from customers, it's a leveraged balance-sheet vehicle whose "earnings" are unrealized Bitcoin price moves.
- **A quiet tax mechanic boosted the reported numbers**: the swing from unrealized gain to unrealized loss flipped a $1.9B deferred tax liability into a deferred tax asset, fully valuation-allowanced to zero — producing a non-cash tax benefit that partially offset the pre-tax loss. Not disclosed as a headline number, buried in the CFO's balance sheet walk.
- **Digitial credit ecosystem growth is real but tiny in absolute terms so far**: $270M of tokenized Stretch exposure appeared in DeFi in eight weeks — genuinely fast growth, but a rounding error against the $8.5B Stretch base.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital allocation is now multi-lever**: sell MSTR, sell Stretch, sell other preferreds, sell USD reserve, or sell Bitcoin itself — Phong Le frames this as a deliberate expansion of "optionality," a term used repeatedly as the central strategic asset of the quarter.
- **The actual scarce resource is market belief, not Bitcoin or capital**: every accretion/dilution calculation in the model hinges on mNAV and volatility assumptions the market sets, not on anything MSTR controls operationally.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: Bitcoin volatility will structurally compress over time (40 vol → 20-30 vol) as the asset matures, which will make their leverage look progressively safer and let them "lever it 8x-1x instead of 3x-1x."
- **Action**: they are not waiting for that compression — they're already selling Stretch aggressively today at an 11.5% fixed-for-now rate and explicitly deferring the "sell more Stretch vs. lower the coupon" decision until rates actually move, which is a real-time, reactive posture dressed as a forward strategy.
- **Gap flagged directly by an analyst and only partly answered**: whether the interest-rate sensitivity framework changes urgency now vs. later — Saylor's answer amounts to "we'll know it when we see it," not a pre-committed rule.
- **Belief vs. action on debt**: they say they want to be "completely debt-free" but the six converts remain outstanding this quarter with no committed retirement schedule — intent stated, capital not yet committed.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No specific Stretch issuance target for the year** — Saylor: "the answer to how much we can sell responsibly is a function of where the Bitcoin price is" — explicitly conditional, no number.
- **No firm floor or ceiling on amplification/leverage** — "we could see ourselves getting 50%, 60% amplification levels over time" is aspirational language, not guidance.
- **Declined to give a precise cost-of-capital figure for Stretch**, instead offering a wide modeled band (~6%-11.5%, "blended... 875 basis points") that depends on SOFR and credit-spread compression assumptions they admit are uncertain.
- **Quantum risk / BTC Security Program update was deferred** — Phong Le: "likely in the next month or so" they'll share who's involved and a combined point of view — effectively no answer today.
