# EXC — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-12)

**The key idea:** Exelon is riding two opposite currents at once — a transmission investment supercycle (capital plan up 9%, growth guided to the top of 5-7%) driven by data-center and industrial load, layered on top of a genuine generation-supply crisis that's pushing customer bills up sharply. Management is explicit that its own capital spend can't fix the underlying problem: it doesn't build generation, and the region isn't building enough of it either.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A regulated utility is quietly becoming a transmission growth story.** The $41.3B four-year capital plan is up $3.3B from the prior plan, with ~70% of that increase in transmission tied to data-center load and grid modernization — Exelon frames itself as structurally advantaged (multistate footprint, 765kV expertise, no single project over 3% of the plan) to capture a market CEO Butler says is "changing at a speed and scale rarely seen."
2. **Guidance discipline is being weaponized as credibility.** Despite visible upside of $12-17B in additional transmission opportunity over the next decade, CFO Jones repeatedly declined to raise the 5-7% EPS CAGR, explaining the philosophy directly: "we are not going to give you a number that I cannot sit here and say that." Analysts pushed hard on this gap across multiple questions.
3. **Affordability, not decarbonization, is now the dominant political framing.** Management cites PJM customers paying $32B more since mid-2024 for less capacity, and leans on a commissioned Charles River Associates study claiming utility-generated power could have saved $9.6-20B in 2028-2029 — a self-interested but pointed argument for utility ownership of new generation.
4. **The BGE reconciliation outcome is a quiet regulatory warning.** Exelon recovered only about half of what it sought in the BGE reconciliation order, and a promised Maryland "lessons learned" review from regulators still has no committed timeline heading into 2026 — a state where Exelon must file a new base rate case regardless.
5. **Storm performance as proof point.** Winter Storm FERN pushed PJM to 97% of its all-time winter peak load over five consecutive days; Exelon reports fewer than 1% of ~11 million customers lost power, used directly to justify the reliability-investment thesis underpinning the capital plan.

## Economy & consumer
- **Load growth is running well above the historical utility baseline.** Management guides to load growth exceeding 3% through 2029 across its jurisdictions, "further reinforced by our large load pipeline" and a growing number of signed transmission security agreements (TSAs) — a proxy for real, contracted data-center demand rather than speculative interconnection queue noise.
- **Consumers are absorbing a supply-cost shock utilities didn't cause.** CFO Jones: customers are "paying more for less" — PJM customers paid over $32B more in supply costs since July 2024 even as market supply capacity fell 1.2 gigawatts, and Mid-Atlantic residential supply costs are up "80% or more" over five years.
- **Political pressure on affordability is now bipartisan and structural**, not rhetorical: Governor Shapiro's budget speech, Governor Moore's State of the State, and new Governor Sherrill in New Jersey are all cited as actively pushing all-of-the-above generation and rate-design reform.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Reliability metrics remain genuinely top-tier**: utilities ranked 1, 2, 4, and 7 among peers on 2024 benchmarking, sustained for over a decade — not a one-quarter flex.
- **Earnings beat guidance again**, full-year 2025 non-GAAP EPS of $2.77, "above expectations," driven partly by favorable weather/storms and regulatory resolutions — non-operating tailwinds that inflate the headline beat somewhat.
- **Regulatory wins were mixed.** Final settlements landed at Atlantic City Electric and Delmarva Gas at 9.6% ROE, and ComEd filed its second multiyear grid plan — but the BGE reconciliation only recovered about half of what was sought, which management says required "realigning capital accordingly."
- **Cost discipline is real and quantified**: $580M in annual O&M savings versus inflation-adjusted trend, O&M growth capped at 2.5% through 2029, expense growth "nearly flat" 2024-2026.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital plan raised to $41.3B, transmission-heavy.** ~$2.3B of the $3.3B plan increase is transmission, driven by data-center load, aging-network replacement, and named large projects (Brandon Shores, Tri County, MISO Tranche 2.1 — the latter not in-service until 2034, yet spend is being pulled forward now).
- **A jointly developed PJM reliability-window award with NextEra** ($1.2B of incremental Exelon investment recommended) signals Exelon is willing to partner with an IPP/generation player on transmission solutions rather than go fully solo.
- **The real constraint is generation supply, not capital access.** Butler is direct: "The supply challenge is real, but not insurmountable" — Exelon can fund and build transmission at scale, but has no lever to force new generation onto the grid, which is the actual bottleneck driving customer costs.
- **Financing is being managed conservatively**: 40% equity funding ratio maintained, $700M of 2026-2029 equity needs already priced via forward ATM contracts, credit metrics targeted 100-200bps above downgrade thresholds.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes:** rate base growth near 8% and EPS growth near the top of 5-7% through 2029, with $12-17B of additional transmission opportunity "strengthening and lengthening" the plan beyond what's currently budgeted.
- **Doing:** deploying ~$10B in 2026 capital, pulling forward MISO Tranche 2.1 spend a decade ahead of in-service, and actively bidding in PJM/other ISO competitive windows — concrete commitments that lag the stated belief in upside, since none of the $12-17B pipeline is yet reflected in guidance.
- **Gap flagged explicitly by analysts and only partially answered:** if $12-17B of visible upside exists, why hasn't the CAGR range itself moved? Jones's answer — conservatism as a design choice, not a signal of a weak pipeline — leaves the timing of any re-rate entirely open.
- **On generation**, the company is investing zero of its own capital directly into new supply, instead lobbying for policy (capacity auction price-cap extension, reliability backstop, utility-generated power authority) to let others — or itself under new legislative authority — build it.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No Pennsylvania rate-case timeline**: "we are considering what is the best approach to action in Pennsylvania" — deliberately left open, contrasted with a firm 2026 Maryland filing commitment.
- **No step-change to growth guidance** despite acknowledged upside — Jones explicitly frames the withheld number as a credibility choice: "we are always going to strive to exceed your expectations."
- **No firm date on the Maryland "lessons learned" review** — Butler: "I wish I could give you a timeline, but we do believe it will happen in 2026," conceding uncertainty about outcomes of a prior contentious rate case.
- **No commitment on generator-utility price alignment** in PA/MD/NJ legislative negotiations — Jones deflects to "get to the right place where there is actual new generation at the right price" without specifying what that price is.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered on **the disconnect between disclosed upside and static guidance** — rate base growth accelerating, $12-17B of incremental transmission opportunity identified, yet the 5-7% CAGR range itself unchanged, pressing repeatedly for what triggers a re-rate.
- Significant focus on **state-by-state regulatory and legislative strategy** (Pennsylvania IPP/PPA negotiations, Maryland lessons-learned timing, New Jersey's new governor), probing whether Exelon can bridge utility-vs-generator interests or whether positions are too far apart — met with process reassurance but few specifics.
- Questions on **financing cost drag against rate base growth** (AFUDC contribution, the 40% equity funding ratio) were answered with more granularity than the growth-rate questions, suggesting management is more comfortable defending funding mechanics than committing to accelerated growth.
- **Compressed worry:** the market believes Exelon's transmission opportunity set is larger than what's priced into guidance, but management won't say when — or if — that upside actually shows up in the number.
