# XEL — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-30)

**The key idea:** Xcel spent the quarter converting regulatory and RFP wins into concrete upside — $6B of new SPS generation pushed total incremental line-of-sight investment from $7B to $10B+ in a single quarter — while carefully keeping that upside out of its official "9%+ EPS growth through 2030" framing. The tension of the call is management repeatedly declining to formalize what its own numbers already imply: growth well above the stated range, driven substantially by data centers they insist aren't in the base plan.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Upside is compounding faster than guidance updates.** Line-of-sight incremental capital jumped from $7B (Q1) to $10B+ this quarter purely from the SPS RFP outcome, with a 4 GW MISO RFP decision (Q4) and ongoing Colorado/Upper Midwest RFPs still to come — CFO Van Abel: "You're right, we had $7 billion of line of sight in Q1. We added $6 billion... We would just simply say $10-plus billion."
2. **Data centers are framed as pure upside even while driving the investment thesis.** Management insists "our base plan has very little data center growth baked into it," yet nearly every growth answer — SPS transmission, the Google Minnesota project, template replication in Colorado and SPS — is data-center-driven. That's a real tension between the stated conservatism and the substance of the answers.
3. **Labor and EPC capacity, not capital, is the actual constraint.** The entire "strategic partnerships" section of prepared remarks is about locking multi-year commitments with tier-1 suppliers specifically to secure crews and equipment through the 2030s — capital is being raised (85% of the 5-year equity need already lined up) faster than the physical capacity to deploy it can be assured.
4. **Customers, not shareholders, are named as the primary beneficiary of new large loads.** Xcel's Clean Energy Accelerator Charge (Minnesota) and Large Load Tariffs make new large customers pay full freight for the generation and interconnection they require — Frenzel: "We expect data centers to pay their full and fair share of the cost to serve them." This is XEL's answer to the industry-wide grid-cost-socialization anxiety.
5. **Nuclear: rhetorical support, zero commitment.** Frenzel calls himself "an unabashed fan of nuclear energy" nationally but states plainly: "We will not be an early adopter of new nuclear power plants at Xcel Energy" — wind/solar/storage/gas is the near-term answer even as peers explore new nuclear for data center load.

## Economy & consumer
- **Broad-based industrial demand, not just AI.** Weather-adjusted sales growth of 2.1% YTD is attributed to "increased activity in the energy sector at SPS and manufacturing sector across all OpCos" — oil-and-gas electrification in the Permian/Delaware basins is called out as a bigger near-term SPS growth driver than data centers.
- **Affordability remains the explicit political constraint.** Six rate case settlements this quarter were achieved "while keeping long-term bill growth at or below the rate of inflation," with expanded energy-assistance program funding bundled into the Colorado and Minnesota settlements — customer bill pressure is being actively managed, not ignored.
- No direct residential-consumer commentary beyond bill-affordability messaging; this is a utility, so the "consumer" read is entirely mediated through regulatory/rate-case dynamics.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Regulatory execution is the standout.** Six active rate cases reached settlement or decision in a single quarter — Frenzel calls this "extraordinary progress" reflecting "strong preparation, early engagement with stakeholders, and disciplined execution."
- **EPS beat driven by real operating drivers plus one accounting reclass.** $0.93 vs $0.75 prior year came from riders/sales growth (+$0.17), AFUDC (+$0.08), and lower D&A (+$0.08) — but the D&A guidance change is explicitly non-operating: "earnings neutral, just given the change in the nuclear depreciation lives... in the Minnesota rate case." Financing costs (higher interest, equity dilution) offset roughly $0.18 of the gains.
- **Balance sheet discipline is being emphasized ahead of a bigger ask.** Already ~85% ($6B of $7B) of the base five-year equity need is locked in via forwards/collars/ATM/junior sub-notes — positioning to absorb a larger capital plan without a shock equity raise later.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital plan re-rating in real time.** Incremental investment line-of-sight moved from $7B to $10B+ this quarter alone, on top of the $70B+ five-year base plan, driven by SPS's RFP award of 2,600 MW (70% of the total portfolio sought).
- **The real bottleneck is people and EPC throughput, not money.** The entire "partner of choice" strategy — multi-year commitments to tier-1 suppliers, standardized/repeatable project designs, consolidated project-execution governance — exists to secure "the labor and equipment capacity that we need to deliver with certainty... well into the 2030s."
- **Workforce pipeline investment is concrete, not aspirational.** Partnerships with high schools, trade programs, and universities are "supporting thousands of students, apprentices, and trainees" — a direct hedge against the labor constraint.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Stated belief:** 9%+ EPS growth through 2030, with data center demand as pure upside on top of a diversified base plan.
- **Actual action:** Filing Large Load Tariffs in four+ jurisdictions, building a Google-anchored carbon-free generation project in Minnesota as an explicit template to "replicate... in our Colorado company... in our SPS company," and reserving $5-6B of generation investment per gigawatt of data center load as a stated rule of thumb — this is a company positioning hard for data-center-driven growth while keeping it rhetorically outside the base case.
- **Gap:** If data centers are truly "upside," the base-case 9%+ number should be conservative by construction — but management resists calling it conservative, and dodges every analyst attempt to get a bigger number stated before Q3. The gap between what they're building for and what they're guiding to is the central story of this call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to pull the 4 GW MISO RFP (due Q4) into current guidance:** "It might be a little bit early in terms of our Q3 call."
- **Declined to commit to 10-year capital/earnings guidance** despite peer moves in that direction — Van Abel: "We'll continue to evaluate it... right now we'll continue to provide a 5-year, but also call out maybe a longer term, if that's helpful."
- **Ruled out new nuclear entirely** for the foreseeable future, despite hedging with general pro-nuclear rhetoric at the policy level.
- **Deferred the full 2027-2031 plan reset** — capital plan, financing plan, and sales growth assumptions — entirely to the Q3 call, declining to give directional detail now beyond "$10-plus billion."

- **Secondary theme: durability and structure of the data center pipeline** — mix of gigawatt-scale campuses vs. smaller loads, large load tariff terms relative to hyperscaler pushback seen elsewhere, and community/regulatory acceptance of the Minnesota Google project.
- **Tertiary theme: Colorado wildfire risk and 2027 legislative strategy**, addressed at length with operational detail (AI cameras, weather stations, EPSS/PSPS days) rather than deflected.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts believe Xcel's actual growth trajectory already exceeds its stated guidance, and are trying to find the moment management will admit it.
