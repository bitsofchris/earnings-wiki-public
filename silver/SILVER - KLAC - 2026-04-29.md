# KLAC — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-04-29)

**The key idea:** KLA is guiding to $140B+ WFE for 2026 and, unusually, is already signaling that 2027 growth will exceed 2026 — not because chip demand estimates changed, but because the industry-wide constraint has shifted from designs and dollars to physical fab and equipment capacity. Management's central claim, repeated in several forms, is that hyperscaler AI capex — even when it looks "lumpy" quarter to quarter — is nowhere close to the ceiling the semiconductor supply chain can actually deliver.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Demand visibility has extended a full year further than normal.** CFO Bren Higgins: "this demand environment gives us confidence in 2027 visibility... we expect the 2027 year-over-year growth rate to be higher than our growth rate expectations for 2026" — an unusually early call on a year that's normally not discussed in April.
2. **Physical capacity, not chip demand, is the bottleneck.** CEO Rick Wallace: "the first constraint is how many fabs do you have? Like, how many shelves can you fill?" Higgins adds the industry is "way under serving" hyperscaler AI infrastructure demand, invoking Elon Musk's Terafab comments as evidence the gap isn't closing anytime soon.
3. **Advanced packaging estimate jumped sharply in 90 days.** KLA's process-control packaging revenue outlook moved from ~$635M to ~$1B for 2026 (high-50%s growth, up from "in excess of 30%" a quarter earlier) — management attributes this to customers securing additional capacity they hadn't visibility into last quarter, concentrated more in the second half.
4. **KLA won't price on scarcity even with customers "fighting for shipment slots."** Higgins, asked directly why memory customers can't be charged for their gross-margin-eroding DRAM cost pass-through faster: "We don't price based on scarcity at KLA. Our pricing is based on cost of ownership improvements from one generation to the next" — a deliberate stance that leaves ~100bps of margin headwind unaddressed through 2026.
5. **Yield-squeeze, not just new fabs, is driving incremental process-control demand.** Even "the leaders have gone back to prior nodes and added process control" to extract more output from existing capacity rather than wait on new fabs — Higgins ties this explicitly to Intel's public commentary on increased metrology usage.

## Economy & consumer
- No direct consumer read (KLA sells to chipmakers, not end consumers); the closest proxy is semiconductor capex urgency, which management describes as **broad-based across foundry logic, memory, and packaging**, not concentrated in one segment.
- Legacy/trailing-edge spend, "pretty weak this year," is flagged as having **some upside into next year**, though Higgins declined to quantify it.
- China spend is characterized as **structurally flat-to-slightly-up**, growing slower than overall WFE, and largely disconnected from the leading-edge boom driving the rest of the business.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Revenue beat guidance ($3.415B vs. $3.35B midpoint), gross margin beat by 45bps on services mix and scale, services revenue up 16% y/y, and KLA gained process-control share across mask inspection, optical wafer inspection, e-beam inspection, and especially advanced packaging (+14 points of share, ~70% y/y growth in 2025).
- **Not working / friction:** OpEx ran above plan on "prototype materials timing and other reserve adjustments"; services growth **decelerated sequentially** (down 1% q/q) purely on revenue-recognition timing; and supply-chain scaling in H1 2026 was **tighter than expected** because the pace of demand ramp surprised the company — Higgins: "the thing that surprised us was the slope and duration of how quickly the business started to ramp."
- Memory chip cost inflation (DRAM used in KLA's own imaging computers) is a **persistent, unhedged ~100bps gross-margin drag** through at least calendar 2026, worse than the 75-100bps previously guided.

## Investing & scarcity
- Capital return remains aggressive: $875M returned in the quarter ($626M buybacks, $249M dividends), a 17th consecutive dividend increase, a new $7B buyback authorization, and a target of **>90% of free cash flow** to shareholders per the March Investor Day model.
- The real constraint articulated repeatedly is **industry fab-building capacity**, not chip design, not even hyperscaler budgets: "you can't go from $140B to $200B in 2026... those fabs have to be built now." KLA itself is racing to add supply-chain and installation/service headcount to keep pace.
- Secondary scarcity signal: the X-ray metrology submarket (Axiom-style tools) remains capped around $75-100M today, constrained by tool throughput/cost-of-ownership economics rather than technical capability — Wallace says broad X-ray adoption in production is not imminent despite active R&D interest.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** 2027 growth will exceed 2026's, driven by greenfield DRAM/NAND fabs and continued foundry-logic broadening; process-control intensity will keep outpacing WFE growth itself (targeting another ~150bps of share gain per the 2030 model).
- **Action:** Building supply-chain and service-organization capacity now, ahead of shipments, specifically to avoid repeating the H1 2026 scaling strain; raised the long-term revenue CAGR target to 13-17% and services CAGR to 13-15% at Investor Day just six weeks before this call.
- **Gap:** Despite raising 2026 WFE to $140B+, management is intentionally cautious about updating the 2030 model (unveiled just six weeks prior) even though Higgins concedes "there are a lot of reasons why you would push that up from that" — a belief that's running ahead of the stated commitment, deliberately not yet re-baselined.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Declined to quantify legacy-node upside for 2027: "I don't wanna quantify that yet."
- On the Huawei-related export-control letter, Higgins limited comment to "we're still looking at it" and called the guided impact "fairly immaterial," without detailing scope or which fabs are affected.
- Refused analyst pressure to reconcile KLA's high-teens 2026 growth against peers citing mid-20s WFE growth as a demand discrepancy — reframed it purely as a market-definition (denominator) issue instead of engaging with whether peers see stronger underlying demand.
- Would not commit to raising prices despite acknowledged shipment-slot scarcity, citing a deliberate value-based (not scarcity-based) pricing philosophy.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Multiple questions probed the credibility of extended 2027 (and implicitly 2028) demand visibility — is it real end demand or customers over-ordering to secure scarce capacity/service resources? Management insisted it's broad-based and real, tied to fab construction schedules.
- Recurring theme: reconciling KLA's WFE growth math against faster peer commentary, and whether $140B+ undersells the moment — management repeatedly framed this as a market-definition dispute, not a demand gap.
- Margin questions centered on whether tight capacity gives KLA pricing power, especially to offset DRAM cost inflation faster — dodged in favor of a "we don't price on scarcity" answer.
- China/export-control risk (Huawei ban news) came up but got a minimal, "immaterial and contemplated in guidance" response with no elaboration.
- Compressed worry: **is the extraordinary 2027 demand signal genuine end-customer conviction, or an artifact of everyone in the supply chain racing to reserve scarce capacity at the same time?**
