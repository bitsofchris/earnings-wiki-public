# CDW — fiscal 2025 Q4 call (2026-02-04)

**The key idea:** CDW closed 2025 with its best quarterly gross margin of the year, powered by cloud, software, and services outrunning a soft hardware/corporate environment — but the entire 2026 outlook is built around a wildcard neither side of the call can pin down: how far memory chip price increases run, and whether supply actually gets tight in the back half. Management's guide is deliberately hedged low, front-loading hardware strength into H1 and quietly assuming a second-half slowdown they can't yet see clearly.

The 2026 guide bakes in a second-half growth dampening they can't yet quantify.

4. **The AI narrative is being told as a rerun of the cloud narrative, but without a landmark partnership moment to prove it.** When directly asked for a "landmark" AI equivalent of CDW's old AWS line-card moment, Leahy pointed to existing relationships with major model providers but offered no specific new strategic tie-up — an answer that reframed the question ("AI is embedded across the entire stack," not a discrete product) rather than naming a concrete proof point.

## Economy & consumer
- **2025 was a year of policy whiplash, not demand collapse.** Tariffs, shifting education/healthcare funding, government spending priority changes, and the longest federal shutdown on record all shaped buying behavior; management still delivered 7% full-year net sales growth despite it.
- **Federal remains impaired into 2026.** Q1 2026 explicitly assumes "a slow start to the year for the federal channel as the pipeline rebuilds following last quarter's government shutdown," even as state and local posted strong double-digit growth offsetting it.
- **Consumer/customer behavior split by size and sector**, not a uniform macro read: small business and state/local surged, corporate and federal lagged, education jumped 13% on a major NYC DOE Chromebook rollout.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Cloud, software, and services are now the profit engine.** Cloud alone drove roughly half of Q4's gross profit growth; software gross profit "rose even faster" than its 12% top-line growth; professional/managed services grew double digits with services-transferred-over-time revenue up 11%.
- **Hardware growth (2%) masks a bifurcated picture** — double-digit notebook and server growth offset by declining storage, plus an anticipated memory-related demand pull-forward of roughly $50 million that management called "minor" in overall impact.
- **Netted-down (agency) revenue keeps climbing as a share of profit** — 36.1% of gross profit, up from 35.8% a year ago — a structural mix shift management leans on to explain margin resilience even as hardware demand gets choppier.
- **SG&A grew faster than revenue in 2025 (up 14.6% in Q4)**, driven by commissions on higher gross profit and performance-based comp normalizing off an unusually low prior-year base — management calls 2025 the new "normal baseline" and expects 2026 leverage to build progressively, not immediately.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital return stayed aggressive: ~$1 billion returned to shareholders in 2025 (90% of adjusted free cash flow)**, well above the original 50-75% target, alongside a twelfth consecutive dividend increase and the Lexicon Tech Solutions tuck-in to strengthen education life-cycle capability.
- **Memory is the binding constraint for 2026 planning**, not compute or people — CFO language ("fluid," "week to week, month to month," "not seeing any significant roadblocks... but we are counting on that... could see [supply challenges] as the year plays out") signals genuine uncertainty rather than confident modeling.
- **Balance sheet stayed disciplined at 2.4x net leverage**, within CDW's 2-3x target band, giving room for continued M&A and buybacks without straining the capital structure.
