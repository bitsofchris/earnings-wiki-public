# VZ — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-24)

**The key idea:** Verizon is telling a turnaround story built on two legs: a genuine churn/net-add inflection from stripping out subsidies and fees, and a brand-new bet on selling dark/lit fiber capacity to hyperscalers for AI data center interconnect. The tension is whether the consumer-facing generosity (no fees, flat-rate plans, loyalty cashback) is sustainable margin expansion or a subsidy shell game moved off the balance sheet — management insists it's "fully contemplated in our outlook," but this is quarter two of a promised multi-year story with only 40 days of real data on the new plans.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **AI Connect is the real news of the call** — a previously unannounced initiative to lease Verizon's long-haul and metro dark/lit fiber to hyperscalers, plus retrofitting old central offices into edge/inference data centers. The Google deal (**over $1 billion**) is framed as the first of several **"multiple billions of dollars"** agreements expected by year-end, with revenue starting in 2027 and "growing substantially" for **5-10 years**. A trial retrofit of a central office **"sold out capability in 24 hours."**
- **Simplicity and Verizon One reprice the whole go-to-market.** Simplicity ($45, no device subsidy bundled) and Verizon One ($70 converged mobility+broadband, all-in) explicitly **separate phone subsidies from wireless pricing** — Dan Schulman calls new Simplicity accounts **"basically subsidy-free,"** which he frames as a structural balance-sheet improvement, not just a pricing gimmick.
- **Loyalty program is universal, not tiered** — cashback, fee elimination (no activation/upgrade fees), and a redemption catalog for every customer, not a status tier. Management says it's "funded within our existing operating envelope."
- **BT joint venture spins out international wireline** ($4B combined revenue at formation, ~$200M annualized synergies, closing 2H2027) — a deliberate narrowing of scope so Verizon can lean into domestic network/fiber/AI infrastructure instead.
- **AWS spectrum auction win** (82 licenses, ~$3.2B, a "slight discount" to 2015 pricing) deployable with **no capital investment**, framed as opportunistic balance-sheet-friendly network densification.
- Dan Schulman's contract was extended through **2028** the same call he unveiled AI Connect — the board tying its confidence directly to this specific pivot.

## What's changing
- **Consumer postpaid churn structurally down**: 84bps, down from 90bps in Q1 and 95bps in Q4 — second straight sequential improvement after what Schulman admits was years of "steadily rising" churn.
- **Cost of acquisition/retention dropped sharply** — promotional COA down ~15% y/y, COR down ~17% y/y, and this is **"before Simplicity impacts."** Upgrade volumes were down nearly 27% y/y, which is also flattering equipment-revenue comps.
- **Consumer postpaid net adds turned positive for the first time since 2021**, and new account growth (not just line growth) turned positive for two straight months — a distinction management leans on hard since accounts, not lines, is the harder metric to move.
- **Guidance raised across the board** for the second straight quarter: mobility/broadband revenue growth to 2.5-3% (Q4 guided to ~4%), adjusted EPS to 6-7%, free cash flow to 9-10% (up from "~7% or more"), buybacks to up to $4.5B (already $3.5B YTD, ahead of the original $3B full-year target).
- **Free cash flow of $6.4B, up 24% y/y** — Schulman contrasts this against the prior five years when FCF and EPS both grew at roughly **-1%** annually — an explicit admission of a multi-year stagnant period preceding this one.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Wireless service revenue is still declining** (-0.7% y/y to $20.8B) even as the blended "mobility and broadband service revenue" metric — the one management leads with — grows 2.8%. The reliance on this blended framing to obscure a shrinking core wireless line is a redefinition worth flagging.
- **Equipment revenue fell nearly 20% (>$1.2B)** on the upgrade-volume drop; management spins this as "disciplined," but it's also mechanically inflating margin and FCF optics this quarter.
- **Fixed wireless net adds are decelerating** — a question directly raised by an analyst ("ads there on a bit of a downward trend") that Schulman answered by pivoting to broadband-in-aggregate and satellite-competition physics rather than addressing the FWA deceleration itself.
- **No mention of enterprise/business wireline core trends** outside the BT carve-out — the segment being spun into a JV is conspicuously the one getting the least standalone color.
- **The new value proposition has only 40 days of data.** Every claim of it "delivering well beyond expectations" (gross adds +16%, net accounts +31% vs. forecast) rests on a very short, freshly-launched sample, presented with high confidence.
- **AI Connect capacity is explicitly finite** — Schulman says "that capacity and pricing is... we're going to have to think about how we handle the demand for that," essentially conceding they don't yet have a plan for a supply-constrained, high-demand asset they're only now monetizing.
