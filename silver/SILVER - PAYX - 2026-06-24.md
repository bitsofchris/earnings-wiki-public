# PAYX — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-06-24)

**The key idea:** Paychex spent the COVID-ERTC cash windfall quietly rebuilding its back office into a modular, standalone-billable platform, and that multi-year infrastructure bet is now the thing enabling both the Paycor integration payoff and an AI product (WISE) that can be sold independent of the payroll relationship that used to be the only door in. The tension: management is confident enough to guide margins to "approximately 44%" and reaccelerate organic growth, but almost all of the acceleration story is "trust us, it's exiting strong" rather than disclosed unit-level detail — and they explicitly declined to break out what share of revenue is now enterprise vs. SMB post-Paycor recategorization.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **WISE, the agentic layer, is being sold as the moat**, drawing on "more than 26 trillion data points" via a "patent-pending AI knowledge mesh" that mines unstructured client interactions (emails, calls) — the CEO frames proprietary HR/payroll data access, not model quality, as the durable differentiator once AI commoditizes routine tasks.
- **The real unlock is billing infrastructure, not the AI itself**: after years of back-office modernization, Paychex can now bill and service clients on individual products (insurance, 401k, HR compliance) without requiring them to be on its core HCM/payroll platform — CEO calls this "the breakthrough here," enabling standalone monetization of Perks (400K+ employees already buying) and a payroll-agnostic compliance product that "integrates with most of the large HCM platforms."
- **PEO remains the structural growth engine**, with worksite-employee growth outpacing the industry (high-single-digits) and healthcare inflation explicitly framed as a tailwind — "unsustainable" cost growth pushes small businesses toward PEO pooling even as it's a "double-edged sword" that also scares away affordability.
- **Broker channel is being rebuilt as a holistic partnership model** (Partner+, new deals with Hub International and one unnamed national broker), explicitly designed to sell the *entire* Paychex portfolio rather than fragment referrals product-by-product.
- **Cross-sell into the Paycor base is the primary growth lever for FY27**, with management expecting "even stronger contribution" than this year's 50+ bps of revenue synergy and $100M+ in realized cost synergies — the acquisition thesis was always "go sell ASO/PEO/retirement into their base," and that motion is described as just now hitting stride as legacy Paycor reps learn to leverage it.
- **Deliberately not growing client count** — CEO is explicit that flat-to-slightly-up client base is the strategy, not a shortfall: "we're not going to do irrational things just to add clients that are not going to be profitable clients." Growth is meant to come from revenue-per-client (roughly half pricing, half share-of-wallet), not units.

## What's changing
- **Organic growth "nearly doubled"** from roughly 3% exiting last fiscal year to a much stronger exit rate this year, with sequential improvement every quarter — CFO ties FY27 guidance directly to this exit rate rather than assuming further acceleration.
- **Margins jumped ~750bps in Q4** (37.7% GAAP operating margin) and adjusted margins guided to "approximately 44%" for FY27, at the high end of the normal 25–50bps annual expansion range — driven by Paycor synergy realization, AI-enabled productivity, and a stated internal metric of service-revenue-per-employee growing faster than revenue itself.
- **Enterprise segment realignment is complete**: Paycor's sub-100-employee clients moved into the SMB segment, Paychex's 100+ clients moved into the Paycor/enterprise brand — a full territory and brand swap that makes prior-year comps "apples to oranges," per the CFO's own characterization.
- **Interest income becomes a headwind**, expected down 4-5% in FY27 as last year's rate cuts fully lap and one-time portfolio-repositioning gains don't repeat — a reversal from this year's tailwind.
- **PEO agency drag is fading**: after weighing on PEO/Insurance growth for much of the year (MPP enrollment issues), management now sees "positive trends... in demand and retention" and expects that headwind to subside.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Client count growth has stalled and management is reframing that as intentional** rather than addressing it as a problem — attrition is concentrated in the low end and mostly "out of business," but the company offered no client-count target or timeline to return to growth, just a promise not to chase unprofitable adds.
- **Refused to disclose the enterprise (100+) share of Management Solutions revenue** when directly asked — the CFO said he didn't have the number "in front of me" and didn't want to guess, on a metric that would clarify exactly how much of the growth story is Paycor-driven vs. legacy Paychex, despite acknowledging it's a reasonable ask for a future IR deck.
- **Paycor's own growth rate implicitly decelerated to roughly 4%** by one analyst's math, and management's response was to reframe the unit of analysis (combined "enterprise" segment across both platforms) rather than defend the standalone Paycor number — a classic redefine-the-metric move, done transparently but still a dodge of the original question.
- **Total corporate leverage remains elevated** at ~$4.6B in borrowings against $1.2B cash, a structural overhang from the Paycor deal that management addressed only via "reduced leverage a half turn" language rather than a deleveraging target.
- **No mention of headcount reductions or restructuring charges tied to AI productivity gains** — despite repeatedly citing AI-driven efficiency (70% error reduction in timekeeping, automated compliance actions, reduced timesheet approvals by "more than 50%"), there's no disclosure of what happens to the labor previously doing that work; framed only as "freeing up resources... to advisory," not as any kind of workforce impact.
