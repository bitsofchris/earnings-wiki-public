# ADBE — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-03-12)

**The key idea:** Shantanu Narayen announced his departure as CEO after 18 years, effective once a successor is named, and used his (likely) final strategic-framing call to explain why Adobe's ARR growth print looks soft even as revenue accelerated: freemium AI products (Firefly, Express) are pulling huge new-user volume that hasn't yet crossed the paywall, while the legacy stock-content business is eroding faster than planned. The bet is that "MAU today, ARR later" repeats Adobe's Reader/Acrobat playbook — the tension is that this quarter's actual data point (net-new ARR dampened, stock down harder than modeled) hasn't yet proven the promised inflection.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Leadership transition overshadows the print.** Narayen is stepping down after "100 earnings calls," staying on as Chair during a Board-led search expected to take "a few months" — this call was as much a valedictory as a results call, and analysts spent much of their airtime on tribute and succession process rather than pressing the model.
2. **AI-first business tripled but is still small relative to the base.** AI-first ending ARR "more than tripled year-over-year" and Firefly's book crossed $250M (up 75% QoQ), but against $26.06B total ARR this remains an emerging line, not yet the growth engine it's being positioned as.
3. **The freemium funnel is real but unproven at scale.** Creative freemium MAU crossed 80M (+50% YoY), generative credit consumption rose 45% QoQ, yet Durn conceded the near-term effect is that ARR growth ex-freemium would have printed at 11.2% instead of 10.9% — the "phase shift" story is directionally plausible but currently a net drag, not a net lift.
4. **Stock-content decline is the one acknowledged miss.** The traditional stock business (~$450M of ARR) "saw a steeper decline than we expected," described candidly as happening "more quickly than we had planned for" — a rare admission of a forecasting error rather than strategic choice.
5. **Model-agnosticism as enterprise moat, not existential threat.** Narayen argues that of the many foundation-model entrants, only "2 or 3 really large language models" will ultimately survive, and Adobe's strategy is to plug into all of them (Google's Nano Banana, OpenAI, Anthropic, NVIDIA, Microsoft) as distribution partners rather than compete model-for-model — a bet that workflow ownership beats model ownership.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Subscription revenue grew 13% YoY — Durn called this the fastest subscription growth "in quite some time." Enterprise lines (AEP and apps, GenStudio) each grew over 30% YoY, and customers with >$10M ARR grew "greater than 20% year-over-year."
- **Not working:** Stock-content book declining faster than modeled; net new ARR growth dampened enough that management had to explain it away on nearly every analyst question. cRPO growth (12% cc) is roughly flat vs. prior quarters — the "structural change" analysts hoped to hear about didn't materialize, per Durn's own answer ("I don't see any reason why the trends... would inflect").

## Investing & scarcity
- Capital allocation unchanged in stated philosophy (organic growth, selective M&A, buybacks) but concretely tilted this quarter toward closing Semrush (expected Q2, pending regulatory approval) to build out "brand visibility" tooling for the LLM-search era.
- Internal cost discipline signal: Adobe now "tracks tokens and token usage within the company" as an operating metric — rising token usage is framed as a *positive* indicator of AI product value, with COGS efficiency managed alongside it.
- Q2 non-GAAP operating margin guided down to ~44.5% from 47.4% actual in Q1, attributed partly to Adobe Summit event spend — a real, if modest, margin give-back for growth investment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** ARR growth reaccelerates in the back half as freemium users convert; AI-first products become "our next billion-dollar business" (Narayen's own framing).
- **Doing:** Building out ChatGPT/Copilot/Claude/Gemini integrations, GenStudio-to-ad-platform pipelines (Amazon, Google, LinkedIn, Meta), and Firefly Foundry custom-model deals with media/entertainment (CAA, UTA, WME) — real distribution build-out consistent with the stated belief.
- **Gap:** The "back half acceleration" is asserted with a reaffirmed 10.2% FY26 ARR target but no quantified bridge — management offered no specific metric or checkpoint for when net-new ARR turns positive, leaving the thesis unverifiable until it either shows up or doesn't in Q2/Q3 results.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- No firm timeline for CEO succession beyond "I would suspect it will take a few months."
- No quantified answer on exactly when net-new ARR growth "goes positive" (Zukin's direct question) — Narayen redirected to qualitative "traffic patterns... only going up and to the right" language rather than a number or quarter.
- Declined to characterize the margin/growth tradeoff as a philosophy shift when pressed by Wells Fargo on whether Adobe would sacrifice margin to grow faster — answer stayed at "we are always looking" generalities plus a token-efficiency deflection.

## The street — what analysts asked
- A large share of Q&A opened with tributes to Narayen before questions, signaling how much this call doubled as a leadership-transition event for investors as well as employees.
- Substantive themes clustered on: (1) mechanics and timeline of the ARR/MAU "phase shift," asked from three different angles (Zukin, Thill, Turrin) without a fully satisfying quantified answer; (2) durability of cRPO/RPO coverage ratios, where Durn's answer was essentially "no reason to expect a different dynamic" — a non-answer to a structural question; (3) capital allocation post-Semrush, where Durn maintained the bar for M&A stays high despite an easier dealmaking environment.
