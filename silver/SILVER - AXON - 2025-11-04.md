# AXON — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-04)

**The key idea:** Axon is making a second bet on public safety infrastructure — not body cameras and TASERs anymore, but the 911 call itself — by acquiring Prepared (AI overlay for existing call centers) and announcing Carbyne (cloud-native call-handling replacement) to become the "nervous system" connecting caller, dispatcher, drone, and officer. The tension: this is the same 911/dispatch space Axon exited a year ago citing "not a lot of room to innovate," and management is now betting two acquisitions and rising R&D spend that AI has changed that calculus.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth is intact but the mix is shifting toward strategy, not just execution.** Revenue grew 31% YoY for a seventh straight 30%+ quarter, but adjusted EBITDA margin dipped to 24.9% as tariffs hit connected devices for a first full quarter and R&D investment ramped — a deliberate trade management defends explicitly: "we're going to make smart investments and those investments are going to pay off... profitability is still important to this company" (Joshua Isner).
2. **Enterprise and international are the quiet accelerants.** A 9-figure European cloud deal (in a smaller, non-flagship country) is framed by Patrick Smith as more significant for being the "alpha patient" proving EU cloud adoption, while enterprise body cameras (ABW Mini) are teased as potentially "the biggest part of our business" — both still pre-revenue-scale bets.
3. **The 911 acquisitions are a bet that AI removes the old objection to entering dispatch.** Smith is explicit that Axon exited CAD (computer-aided dispatch) because legacy code and training requirements left "0 appetite" for disruption, but now sees a parallel path in via AI voice layers (Prepared) and cloud call-handling (Carbyne) that doesn't require touching CAD directly — a narrower entry angle, not a full reversal, but one worth watching for scope creep.
4. **Domestic deceleration is explained away as a recognition artifact, not defended with data.** When pressed twice on why U.S. revenue decelerated despite reaccelerating software growth, Isner and Smith redirect entirely to bookings ("no concerns there... it's just a function of how we recognize software"), never addressing the revenue-metric question directly.
5. **Drone-based violence and organized retail crime are treated as the two live physical-security narratives driving new-market expansion** — Dedrone (counter-drone) into federal/international, and body cameras/Fusus into enterprise retail loss-prevention, both pitched as directly monetizable fear.

## Economy & consumer
No macro or consumer-spending commentary — Axon's demand signal is entirely institutional: agency budgets, political will around retail crime and drones, and government contract cycles (Q4 is seasonally the largest domestic quarter). The closest thing to a "consumer" read is retail enterprise customers reporting **theft and organized retail crime as a business-model threat**, with Smith citing "the experiment with decriminalizing theft was a disaster" as a driver of enterprise body-camera demand.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Software/services is the standout**, growing 41% YoY to $305M with NRR at 124% and ARR up 41% to $1.3B — the highest growth rate of any 2025 quarter for that segment.
- **Connected devices decelerated to 24% YoY** (TASER +17%, Personal Sensors +20%, Platform Solutions +71% off a small base) with gross margin down 50bps, entirely attributed to a first full quarter of tariff impact: "once it's in, it's in" (Brittany Bagley).
- **Domestic (U.S. state and local) bookings are allegedly accelerating "beyond what we planned,"** per Isner, even as investors flagged softer domestic revenue — management leans hard on trusting bookings over revenue optics for this segment.
- **International is inflecting**, with a 9-figure European cloud deal and TASER 10 driving 7 of the top 10 international Q3 deals, though this remains lumpy, deal-by-deal momentum rather than broad-based.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Two acquisitions in one year in the same adjacent space** (Prepared, closing; Carbyne, expected early 2026) — both "immaterial from a financial perspective at close," bought for team and category potential, not existing revenue: "We're not buying an existing business. We're buying an opportunity" (Bagley).
- **R&D spend stepped up deliberately** into vehicle intelligence/ALPR, ABW Mini, and AI Era Plan features (live translation, policy chat), explicitly funded by absorbing tariff cost rather than cutting other investment.
- **The real constraint is legal/regulatory, not technical or capital**: U.S. state and local police currently lack legal authority to mitigate drones, which Smith says is holding back Dedrone's core domestic market until "one bad incident" forces Congressional action — an unusually candid acknowledgment that they're waiting on a negative catalyst.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
Management states unambiguously that bookings growth is accelerating into the high-30s% YoY and that 2026 will be "another record year," with international and enterprise cited as the biggest structural upside. The action behind that belief is concrete on the supply side — two 911 acquisitions, a stepped-up R&D budget, continued acquisition-fueled expansion (Fusus, Dedrone up >3x YTD bookings) — but the **domestic revenue-vs-bookings gap remains asserted, not evidenced on this call**; investors are being asked to trust the bookings number without seeing it reconcile to revenue this quarter.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to disclose the 5-year vs. 10-year contract mix**, despite confirming a real shift toward longer contracts and that even 5-year-normalized bookings growth is accelerating.
- **Declined to comment market-by-market on Dedrone/NATO country traction**, batching it into "federal, state and local and international" without granular color.
- **Guidance still assumes zero federal contribution** even while flagging Q4 as "our best federal quarter of the year" — an explicit choice to keep upside out of the base case rather than raise the bar.
- **No margin-mix guidance beyond generalities** ("product mix," "long-term view") when asked directly how new acquisitions and organic investments will move margins forward.

## The street — what analysts asked
Analysts converged on three anxieties: (1) whether bookings guidance implies deceleration despite the AI Era Plan being the fastest-booked product ever, forcing management to reassert a high-30s bookings growth number; (2) why domestic revenue looked soft against strong software growth, which management answered by pivoting entirely to bookings rather than addressing revenue directly; (3) competitive and integration risk from stacking two 911-adjacent acquisitions (Prepared, Carbyne) atop legacy CAD/LMR systems, and whether Motorola's new SVX body camera or LMR footprint threatens Axon's position — dismissed by Isner as market noise with "no headwinds." The compressed worry: **is Axon's growth engine actually accelerating, or is bookings language being used to paper over a revenue deceleration investors can see but can't yet verify?**
