# TTWO — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-02-03)

**The key idea:** Every label beat guidance meaningfully and management raised full-year net bookings for a third time, but the entire call is really a pre-show for GTA VI — Rockstar's marketing starts this summer, GTA Online is being kept humming (RCS +27%) specifically to prove the franchise doesn't go quiet ahead of launch, and every capital-allocation and AI question got redirected toward "the coming months" rather than answered directly.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **This was a blowout quarter used almost entirely to set up next year's story.** Net bookings of $1.76B beat the top of guidance, recurrent consumer spending grew 23% against an 8% guide, and full-year net bookings guidance was raised to $6.65-$7B — Zelnick's framing throughout was "next year is groundbreaking," not "this quarter was great."
2. **Zelnick used the earnings call to publicly rebut the market's AI selloff.** Asked directly why gaming stocks got hit on AI fears, he said "I'm a little confused" and argued the industry has always been built on ML — a notably combative, on-the-record disagreement with how the market priced the sector days before the call.
3. **NBA 2K is compounding against its own record, not decelerating.** Recurrent consumer spending, DAUs, and MyCareer DAUs all grew 30% year-over-year against what should be a hard comp, credited to "perpetual diligence" in live-ops (Karl Slatoff) rather than any single feature.
4. **Direct-to-consumer mobile monetization is becoming a real margin lever, but they won't quantify it.** Management called it their "strongest quarter on record" for mobile DTC and cited a friendlier regulatory backdrop reducing third-party take rates, yet twice declined to disclose what share of mobile RCS it now represents.
5. **Genie/world models got the most pointed AI dismissal of the call.** Slatoff called it "not even in the same ballpark" as a proprietary game engine and reduced it to "a procedurally generated interactive video" — a clear defense of the moat narrative rather than a technical assessment.

## Economy & consumer
- **Mobile market tailwind acknowledged, not claimed as company-specific:** Zelnick conceded "a rising tide lifts all ships" in mobile, referencing the sector's recovery from a mid-2022 downturn that coincided with the Zynga acquisition.
- **Affordability reframed as a strength:** amid analyst questions about console-cycle price inflation and memory costs, Zelnick argued interactive entertainment is "getting more and more affordable" on a real basis, with mobile experiences offered free.
- **No broader consumer-spending or discretionary-pullback commentary** — unusual for a discretionary-spend business, suggesting management sees no macro headwind worth flagging.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Every label outperformed:** Zynga mobile (+19% YoY), 2K (NBA 2K, WWE), and Rockstar (GTA) all beat internal forecasts, per Zelnick and Goldstein.
- **TuneBlast crossed $3B lifetime bookings** after eight years and grew 43% YoY — an unusually long tail for a mobile hit, credited to a "diverse portfolio" match-three strategy.
- **Mobile advertising revenue grew 10%,** the second year of growth since the Zynga deal, driven by higher ad units and ARPDAU — management called out that fewer than 20% of mobile users ever pay, making ads a meaningful incremental monetization layer.
- **GTA Online's Safe House update drove 27% RCS growth** on a game over a decade old, which management explicitly used to rebut "trepidation" that GTA VI's approach would cannibalize engagement with the current title.
- **Operating expense leverage was real:** management-basis opex grew 13% against much larger topline growth, and full-year opex growth guidance was trimmed to ~8%, partly from marketing dollars pushed into next fiscal year.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex guidance unchanged at ~$180M** despite the operating cash flow guide nearly doubling (to $450M from $250M) — investment intensity isn't scaling with the earnings beat.
- **Capital allocation framework restated as unchanged:** organic growth first, then selective/accretive M&A ("most corporate M&A fails" because empire-building crowds out discipline — Zelnick), then opportunistic buybacks, with the prior buyback at ~$158/share cited as validated in hindsight.
- **The real scarce input is hit-making creative talent, not money:** Zelnick said explicitly there are only "two companies" reliably producing new mobile hits, and framed Zynga's turnaround as protecting scarce talent and letting them "pursue their passions" rather than a spending program.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** fiscal 2027 will be a new, higher financial baseline, driven by GTA VI (Nov 19, 2026) plus WWE 2K26, continued NBA 2K strength, and a "very robust" multi-year release slate.
- **Action:** Rockstar's GTA VI launch marketing is confirmed to begin this summer — a concrete, dated commitment — and some marketing spend was deliberately shifted from this fiscal year into next to align with that push.
- **Gap:** management gave no numbers behind the FY2027 optimism (a full three-year pipeline is deferred to the May call), so the "record levels" and "new baseline" language is currently a verbal commitment without a quantified plan — Goldstein was explicit that even modest release-schedule shifts "can have a significant effect," which hedges the confident framing.
- **DTC is a stated growth priority with real product action** (personalized offers, flexible pricing, alternative payment methods) but the company still won't size the opportunity, meaning the "meaningful growth driver" claim is asserted, not evidenced.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Declined to characterize Genie's technical capabilities in detail,** citing its early stage, while simultaneously using it to dismiss competitive threat — an inconsistency (detailed enough to dismiss, too early to describe).
- **Declined to comment on third-party app-store-fee-avoidance solutions** (e.g., Unity's cross-platform commerce tooling), redirecting entirely to in-house DTC efforts.
- **Declined to say what happens to GTA Online after GTA VI ships,** deferring completely to Rockstar as "the locus of information" on content and marketing decisions.
- **Declined to give guidance beyond fiscal 2027,** citing release-schedule volatility as the reason no multi-year detailed guidance will be offered.
