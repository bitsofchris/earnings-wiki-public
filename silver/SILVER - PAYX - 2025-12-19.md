# PAYX — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2025-12-19)

**The key idea:** Headline numbers look strong (revenue +18%, adjusted EPS +11%, cost synergies raised to $100M), yet the stock fell ~3.5% because analysts couldn't make the Paycor math work — pro forma growth of 8-9% versus the "double-digit" framing set at deal announcement. Underneath that is a second, quieter story: new-client deal sizes and module attachment are softening across the entire book, not just Paycor, which management attributes to a more price-conscious, shopping-heavy small-business buyer rather than competitive pressure.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Paycor's true growth rate is lower than advertised, and management can't precisely say what it is.** CFO Bob Schrader pegged pro forma Paycor growth at "8% to 9%," walking back the earlier "double digit" language to "if not low double digits, high single-digit" — while insisting a precise number "would imply a level of specificity that just isn't there" because clients are being actively migrated between the Paycor and Flex platforms.
2. **Deal sizes and attachment are softening company-wide, not just at Paycor.** CFO: "we're generally adding two modules additional, or three modules. Maybe we're adding two today." CEO John Gibson: clients "are picking the lower bundles... It's not that we're not getting attachment. We're just not getting the attachment at the rate that we had put in the plan."
3. **PEO is quietly the strongest part of the business and getting stronger.** PEO-and-insurance growth accelerated from 3% to 6% sequentially, aided by easier annual-enrollment comps and near-record retention; the insurance agency is the one drag they're actively working to fix.
4. **Guidance was nudged to the low end even as EPS guidance was raised** — a signal that revenue conservatism, not earnings weakness, is driving the tone; a chunk of the EPS raise comes from realized gains on repositioning Paycor's client-fund investment portfolio, a non-operating boost.
5. **AI is being framed defensively as much as offensively.** Management published a standalone presentation arguing Paychex is *less* exposed to AI-driven client-employee displacement (70%+ of clients' employees are in blue/gray-collar roles) while simultaneously touting agentic AI pilots that handled "thousands of payroll calls and emails with near 100% accuracy" — a dual narrative of AI-as-risk-shield and AI-as-cost-lever.

## Economy & consumer
- **No recession signal, but a visibly thriftier buyer.** Same-store employment is flat and the small-business employment watch index is "relatively stable," yet Gibson repeatedly describes a market of "shoppers" who "want a lot of things" but "are only gonna pay a certain amount."
- **Healthcare inflation is a live pressure point.** Schrader: "Health care inflation is a real issue" with clients facing "10 to 15% increases," though fewer clients dropped coverage this cycle than last year — a modest positive.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: PEO, retention, cost discipline.** Mid-single-digit worksite-employee growth, "near-record retention," and operating margin expansion to 41.7% via AI-assisted productivity.
- **Not working: revenue-per-client growth across Management Solutions**, softer specifically in the HR-outsourcing (ASO) line — "one of our highest value solutions" — where volumes held but rate softened versus plan.
- **Integration friction is real but framed as manageable.** Broker bookings on the Paycor side dropped after the January deal announcement and only returned to pre-acquisition levels this quarter — a nine-month round trip.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Buyback stepped up opportunistically.** $514M returned to shareholders in the quarter, more than the usual dilution-offset pace, which Schrader attributes to buying weakness rather than a strategic pivot: "our future growth opportunities are any less today than they were six months ago."
- **Portfolio repositioning for yield capture.** Took over Paycor's short-duration client-fund portfolio and extended duration before further rate cuts — a deliberate, already-executed move that boosted Q2 EPS via realized gains.
- **The binding constraint is client willingness to pay, not any resource shortage** — reps have the products to sell but buyers are trading down to cheaper bundles.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** Management Solutions organic growth reaccelerates toward ~5% and PEO/insurance growth keeps climbing as comps ease in the back half.
- **Doing:** Raised cost-synergy target to $100M, reset Paycor sales territories and the Partner Plus broker program, and pushed a GenAI sales tool that went from pilot to essentially full deployment across the Paychex sales force within ~60 days ("it went rogue on us... people started demanding it").
- **Gap:** The acceleration thesis leans heavily on easier prior-year comparisons (anniversarying last year's MPP/enrollment headwinds) rather than on demonstrated new-bookings strength — management concedes "there's probably an element of conservatism" baked into guidance, which reads as hedging the very acceleration they're forecasting.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused a precise Paycor organic growth figure**, repeatedly falling back on "our best estimate" language and blaming cross-platform client migration for the imprecision.
- **Declined to commit to a post-integration long-term Management Solutions growth rate**, saying that's a question for after this fiscal year's integration work is done.
- **Would not attribute softness to competition**, holding firmly to a macro explanation ("There's nothing competitive that I'm seeing") even under repeated questioning.

## The street — what analysts asked
Nearly every question circled back to one of two things: reconciling Paycor's real growth rate against the deal's original double-digit framing, and understanding whether the revenue-per-client softness is macro or something more structural (competition, pricing power, go-to-market disruption). Analysts pressed on the buyback step-up and the AI monetization roadmap as secondary threads. The compressed worry: **is Paycor actually a high-single-digit grower dressed up as a strategic win, and is Paychex's core small-business buyer trading down for real?**
