# CSX — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-22)

**The key idea:** This is a cost-cutting story riding on top of a flat-to-soft freight economy. CSX delivered 20% operating income growth almost entirely by cutting headcount, vehicles, and PS&O spend faster than revenue was growing — not because end demand improved. Management is candid that housing and auto are still "pretty bleak" and that the year's raised revenue guide is a fuel-price pass-through, not a volume story, while quietly admitting their own efficiency push is temporarily degrading the operating metrics (dwell, train speed) it's supposed to fix.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Cost cuts, not demand, are driving the beat.** Revenue grew 2% on 3% volume, but expenses fell 6%, delivering 20% operating income growth and 26% EPS growth — CEO Steve Angel frames the whole quarter around "operating expense moved substantially lower," not top-line strength.
2. **The guidance raise is fuel arithmetic, not a macro call.** CSX bumped full-year revenue guidance from low-single to mid-single digits "largely driven by higher than expected energy prices, particularly diesel," which lifts both revenue and expense — management explicitly says this "can pressure reported margin" even as they raise the operating-margin outlook to the high end of 200-300bps.
3. **Housing and auto haven't turned; management says so plainly.** Maryclare Kenney: "we haven't really seen improvement in either of those areas" versus the January outlook — auto production down ~2% with a major plant retooling all year, and forest products still cycling 2025 mill closures.
4. **The efficiency push is breaking things on the way to fixing them.** Mike Cory admits that hitting near-100% curfew completion on track work (versus 60-70% historically) has "caused some rerouting of trains and traffic, and it has caused delay" — an explicit trade-off between capital/maintenance discipline and near-term service metrics, framed as "a learning opportunity."
5. **Middle East conflict is an accidental tailwind.** Higher energy prices are boosting frac sand (data-center-driven gas production), domestic plastics (global supply disruption), and truck-to-rail conversion economics — a macro shock turned into a commercial opportunity, though Angel flags it also raises "broader concerns about inflationary pressure and potential effects on consumer sentiment."

## Economy & consumer
- **Housing and auto remain the two soft spots**, unchanged since January guidance: "affordability issue," high interest rates that "bounced back up a little bit after everything that's happened in the Middle East," and auto production down ~2% with a major retooling plant offline all year.
- **Higher fuel/diesel is a two-sided macro force**: it's lifting rail's value proposition against trucking ("higher fuel prices... does increase the value proposition of rail") while also being the primary driver of the raised revenue guide and a margin headwind.
- **No macro recovery is assumed in guidance** — management confirmed the plan still bakes in flat-to-soft housing/auto/industrial production, with upside coming only from company-specific execution and fuel pass-through.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Cost structure is the clear win**: headcount down 5%, overtime down $10M, vehicle fleet down 7% since end-2024, and $100M+ of year-over-year efficiency savings offsetting inflation and fuel headwinds.
- **Safety and fuel efficiency hit records**: FRA injury rate improved 13%, train accident rate improved over 30%, and Q1 fuel efficiency of 0.97 gallons/thousand GTM was a first-quarter record (0.93 in March, best since 2021).
- **Merchandise is mixed under the hood**: flat volume overall, with minerals/chemicals/fertilizers growing but forest products down 9% on weak housing and difficult closure comps — "mix" dragged total revenue per unit down 1% despite "same-store pricing... in line with expectations."
- **Intermodal is the bright spot**, up 6% volume/5% revenue on new customer business and truck-conversion tailwinds, though RPU fell 1% on mix toward shorter-haul inland-port freight.
- **A one-time $44M real estate gain** boosted Q1 PS&O and will not repeat in Q2 — a clean example of a non-operating prop that flattered the quarter's reported cost line.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital discipline is explicit and structural**: CEO Angel describes moving from many simultaneous projects to "block mode" execution, requiring every capital project to "stand on its own," plus predictive analytics to prioritize infrastructure spend over habit-based maintenance assumptions — full-year capex still guided below $2.4B despite the revenue raise.
- **Howard Street Tunnel and Meridian & Bigbee/SMX (with CPKC) are near completion**, expected to unlock double-stack capacity on the East-West corridor and I-95 corridor and shave a day off transit — but Kenney cautions "new services typically takes a couple of bid seasons to really see it get to kind of full ramp."
- **The binding constraint is organizational bandwidth, not capital or physical assets**: 100+ discrete productivity initiatives are already in motion, and management is now also building a *separate* pipeline of 2027 initiatives in parallel — the scarce resource is leadership focus and execution capacity, explicitly named as needing to "build that productivity muscle."
- **Energy/vehicle spend named as the next targets**: CFO Kevin Boone says energy "over the next few months is going to be in the crosshairs of everything we're trying to do," alongside freight car hire (>$1M/day spend pool) getting new field-level visibility tools.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: management expects same-store pricing to strengthen through the year and into 2027, and expects the productivity gains to compound rather than be one-time. **Action**: they've already begun building a 2027 initiatives pipeline mid-2026, run in parallel with 2026 execution — a real resourcing commitment, not just a stated hope.
- **Belief**: coal RPU stabilization and strong data-center-driven power demand could support domestic coal. **Action**: no capital commitment described — this is framed as dependent on whether two scheduled utility-plant closures actually proceed or get extended, entirely outside CSX's control.
- **Belief**: industrial development pipeline (~600 active projects, ~100 expected in service this year, 50% more volume at full ramp than last year's 85 projects) represents durable, diversified growth. **Action**: real investment behind specific projects (Keystone Terminals rail extension, Martin Marietta aggregate facility, Diamond Pet Foods site design) shows this is being actively built, not just marketed — a genuine talk-meets-action alignment.
- **Gap flagged**: management says the 2026 cost plan positions ROIC improvement via the "numerator" (margin), yet Mike Cory concedes the very productivity initiatives meant to drive that improvement are currently causing worse dwell times and train delays — a real-time tension between the stated efficiency thesis and its operational side effects.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No PS&O "run rate" commitment**: when pressed on whether the ~$660M PS&O level was sustainable, Boone would only say "we're never done there" and pointed vaguely to more opportunity without quantifying it.
- **No specificity on industrial-development carload math**: asked to reconcile the 21 projects (33,000 carloads) against the full 100-project target, Kenney declined to extrapolate, saying only "that's probably all we're going to give from a guidance perspective at this point."
- **Declined to isolate company-specific growth from macro drag**: when asked to split project-driven growth versus macro headwind in the flat guide, Kenney avoided giving a number, saying only that ID is "certainly a net positive" without breaking out the offsetting closures.
- **No conviction call on coal recovery**: Jonathan Chappell explicitly asked if flat/sequential coal RPU growth marks "the start of a recovery" — Kenney reframed it as benchmark-gap math and utility-closure uncertainty rather than answering the recovery question directly.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Sustainability of cost cuts dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts (Wetherbee, Group, Shanker) pushed for hard numbers on PS&O savings run-rate and 2027 initiative sizing; management consistently deflected to "the plan we laid out in the fourth quarter" without new figures.
- **Sequential margin bridge into Q2** was probed repeatedly given the lapping $44M real estate gain, higher fuel, engine overhauls, and M&A advisory costs — CFO confirmed margin pressure sequentially but reframed it as motivation for more cost work.
- **Infrastructure project ramp timing** (Howard Street Tunnel, industrial development) drew multiple questions on when volume actually shows up — answers consistently pointed to multi-quarter/multi-year ramps, tempering near-term expectations.
- **Truck market tightening and pricing pass-through** (Wadewitz) probed whether rail can capture pricing gains now or only in 2027, given only ~50% of the freight book reprices in a given year — a structural lag CSX can't accelerate.
- **Rail industry consolidation** (a potential transcon merger) came up directly — Angel's answer emphasized "focus on execution" and being "in a position of strength" rather than any specific competitive countermeasure, an implicit acknowledgment CSX has no defined M&A response of its own.
- **Compressed worry**: is this quarter's earnings strength repeatable cost discipline building real operating leverage into 2027, or a fuel-driven, real-estate-gain-flattered beat masking still-soft underlying freight demand and self-inflicted service degradation?
