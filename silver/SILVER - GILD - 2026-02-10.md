# GILD — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-10)

**The key idea:** Gilead closed out a genuinely strong diversification year — HIV, liver, and Trodelvy all growing, Kite the lone structural decliner — and is now betting the next leg of growth on YES2GO's twice-yearly PrEP injectable scaling from $150M to a guided $800M. But when analysts pressed on the two inputs that actually determine whether that number is real — refill/persistency rates and net price trajectory — management had no answer for the first and refused to discuss the second, leaving the most consequential guide on the call resting on "trust the momentum."

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **YES2GO's access ramp beat plan, but conversion is still unproven.** 90% payer coverage and ~90% $0 co-pay access were hit in under six months, "well ahead of our one-year target" — but Johanna Mercier conceded pull-through requires "navigating logistics... account by account," and on refill rates for the twice-yearly shot she said flatly, "we don't have an assumption at this point in time because it's still really quite early."
2. **The $800M YES2GO guide didn't survive analyst arithmetic cleanly.** Pressed with math implying flat new-patient starts and a >10% price cut were embedded in the guidance, Mercier's response was "I'm not sure I'm tracking your modeling," followed by momentum language rather than a rebuttal of the numbers — a real gap between the confident guide and management's ability to defend its components live.
3. **Kite is the one visibly deteriorating business.** Cell therapy sales fell 7% in 2025 and are guided down ~10% more in 2026, hit by both commercial competitors and a rising number of competing clinical trials pulling patients away — Gilead's own words: "this represents another near-term headwind."
4. **Policy, not economic cycle, is the macro variable that matters here.** Management quantified an ~$900M Part D redesign headwind in 2025 and layered on a new Medicaid pricing agreement plus proposed ACA channel-mix shifts worth another ~2% drag on 2026 HIV growth — all specific, negotiated/regulatory items rather than demand-cycle effects.
5. **Pipeline breadth is real and self-funded.** Up to 10 launches through 2027, five Phase III readouts and five FDA decisions expected in 2026 alone, funded from operating discipline (R&D flat, SG&A down 5% in 2025) rather than debt or dilution — and two internal long-acting HIV programs (GS-1219, GS-1614) were killed outright to concentrate resources on the winner, GS-3242.

## Economy & consumer
- **No cyclical/macro read exists on this call** — Gilead's environment is defined by drug-specific policy mechanics (Part D redesign, Medicaid pricing agreement, ACA subsidy changes), not consumer spending or employment. Absent these headwinds, management says 2026 base-business growth would run 6-7% instead of guided 4-5%.
- **"Consumer" here means insured-patient access economics**, and YES2GO's launch metrics (90% coverage, 90% at $0 co-pay) show the payer side solved faster than expected — the bottleneck moved downstream to prescriber/patient behavior change.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Biktarvy still gaining US HIV-treatment share every quarter since launch (>52%), Descovy PrEP up 33% YoY in Q4 on record ~45%+ US share, Libdelzi in PBC up 42% sequentially aided by a competitor's US withdrawal, and Trodelvy's back-to-back positive Phase III readouts (ASCENT-03/04) earning NCCN recommendation as the only ADC endorsed across first- and second-line metastatic TNBC.
- **Not working:** Kite cell therapy down 7% in 2025, guided down ~10% more in 2026 — management is explicit this is competitive share loss compounded by trial-driven volume diversion, not a temporary blip.

## Investing & scarcity
- Acquired IPR&D (early-stage deal spend) held at roughly $1B/year "agnostic to therapeutic area," with SG&A growth in 2026 explicitly reallocated toward launch marketing for the four new products rather than G&A.
- **The real constraint is operational conversion, not capital or supply** — Kite already built out manufacturing to serve Anidocel at launch (99% reliability, 16-day turnaround) ahead of approval, and YES2GO's bottleneck is field logistics (scheduling injectable administration in a market built for oral drugs), not access or manufacturing capacity.
- On larger M&A, CEO Dan O'Day was direct that discipline trumps urgency: "we may not have the urgency of other companies in this sector... we're gonna be disciplined around that."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management frames YES2GO as inevitably becoming the HIV-prevention market leader and a "blockbuster" business, with Descovy continuing to grow in parallel through 2026 before eventually eroding to YES2GO.
- **Action backing it:** a dedicated DTC "One to PrEP" campaign, account-by-account field logistics support, and specialty-pharmacy refill outreach — real spend and headcount behind the belief.
- **The gap:** none of that infrastructure yet produces an actual refill-rate number management is willing to commit to, and the 2026 revenue guide wasn't defended against direct modeling scrutiny — belief and marketing spend are ahead of demonstrated retention data.
- A parallel case of "acting ahead of full certainty": long-acting HIV programs GS-1219 and GS-1614 were discontinued outright to double down on GS-3242 — a real, funded bet rather than a hedge.
