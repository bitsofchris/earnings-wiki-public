# PG — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-10-24)

**The key idea:** P&G posted its 40th straight quarter of organic growth but is quietly playing defense — global share down 30bps, U.S. Fabric Care and Baby Care both admittedly underperforming against a wave of competitive promotion. Management's answer is to fund a much bigger innovation and marketing push (Tide's biggest liquid reformulation in 20 years, Tide evo, Baby Dry restage) by cutting up to 15% of the non-manufacturing workforce and squeezing $1.5B from the supply chain — betting that "integrated superiority" beats price competition on a longer time horizon than Wall Street is comfortable waiting.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Share erosion is real, but management insists it's cyclical, not structural.** Global aggregate share down 30bps with only 24 of 50 category-country combos holding or growing; Schulten's counter is that U.S. absolute share is climbing sequentially ($33.6% → $34.9% over the past 12/6/3/1-month reads) even while annualizing a strong prior-year base.
2. **Restructuring is the actual growth engine, not a cost story.** The $1.5B COGS savings target and 7,000-role cut aren't framed as margin protection — they're explicitly "the enabler for us to... fuel investments in superiority" for the next 12-18 months. Schulten: "the short-term benefit is cost and fuel for us to be able to invest."
3. **China has turned a corner via a full go-to-market rebuild, not just easier comps.** Distributor incentive systems, local-insight innovation, and communication overhauls drove Baby Care +20%, SK-II +12% (even travel retail turned positive), and Fabric Care +5% — a genuine operating turnaround Schulten calls "two points... both pointing in the right direction," with an explicit caution against calling it a straight line.
4. **Guidance headroom is being hoarded, not spent.** Tariff/commodity headwinds roughly halved ($0.39 to $0.19/share) yet full-year EPS guidance was held flat — the freed-up $0.20 is earmarked for reinvestment in competitiveness, an implicit admission the current spend level isn't winning share.
5. **Value bifurcation, not simple trade-down.** Private label share dropped below 16% for the first time (a historical threshold) even as premium tiers drove most category growth — evidence the consumer squeeze is showing up as polarization between value-seeking and premiumizing shoppers, not a uniform pullback.

## Economy & consumer
- **North America consumption decelerated through the quarter**, from ~2.4% value growth entering the year to ~1.8-1.9%, with unit volumes "essentially flat for both markets and P&G brands" — management expects 1.5-2% for the next two quarters.
- **Private label lost ground, not gained it** — shares fell 50bps and dropped below the historically sticky 16% threshold, which Schulten uses as evidence against a simple "consumers trading down" narrative.
- **Bifurcated basket:** premium innovation (Olay super serum, SK-II super premium launches, premium body wash in China) is driving growth in "the majority of growth in our categories," while a paycheck-to-paycheck segment hunts smaller pack sizes and low promoted prices.
- **Europe mirrors North America** — value growth "around 2% weak 2%" with the same competitive dynamics, though European rivals headquartered locally are "getting back in the arena," which Schulten frames as double-edged: some of it is welcome innovation-driven competition, some is "very heavy promotion" (Germany Fabric Care down after being up 33% a year ago).

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Skin & Personal Care led all categories** (high single digits), while **Fabric Care and Family Care declined low singles** — Schulten directly concedes Fabric Care and Baby Care in the U.S. "are not delivering at the level that we want them to deliver."
- **China is the standout turnaround**: organic sales +5%, sixth of seven categories growing, driven by a full go-to-market rebuild — new distributor incentive structure, localized innovation, and closer collaboration with online retail partners.
- **Latin America is quietly excellent**: +7% organic sales, broad-based across Mexico, Brazil (+~30% in Personal Health Care) and smaller distributor markets — the cleanest growth story on the call with no caveats attached.
- **Margins held roughly flat despite the noise**: core operating margin equal to prior year, gross margin down 50bps, offset by 230bps of productivity — the restructuring is already showing up as an offset to cost/currency headwinds, not yet as a growth accelerant.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Innovation spend is being front-loaded and broadened across tiers simultaneously**: Tide liquid's biggest reformulation in 20 years, Tide evo's plastic-free national expansion, a three-tier Cascade Auto Dish reformulation, and Pampers Baby Dry mid-tier restage all launching in the same window — described as deliberate, not scattershot: "where we add new elements to our brands... we ensure the more is sufficient in size to warrant full brand communication."
- **The binding constraint is internal capacity/capital allocation, not external supply** — the entire restructuring logic (headcount cuts, "smaller teams... digitally enabled") exists to free resources for demand-creation spend, explicitly named as the scarce resource management is rationing.
- **Tariff exposure eased mainly via ingredient exclusions** (eucalyptus pulp, psyllium imported from India) and Canada rescinding retaliatory tariffs — full-year tariff cost estimate still sits near $500M pretax, and management flags it "continues to be volatile."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** category growth returns to 3-4% globally (from a weak ~2% today) if superiority investment works — "that's job 1, 2 and 3, drive more users in the category, drive more usage and drive value per use."
- **Action matches on the innovation side**: concrete national launches (Tide evo, Baby Dry) and manufacturing capacity additions are underway, not just promised.
- **Gap:** on guidance, belief that investment will pay off is not yet backed by raised full-year EPS guidance — the extra tariff/commodity headroom is being held as optionality rather than committed to a specific numeric outcome, meaning the "double down" is still unproven in the P&L.
- **U.S. share recovery is explicitly framed as a multi-quarter bet, not an event**: "this plan takes longer. It's not as easy as throwing promotion funding out there" — management is choosing not to chase competitors' promotional intensity dollar-for-dollar.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to raise EPS guidance despite $0.20/share of freed-up headroom** from lower tariff/commodity costs — reserved explicitly for reinvestment optionality given "still early" and "volatile" tariff policy.
- **No large portfolio moves floated** despite a direct question about bigger M&A or culling — Schulten calls current restructuring actions "on the fringes" and states there's "no need to have any transformational acquisition," while leaving the door open only vaguely ("if there is an attractive opportunity, we'll always look at it").
- **Would not commit to a timeline for U.S./Fabric Care share inflection** beyond "confident that the share pattern will follow the same playbook" — no quarter named.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Restructuring execution and morale** — how the 7,000-role cut is landing internally and whether it strengthens or destabilizes competitiveness during a hard market.
- **Category-level competitive intensity** in U.S. Fabric Care and Baby Care — analysts pushed for specifics on promotional response versus the "integrated superiority" answer, sensing the latter is slower and less certain.
- **The 24-of-50 share statistic** drew the sharpest pushback — one analyst called it "a pretty stark statistic" and asked management to walk through the losing combinations rather than accept the aggregate framing.
- **Where the freed-up tariff/commodity guidance cushion is actually being spent** — analysts clearly suspect it's funding defensive promotion rather than the innovation story management prefers to tell.
- Compressed worry: **is "integrated superiority" a genuine multi-year growth engine, or a slower-moving hedge while P&G quietly loses share to more aggressive, faster-moving competitors.**
