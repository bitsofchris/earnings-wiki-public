# VRT — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-11)

**The key idea:** Vertiv posted a blowout quarter — orders up 152% YoY, backlog more than doubled to $15B, book-to-bill near 3x — and used that exact moment to stop reporting quarterly orders and backlog going forward. The tension of the call is that management asks investors to trust a demand story so strong it no longer needs to be shown quarterly, while several analysts politely pushed back on whether the backlog's stretched conversion window (12-18 months vs. the historical 9) is really just "timing" rather than early signs of lumpiness turning into softness.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Best quarter in the company's history, then less disclosure, not more.** Q4 organic orders +152% YoY, book-to-bill 2.9x, backlog "more than double last year's" — and management used this print to announce it will "no longer report actual orders, orders forecast, or backlog with quarterly earnings," shifting to annual-only disclosure in the 10-K. Framed as reducing "unnecessary volatility," but it removes the single most-watched forward indicator right after the best number ever.
2. **CapEx is stepping up structurally, not cyclically.** Vertiv is raising capital spending to 3-4% of sales in 2026 from a historical 2-3%, funding new factories to chase backlog conversion — CFO Chamberlain called it a deliberate near-term drag on incremental margins ("a higher level of investment... has a little bit of pressure on us").
3. **Regional divergence is the real story under the headline growth.** Americas: +46% organic, margin +450bps. APAC: -9% organic, margin -270bps on deleverage (China "muted," rest of Asia "robustly accelerating"). EMEA: -14% organic, margin down to 22.1% from 26.6%, but Giordano Albertazzi calls it "the coiled spring... uncoiling" with orders inflecting.
4. **Backlog is stretching in duration, and management's explanation is pure timing.** Guidance implies ~15 months of backlog-to-revenue conversion versus a typical 9 months. Albertazzi attributes this entirely to phasing of a strong H2/Q4 order intake pushing deliveries into 2027, not to demand softening or execution risk — a claim that deserves tracking next quarter since it's unverifiable without the disclosure just eliminated.
5. **Services and system-level bundling (OneCore, SmartRun, PerchRight) are the actual moat argument.** Life-cycle services orders grew "north of 25%," field service headcount is approaching 5,000, and the PerchRight fluid-management acquisition is pitched as protecting "hardware worth millions of dollars per rack" — this is where Vertiv claims genuine differentiation versus commoditizing power/cooling hardware.

## Economy & consumer
- **Americas is doing all the heavy lifting.** Organic sales +46% in Q4, guided high-30s% growth for 2026 — "The Americas continues to be the primary engine of our growth," per Albertazzi, with pipeline "continu[ing] to grow" even after the huge Q4 order intake.
- **EMEA recovery is sentiment-led, not yet revenue-led.** Sales still down 14% organically in Q4, but Albertazzi describes "a reacceleration in the market" and expects EMEA to "return to sales growth in the second half" of 2026 — a forward claim not yet visible in the numbers.
- **China is flat-out weak, and management won't spin it.** APAC sales down 9% organically "primarily due to macroeconomic conditions in China," expected to stay "muted" through 2026; Albertazzi explicitly rejected the idea that Western vendors are being frozen out, calling it "a general market situation" instead.
- omit consumer — no end-market consumer exposure.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Margin expansion is real but geographically lopsided.** Total adjusted operating margin +170bps to 23.2% in Q4, but this masks Americas margin +450bps against APAC -270bps and EMEA compressing from 26.6% to 22.1% — the consolidated number flatters what is really an Americas-only leverage story.
- **Incrementals are guided at the low end of the long-term range.** CFO Chamberlain: incremental margins are running near 30%, "the low end of the 30% to 35% that we have said in the past," explicitly because of accelerated growth investment — a near-term cost of chasing the backlog.
- **Cash conversion benefited from a one-time-ish dynamic.** Adjusted free cash flow of $910M in Q4 (+151% YoY) was boosted by "larger orders in the quarter [that] came with corresponding larger advance payments" — a positive working-capital swing tied to lumpy big-order timing, flagged by an analyst as unusual for low/medium-voltage electrical equipment.
- **Pricing continues to outrun inflation.** "2025 pricing exceeded inflation, and we expect the same in 2026," with tariff impact expected to be "materially offset" on margin by Q1 2026 exit rate.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx steps up to fund capacity, described as gradual.** Moving from 2-3% to 3-4% of sales for 2026, justified as necessary to convert the elongated backlog; Albertazzi insists "capacity expansion does not happen in big steps... we like many steps that are meaningful," resisting the idea of a lumpy capital response to lumpy orders.
- **Capacity discipline is intentional, not reactive.** Vertiv designs factory loading with "wiggle room" of 20-25% specifically so it can flex output without overbuilding — a stated hedge against demand volatility that also implies genuine limits if demand keeps outrunning the buffer.
- **Field service headcount is the other scarce resource being built out.** Approaching 5,000 people, growing "very similar to the delivery capacity," positioned as necessary infrastructure for commissioning and startups on a rapidly growing installed base.
- **PerchRight acquisition targets a specific technical bottleneck.** Fluid management in chilled-water and liquid-cooled systems is called "one of the most technically demanding and financially consequential aspects of running a modern data center" — a scarcity of specialized expertise Vertiv is buying rather than building.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: orders and backlog will keep growing in 2026 — action: capital committed to capacity, not to more transparency.** Management is directionally confident ("we believe we will continue to build a backlog") but backed away from the disclosure that would let investors verify that belief quarter to quarter, even while simultaneously ramping CapEx to prepare for it.
- **Belief: system-level bundling (OneCore, SmartRun) is winning share — action: real deployments cited (Hut 8, Compass Data Centers) but no market-share numbers given.** The system narrative is qualitatively rich (co-engineering roadmaps "two, three years out" with silicon partners) but quantitatively thin.
- **Belief: EMEA inflects in H2 2026 — action: no incremental EMEA-specific investment detailed**, beyond generic capacity expansion; the reacceleration claim rests on pipeline commentary, not committed spend earmarked to the region.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to affirm or deny content-per-megawatt upside.** Asked directly whether TAM-per-megawatt is rising above the $3-3.5M framework, Albertazzi called it "premature at this stage" and punted to the next quarter.
- **Refused to give a 2027 CapEx-to-sales number**, with Chamberlain saying explicitly "we would not really put a number out there for 2027 yet until we see what the market is going to look like."
- **Would not guide orders directly**, despite multiple analysts pushing for it — Albertazzi said "I would not go all the way to guiding orders," offering only qualitative directional confidence instead, consistent with the newly reduced disclosure policy.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Sustainability and "realness" of the order surge dominated the queue.** Multiple analysts (JPMorgan, Melius, UBS) asked variations of whether the order spike reflects genuine demand versus pull-forward, pricing games, or one-off large deals — Albertazzi repeatedly answered "nothing unusual," "no big anomalies," reiterating that orders are simply "getting bigger" industry-wide.
- **Backlog convertibility and capacity execution was the second cluster.** Analysts wanted to know what could go wrong translating $15B backlog into revenue — supply chain, capacity, or lead times — and whether the stretched 15-month conversion window signals risk; management attributed it purely to order-timing mix.
- **Cooling technology mix (CDU vs. direct cooling vs. chillers) drew a detailed but ultimately hedged answer** — Vertiv argues for a "hybrid" thermal-chain future and dismissed rack-level chip cooling bypassing CDUs as too risky ("blast radius is a little bit too big"), positioning itself as agnostic across designs rather than picking a winner.
- **Compressed worry: is this backlog as durable and deliverable as management insists, or are investors being asked to take that on faith right as the company stops reporting the numbers that would prove it?**
