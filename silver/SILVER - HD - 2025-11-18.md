# HD — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-18)

**The key idea:** Home Depot's headline miss is a weather story on the surface — a near-total absence of storm activity gutted roofing, power generation, and plywood demand — but underneath it, management is quietly conceding that housing turnover at 40-year lows and consumer financing hesitancy have flatlined underlying demand at ~1% for two straight quarters. The GMS acquisition and continued Pro-ecosystem investment show HD betting on wholesale consolidation and share gains to win a flat market, not on a demand recovery it's willing to forecast.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The "storm excuse" is real but is covering for a second flat quarter.** Decker states plainly that stripping out storm effects, "underlying business comp was essentially the exact same as Q2" — call it ~1% both quarters — meaning HD's growth story right now is entirely about outperforming a flat market, not participating in a recovering one.
2. **Housing, not consumer spending power, is the actual constraint.** Turnover is "truly a 40-year low," at 2.9% of housing stock, and home price appreciation is now softening in "even more markets" — Decker frames this as structurally distinct from and more binding than general consumer weakness.
3. **GMS closes the wholesale trifecta but dilutes margins on arrival.** The deal adds ~$2B in sales but roughly 35bps of full-year operating margin pressure (SRS + GMS combined), and McPhail repeatedly steers analysts to full-year rather than quarterly numbers to avoid getting pinned down on messy seasonal comparisons.
4. **Pro backlogs are softening — a leading indicator management doesn't want to over-index on.** "We have seen Pro backlogs and larger backlogs start to diminish a little bit," a shift from a market where "you couldn't find a Pro" — this is the closest thing to a forward-looking crack in the Pro thesis on the call.

## Economy & consumer
- **K-shaped macro, not K-shaped HD demand:** Decker: "you look at GDP, you look at PCE, those are both strong. But on the other hand, what's impacting us... is the ongoing pressure in housing." He explicitly declines to tie any weakness to a specific income cohort.
- **No broad trade-down, but selective softness:** Billy Bastek says the company has "not seen trade down across the broader assortment," aside from previously flagged countertop softness — ticket growth is from mix-up and modest price, not deflation.
- **Financing-dependent projects are the weak link:** "continued pressure in larger discretionary projects where customers typically use financing" — distinguishes big-ticket *item* strength (appliances, power tools) from big-ticket *project* weakness.
- **Affordability and job anxiety cited as the demand suppressant**, not layoffs actually hitting HD's own customer base directly — Decker calls it "ongoing consumer uncertainty... largely now due to living costs... layoffs, increased job concerns."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Comp barely positive, missed plan:** total comp +0.2%, US +0.1%, EPS $3.74 vs $3.78 a year ago — a rare outright EPS decline for HD.
- **Storm-sensitive categories cratered:** roofing, power generation, plywood pressured by zero storm activity versus a heavy 2024; October alone swung to -1.7% US comp as the storm gap widened.
- **Online and fulfillment are genuine bright spots:** digital comp sales +11%, with a "400 basis point increase in customer satisfaction scores" attributed to faster fulfillment — one of the few unambiguous wins on the call.
- **Big-ticket (>$1,000) transactions +2.3%,** but management is explicit this reflects item-level trade-up (appliances, portable power) and Pro managed-account share gains, not a read-through to broader project demand.
- **SRS "comped flat" against an industry down double digits** in roofing shipments — framed as clear share gain inside a shrinking category.

## Investing & scarcity
- **GMS acquisition closed in September**, ~$900M/8 weeks of sales already in Q3 results — positioned as completing a distributor trifecta with SRS for drywall, ceiling, and steel-framing categories.
- **Capex held at ~2.5% of sales** for fiscal 2025 despite the earnings cut — investment pace wasn't touched even as guidance came down.
- **Inventory grew ~$2.3B / turns fell to 4.5x from 4.8x** — mostly GMS consolidation plus deliberate investment in delivery-speed inventory positioning, per McPhail, not a demand-mismatch buildup.
- **The real scarcity is demand, not capacity or capital** — no mention of labor, supply chain, or component constraints; the binding constraint all call long is storm activity, housing turnover, and consumer willingness to finance.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** a ~$50B cumulative "under-spend" in repair/remodel exists from working off COVID pull-forward, and lower rates plus stronger 2026 tax refunds should eventually help — but Decker won't project when that shows up in comps.
- **Action:** continued organic and inorganic investment in the Pro wholesale ecosystem (SRS + GMS, 40-50 new branches/year, tuck-in M&A) and delivery infrastructure, funded regardless of the demand outlook — management frames this as an "asset-light" bet that doesn't require a recovery to pay off through share gains.
- **The gap:** guidance was cut and EPS is now expected to *decline* ~5-6% for the year, yet capex and M&A pace are unchanged — HD is choosing to keep investing through a demand trough rather than pull back, betting the structural share-gain thesis outlasts the cyclical housing pressure.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 guidance or demand-recovery timeline** offered despite repeated analyst prompts; McPhail redirects every Q4-specific margin question back to "full year guide is the right jumping off point."
- **Declined to quantify tariff-driven price elasticity:** Bastek: "it's a little early to say how much of that... elasticity piece will play out."
- **No formula for SRS/GMS margin recovery:** McPhail explicitly says "I wouldn't put a formula on it" when asked to project margin improvement from cross-sell synergies.
- **Won't confirm SRS steps down permanently to low-single-digit growth** — insists it's "principally a storm impacted dynamic," but offers no evidence beyond assertion.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Recurring theme: disentangling GMS/SRS dilution from organic deceleration** — multiple analysts tried to isolate how much of the guide-down is acquisition math (transaction fees, 53rd-week lap, wholesale seasonality) versus genuine demand softening, and management consistently answered with the "full year, not the quarter" framing rather than a clean number.
- **Persistent probing on whether 2026 starts from this ~1% underlying run rate or better** — analysts fished for a bull case (replacement-cycle demand, tax refunds, Fed cuts); Decker conceded the tailwinds exist but wouldn't commit to an inflection.
- **Questions on fixed-cost leverage risk from Pro investment** — addressed directly, with Decker arguing the buildout is "reasonably asset light" and thus a smaller earnings-recovery lever than some feared.
- **The compressed worry:** analysts don't quite believe HD's 1% underlying demand is the trough, but management won't give them a floor or a date — leaving the guide-down looking more open-ended than a one-quarter storm miss would suggest.
