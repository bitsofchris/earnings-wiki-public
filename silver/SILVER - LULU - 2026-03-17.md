# LULU — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-03-17)

**The key idea:** Lululemon is running the company without a permanent CEO through a self-inflicted North America crisis — markdown-trained guests and a stale assortment — while China compensates almost entirely on strength. The entire call is a bet that tripling new-product penetration (23%→35%) and cutting go-to-market time nearly in half can restore full-price selling before the discounting habit becomes structural, with management explicitly telling investors not to expect the inflection until the second half of 2026, bleeding into 2027.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **North America is in a self-imposed markdown detox.** Q4 comps were down 2% domestically even as gross margin collapsed 550bp, driven by a 520bp tariff hit and 130bp of incremental markdowns; management's fix is to *reduce* supply (flat-to-down units, SKU cuts) rather than chase volume, betting that scarcity plus newness restores pricing power.
2. **China is doing the heavy lifting.** Comps +26% in Q4 and a guided +25-30% start to FY26 — Meghan Frank stated plainly international has "not seen the headwind we've seen in North America," making China's momentum the thing funding the U.S. turnaround experiment.
3. **The CEO vacancy is not incidental context — it's the whole call's subtext.** Two interim co-CEOs are running an "action plan" with new board additions (Chip Bergh from Levi's) mid-search, and a proxy contest is explicitly cited as a real cost line in the FY26 guide — governance instability is bleeding into the P&L.
4. **The "transitory" margin framing deserves skepticism.** Management attributes most of the 250bp FY26 operating margin decline to add-backs (incentive comp, labor, proxy costs) they themselves cut the year before — convenient math that makes a second straight year of North America revenue decline look like a rounding error rather than a demand problem.
5. **AI shows up only as an internal tooling bet**, not a product story — capex is going toward "shoring up data and baseline" and a newly hired AI-focused head of technology to compress the 18-24 month go-to-market cycle toward 12-14 months.

## Economy & consumer
- **Tariffs are the only macro variable named**, and they're getting worse before they get better: gross tariff impact rises from $275M (2025) to a guided $380M (2026), with only ~$160M offset expected via "enterprise efficiency."
- **The North American guest is intact in engagement metrics but conditioned on discounts.** André Maestrini says new guest acquisition and retention "will all remain solid," yet Meghan Frank concedes Canada's consumer is "a little bit more sensitive to markdown," which is why Canada guides *worse* than the US despite historically outperforming it.
- **Management dodged the high-value-guest question directly.** Asked whether high-value guests were returning as newness flows in, Frank answered: "I'd like a little more time just to understand what's going on with the high-value guest" — a tell that the core repeat-purchase cohort hasn't clearly turned yet.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working: China and rest-of-world.** China Mainland revenue +28% in Q4 (comps +26%) despite calendar headwinds from shifted 11/11 and Chinese New Year timing; rest-of-world grew comps +5% on strength in South Korea and EMEA (100th store opened in Warsaw).
- **What's not: North America full-price economics.** Full-year 2025 markdown penetration rose 60bp, with a 130bp spike in Q4 alone — "the lowest waterline" of the year, per Frank — driving gross margin down 550bp to 54.9% and operating margin down to 22.3% from 28.9% year-over-year.
- **EPS cratered on the margin compression:** Q4 diluted EPS was $5.01 vs. $6.14 a year ago, and FY26 guidance of $12.10-$12.30 sits below FY25's $13.26 — a guided second consecutive year of profit decline.
- **Store productivity remains a genuine strength**: new store openings are returning "above 100% ROI across both North America and international markets, representing a payback period of less than a year," and top large-format stores post sales productivity "over $1,400" per square foot.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Store growth is being redirected toward China/international**: of ~40-45 planned net new stores in FY26, only ~15 are in North America (mostly Mexico), with 25-30 in international markets, majority in China — a clear capital-allocation vote of confidence away from the US.
- **The binding constraint they're choosing is scarcity of product, not scarcity of supply chain capacity.** Inventory units are guided flat-to-down for FY26, an explicit tactic to force full-price realization rather than a supply constraint imposed on them — this is self-inflicted discipline, not an external binding limit.
- **AI/tech investment is narrowly scoped to internal enablement**: "shoring up our data and baseline," a new AI-focused head of technology, and automation aimed at compressing the go-to-market calendar — no AI-driven customer-facing product mentioned. Limited direct relevance to observability/infra vendors beyond generic enterprise data-platform investment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: North America full-price trend flips positive in 2H 2026.** Action: newness penetration triples from 23% to 35%, three new product platforms launched (Unrestricted Power, ShowZero, ThermoZen), and SKU reduction to sharpen the assortment — money and product roadmap are actually behind this, not just talk.
- **Belief: shorter go-to-market cycles will let them "read and react" to guest demand.** Action: go-to-market timeline target cut from 18-24 months to 12-14 months, backed by new tools/process investment and the AI-focused technology hire — a real commitment, though the timeline itself signals this is a multi-year unwind, not a quick fix.
- **Gap:** management projects confidence in "green shoots" language throughout, yet simultaneously guides North America revenue DOWN 1-3% for the full year and declines to quantify the actual full-price percentage — the stated optimism isn't matched by a willingness to commit to a number.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Declined to break out actual full-price/markdown penetration percentages** for 2026, offering only directional commentary ("meaningful improvement," "modest improvement") instead of numbers investors could hold them to.
- **No commitment on operating margin recovery trajectory beyond 2026**: "We haven't put a fine point on the leverage aspect... I think it'll depend on the trajectory of the business."
- **Real estate commitments hedged as "case-by-case"** despite giving a topline square-footage growth number, leaving room to pull back if a new CEO wants "a fresh look."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Nearly every question was a variant of "when does North America actually inflect,"** probed from multiple angles: the bridge from Q4's 6% underlying growth to 1-3% Q1 guidance, the sequencing of full-price recovery quarter by quarter, and Canada's steeper-than-US markdown sensitivity.
- **Margin quality was directly challenged** — one analyst pushed on how much of the 250bp operating margin decline is "transitory," getting Frank to concede it's "the majority... add backs of incentive comp and labor... and the proxy contest expenses," effectively admitting the guide bakes in one-time costs stacked atop real tariff and mix pressure.
- **Newness mechanics (what's being sunset, who owns merchandising decisions, AI capex allocation) got unusually granular scrutiny**, reflecting analyst skepticism that a stated jump from 23% to 35% newness is credible without organizational change to back it.
- The compressed worry: **can Lululemon retrain a North American guest it spent two years teaching to wait for a markdown, fast enough to hit a second-half-2026 inflection, before a still-unnamed CEO potentially resets the whole plan.**
