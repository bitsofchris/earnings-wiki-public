# TXN — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-22)

**The key idea:** TI delivered a blowout quarter (+19% YoY, above guide) riding a broad industrial and data-center recovery, and used the call to announce the Silicon Labs acquisition — but every answer about the second half of 2026 came wrapped in an explicit callback to 2025, when an equally strong start "took a breather" and never accelerated. This is a company trying to convince the Street its upcycle is different while refusing to actually promise that it is.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The "2025 false start" is the whole call.** CEO Ilan brought up last year's fade unprompted, multiple times, essentially pre-empting the bear case: "We had a good year in Analog, but it did not accelerate in the second half. It actually slowed down a little bit." Every forward-looking answer is qualified against that memory.
2. **Data center is now TI's standout growth engine, not just an industrial story.** Revenue there grew ~90% YoY and >25% sequentially, and Ilan pitched TI's moat as breadth (thousands of general-purpose SKUs per rack) plus reliable US-based supply when competitors have shortages: "we have seen cases where our customers needed help because they had supply shortages from their other suppliers, and we come in and solve the problem."
3. **Pricing is quietly turning.** After a year of low-single-digit YoY price declines, Q1 pricing was flat, and Ilan floated H2 increases contingent on demand holding — a notable inflection for an industry that's spent years in price erosion, but he stopped well short of committing to it.
4. **Assembly/test, not wafer capacity, is the constraint that's actually biting.** TI has ample fab capacity and inventory, but is redirecting CapEx to internalize back-end (assembly/test) supply because external bottlenecks are emerging — a supply-chain tell for anyone watching semis broadly.
5. **Silicon Labs acquisition adds real near-term GAAP noise.** CFO Lizardi flagged ongoing acquisition charges each quarter until the 2027 close (then a step-up), and committed to reporting GAAP with supplemental line items rather than pro forma — a transparency stance Bernstein's analyst explicitly praised relative to peers.

## Economy & consumer
- **Broad-based industrial reacceleration:** "all sectors, all geographies grew sequentially," including the small-customer "tail" waking up after a "long hibernation period" — the broadest industrial signal in multiple quarters.
- **Auto diverging by geography:** China auto down, rest-of-world up, net flat sequentially; Ilan frames auto as "steady at an elevated level," recalling that autos were "the last to join in, also the last to peak" in the COVID cycle.
- **No visible consumer demand destruction from memory costs:** personal electronics ran flat YoY / slightly above seasonal, though management's own caveat ("you can't rule that out") undercuts the reassurance.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Beat-and-raise on broad strength:** $4.8B revenue (+9% QoQ, +19% YoY), gross margin up 210bps to 58%, operating profit +37% YoY — Analog +22% YoY, Embedded +12% YoY, "Other" -16% YoY (the one soft spot, unaddressed on the call).
- **Pricing stabilized for the first time in a year:** flat sequentially and YoY versus the typical low-single-digit seasonal decline, which Ilan called "better than our model."
- **Inventory being deliberately drawn down to serve demand:** days fell from 222 to 209, consistent with an upturn playbook where inventory should "drift toward the lower end" of the 150-250 day range.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Silicon Labs acquisition** to expand embedded wireless connectivity, expected to close H1 2027; will carry recurring acquisition charges (cash cost, then non-cash post-close) that CFO Lizardi asked analysts to strip out manually rather than TI reporting pro forma.
- **2026 CapEx held at $2-3B**, with a rising share going to assembly/test rather than wafer fabs — "we are seeing more and more bottlenecks in the market that are popping up" externally in AT, and TI's response is to internalize that capacity.
- **Long-term CapEx framework reaffirmed at ~1.2x revenue growth** (5% revenue growth → ~6% CapEx intensity), with depreciation guided at $2.2-2.4B for 2026 and "continued upward pressure" (at a slower rate) into 2027.
- **CHIPS Act funding still flowing:** received over $500M in Q1 (part of $630M received to date out of up to $1.6B in direct funding); Lizardi flagged the 35% investment tax credit as the more durable long-term benefit versus the one-time direct funding.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** demand strength could justify H2 price increases and accelerating application-specific data-center socket wins into 2027. **Action:** no committed price hikes yet — "this is going to be a case-by-case discussion" — and CapEx stayed at the previously-guided $2-3B rather than being raised despite 17-18% YoY H1 growth. The gap is explicit: TI is positioned to move fast (modulating wafer starts "real time," internalizing AT capacity) but isn't yet spending ahead of confirmed demand.
- **Belief:** industrial and data center still have real room to run since industrial remains ~15% below its 2022 peak. **Action:** inventory is being drawn down to fund the current surge rather than building further — a bet that existing capacity plus depleting inventory is sufficient, not a signal of confidence requiring new capacity commitments.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to break out Q2 guidance by end market:** "I won't break it out between the two [industrial and data center], but we see strength in both."
- **Refused to characterize H2 2026 at all**, repeating "let 2Q play out" as the load-bearing phrase across multiple questions on demand sustainability, pricing, and CapEx.
- **Wouldn't quantify free cash flow per share upside** despite acknowledging H1 tracking 15-20% revenue growth versus the framework's baseline: "I'm not going to say what the number is."

## The street — what analysts asked
- Nearly every question — from UBS, BofA, Morgan Stanley, Bernstein, Deutsche Bank, Stifel, Cantor, Wells Fargo, Wolfe — circled the same anxiety: is this a durable analog/industrial upcycle or a repeat of 2025's early-strength-then-fade pattern.
- Secondary cluster on **gross margin mechanics and Silicon Labs GAAP treatment** (Bernstein pressed hard on how accretion claims reconcile with GAAP given ongoing acquisition charges).
- Management's consistent dodge was to defer the real answer to the July call, while emphasizing readiness ("we are prepared for every scenario") rather than commitment.
- **Compressed worry:** the Street doesn't doubt Q1's strength — it doubts whether TI (or Ilan himself) actually knows if this cycle behaves differently than the one that fizzled last year.
