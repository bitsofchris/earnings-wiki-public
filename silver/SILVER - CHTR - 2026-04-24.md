# CHTR — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-24)

**The key idea:** Charter's standalone business is stuck in a low-teens ARPU, low-churn holding pattern — good retention, weak new-customer sales — while nearly all strategic energy goes into closing and integrating Cox, which management pitches as a chance to relaunch broadband under a "big bang" new brand with lower pricing, mobile/video attach, and years of capex discipline behind it. The tension: management insists the growth problem is "top of funnel," not product or price, while simultaneously declining to commit to the pricing tools (price locks, broadband price hikes) that competitors are using to solve exactly that problem.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
2. **Cox is the whole strategy now.** All approvals are in except California; management is banking on a "big bang" re-entry with a new brand, lower retail/promo broadband pricing, free mobile for a year, and a fully built-out video product — explicitly contrasted with the underdeveloped Cox mobile/video penetration. Synergy estimate raised from $500M to at least $800M with more expected.
3. **Capex discipline is the real bull case being sold to investors.** Post-2028 run-rate capex "should be below $8 billion," down from ~$11.7B in 2025 — CFO Jessica Fischer quantified this as equivalent to "over $28 of free cash flow per share" and implying a 25%+ FCF yield on the current stock price if 2028 capex is substituted into consensus 2026 FCF.
4. **Management explicitly declines the pricing tools its peers are using.** No commitment to five-year price locks (tried, "haven't seen the necessary lift") and no commitment to a 2026 broadband price increase — leaving Internet ARPU growth for the year "close either way."
5. **AI shows up only in service ops, not infrastructure economics.** "New AI tools now used by our service agents, driving higher customer satisfaction and reducing call times" — a customer-service application, not a capacity or cost story; no mention of AI as a network-demand driver beyond generic upstream growth from "self-driving cars."

## Economy & consumer
- **Housing is the named macro drag.** "A continued muted housing environment with slow household formation and... low move rates" is cited as an external structural headwind to broadband sales, alongside "mobile substitution growth" that "seems to be slowing a little bit, hopefully."
- **Consumers are trading down, not out.** Residential revenue per customer fell 1.4% y/y as customers shift into lower-priced video packages; ex-streaming-app-allocation headwinds, per-customer revenue actually grew 0.3%, so the headline decline is partly a reporting artifact of Charter absorbing streaming-app costs into video revenue ($218M netted vs. $47M prior year).
- **Political advertising was the one clean tailwind** — total ad revenue grew 5.3% y/y on political spend; ex-political, advertising fell 3.4%, underscoring that core ad demand is soft.
- **Movers are a net positive, not a risk**, per CFO: "more movement between homes in the marketplace, and more movers actually is an overall benefit" given Charter's footprint scale — a data point that runs against the instinct that housing churn hurts an incumbent.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Video and voice losses improved sharply.** Video customer loss of 60K was less than a third of the prior year's 181K loss, "driven by significant product improvements," and voice losses improved on lower churn.
- **Internet sales, not retention, are the weak point.** 120K Internet customer loss driven by "lower connects year-over-year, partly offset by slightly lower churn" — competitive intensity from fixed wireless, fiber overbuild, and mobile substitution named as causes.
- **Mobile growth remains strong but decelerating on net adds.** 370K net Spectrum Mobile lines added, 17%+ line growth over 12 months, but net adds were suppressed by "heavy device subsidy activity by the big telco competitors, including the iPhone 17" — a direct read on carrier promotional intensity.
- **Streaming-app attach is quietly working as a retention lever.** Over 50% of expanded-basic video customers have activated at least one bundled streaming app (averaging ~4 apps), and churn for that cohort is a third lower — a genuine product win buried under the headline video-revenue accounting noise.
- **Adjusted EBITDA declined 1.8%** (ex-transition costs) largely on a prior-year one-time benefit, not underlying deterioration — management still guides to slight full-year EBITDA growth.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex is front-loaded now, promised to fall later.** Q1 capex of $2.9B was $456M higher y/y on network evolution spend and new CPE (Wi-Fi 7 routers, Invincible WiFi); full-year 2026 guide holds at ~$11.4B, with the real investment pitch being the drop to sub-$8B by 2028.
- **Invincible WiFi hit a physical supply constraint, not a demand one.** "The upgrade and attach rate was much higher than expected, and we've had to prioritize our supply to a smaller audience until we get the right level of supply" — a rare admission of underestimating a product launch.
- **Network upgrade pacing is deliberately sequenced around the Cox deal.** ~50% of the Spectrum network will be symmetrical/multi-gig by year-end; Cox's mid-split work is "nearly complete," letting Charter defer higher-cost DOCSIS 4.0/high-split there until after finishing its own footprint — explicit capital-timing optionality from the acquisition.
- **Upstream bandwidth is the named forward network constraint:** "20% annual growth driven by things like self-driving cars, a significant and increasing upload" is cited as the reason symmetrical/multi-gig upgrades matter longer-term.
- **B2B network assets (edge cache, GPU-as-a-service, sub-10ms latency offerings) are an emerging, still-small investment thread** — mentioned as differentiated infrastructure (AWS authenticated offload, Spectrum Front Row) but with no revenue disclosure, signaling early-stage optionality rather than a committed capital program.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** broadband growth returns once housing recovers and execution improves. **Action:** no near-term marketing spend increase planned — "I don't think we need to spend anything more in marketing" — the bet is entirely on message discipline and Cox-driven footprint expansion, not new investment.
- **Belief:** Cox integration will preserve or grow customer-level ARPU despite lower reported broadband ARPU. **Action:** committing $800M+ in synergy capture, new field sales force, new brand rollout, and insourced same-day service infrastructure across the Cox footprint — this is the one area where stated belief and hard capital/headcount commitment are fully aligned.
- **Belief:** satellite (Starlink, Amazon) is "more friend than foe" long-term. **Action:** exploring becoming a satellite reseller bundled with Invincible WiFi backup — an actual product move, still speculative and unconfirmed ("we're thinking through all those things").
- **Belief:** further cable M&A makes sense given regulatory logic (regional players facing national/global competitors). **Action:** none currently — "there's nothing that we're looking at today or doing today other than just finishing the Cox transaction," a clean stated-belief-without-action gap the CEO flagged himself.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 broadband price increase commitment:** "We haven't made any decisions on that front yet," despite acknowledging inflationary cost pressure in parts of the business.
- **No five-year price-lock commitment** matching Comcast/Optimum, despite having trialed it: "we haven't seen the necessary lift ourselves," with a hedge that trial scale may have understated the potential ("maybe that's because we didn't do it at scale").
- **Declined to name any specific M&A target or process** beyond generic regulatory framing — "I'm not commenting on any particular company or assets."
- **Internet/broadband ARPU guidance for the year kept deliberately vague** — "close either way" — rather than a directional commitment.
