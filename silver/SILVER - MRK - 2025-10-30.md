# MRK — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** Merck's two legacy pillars are cracking at different speeds — GARDASIL down sharply, KEYTRUDA nearing peak penetration with a slower 2026 ahead — just as the company throws its balance sheet behind a wall of new launches (WINREVAIR, OHTUVAYRE, CAPVAXIVE, ENFLONSIA) and a record ~80-trial Phase III pipeline. The bet is that the launch cohort and pipeline conversion outrun the LOE cliff (BRIDION, JANUVIA, DIFICID) and GARDASIL's structural decline before 2026-2027 growth has to prove itself without KEYTRUDA doing the heavy lifting.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **GARDASIL's decline is now structural, not seasonal.** Reported sales fell 25%, and even ex-China the franchise is down 3%, with management attributing adolescent softness to "a reduction in the eligible population" and unspecified "macro factors" — a euphemism covering vaccine-hesitancy headwinds it won't name directly. Management guides only to "modest growth" going forward, a marked reset from prior "robust return to growth" consensus expectations the analyst explicitly flagged.
2. **KEYTRUDA is transitioning off its own growth engine.** CFO Caroline Litchfield confirmed 2026 KEYTRUDA growth will be "slightly slower" as the drug approaches "peak penetration" in existing indications, even as early-stage-cancer use — now "exceeding 25%" of sales and "driving over half" of growth — becomes the primary offset. The subcutaneous QLEX launch is deliberately throttled by reimbursement mechanics: no permanent J-code for ~6 months, which management says will suppress uptake regardless of clinical demand.
3. **The launch numbers are inflated by channel stocking, not demand — and management says so.** ENFLONSIA's $79M and WINREVAIR's quarterly swing both got explicit "this was stocking, not usage" caveats from Caroline Litchfield — an unusually direct admission that headline revenue for the new-launch story is running ahead of actual patient uptake this quarter.
4. **Capital is shifting hard toward domestic manufacturing and pipeline breadth, not M&A scale.** The $70B+ US manufacturing/R&D commitment and Verona Pharma's OHTUVAYRE ($500M+ incremental 2026 investment behind it) show real dollars behind stated strategy. CEO Rob Davis explicitly rejected "transformative," synergy-driven mega-mergers as "disruptive" to the pipeline, capping BD appetite at $1B-$15B — a clear, repeatable answer, not a hedge.
5. **Policy risk is unresolved and unquantified heading into 2026.** Management repeatedly declined to give any MFN/tariff-deal specifics despite Pfizer and AstraZeneca already having struck administration agreements, saying only it is "optimistic" — leaving a real 2026 P&L variable (foreign price increases vs. US out-of-pocket cuts) completely outside guidance.

## Economy & consumer

- **US drug pricing policy, not consumer demand, is the macro axis this quarter.** Davis: "we share the administration's goal of decreasing patient out-of-pocket costs... while at the same time, realizing greater prices for our medicines and vaccines in countries that have not been paying fair value" — a direct alignment statement issued without a term sheet.
- **Animal Health decoupled consumer (companion) from commercial (livestock) demand.** Companion animal sales fell 3% on "a reduction in vet visits and competition," while livestock grew 14% on institutional/farm demand — a split worth watching as a proxy for discretionary household spend on pets.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Oncology is the reliable engine.** KEYTRUDA +8% to $8.1B and WELIREG +41% (still small at $196M) both grew on "strong demand" language backed by real indication expansion (10 approved earlier-stage uses, 5 now with overall-survival data).
- **The vaccine franchise is uneven at best.** GARDASIL -25% reported; VAXNEUVANCE -7% on a "competitive preferential recommendation" loss in Japan; CAPVAXIVE ($244M) is the bright spot, benefiting from "seasonal inventory build" as much as underlying demand.
- **New launches are early and stocking-driven.** WINREVAIR's $360M included a ~$40M negative one-time distributor-timing hit that "fully reversed in October," and ENFLONSIA's initial $79M was explicitly "stocking ahead of expected usage," not season-open sell-through.
- **Margin is healthy on mix, not efficiency.** Gross margin rose 1.4 points to 81.9% on "favorable product mix," while opex actually fell (helped by a drop in BD charges from $2.2B to $300M) — a comp effect, not a structural efficiency gain, and management flags opex will "accelerate" in 2026.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Manufacturing reshoring is the largest capital commitment on the call.** "More than $70 billion" in domestic manufacturing and R&D, headlined by the Elkton, Virginia groundbreaking — sized explicitly as a hedge against tariff and policy exposure, not just capacity growth.
- **Verona/OHTUVAYRE gets dedicated 2026 spend.** ">$0.5 billion of investment to maximize the potential of OHTUVAYRE" in 2026 alone — real incremental commercial spend behind a freshly closed acquisition, not a placeholder line.
- **The actual constraint is administrative, not physical.** The 6-month wait for a permanent QLEX J-code is a self-identified bottleneck on revenue realization — a reimbursement-mechanics constraint, not compute, supply chain, or people, and one management says it has tried to engineer around via "commercial contracting strategy" rather than can actually remove.
- **R&D breadth continues to expand rather than concentrate.** ~80 Phase III trials running, 15+ started in 2025 with "an increasing number" planned for 2026 — capital is being spread across cardiopulmonary, immunology, HIV, ophthalmology and oncology simultaneously rather than concentrated in fewer high-conviction bets.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief: 2026 top-line growth will be "solid," fueled by the launch cohort.** Action backing it up: dedicated incremental spend behind OHTUVAYRE, continued Phase III expansion, and Japan/international rollout of WINREVAIR — real dollars follow the stated belief here.
- **Belief: MFN/pricing dynamics will resolve constructively.** Action: none disclosed. Management repeatedly declined to share deal terms, timeline, or even a framework — a case where the stated optimism runs well ahead of any visible commitment, unlike Pfizer and AstraZeneca who already signed.
- **Belief: QLEX will hit 30-40% patient adoption within 18-24 months.** Action: real — a "commercial contracting strategy" designed to make conversion "frictionless," explicitly built from lessons learned off rival subcutaneous launches. This is one of the tighter belief-to-action pairs on the call.
- **Belief: large-scale M&A isn't warranted given pipeline strength.** Action matches word: Verona ($1-15B range) is cited as the model going forward, explicitly rejecting synergy-driven mega-deals as "disruptive" — a consistent, unhedged position.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No 2026 revenue guidance whatsoever**, despite giving detailed opex and pipeline commentary — Litchfield: gave "pushes and pulls" but declined numeric targets.
- **No MFN/tariff deal terms or timeline**, even after being asked directly whether the Pfizer/AstraZeneca framework applies to Merck: "I'm not going to give any specific updates."
- **No US/ex-US revenue mix projection**, when asked directly about the 5-year outlook on the current ~50/50 split: "I'm not going to get into specific guidance... it's too early to say."
- **No BRUNELLO (EyeBio/MK-3000) success threshold** given despite being asked point-blank what "bar" defines trial success — Dean Li deferred to "let's see the data" rather than pre-committing to an endpoint bar.
