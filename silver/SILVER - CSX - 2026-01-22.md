# CSX — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-22)

**The key idea:** A new CEO (three months in) is resetting expectations downward on the calendar but not on ambition — pulling the long-term 2025-2027 targets, replacing them with one year of guidance built almost entirely on self-help cost cuts, and asking the Street for patience ("a few quarters") before he'll commit to a margin destination or timeline. The tension: management insists the 200-300bps of margin expansion "is not depending on the market," yet roughly $150M of it is simply non-recurring 2025 charges rolling off — a mix of genuine productivity work and easy comps dressed the same way.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Long-term guidance got pulled, not extended.** Angel replaced the 2024 Investor Day's 2025-2027 targets with 2026-only guidance, saying the "macroeconomic environment and industry dynamics were meaningfully different" — a tacit admission the old targets were dead, without offering a new destination.
2. **The margin bridge is half comp relief, half real cuts.** CFO Boone quantified ~$150M of non-recurring 2025 charges (severance, tech write-offs, Blue Ridge/Howard Street disruption costs) rolling off — a chunk of the 200-300bps guide analysts had to drag out of him rather than getting it upfront.
3. **Intermodal and Howard Street Tunnel are the lone bright growth spots.** Intermodal revenue was up sharply on volume gains and new lanes; the Howard Street double-stack clearance (first of two bridges done) lets West Coast freight reach Baltimore without a Chicago rubber-tire transfer, with customers "bidding on business now" for Q2 start.
4. **Coal is a mix story, not a demand story.** Domestic utility tonnage rose on power demand and higher natural gas prices, but export met coal pricing stayed weak and the East Coast-to-Australian benchmark discount widened, denting yield even as tonnage grew.
5. **The Norfolk Southern-adjacent mega-merger looms but gets no real strategic answer.** Angel's stance is essentially "run the business better regardless" — he explicitly won't game out contingencies publicly, calling it a process that could take years ("deal purgatory").

## Economy & consumer
- **No catalyst on the horizon.** Chief Commercial Officer Kenny: "there is no short-term catalyst on the horizon to lift the major industrial market," with 2026 guidance built on flat industrial production and modest GDP growth.
- **Chemicals and forest products are the weak spots.** Chemicals volume fell as customers "carefully controlling freight spend" amid inflation/tariff pressure; forest products volume dropped sharply on pulp/container-board plant closures.
- **Auto is supply-constrained, not demand-driven.** Automotive volume fell as chip and metals shortages limited output at some plants even as others gained momentum — plus a large customer plant is scheduled to be down for all of 2026.
- **No consumer-facing segment** — CSX is a B2B freight carrier; end-consumer conditions show up only indirectly via housing/auto/industrial demand, which management flags as a headwind (housing starts expected to decline modestly).

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Service and safety hit multi-year highs.** COO Cory: velocity, cars-online, dwell, and trip-plan compliance "all showed substantial improvement from Q1 to Q4," with the best injury/accident rates of the year in Q4.
- **But P&L results moved the wrong way.** Q4 operating income and EPS fell sharply year over year on flat volume and declining revenue, even before counting the ~$50M in separation/technology charges.
- **Merchandise franchise is structurally under pressure.** Volume and revenue both fell, driven by market softness rather than execution — a genuinely different problem from the coal/service issues of prior years.
- **Fertilizer and minerals are underappreciated strength.** Fertilizer volume rose on phosphate rock production and new nitrogen business; minerals stayed supported by infrastructure-driven aggregate and cement demand.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx cut hard for 2026** — guided below $2.4B, "a substantial reduction from last year," as Blue Ridge wraps and management tightens ROI screening on every capital dollar.
- **Depreciation flat despite normal asset growth**, offset by an equipment life study, asset retirements, and cuts to technology/aviation assets — a sign of belt-tightening beyond headcount.
- **The binding constraint is demand, not capacity.** CEO Angel: they will "be ready and able to respond when demand increases" — the network has slack built in, unlike the service-crisis years.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: modest 2026 improvement, no macro help required.** Guidance calls for low-single-digit revenue growth and 200-300bps of margin expansion "based on flat industrial production" — explicitly not counting on the economy turning.
- **Action: aggressive but diffuse cost-cutting.** Headcount down 3%+, "well over 100 diverse savings initiatives," a new incentive-comp structure tied to three metrics (operating margin, operating income, safety), and a promise of "an additional list" of cuts by February.
- **The gap:** CFO Boone says the plan is "not depending on the market," but when pressed for a hard-dollar bridge, the biggest single component (~$150M) is simply non-recurring 2025 charges — genuine incremental productivity is real but smaller and less specified than the headline 200-300bps suggests.
- **Pricing is a multi-year project, not a 2026 lever.** Angel: it will "take until about this time next year before we had a chance to touch every contract" — pricing gains are directionally better than 2025 but structurally slow due to contract roll-off timing.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No long-term margin target or timeline.** Angel repeatedly declined to name a destination OR or a "sub-60" level, saying only "I know what that is... I have confidence we can get there," and asking for "a few quarters" before committing.
- **No multiyear guidance framework.** The 2025-2027 Investor Day targets were formally retired and replaced with one year only — a real hedge given the framework's public prior existence.
- **No scenario planning shared on the pending mega-merger.** Angel: "I don't know what conditions are gonna be required for approval... this is a long process" — he declined to game out approved-vs-blocked strategy publicly.
- **No hard headcount or dollar target disclosed** for the "100+ savings initiatives" despite direct analyst requests to bucketize them — management offered qualitative color (PS&O vs. labor split) but not a dollar table.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: (1) **is the 200-300bps margin guide real productivity or comp relief** — asked from multiple angles (Ossenbeck, Hoexter, McKinney/Deutsche Bank, Vernon) and never fully bridged with hard numbers; (2) **where's the long-term margin destination** now that 2025-2027 targets are gone (Group, Rosa, Shanker) — met with "give me time to get grounded" rather than a number; (3) **storm/operational execution risk** given last year's multi-quarter network meltdown (Rosa, Oglenski) — management gave a confident, detailed readiness answer here, the one question fully answered with specifics.
- The Norfolk Southern-related mega-merger drew only one direct question and got a deliberately noncommittal answer, suggesting management doesn't want to be pinned down on strategy for a deal outcome that's still years from resolution.
- Compressed worry: **is CSX's 2026 margin story productivity or just easy comps, and will a first-year CEO actually attach a number to "best-in-class" before the market loses patience?**
