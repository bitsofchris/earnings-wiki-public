# ADP — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-01-28)

**The key idea:** ADP delivered a clean beat-and-raise quarter — broad-based bookings, record client satisfaction, reaccelerating enterprise wins on its Lyric platform — while quietly absorbing a soft spot in the PEO business, where worksite-employee growth, bookings, and pays-per-control all came in a notch below plan. The subtext across the whole call is management actively hunting for AI-driven layoff signals in its own payroll data and coming up empty, positioning ADP's dataset as a real-time referee in the AI-jobs debate even as it invests heavily in AI tooling for its own sales force and products.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **PEO is the one soft data point in an otherwise strong quarter.** PEO revenue ex. pass-throughs decelerated sharply from 6% to 3% quarter over quarter, driven by softer worksite-employee growth, a tough prior-year SUI comp, and slower wage growth; CFO Peter Hadley: "we still think there is plenty of room to grow," but guidance was walked toward the low end of the range.
2. **Enterprise HCM (Lyric) is the standout growth engine.** Two new client wins exceeded 20,000 employees each — the largest sold on the platform to date — with 70%+ of Lyric bookings and pipeline coming from new logos, a clear share-gain signal against incumbents.
3. **Management used its own payroll data to push back on AI-layoff narratives.** Hadley: looking at 10-12 industry groups including those "you may think of as being more subject to being at risk with AI" — financial services, professional services, tech — "we are actually seeing reasonably healthy growth," directly contradicting the framing behind recent AI-layoff headlines.
4. **Client satisfaction hit a record high while retention kept sliding — a real divergence.** CEO Maria Black called it "the single best quarter in ADP history" for satisfaction, yet retention still declined modestly, attributed to a deliberate plan for out-of-business normalization rather than any service failure — plausible, but a pairing worth tracking each quarter.
5. **International is a long-duration bet paying off slowly.** A 75,000-employee European bank win was cited as a flagship proof point, but both Black and Hadley stressed these enterprise/international deals "take quite some time to come through to revenue generation" and haven't moved ADP's overall revenue mix yet.

## Economy & consumer
- **Labor market "stable" but muted, no AI-layoff signal found in the data.** Hadley: hiring and job openings remain muted, layoffs continue broadly, but "we are not really seeing anything discernible" tying AI specifically to elevated layoffs in exposed industry verticals — most recent layoff headlines are framed as post-pandemic hiring "corporate realignment," not AI substitution.
- **Small-business segment more resilient than the broader economy.** Hadley noted ADP's own down-market base "tended to prove to be more resilient... with respect to hiring than the wider small business segment," implying the broader small-business labor market is softer than what shows up in ADP's client base.
- **No direct consumer-demand read** — this is a B2B HR/payroll platform, not a consumer-facing business.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broad-based bookings strength.** 6% reported revenue growth, 80bps adjusted EBIT margin expansion, 11% adjusted EPS growth, with new-business bookings growth spread across international, US enterprise, compliance, small business, and mid-market — "every single business contributed to that growth narrative."
- **Record client satisfaction, best quarter in company history**, attributed to AI tooling (ADP Assist agents), the Workforce Suite integration, and internal productivity investments — Black linked this directly to retention support, though the retention number itself still declined.
- **PEO the clear underperformer this quarter.** Bookings "came in slightly below our expectations," worksite-employee growth guidance cut to about 2% for the year, and pays-per-control softened to match (rather than lead) the ES segment for the first time in a while.
- **Pricing holding steady, not accelerating.** ~100bps of price contribution expected for fiscal 2026 — "a little lower... than what we had in fiscal 2025," suggesting no unusual pricing power being exercised despite strong demand.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Product investment concentrated in Workforce Now NextGen, Lyric, and the new Workforce Suite** (unifying time/pay/HR from the Workforce Software acquisition), plus embedding Fiserv's Cash Flow Central into RUN for small-business cash management — described as still pre-revenue ("just completed that integration in December").
- **New pooled-employer 401(k) plan (PEP)** launched in retirement services, shifting fiduciary/compliance burden from employers to ADP as pooled plan provider — a scale-and-stickiness play, not a near-term revenue driver.
- **The binding constraint is distribution, not capital or compute.** Growth is gated by seller headcount, lead-targeting sophistication, and account-mining tools ("investments into tools, technology, to figure out who the exact right fit is") — management explicitly said AI has not changed sales headcount plans, only seller productivity.
- **$6B buyback authorization** (replacing the $5B 2022 program) plus a 10% dividend increase signal confidence but also that capital allocation is tilted toward returns over major new investment categories this cycle.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** retention will keep drifting down modestly (10-30bps for the year) as out-of-business rates normalize toward pre-pandemic levels — a structural planning assumption, not a market-share worry.
- **Action:** heavy reinvestment in AI-driven client-facing tools (ADP Assist payroll/HR/tax/analytics agents) and internal associate tools, explicitly framed as the retention/satisfaction lever — yet this hasn't yet reversed the retention decline, so the causal claim is stated confidence rather than demonstrated result.
- **Belief:** international and enterprise (Lyric) are the biggest multi-year growth opportunities, "the strength that we see in our offering is just getting started."
- **Action:** the 75,000-employee European bank win and the >20,000-employee Lyric wins are real, funded proof points — but management is candid that these deals "take quite some time to come through to revenue generation" and have not yet shifted overall revenue mix, so the money and pipeline are ahead of the P&L impact.
- **Belief:** AI won't materially displace the human-anchored nature of payroll/HR work over the medium term — Black: "do I see a world where a bunch of humanoids are going to be sleeping in offices to get payroll done... I cannot see it."
- **Action:** simultaneously scaling AI agents deeply into the product and sales stack — a real tension between a "AI won't replace the human core" narrative and an aggressive AI-automation roadmap, though management frames it as augmentation rather than substitution.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to specify where in the 3-5% PEO revenue-ex-pass-through range they'll land**, citing bookings, pays-per-control, and retention as still-open variables for the back half.
- **Declined to give quarter-specific ES pays-per-control guidance**, framing the flat-for-the-year outlook as "either tens of basis points above or one or two... below the half a point mark" rather than committing to a number.
- **Declined to characterize intra-quarter bookings trends** (Feb relative to Q1) beyond "solid... broad-based," avoiding a specific read on sales-cycle momentum.
- **No specific answer on long-run AI/terminal-value impact to the business model** — Black gave a philosophical, human-centricity-based answer rather than modeling out headcount or margin implications over a 3-5 year AI-adoption horizon.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **AI's effect on hiring/layoffs came up twice** — once on the macro labor market, once on ADP's own sales-force headcount — with management using its proprietary data to rebut AI-driven-layoff narratives while confirming AI hasn't reduced ADP's own seller headcount plans.
- **International/Lyric monetization timing** was probed repeatedly — analysts wanted to know if large deal wins were shifting revenue mix; management was consistent that these are multi-year, lower-margin-but-high-retention builds, not near-term mix-movers.
- **Margin cadence and float-rate sensitivity** drew detailed follow-ups on Q3 vs. Q4, with management transparently walking through the ~75bps Fed-funds-driven yield headwind on the short portfolio in the seasonally high-balance Q3.
- **One direct competitive question** (Paychex citing lower revenue-per-client) was addressed head-on and rebutted with ADP's own attach-rate and retirement/insurance bookings data — no dodge there.
- **Compressed worry:** is the PEO slowdown an early crack in small-business hiring demand, or just noise in a still-healthy overall franchise — management's answer was confident but the guidance cut to the low end suggests they're hedging toward the more cautious read.
