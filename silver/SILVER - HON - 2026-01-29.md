# HON — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-29)

**The key idea:** Honeywell closed 2025 with orders up 23% and record backlog, using that strength to justify a "second-half-loaded" 2026 guide while simultaneously racing to finish its three-way breakup (Solstice already spun, aerospace targeted for Q3 2026) and pour incremental capital into Quantinuum ahead of a confidential S-1. The tension: management is asking investors to trust a backlog-driven acceleration story even as it explicitly guides industrial automation and process automation assuming *no* recovery in short-cycle demand — a hedge that sits awkwardly next to 23% order growth.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Backlog conversion is the whole 2026 bull case.** Orders grew 23% organically in Q4 (third straight quarter of double-digit growth), pushing backlog to a record $37B+/up 15%, and management is banking on LNG/refining projects booked in mid-2025 converting to revenue on a 12-18 month lag through 2026-2027 — a long-cycle bet that leaves little room for near-term disappointment to be absorbed.
2. **Short-cycle industrial is genuinely weak and they're not pretending otherwise.** CFO Mike Stepniak: petrochemical catalyst orders are soft on customer overcapacity, and industrial automation in Europe and China is under pressure — "just okay, not great." The 2026 IA guide (down low-single-digits to flat) "doesn't factor any change" in that weakness, a deliberately conservative stance layered under an otherwise upbeat headline.
3. **Quantinuum is now a real drag on consolidated margins, not a rounding error.** The quantum unit raised $840M at a $10B pre-money valuation and remains fully consolidated, adding a ~30bps margin headwind in 2026 (~$100M step-up in spend) even as it stays off cash flow guidance; CEO Vimal Kapur calls it "a lot of wheels in motion" while declining to detail commercial economics ahead of the confidential S-1.
4. **Pricing power has structurally reset, not just passed through tariffs.** Price contributed ~4 points to Q4 growth and is guided to ~3.5% for 2026, which Kapur attributes to persistent labor/electronics/commodity inflation and a maturing regional/SKU-level pricing strategy — "our pricing strategy... has become more mature" — not simply tariff cost pass-through, per direct analyst questioning.
5. **The pending three-way split adds execution risk on top of an already complex quarter.** Aerospace spin moved up to Q3 2026 (from original "sometime in 2026" language) with a full new leadership slate (Tim Courier CEO, Josh Jepsen CFO, Craig Arnold as non-exec chair) already named, while Productivity Solutions & Services and Warehouse and Workflow Solutions are simultaneously being shopped for sale — three simultaneous portfolio actions layered onto normal quarterly execution.

## Economy & consumer
- **No direct consumer exposure** — Honeywell sells to airlines, industrials, building owners, and process/energy customers, so end-consumer health doesn't appear on this call; omit as not applicable.
- **Bifurcated macro by geography and cycle length.** Kapur: "US and META, India, short cycle orders performed well," while "Europe and China... just okay, not great" — a geography split specific to industrial automation, not building automation or aerospace, which don't show the same regional weakness.
- **Long-cycle capex (LNG, refining, defense, data centers) is accelerating** even as short-cycle industrial demand stalls — a divergence that shows up directly in backlog (+15%) against flat-to-down IA/PT organic sales.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Aerospace and building automation carried the quarter.** Aerospace grew 11% organically ex-Bombardier with a third straight quarter of double-digit order growth and book-to-bill of 1.2; building automation grew 8%, with data center exposure "inching towards... greater than 5% of revenue" from a standing start a few years ago.
- **Industrial automation and energy/sustainability solutions are the soft spots.** ESS organic sales declined 7% on lower petrochemical catalyst shipments and "continued project deferrals"; IA grew only 1% and 2026 guide assumes flat-to-down.
- **Margin math is muddied by the Bombardier comp and portfolio actions.** Adjusted segment profit "increased 23% or 2% excluding Bombardier" — the two-line reporting makes it hard to isolate organic operating leverage from a one-time 2024 agreement.

## Investing & scarcity
- **R&D reset to a new sustained level.** R&D stepped up to ~4.4-4.8% of sales in 2025 (adding ~600 engineers) and CFO says this is now the "sweet spot," normalizing rather than growing further in 2026 — removing what was a ~50bps margin headwind last year.
- **CapEx is chasing aerospace supply-chain capacity specifically.** Of the ~$250M CapEx increase, "Aero is a large part of it," funded mostly through working-capital improvement rather than incremental cash — management frames more aerospace volume as the constraint to relieve, not demand.
- **Quantinuum spend is discretionary and rising.** ~$100M year-over-year increase, justified by proximity to "time to value" with banks, pharma, and governments, and a committed 2027 next-gen machine — a bet made ahead of, not validated by, disclosed commercial revenue.
- **The binding constraint is cost inflation, not raw material scarcity.** Kapur explicitly ranks labor (3-4% annual increases), electronics/memory pricing, and broader commodity costs as the "persistent" drivers behind higher structural pricing — a cost-side scarcity story rather than a supply-side one.
