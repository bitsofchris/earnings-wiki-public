# ORLY — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-05)

**The key idea:** O'Reilly delivered another record year (33rd straight comp increase) on the back of a professional business firing on all cylinders, but the call's real tension is a slow-motion cost problem — self-insurance and healthcare inflation that's persisted longer and run hotter than management expected, quietly eating into the SG&A leverage story even as they accelerate store growth into new, unproven Northeast territory.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Two-speed consumer, cleanly split by channel.** Professional/DIFM comps topped 10% for a second straight quarter and gained share, while DIY traffic was "slightly negative" and, for the first time, showed deferral bleeding into non-discretionary "failure and maintenance" jobs, not just cosmetic/accessory categories. Brad Beckham: "we've seen it for a longer period of time with discretionary stuff" but Q4 marked bigger-ticket jobs joining the pressure list.

2. **The real story isn't sales, it's self-insurance cost inflation nobody can time.** Health care, workers' comp, litigation, and auto liability reserve costs drove an SG&A per-store miss (3.3% vs. plan) and CFO Jeremy Fletcher admitted the pressure "has persisted longer than we would normally expect and has been a little bit of a story of increases on top of increases that we thought were already pretty dramatic." Guidance builds in continued caution rather than a recovery timeline.

3. **2026 comp guidance is really an inflation-timing story, not a demand call.** Management confirmed the 3-5% range (100bps above where 2025 started) reflects better visibility into calendared same-SKU inflation (tariff pass-throughs from late 2025), not an improving consumer — H1 should run at the high end on easy comps, H2 faces tougher ones.

4. **O'Reilly is betting capital on new-store acceleration into territory it's never held.** 225-235 net new stores (up ~25 from 2025), stepped-up CapEx to $1.3-1.4B, and — most notably — the new Stafford, VA DC opens a Northeast/Mid-Atlantic corridor (D.C., Baltimore, Philadelphia, NYC) where Brad Beckham says the company has literally "no presence... it would be a 0 and all opportunity for us."

5. **Gross margin strength papers over the SG&A weakness.** Full-year gross margin hit 51.6% (top half of guidance, +39bps YoY) via acquisition-cost management and distribution efficiency even while the higher-margin-dilutive professional mix grew faster — but that gain got offset at the operating-margin line by the SG&A cost overruns, leaving full-year operating margin flat at 19.5%.

## Economy & consumer

- **Industry framed as structurally stable, not strong.** Management repeatedly leans on the nondiscretionary repair-and-maintenance thesis and a car-parc/miles-driven backdrop growing only ~1% over two years — this is a "hold steady" macro read, not a growth one.
- **DIY consumer stayed cautious but didn't deteriorate further.** Brad Beckham: transaction pressure "was really similar to what we saw last quarter," with December improving on winter weather. No further negative inflection, but no recovery either.
- **Tax-refund season is an open question, deliberately unresolved.** Management flagged elevated refund expectations but explicitly declined to bake in a benefit: "we still kind of want to wait and see just kind of how that really plays out across the different income levels."

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Professional is the growth engine and the margin drag simultaneously.** Over 10% comps for two straight quarters, but Brent Kirby noted the mix "was a headwind" to gross margin because DIFM carries lower margin than DIY — strength and strain from the same source.
- **DIY guidance for 2026 explicitly assumes continued erosion.** Management expects DIY transaction counts to stay "pressured and slightly negative," attributing it partly to secular better-engineered/longer-interval parts, not just cyclical consumer weakness — a structural headwind they're not fighting, just absorbing via share gains elsewhere in the basket.
- **SG&A per-store growth is the one line missing its own targets two years running.** Guidance for 2026 (3-4% per-store growth) mirrors 2025's initial guide, which itself came in hot — an admitted pattern management couldn't fully explain away when pressed twice on the same question by different analysts.

## Investing & scarcity

- **CapEx up ~10-15% to fund store count, not efficiency.** The step-up to $1.3-1.4B is "primarily" new-store driven; distribution CapEx is actually "down slightly" even as Fort Worth DC construction continues (targeted Q1 2028), because Stafford VA just came online.
- **The real bottleneck is people/claims-cost management, not physical capacity.** Fletcher was candid this is "a part of our cost structure" that's "been pretty tightly and effectively controlled... for a long time" and now lacks "easy and quick levers" — a rare admission that a historically well-managed cost center is currently out of their control.
- **Inventory investment running ahead of plan.** Per-store inventory rose 9% in 2025 (vs. a lower initial plan) on "opportunistic" hub and assortment investment; 2026 guides to another ~5% increase — capital is following the professional-side momentum.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** cost inflation "will stabilize over time." **Action:** budgeted for continued pressure through 2026 anyway, with SG&A growth guided disproportionately heavier in H1 — the stated optimism and the actual budget don't match; they're planning for the pain to continue even while saying they expect it to fade.
- **Belief:** tariffs/pricing will stay "rational" with no rollback modeled. **Action:** guidance holds prices flat from current levels with no incremental tariff assumption either direction — a genuinely neutral stance, not spin, since they built no upside or downside tariff scenario into the plan.
- **Belief:** the Northeast is a wide-open, multi-year share opportunity. **Action:** one DC (Stafford) and "a handful" of Canada-style greenfield stores in 2026 — real money committed, but Beckham was explicit the 2026 store cohort stays "evenly spread" nationally rather than concentrated in the new corridor, so the action so far is modest relative to the stated size of the opportunity.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No exit-rate or 2027 SG&A view.** Fletcher declined directly: "I don't know that any of us would feel super comfortable talking maybe to where exit rate would be."
- **No timeline for self-insurance cost normalization**, despite two separate analyst attempts to pin one down — only "we expect it to moderate and kind of stabilize over the course of time," with no date attached.
- **No view on whether elevated tax refunds will lift DIY.** Management flagged the setup (weather + refunds) but declined to build any of it into guidance commentary, calling it "still yet to be seen."

## The street — what analysts asked

- The Q&A was dominated by one anxiety, asked in at least three different framings: is the SG&A/self-insurance cost inflation transitory or the new cost-of-doing-business baseline, and is O'Reilly's cost control actually still working? Management's answer stayed consistent but noncommittal — "cautious," no firm timeline, no exit rate.
- Secondary cluster: Northeast expansion economics — whether field build-out (district managers, commercial staff) creates a deleverage drag, and how O'Reilly's ~10% national share maps onto a market where it currently has zero presence.
- A tariff-rollback/deflation scenario was raised directly; management pushed back that pricing tends to stay sticky once passed through and that the administration has other levers regardless.
- **Compressed worry:** can O'Reilly's famously tight cost discipline still hold the line when the pressure is coming from line items — health care, litigation, insurance — that management itself says it has never had "easy and quick levers" to pull?
