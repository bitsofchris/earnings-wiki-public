# MU — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-12-17)

**The key idea:** Micron delivered the best quarter in its history — records across every line item — while simultaneously telling customers it can only fill half to two-thirds of their demand. The tension of the call is between a company insisting it's being "disciplined" on CapEx (raised to $20B, still below its historical capital-intensity target) and a supply-demand gap management describes as the widest they've ever seen, with HBM's TAM pulled forward two years and clean-room lead times lengthening industry-wide.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A once-unthinkable supply gap is now the whole story.** Sanjay's flat admission — "in the medium term, we are only able to meet about 50% to two-thirds of our demand from several key customers" — is the single most important sentence on the call; everything else (CapEx, LTAs, fab timelines) is a response to that gap.
2. **HBM's TAM got yanked forward two years, mid-quarter.** The $100B-by-2028 call (from $35B in 2025) arrived unprompted in prepared remarks, and Sanjay flags it's larger than the entire 2024 DRAM market — a scale shift, not an estimate tweak.
3. **Contract structure changed, not just size.** Sanjay repeatedly stresses new multiyear LTAs have "specific commitments" and "much stronger contract structure" versus prior agreements that were "money and contracts" — implying legacy LTAs were softer than the market assumed, though he won't give terms.
4. **Capital discipline sits oddly next to the demand story.** CapEx up to $20B (from $18B) but capital intensity is described as "dropping" versus the historical ~35% target, with '27 CapEx merely "expected to be up" — restraint that reads as a deliberate choice not to chase every dollar of demand, or as a real physical (clean-room lead-time) ceiling.
5. **Every product line broke records simultaneously** — DRAM, NAND, HBM, data center revenue, and all four business units — a breadth of strength that's unusual and suggests this isn't one hot segment carrying the company.

## Economy & consumer
- **Demand forecasts revised up across the board within one quarter**: 2025 DRAM bit demand to low-20% growth (from high teens), NAND to high-teens (from low-to-mid-teens), server units to high-teens (from 10%), PC units to high-single-digit (from mid-single-digit).
- **AI content is the growth driver at the edge, not just the data center**: flagship smartphones with 12GB DRAM hit 59% of shipment mix, "more than twice the level from a year ago."
- **Price sensitivity is real but contained**: "some of the unit demand may get impacted" in consumer markets and OEMs "may have some mix adjustments... to address available supply," but Sanjay says this is already reflected in guidance.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Records everywhere**: total revenue $13.6B (+21% QoQ, +57% YoY), DRAM $10.8B record (+69% YoY), NAND $2.7B record (+22% YoY), gross margin 56.8% (+11pts QoQ), free cash flow $3.9B — a new record beating the prior 2018 peak by over 20%.
- **Balance sheet is being actively repaired**: $2.7B of debt paid down in the quarter, ending with net cash over $250M for the first time in a while.
- **What's not working is capacity, not product**: "Despite significant efforts, we are disappointed to be unable to meet demand from other customers across all market segments" — a direct admission of lost/unfilled business.
- **Enterprise SSD reaccelerated sharply** after two-to-three muted quarters, with third-party estimates putting sequential growth around 25%, tied to inferencing and generative video workloads.

## Investing & scarcity
- **FY26 CapEx raised to ~$20B** (from $18B), weighted to H2, aimed at HBM capacity and 1-gamma DRAM supply; brick-and-mortar construction CapEx is set to roughly double year over year.
- **Fab timelines are being compressed under pressure**: Idaho fab 1 pulled in to mid-CY2027 first wafer (from H2 2027); Idaho fab 2 breaks ground 2026, operational 2028; New York groundbreaking early 2026, output "2030 and beyond"; Singapore HBM packaging facility contributing meaningfully in 2027.
- **The actual binding constraint is clean-room space, not equipment or yield**: "lead times for clean room build-out are lengthening across geographies," and the HBM-to-DDR5 3:1 trade ratio (worsening with each HBM generation) is compounding the shortage.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: tightness in DRAM and NAND persists "beyond calendar 2026," and HBM TAM triples to $100B by 2028, two years sooner than previously modeled.
- **Action**: HBM4 pricing and volume locked for all of calendar 2026; multiyear LTAs with "specific commitments" under negotiation with several key customers, spanning DRAM, NAND, and SSDs, not just HBM.
- **The gap**: CapEx growth (~11% up in dollar terms) is modest relative to a demand-supply gap management calls the widest ever seen, and capital intensity is explicitly falling versus their own historical ~35% target — the stated belief in a multiyear supercycle isn't being met with proportionally aggressive capital commitment, which Mark Murphy frames as "discipline" rather than constraint.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No FY26 full-year revenue guide**, despite guiding Q2 explicitly to a record $18.7B — leaves capital-intensity and full-year margin trajectory impossible to pin down.
- **No HBM dollar figure or HBM-as-%-of-DRAM disclosure**, repeatedly declined across multiple analyst attempts, including a direct ask about HBM market share versus DRAM share by 2028.
- **No gross margin guidance beyond fiscal Q2**, and no specifics on cost impact from the HBM 3E-to-4 transition, calling it dependent on "many factors, including mix."
- **No specifics on LTA terms, timing of signing, or whether AI customers are contributing capital to new fab construction** — Sanjay repeatedly said "not really going to get into the specifics."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **LTA structure and bindingness dominated the Q&A**: multiple analysts pushed on contract specifics, length, and whether customers are funding capacity directly — all deflected with the same "specific commitments, stronger structure" language and no numbers.
- **CapEx-vs-demand-gap tension came up from two separate analysts** (UBS, Cantor) essentially asking "if the shortage is this severe, why isn't CapEx growing faster?" — answered with a mix of "clean room takes time" and explicit capital discipline framing.
- **HBM competitive positioning and share** was probed repeatedly (Barclays, TD Cowen) given a "large competitor... looking to become more competitive" — Micron leaned entirely on power/performance specs (11+ Gbps, 30% lower power than competitors) rather than share numbers.
- **One-sentence worry**: analysts don't fully believe Micron will spend enough, fast enough, to actually close the gap it just told them was the widest in company history.
