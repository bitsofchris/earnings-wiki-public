# V — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** Visa's growth mix has permanently shifted — value-added services and commercial/money-movement are now compounding faster than the core consumer network, while management uses an agentic-AI-driven engineering reorg to justify simultaneously accelerating investment and cutting headcount. The World Cup gave the quarter a real but fading sugar high, and Visa is explicitly hedging its stablecoin bet by refusing to pick a winner.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Agentic AI restructured how Visa builds software, not just what it builds.** Product teams shrank from "10 or more" to "agentic squads of two to four," with management citing 80% more code commits, requirement definition compressed from 30 days to five, and 65%+ faster feature development — concrete, unusually specific productivity claims for a non-tech incumbent.
- **The stablecoin strategy is deliberately non-committal.** Visa joined Open Standard to help issue OpenUSD and launched the Visa Stablecoin Platform for minting/movement/wallet infrastructure, but the CEO was explicit: **"our role is not to pick winners... we will remain multi-coin, multi-chain,"** positioning Visa as infrastructure/toll-taker across whichever coin wins rather than backing one.
- **Agentic commerce is being framed as a multi-year adoption curve, not a near-term revenue line.** Partnerships with OpenAI and Meta (payments inside Facebook/Instagram via Visa Intelligent Commerce) are early plumbing — Agent Score, Agent Directory, and a Token Assurance Framework — with the CEO calling it explicitly **"a when, not an if,"** echoing the multi-year buildout arcs of e-commerce and tap-to-pay.
- **Issuer processing is bifurcating by bank size.** DPS keeps large banks on customized, separate debit/credit platforms; Pismo (cloud-native, API-based) targets small/mid-size banks and fintechs wanting integrated debit+credit processing — a new "DPS Full Service Credit" pilots in Q4 with one client secured, GA next year. Pismo has entered 19 new markets since acquisition and is also selling core-banking modernization to large banks (Wells Fargo cited) separately from processing.
- **Value-added services broke out from a single-narrative growth story into broad-based strength.** All four VAS portfolios (issuing, acceptance, risk/security, advisory) individually grew faster than the rates Visa disclosed at its 2025 Investor Day, with issuing+acceptance+risk collectively growing north of 20% for twelve straight months — not just a FIFA marketing blip.
- **Security tooling is being open-sourced as a lead-gen/trust play**: the "Visa Vulnerability Agentic Harness," built on models like Mythos to find and fix issues at AI speed, was released on GitHub with a technical blueprint and remediation/validation agents.

## What's changing
- **The revenue algorithm has structurally diverged from Investor Day guidance.** Consumer payments is growing low-single-digits while CMS and VAS run in the mid-20s%, pushing total net revenue growth (13-14%) well above the 9-11% long-term algo Visa laid out in Feb 2025 — management acknowledged the gap but declined to reset guidance, deferring to "guidance we will provide next quarter" for FY2027.
- **U.S. payments volume hit its fastest growth since fiscal 2019** (excluding post-COVID recovery), up 10% year-over-year, with acceleration broad-based across credit, debit, card-present, card-not-present, and every spend band — management stated plainly **"we do not see signs of the lower spend consumer weakening."**
- **Cross-border e-commerce growth accelerated intra-quarter to a June peak, then moderated in July** as the promotional-shopping and days-mix tailwinds reversed — CFO flagged this explicitly as a normalization, not a new trend: "June and July are unusually high... I would anticipate it settle back down."
- **Client incentives stepped up 4 points sequentially** (18% growth) mostly from lapping weak comps and strong renewal activity — Visa has now renewed about 20% of payment volume for the year, a mechanical tailwind that fades once lapped.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Layoffs are being framed purely as reinvestment fuel, not a cost problem.** $563M in GAAP severance charges hit mostly technology and product teams — the same teams credited with AI productivity gains — with management repeatedly declining to say whether any savings drop to the bottom line ("the flywheel is working... we've done that while maintaining our industry-leading operating margins"). No specific headcount number was given.
- **International transaction revenue (6% growth) badly lagged cross-border volume growth (12%)**, a gap CFO attributed to lapping last year's volatility peak and mix shift toward lower-yield products like Visa Direct — a real margin dilution story dressed as a timing/lapping issue.
- **CMS revenue growth stepped down from Q2** specifically because one-time "performance adjustment" benefits rolled off — a reminder that some of the CMS/VAS acceleration analysts are extrapolating includes non-recurring items.
- **When directly asked whether the elevated VAS/CMS growth mix is durable or a multi-year anomaly relative to the stated long-term algo, the CFO didn't answer the question** — he pivoted to "we're focused on running the company" and deferred any recalibration to next quarter's guide.
- **No mention of Mastercard, Amex, or any specific competitive share loss** despite a direct question about European local schemes — the answer was pure Visa self-promotion (investment scale, product breadth) with zero acknowledgment of where share is actually being lost or defended.
