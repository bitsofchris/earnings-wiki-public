# MAR — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-10)

**The key idea:** This was Kathleen "Leeny" Oberg's farewell call, and the headline number — a ~35% jump in 2026 credit-card fee guidance — comes almost entirely from Marriott unilaterally raising its own royalty rate on an amended contract, not from underlying spend growth (which stays a steady high-single-digit rate). Underneath the fee-engineering, the real story is a bifurcated consumer (luxury/leisure strong, government/select-service weak) and an owner base still under margin pressure, prompting a "blank sheet of paper" review of the entire hotel operating model.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The credit-card royalty hike is a lever, not organic growth.** CFO Oberg was asked about the ~35% co-branded card fee jump five different ways and gave the same non-answer each time: "the two are totally separate," declining to say whether it signals upside once the Visa/Chase/Amex deals close. The base business, she confirmed, grows at "the same high single-digit growth rate" it's always had.
2. **Franchisee economics are strained enough that Marriott is rebuilding the operating model from scratch.** CEO Capuano acknowledged publicly discussing "the economic model for franchisees becoming less favorable" and said the team is looking at staffing, purchasing, and affiliation costs "with a blank sheet of paper" — a notable admission for an asset-light franchisor whose growth depends on owner returns.
3. **Leisure's share gain looks structural, not cyclical.** Leisure is now 45% of global nights (up several points from 2019) and Oberg says that share gain "has absolutely stuck," while business transient remains "several percentage points lower than it was in 2019" — a durable travel-mix shift, not pandemic noise.
4. **The K-shaped consumer shows up directly in the numbers.** Government RevPAR fell over 30% during the 43-day shutdown, moderating to -15% after — a clean read on the low end — while luxury RevPAR rose over 6% for the year and 10% of the entire portfolio (open and pipeline) is now luxury.
5. **AI distribution is philosophically important but financially nascent.** Capuano's own framing: "we're pulling into the players' parking lot. We're not even in uniform or on the field" on the Google AI Mode and OpenAI Ad Pilot partnerships — early collaboration to shape a future channel, not a current revenue driver.

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro read is "steady as she goes."** Full-year global RevPAR +2%, with international (+5%) outpacing U.S./Canada (+0.7%); management assumes "a relatively steady macroeconomic environment" for 2026 guidance.
- **Bifurcation persists.** "This disparity between the top end and the bottom end, we expect to continue," per Oberg, though "perhaps not to be quite as wide as it was in twenty-five."
- **Government shutdown was a real, quantified hit.** Government RevPAR down >30% during the 43-day shutdown, still down ~15% after; this was the primary driver of Q4 U.S./Canada business-transient RevPAR falling 3%.
- **Greater China stays macro-challenged.** RevPAR only returned to growth (+3%) via leisure/inbound travel and ADR strength in Hong Kong/Taiwan/tier-1 markets, offsetting "continued softness in tertiary markets."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record pipeline, accelerating growth guide.** Pipeline hit 610,000 rooms (+6% YoY, +2% QoQ), with 2026 net rooms growth guided to accelerate to 4.5-5%, all organic.
- **Conversions are the growth engine.** ~75% of conversion rooms open and start generating fees within 12 months of signing; conversions drove ~a third of signings/openings for the year, aided by "the lowest [affiliation costs] in the industry" on midscale brands.
- **U.S./Canada is the soft spot.** Q4 RevPAR "around flat" with luxury gains offset by select-service declines (full-year select-service RevPAR -30bps) — the segment most exposed to the weaker consumer.
- **Franchisee returns are explicitly under repair.** Capuano: attacking "every variable in the equation that drives owner returns," including a prior cut to Bonvoy charge-out weight and an ongoing review of staffing/scheduling/purchasing models property-by-property.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Tech replatforming is moving from build to deploy.** Core reservations, PMS, and loyalty systems are now live in pilot select-service hotels with "a lot fewer bugs than we expected," rolling out more broadly through 2026; spend pace is expected to stay roughly flat versus prior quarters' guidance.
- **Key money creeping up but discipline intact.** Oberg: "we have seen a bit more key money required across all the tiers" industry-wide, driven by financing costs and construction costs, but per-deal key money last year was actually lower than in 2019 despite record 1,200 deals signed.
- **Constraint is owner-side returns, not capital availability.** Both executives stressed Marriott has "the free cash flow to absolutely go" when good deals appear — the limiting factor is deal economics (ROIC discipline), not balance-sheet capacity.
- **Capital returns stay large.** $4B+ returned to shareholders in 2025 via dividends/buybacks, guided to $4.3B+ in 2026, alongside continued investment-grade commitment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes:** event-driven travel (World Cup, Olympics, "special" events becoming "the norm") will keep boosting leisure demand structurally. **Does:** became official hotel supporter of the 2026 FIFA World Cup across all 16 host cities and is quantifying a ~30-35bps global RevPAR tailwind plus ~100bps to Q1 EMEA RevPAR from the Winter Olympics — real, sized commitments behind the belief.
- **Believes:** AI will "redefine the customer acquisition paradigm" in travel. **Does:** only in H1 2026 rollout of natural-language search on marriott.com/app, plus early, unpriced pilot integrations with Google AI Mode and OpenAI Ad Pilot — the conviction in the words far outpaces committed spend or scope, consistent with "not even in uniform or on the field."
- **Believes:** franchisee economics need structural repair. **Does:** a genuine ground-up review of hotel operating costs is underway, but the concrete deliverable cited (lower Bonvoy charge-out weight) predates this call — the "blank sheet of paper" review has not yet produced new committed actions this quarter.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No specifics on royalty-rate magnitude or its interaction with pending card deals.** Repeated refusal across multiple questions to say whether the self-initiated rate increase is a "one-time" step or represents a floor that carries into the Visa/Chase/Amex renegotiation outcomes.
- **No update to 2027 guidance implied by the step-up**, explicitly declined when asked directly whether the underlying growth rate reverts to high-single-digit after 2026's one-time increase.
- **No precise trip-purpose attribution anymore.** Capuano conceded blended leisure/business trips have made it impossible to give "the exact same precise answer" on market mix as in the past — a data-quality hedge dressed as a positive trend.
