# LRCX — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-01-28)

**The key idea:** Lam closed out a record 2025 and is guiding to an even bigger 2026 (WFE from ~$110B to ~$135B), but the entire story is about a market that could be growing faster if not for a hard physical constraint: clean room space. Every technology transition Lam is riding — gate-all-around, backside power, advanced packaging, HBM4, Moly ALD — increases equipment intensity per wafer, which is exactly what benefits Lam's SAM and share, but it also means the industry needs more square footage per unit of output, and that footage isn't there yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The constraint is real estate, not chips.** Tim Archer: "there's just not enough clean room out there" — repeated by both executives as the reason 2026 is guided at $135B WFE rather than an analyst-modeled $150B, and management explicitly declined to quantify the gap.
2. **Technology transitions are doing double duty for Lam.** Gate-all-around adds roughly $1B of Lam SAM per 100,000 WSPM, and each new HBM generation (now up to 16-layer stacking for HBM4/4e) requires *more* clean room and tooling per bit — Lam benefits from the very constraint squeezing the rest of the industry.
3. **NAND found unexpected new demand from AI inference itself.** Non-volatile "context memory" for large-scale AI inference is adding incremental NAND bit demand — "for every two to three million accelerators sold, we estimate an incremental one-point increase in overall NAND bit demand growth" — a use case Lam says it "didn't see... coming."
4. **Visibility into 2027 is unusually strong** because customers are publicly announcing fabs and locking in multiyear supply agreements to secure tool allocation before those fabs even open — a rare admission of forward clarity from a cyclical capital-equipment company.
5. **China is deliberately shrinking as a share of the pie**, guided flattish in absolute WFE terms while the rest of the world grows — a mix shift management flagged as a gross-margin headwind for the March quarter.

## Economy & consumer
- **Industry-wide sold-out conditions**: "chipmakers have been public about their efforts to alleviate constraints, but they've also commented on sold-out conditions persisting."
- **2026 WFE guided to ~$135B** from ~$110B in 2025, second-half weighted, with DRAM and leading-edge foundry logic leading growth.
- No end-consumer discussion — Lam sells capital equipment to fabs, several steps removed from any consumer read-through.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record 2025 across every P&L line**: revenue $20.6B (+27% y/y), gross margin 49.9% (highest since the 2012 Novellus merger), operating margin 34.1%, EPS $4.89 (+49% y/y).
- **December quarter beat the high end** on gross margin, operating margin, and EPS; tenth consecutive quarter of revenue growth.
- **DRAM was the standout segment** — record revenue, 23% of systems revenue up from 16% — while **NAND systems revenue fell to 11% of systems revenue from 18%** as memory makers prioritize DRAM's better near-term profitability.
- **CSBG (services) hit a milestone**: installed base topped 100,000 chambers, with NAND upgrade revenue up more than 90% y/y in 2025.
- **Deferred revenue balance fell ~$500M sequentially** on lower customer advance down payments — worth watching as a leading indicator, though management didn't flag it as a concern.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Manufacturing footprint nearly doubled in four years**; new automated warehouses launched in 2025; a new Arizona building purchased in the quarter to support the region's growing industry footprint.
- **CapEx held to 4-5% of revenue** even amid the buildout — discipline maintained despite accelerating demand.
- **Inventory will deliberately rise in dollar terms** ahead of top-line growth: "it's very likely that we're gonna need to build some inventory in total dollar terms as we go through the year."
- **The binding constraint is clean room space at customers, not Lam's own supply chain or manufacturing** — Doug Bettinger: "today, I would say we're not the big constraint... compared to clean room space being a constraint to the industry."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: 2026 will show growth "every quarter," second-half weighted, with 2027 already shaping up as "another year where everything is growing," driven by publicly-announced fabs coming online in '27/'28.
- **Action**: Capacity investment (manufacturing footprint, warehouses, Arizona building) and R&D velocity (Velocity Labs, digital twins) are being scaled now to meet demand that mostly materializes in future years — a genuine talk-matches-action alignment here, unlike calls where guidance outruns capital commitment.
- **Gap worth flagging**: management is confident WFE will "outperform" its own $135B guide via share gains, yet simultaneously won't quantify how much of the addressable opportunity is currently being left on the table by clean-room constraints — the bullish framing and the refusal to size the shortfall sit in tension.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Declined to size the dollar cost of clean-room constraints to 2026 WFE despite a direct, specific analyst ask.
- Declined to give a precise China revenue-mix number for March, only "plus or minus... low thirties or high twenties."
- Declined to rank-order foundry/DRAM/NAND growth rates for 2027: "I'm not gonna rank order it quite yet."
- Declined to refresh the $40B NAND upgrade opportunity figure from Investor Day, saying only it's "happening faster than expected" and a formal update will come "later in the year."

## The street — what analysts asked
- Repeated, pointed attempts to get management to quantify the unquantifiable: the dollar size of the clean-room shortfall, the precise China mix, and forward growth rank-ordering — all declined.
- Multiple questions probed whether NAND's upgrade-cycle strength will convert into greenfield capacity spending, and when — management pointed to 2027/2028, consistent with the broader "constrained until new fabs open" narrative.
- Gross margin questions converged on customer/China mix rather than volume, with Lam repeatedly reminding analysts it isn't a fixed-cost business, so mix — not revenue growth — drives margin.
- Underlying anxiety, compressed: is 2026 upside being capped not by demand but by a physical building constraint nobody can override on short notice, and if so, how much of that "some day" growth is real versus continually deferred.
