# HD — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-24)

**The key idea:** Home Depot is compounding market-share gains inside a housing market that isn't growing — comps are barely positive and built almost entirely on price/mix, not more transactions, while the one metric management says would signal a real turn (big-ticket discretionary projects) still hasn't moved. The SRS/GMS roll-up is the growth lever management is actually pulling, buying roofing share in an industry posting its worst shipment year since 2019.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth is share-shift, not demand growth.** FY comps were +0.3% with transactions down 1.6% and ticket up 2.4%; CEO Ted Decker frames the whole year as taking share in a flat-to-declining market, not participating in expansion.
2. **The tell for a real recovery hasn't arrived.** Decker is explicit: "we still have not seen the increase in big ticket. And that will be a telltale for a turn in the market" — everything else (record events, Pro strength) is consistent with a stagnant-but-share-losing-to-HD environment.
3. **SRS bought share in a collapsing roofing market.** Industry shingle shipments fell 28% YoY, "the lowest industry volume since 2019," and SRS deliberately sacrificed Q4/Q1 margin to hold and grow share there — a clear, quantified capital-for-share trade.
4. **Tax stimulus and tariff relief are explicitly discounted as tailwinds.** Management ran the numbers on refund upside ($70-200B range) and concluded it's worth ~0.5 point of comp at best, refusing to build it into guidance — a disciplined but telling signal that management doesn't trust exogenous macro support to show up as spending.
5. **The Pro ecosystem (order management, trade credit, AI takeoff tools) is the demonstrated retention wedge.** Delivery reliability hit "2 Sigma on time and complete," and management describes tens of thousands of AI-assisted project lists being built weekly — a real, working AI-in-commerce deployment, not a slide.

## Economy & consumer
- **Turnover stuck at multi-decade lows** with prices up ~50% since 2019 has "significantly reduced demand for projects," and management sees no 2026 catalyst for inflection.
- **No broad trade-down, but no big-ticket recovery either** — Billy Bastek: "we haven't seen a lot of trade down... but haven't seen that more broadly" outside select categories like countertops and appliances.
- **Consumers anticipating a move behave differently**: Decker notes people planning to relocate underspend on their current home, "a bit more repair than replace," part of a cited $22B cumulative home-improvement underspend.
- **Elasticity risk flagged as a downside scenario** — Decker warns that even modest planned price increases "could see higher responses of elasticity" if the macro backdrop worsens.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: Pro and maintenance/repair categories.** Power, plumbing, electrical, gypsum, wire, concrete drove Q4 strength; big-ticket transactions (>$1,000) were positive for multiple quarters running.
- **Not working: large discretionary projects.** "Larger discretionary projects remain under pressure" — the category most sensitive to consumer confidence and financing costs stayed soft all year.
- **SRS/GMS integration is real but margin-costly near-term.** SRS grew organic sales low-single-digit and took share despite a brutal roofing market, but did so by "investing in price," directly pressuring Q1 2026 gross margin.
- **Associate/operations changes are showing measurable payoff**: customer satisfaction rose every quarter, hourly associate tenure is the highest since 2017, and shifting merchandising "tasking" to a dedicated MET team freed Orange Apron associates for selling — an internal productivity lever, not a demand lever.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex held flat at ~2.5% of sales** for 2026 — no acceleration, no pullback, despite the acquisition-heavy year (GMS) already done.
- **The constraint is demand, not capacity or supply.** Inventory is up largely because of GMS consolidation and tariff-driven cost inflation, not scarcity; management explicitly says "we feel fantastic about" inventory position.
- **Capital return is being deliberately deferred**: buybacks are on hold until an "excess cash position," expected "sometime in the first half of 2027" — dividends continue, but the balance sheet is being run to absorb acquisition debt first.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: the market stays roughly flat (-1% to +1%) through 2026** with no turnover catalyst in sight. **Action: they're not waiting for the catalyst** — continuing full investment pace in stores, interconnected/delivery infrastructure, and Pro capabilities regardless, betting share gains compound even in a flat market.
- **Belief: tax refunds and tariff relief are unreliable tailwinds.** **Action: guidance excludes them entirely** — a rare case of a retailer refusing to credit itself for plausible near-term stimulus, understating rather than overstating the setup.
- **Belief: SRS's Q4 roofing price aggression was a one-quarter phenomenon tied to a uniquely bad demand shock.** **Action: they're deliberately extending that price investment into Q1 2026** to lock in share — a real gap between "this was temporary" framing and a guidance book that bakes the margin drag in for another quarter.
- **Belief: big-ticket discretionary recovery would be the real signal to lean in harder.** **Action: no incremental investment is contingent on it** — capex and store openings (15 new HD stores, 40-50 SRS locations) are already locked for 2026 regardless of whether that signal appears.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to bank stimulus into guidance** despite modeling it themselves, calling the outcome "just as likely" to go to debt paydown or high-income savings as to spending.
- **Wouldn't forecast when/if housing turnover recovers** — repeatedly describes the market as "bouncing along the bottom" without projecting a turn date.
- **No commitment on tariff pass-through duration** — Bastek says pricing actions are "mostly done" for now but immediately caveats that tariff news is "breaking by the moment," leaving the door open to further price moves later in 2026.
- **Avoided quantifying the multifamily/national-account cross-sell opportunity** between HD Supply, SRS, and GMS beyond anecdotes — no revenue target attached to the synergy story yet.

## The street — what analysts asked
Analyst questions clustered around three anxieties: whether SRS's Q4 price aggression in roofing is a one-off or a durable margin drag into 2026; whether depressed housing turnover through 2027 would force a change in capital-allocation priorities (buybacks vs. continued Pro/tech investment); and whether ticket-driven comps (rather than transaction growth) and still-soft big-ticket discretionary spending signal the industry is bottoming or just stabilizing at a low level. Management's answers were consistent and calm but didn't fully resolve the tension — they conceded the SRS margin hit "will bleed into" Q1 and that big-ticket hasn't turned, while still affirming full-year guidance built on an assumption of continued negative transactions. The compressed worry: Home Depot's growth story currently depends entirely on out-executing a market that isn't growing, and no one — including management — can identify what changes that.
