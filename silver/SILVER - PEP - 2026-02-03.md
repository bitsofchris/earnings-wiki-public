# PEP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-03)

**The key idea:** PepsiCo is funding a deliberate, "surgical" affordability push in Frito-Lay — priced against a leaked report of up to 15% cuts on some items — using productivity savings freed up by 2025's rightsizing, betting that lower price points plus double-digit shelf-space gains will re-accelerate volume without giving back the margin recovery. Layered on top: four major brand relaunches (Lay's, Tostitos, Gatorade, Quaker) and a GLP-1 reformulation push that management insists is more opportunity than threat.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Affordability investment is the whole story, and management wants it read narrowly.** Ramon Laguarta repeatedly downplayed the "up to 15%" price-cut figure from a news report, calling it "the maximum" and stressing the real plan is "very surgical... particular brands, particular formats, particular channels." The repeated redirection across three separate analyst questions on the same topic suggests the leaked number understated management's discomfort, not overstated it.
2. **Shelf space is the actual constraint, not shelf demand.** Frito-Lay expects "double-digit" space gains at March/April resets in both main aisle and perimeter — framed as a direct consequence of higher unit throughput from affordability testing. This is a real, funded bet with retailers already committed, not aspirational language.
3. **GLP-1 is now a baseline planning assumption, not a tail risk.** Laguarta stated plainly: "we should assume that there will be a broader adoption of GLP-1 medicines... I think that should be an assumption now." The response is portfolio-wide: portion control (70%+ of U.S. food business already single-serve), fiber (Quaker relaunch), protein, hydration (Gatorade/Propel), and cooking-method R&D (air frying). This is the most concrete admission yet from a snacking/beverage major that GLP-1 is reshaping product roadmaps, not just marketing copy.
4. **Advertising spend fell about $500M in 2025 even during a stated "invest in the brand" year** — attributed to "efficiency" in working/non-working ad lines — and management now says spend needs to rise in 2026 to support the four brand relaunches. Read skeptically: either 2025 marketing efficiency was real and durable, or the ad cuts were a margin lever now being unwound as a "growth investment."
5. **CELSIUS/Alani energy bet is early but tracking.** PepsiCo frames its ~20% energy category share as achieved via distribution margin plus equity ownership rather than a house brand, with Alani Nu integration still incomplete ("still haven't completed all the distributors around the country").

## Economy & consumer

- **Low/middle-income U.S. consumer remains "stretched and choiceful"** — the phrase used by Laguarta to describe the persistent 2025 dynamic guidance is built against, with affordability, not brand loyalty, cited as the binding constraint on category frequency.
- **International divergence, not a uniform macro read:** Mexico "improving," China "positive," Middle East "good consumer," Western Europe "a bit weaker," Brazil "kind of neutral" — International overall guided to continue at "mid-single-digit growth," a run-rate Laguarta says has held for "19 quarters."
- **GLP-1 adoption treated as a demand-side structural shift already underway,** not a future scenario — management says affected households "continue to engage in our category, but they do it in smaller portions."

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **PFNA (Frito-Lay) inflection expected early, not late:** "We expect Frito-Lay to grow volume, net revenue and operating margin this year... this growth will come early in the year" — a reversal from 2025's rightsizing-driven contraction.
- **Trademark Pepsi grew both volume and dollars in 2025**, credited to the "Food Deserves Pepsi" campaign and expanded away-from-home availability; management still says "we're not satisfied yet."
- **Mountain Dew stayed weak** — Laguarta conceded directly: "'25 was better than '24 and '26 will be better than '25," implicit admission the brand has been underperforming for multiple years, with Baja flavors as the one cited bright spot.
- **Advertising spend fell ~$500M (described as "double digits") in 2025** on cost-of-sales efficiency in working/non-working ad lines — a benefit CFO Steve Schmitt said won't repeat in 2026.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Productivity/rightsizing savings are the funding source** for both the affordability push and brand relaunches — Schmitt: "this investment is manageable for the business. It's included in our guidance."
- **Shelf space is the binding physical constraint:** double-digit space gains negotiated for Frito-Lay's spring resets, explicitly tied to needing "more capacity in the store to either fulfill online or... in-store experience" as unit volume rises.
- **Four brand relaunches are capital- and headcount-intensive, sequenced deliberately:** Lay's and Tostitos first (Q1/Super Bowl timing), Gatorade and Quaker later in 2026 — reformulated with avocado/olive oil, no artificial ingredients, and new packaging.
- **No mention of AI, automation, or supply-chain/logistics technology investment** on this call — notable for a CPG of this scale; the only technology reference was IT systems work supporting the food+beverage distribution merger test in Texas/Florida.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** GLP-1 adoption will broaden and become "more affordable," reshaping consumption toward smaller, more functional portions.
  **Action:** Concrete reformulation and portioning work already in market (single-serve expansion, Gatorade/Propel low-sugar reformulation, Quaker fiber push) — this is one of the few beliefs backed by funded, dated product launches rather than just messaging.
- **Belief:** Affordability investment will drive a strong ROI and volume recovery in PFNA.
  **Action:** Funded via realized productivity savings and already tested "at scale in multiple markets" — but management repeatedly declined to give the magnitude of price investment or payback ratio, undercutting confidence that the ROI claim is fully underwritten yet.
- **Belief:** Merged food+beverage U.S. distribution will create efficiency and better customer service.
  **Action:** Still in pilot (Texas, Florida) with "specific details" deferred to "later in the year" — a strategic review that's been running long enough to generate "very positive initial numbers" but with no committed scale-up date or model, and refranchising floated only as a fallback for select markets.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Would not confirm or deny the reported up-to-15% PFNA price cut figure**, characterizing it only as a media-sourced "maximum" rather than validating or correcting it.
- **Declined to quantify the organic growth contribution from poppi, Siete, and Alani Nu** as they roll into the organic base through the year: "We haven't been specific on exactly what that will be."
- **No committed structure or timeline for the North America beverage distribution review** beyond "later in the year," and explicitly disclaimed a uniform national model, leaving the door open to case-by-case refranchising without specifying where.
