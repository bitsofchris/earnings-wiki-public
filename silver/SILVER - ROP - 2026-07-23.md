# ROP — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-23)

**The key idea:** Roper is betting that its 21 vertical-software businesses' "system of record" position — embedded workflows, regulatory trust, proprietary data — is the real moat in the agentic era, and it's racing to ship agent SKUs across the portfolio before proving out how any of it gets monetized. Meanwhile capital allocation has quietly flipped: buybacks (8% of shares in eight months) were the trade while M&A stayed frozen, and management is now signaling it expects that to reverse as private valuations catch down to public ones.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Agentic release cadence spiked across nearly every business this quarter** — Deltek (RFP development, month-end close), Vertafore (AI Velocity platform, six agentic SKUs), Aderant (collections, billing appeals, time capture), Procare (RoomRunner), CentralReach, DAT (proactive load-recommendation engine), plus SoftWriters, iTrade, Foundry, ConstructConnect. Management called this out explicitly as "precisely the point" — breadth over depth for now.
- **Vertafore's agentic strategy is the clearest articulated model**: five workflow areas (digital servicing, smart submissions, accounting automation, producer workflows, BI), starting with "high-volume, labor-intensive work where customer value is immediate" before expanding into complex workflows. Bain-assisted sizing pegs the TAM expansion from agentic work at roughly **a doubling of Vertafore's addressable market** — with the six launched SKUs described as "a distinct minority" of that opportunity.
- **The SoftWriters pharmacy-order-entry demo was the most concrete AI ROI story on the call**: CEO Neil Hunn personally observed processing time drop from ~90 seconds to ~18 seconds per order — an 80% reduction — with pricing set transactionally, tied directly to orders automated and labor saved.
- **Monetization model is coalescing around a subscription/tiered-consumption layer, not straight usage pricing**: "customers have been very clear they need to have an amount they can budget." Only a few businesses (DAT, SoftWriters) will price transactionally; Deltek folds AI value into cloud-migration upsell instead of a standalone SKU.
- **Capital allocation math has inverted**: for most of Roper's history M&A dominated the return math versus buybacks; over the "last 12-18 months" compressed software multiples made buybacks "significantly more attractive" — the company bought back **8% of shares outstanding in eight months**, returning share count to 2013 levels. Management now expects private valuations to mirror public ones and wants **$5 billion+ deployed toward M&A over the next 12-18 months**, prioritizing near-term de-leveraging (currently 3.4x net debt/EBITDA) over further buybacks to rebuild dry powder.
- Sponsors are reportedly capitulating on price expectations — paraphrased as "the new 15 is yesterday's 25" — and bringing more proprietary one-off deals directly to Roper, which management reads as an early thaw signal, not yet a breakout.

## What's changing
- **AI investment spend is running roughly 3x its January level on an annualized basis**, and CFO Jason Conley expects it to keep climbing through year-end — with the company now standardizing gateways, auto-routing, and reporting controls across the portfolio to manage token cost, while deliberately avoiding frontier models where cheaper tools suffice.
- **The AI development philosophy shifted from "methodical, lower-risk" to deliberately faster and riskier**, on the logic that AI dev costs are low enough that mistake-cost is low too — a explicit departure from Roper's historically conservative product cadence.
- **DAT's freight market is turning for the first time in years**: rising carrier additions, spot pricing strengthening versus contract rates, rising rejection rates — though load volumes still haven't confirmed the recovery, and management is deliberately not calling it a full inflection.
- **SaaS ground-to-cloud migration is accelerating past early adopters** — Aderant's cloud push is now reaching larger law firms (not just small ones), and Deltek just end-of-lifed its on-prem Costpoint GovCon product (N-2 support only), forcing a multi-year forced-march to cloud.
- A Supreme Court ruling harmonizing state broker-liability laws at the federal level was framed as a tailwind, not a risk: management says it pushes freight brokers toward carrier vetting, "precisely what Convoy and DAT does extremely well."

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Deltek's GovCon recovery still isn't confirmed** — "encouraging," "decent," "not enough yet to change our outlook" were repeated multiple times; management explicitly wants to see pipeline conversion sustain "over the next few quarters" before calling a turn, despite OBBB-driven appropriations tailwinds.
- **Procare is "a work in progress"** — the plainest admission of underperformance on the call, with go-to-market execution and implementation capability still being "shored up" even as new AI features ship.
- **Core margins fell across the board** — TEP segment core margins down substantially more than the 70bps enterprise-wide decline, driven by bronze/copper input-cost inflation at Neptune (explicitly tied to data-center copper demand competition) and a mix shift toward lower-margin (but stickier) consumables at NDI and Verathon. Software core margins also dipped 10bps, absorbing AI team investment.
- **No material AI revenue is expected this year, by design** — asked directly when agentic products flip to "big, large production workloads," management wouldn't commit past "not material revenue in the second half," pushing meaningful monetization into 2027 planning. This is a sizeable gap between narrative energy (four-hour 20% adoption on a Procare feature) and actual dollars.
- **Notable absence**: despite extensive AI talk, no discussion of headcount reduction or margin expansion from AI-driven labor savings within Roper's own businesses — the framing is entirely about expanding into customers' labor spend, not Roper's own opex efficiency. For a company running 21 software verticals, that's a conspicuous gap in a quarter this AI-saturated.
- Network segment recurring revenue growth was masked by weakness at MHA (large customers, lower unit economics) and iPipeline (service timing) — glossed as "nothing to really call out."
