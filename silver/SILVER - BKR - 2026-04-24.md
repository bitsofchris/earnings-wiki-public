# BKR — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-24)

**The key idea:** Baker Hughes is running two stories in parallel — a Middle East conflict actively crushing OFSE (oilfield services) volumes and product logistics, and an IET (industrial & energy technology) business setting records anyway on data-center power demand and LNG/gas-infrastructure orders. Management's bet is that the same conflict that hurts them near-term will structurally re-rate global energy security spending in their favor for years, and they're already raising the long-term IET order target on that thesis before the conflict has even resolved.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **IET is capacity-constrained, not demand-constrained.** NovaLT gas turbines are "effectively sold out... through 2028," and CEO Lorenzo Simonelli says the "tightness across the broader turbine market is well understood" — the company is expanding BRUSH generator and synchronous-condenser capacity and building a second NovaLT aftermarket site in Italy just to keep up, and is explicitly evaluating whether to expand *beyond* the already-announced doubling plan.
2. **Power Systems orders are now a AI-data-center story as much as an oil-and-gas one.** $1.4B of Power Systems orders (~30% of total IET orders) included a 1GW North America data-center award and a Boom Supersonic generator deal sized for 1.21GW of data-center capacity; management frames power demand as doubling by 2040 and pegs the behind-the-meter market at $60B by 2030, rising to a >$100B total addressable opportunity across generation, grid stability and energy management.
3. **The Middle East conflict is a live, quantified hit to OFSE, not a rounding error.** CFO Ahmed Moghal guides Middle East OFSE revenue to fall "more than 20% sequentially" in Q2 — double Q1's decline rate — driven mostly by product shipping/import-export logistics rather than services, with full-year OFSE guidance now at risk of missing the midpoint.
4. **Management is monetizing the "energy security" narrative into a bigger order target before the underlying conflict is even resolved.** They now say they're "increasingly confident" of exceeding the $40B Horizon 2 IET order target — a raise justified by a war whose duration they admit is unknowable, which is an aggressive extrapolation from disruption to durable spending.
5. **Balance sheet was pre-loaded for Chart.** An inaugural €3B European bond plus $6.5B US bond issuance pushed cash to $14.8B ahead of the Chart acquisition close, alongside ~$3B of 2026 divestiture proceeds (PSI, SPC JV, Waygate, HMH IPO) — portfolio-shrinking and empire-building (Chart) happening simultaneously.

## Economy & consumer
- **Strait of Hormuz disruption is the dominant macro fact this quarter.** Over 10% of global oil volumes and 20% of world LNG capacity are described as affected/offline, with management flagging "growing risk of undersupply in 2026" and calling for inventory rebuilds "above historical levels."
- **Fuel-switching is already visible in Asia.** Higher LNG prices have pushed some Asian demand to coal, which management says has "helped moderate additional upward pressure on LNG prices" — a real-time substitution effect, not a hypothetical.
- **European gas storage is entering injection season already behind.** Storage at 30% of capacity, "6% below last year and 13% below the seasonal average" — a concrete data point on tightening European energy security heading into next winter.
- No direct consumer read — BKR sells to national oil companies, IOCs, utilities and industrials, not end consumers.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **IET margin expansion is real and broad-based:** 310bps YoY to 20.2%, "driven by favorable backlog pricing, elevated project closeout and productivity" — not one-time noise, though "elevated project closeout" is itself a quarter-specific tailwind Moghal flags will not repeat at the same magnitude into Q2.
- **OFSE is absorbing multiple simultaneous headwinds:** the SPC joint-venture deconsolidation (4pts of the 9% sequential revenue decline), Middle East disruption (~2pts), and normal seasonality, yet still beat the midpoint of EBITDA guidance — regional strength in Mexico, Sub-Saharan Africa and the Gulf of Mexico offset the drag.
- **Free cash flow was weak and partly self-inflicted:** just $210M, attributed to normal Q1 seasonality "further affected by some delays in customer payments" — a soft admission of working-capital slippage worth watching next quarter.
- **SSPS (subsea) orders up 22% YoY (82% ex-SPC)** — a quiet standout inside OFSE, backed by major Petrobras and Turkish Petroleum contract wins.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Turbine and generator manufacturing capacity is the near-term ceiling on IET growth**, ahead of demand: NovaLT sold out through 2028, BRUSH lines being expanded, Frame 5 turbines only available in '27-'28 "should the demand materialize."
- **Capital is flowing toward Chart integration and power-infrastructure capacity, funded by simultaneous divestiture (PSI, SPC JV, Waygate, HMH) and debt issuance** — the company is reshaping its portfolio (shedding non-core, doubling down on power/industrial) at the same time it's raising unprecedented debt ($9.5B combined US/EU bonds) for the acquisition.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** energy security becomes "a foundational priority for governments and industry," driving durable upstream investment, inventory rebuilding, and diversified/redundant infrastructure for years beyond this conflict.
- **Action backing it:** capacity expansion in NovaLT/BRUSH, new aftermarket facility in Italy, a raised (if qualitative) Horizon 2 IET order target, and continued LNG/gas-infrastructure order wins (Qatar Energy North Field West, ST LNG Texas terminal).
- **Gap:** the raised long-term order confidence is not yet matched by hard 2026 guidance — full-year company revenue/EBITDA is still expected "slightly below the midpoint," and OFSE full-year guidance is explicitly at risk. Management is asking investors to trust a multi-year secular story while the current-year numbers underdeliver against original plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No firm timeline on Middle East resolution** — guidance is built on an assumption (conflict persists through June, Hormuz reopens in H2) that management repeatedly stresses is "quite fluid" and "subject to a wide range of potential outcomes."
- **Declined to commit capacity expansion beyond the current NovaLT doubling plan** without customers first locking in "financing and offtake... firmly in place" — disciplined optionality rather than chasing every inbound order.
- **No specific date or magnitude given for OFSE Middle East recovery** — only a "measured ramp" assumption, with upside acknowledged as possible but "somewhat delayed" given the product-heavy, logistics-constrained mix.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Structural upside from the conflict:** multiple questions pushed management to quantify how much of the Middle East disruption converts into durable infrastructure/redundancy spend — management answered qualitatively (raised Horizon 2 confidence) without hard incremental dollar figures.
- **Guidance-quality scrutiny:** analysts probed why the Q2 IET guide looked "flattish" despite a blowout Q1 and a normally strong seasonal step-up — management's answer (pulled-forward aeroderivative backlog work, better linearity) reads as reassurance that Q1 wasn't simply pulled-forward Q2.
- **Portfolio-management endgame:** with divestitures already exceeding the $1B target, analysts asked if BKR is "largely done" — Moghal declined to call it finished, describing it as "a continuum," with near-term focus explicitly redirected to closing Chart.
- **Capacity vs. demand for Power Systems:** the sharpest question was whether NovaLT/BRUSH capacity, not demand, caps 2026-2028 upside — management confirmed sold-out status through 2028 rather than deflecting it.
- Compressed worry: *is Baker Hughes now turning away Power Systems demand it can't build fast enough to fill, while its oilfield-services business eats a war it can't control the end date of?*
