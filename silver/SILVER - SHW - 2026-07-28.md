# SHW — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** Sherwin-Williams beat guidance with essentially zero help from the market — new residential starts negative most of the year, DIY still waiting for a catalyst, commercial "soft" — purely on share gains and price discipline. The subplot is a walked-away AkzoNobel joint bid with Nippon: management insists it wasn't desperate, but the timeline (bid, slide deck, days later withdrawal) drew repeated skeptical questions they never fully resolved.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **"We have to be our own catalyst for growth"** is the explicit operating thesis — management repeated variants of this line at least four times, framing the entire quarter as share-taking in a flat-to-negative market rather than riding demand.
- Protective & Marine is the standout, its **eighth straight quarter of at least high-single-digit growth**, explicitly tied to data center build-out, semiconductor fab infrastructure, and manufacturing onshoring — CEO Petz name-checked "the race of these hyperscalers" and pitched SHW as a one-shop coatings vendor for that build-out, a rare direct AI-capex tailwind showing up in a paint company's numbers.
- Store footprint is being **actively pruned then re-accelerated**: 57 closures (about 1% of Paint Stores Group) this year against only 45 openings, explicitly because "they didn't meet the profitability threshold," with management stating openings return to the high end of the 80-100/year target starting next year now that the pruning is "behind us."
- Digital and data are pitched as the next moat: **"whoever gets demand signals the quickest... get[s] the disproportionate amount of share,"** with CFO Meisenzahl citing ERP modernization and CRM work, and CEO Petz pointing to "the world's largest database of painting contractors" as an underused asset.
- Suvinil integration is framed as a case study in capital discipline — a decade of watching the asset before buying it, prioritizing "customer and employee first," and still guided to be only an **immaterial EPS tailwind** this year despite mid-teens sales contribution.
- An 8% architectural price increase is coming **September 1, deliberately delayed** past peak painting season to avoid disrupting customers — a contrast to industrial coatings, where pricing has been going out "more surgically by business unit or by region" all year given faster raw material inflation there.

## What's changing
- Raw material inflation is **re-accelerating**: expected up high-single-digits in the second half versus mid-single-digits for the full year, described as driven by "higher oil and related cost pressures," and explicitly why second-half EPS growth (~$0.10+) is guided far below first-half growth (~$0.40).
- Commercial and residential-repaint share gains that were "targeted over the past 24 months" are now **visibly showing up** — high-single-digit commercial growth in an underlying market still described as soft, a shift management attributes to multi-year account-conversion projects finally closing.
- Packaging is being reshaped by regulation: the **European BPA ban taking effect in Q2** is cited as an active driver of customer conversion into next year, on top of already-strong beverage-can demand.
- Capital return got a **direct assist from the deal collapse** — CFO Meisenzahl confirmed they ran an accelerated share repurchase in the window between walking away from the Akzo bid and the pre-earnings blackout, "taking decisive actions in an environment where our share price is on sale."

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **New residential construction remains outright negative** — single-family starts and completions negative five of the last six months, monthly completions down high-single-digits — and it's the one segment CEO Petz named unprompted as "more challenging than I would've thought."
- DIY ticked down after three quarters of growth; management called it **"more nominal than material"** and repeated that they're "still waiting for the catalyst," an admission that a chunk of their addressable market simply isn't coming back on its own.
- The **AkzoNobel bid-and-withdrawal got three separate follow-up questions** in one call — unusual repetition — with analysts openly pressing on whether the timing (slide deck out, then a walk-away days later) meant something beyond stated "price discipline." Management's answer leaned on "we're not desperate for these assets" without ever detailing what the "level of engagement" gap actually was.
- Consumer Brands Group's headline 210bp margin expansion was **half core operating leverage, half "favorable non-operating items"** — CFO Meisenzahl conceded that stripping those out, margins were "more flattish" quarter over quarter, a direct example of a number getting props from below-the-line items.
- Full-year 2027 pricing commitments were explicitly punted: **"We're not ready to call anything beyond 2026 right now"** — a hedge given accelerating raw material costs into year-end.
