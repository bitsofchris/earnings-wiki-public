# MSTR — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-31)

**The key idea:** This is not an earnings call for an operating company — it's a sales pitch for a financial-engineering product line built on top of Bitcoin. The GAAP profit ($2.8B net income) is almost entirely fair-value marks on Bitcoin, not business performance, and the entire hour is spent explaining why "digital credit" (four perpetual preferred stocks stripping volatility off BTC) is superior to every category of traditional fixed income. The tension: management insists leverage is going to zero while running a capital-raising machine that depends on continuously selling new securities to fund dividends on old ones.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The software company is gone from the narrative.** Neither Kang, Le, nor Saylor mention the legacy enterprise software business as an operating driver at any point except as a tax/liability consideration ("we wouldn't sell the software business... that would cause income and positive E&P"). MSTR now describes itself purely as a "digital treasury company" and "structured finance company."
2. **Fair-value accounting did the heavy lifting on the headline beat.** Kang states the adoption of fair-value accounting alone "added approximately $18 billion to our digital assets and $12.7 billion to total equity" at adoption — meaning the "transformative" GAAP profit story is largely an accounting regime change, not new value creation.
3. **The credit rating is the actual quarter's news, and it's a B- (junk).** Getting a first-ever S&P rating for a Bitcoin treasury company is presented as historic, but Le is candid that Bitcoin currently gets "no credit... it's deducted from our equity" under S&P's framework — the rating exists despite the collateral being penalized, not because of it.
4. **The whole product suite is reflexive leverage on a single asset.** Strike, Stride, Strife, and Stretch are all repackaged Bitcoin exposure with volatility stripped to different degrees; Saylor is explicit that "the volatility that we strip off of BTC accrues to MSTR" — meaning MSTR common equity absorbs the risk being sold away from preferred buyers.
5. **Retail distribution, not institutional demand, is funding the growth.** Robinhood becoming the first venue to list all four preferreds, and Stretch's retail share jumping from 4% to 23% between IPOs, shows the capital base is shifting toward less sophisticated buyers just as instrument complexity increases.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro or consumer read exists on this call.** Every reference to "the market" is the Bitcoin/crypto market, rate environments in different currencies, or capital markets access — there is no commentary on the real economy, employment, or discretionary spending.
- **Retail is a distribution channel, explicitly.** Saylor frames the sales pitch as needing "military retirees" and people who "like money" rather than sophisticated credit investors — a signal the buyer base skews unsophisticated relative to instrument complexity.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working: capital-raising velocity.** $19.8B raised YTD, four preferred IPOs launched since January 2025, and a credit rating obtained inside nine months of launching the strategy — genuinely fast execution on a capital-markets timeline.
- **What's not working: BTC yield is trailing its own target.** 26% YTD BTC yield against a revised 30% full-year goal, with an analyst (Lance Vitanza) directly noting the shortfall implies needing "at least another couple billion dollars" raised non-dilutively in the final two months — management's answer ("we'll be racing... two good months") is not a plan, it's a hope.
- **mNAV compression is undercutting the equity story.** Saylor concedes mNAV "has been trending down... as the volatility decreases" — the premium that justified buying MSTR stock over Bitcoin directly is eroding as the asset class matures and IBIT's derivatives market absorbs the same volatility-stripping function for free.

## Investing & scarcity
- **All capital flows to one asset.** Every dollar raised — equity, converts, or preferred — is immediately converted to Bitcoin; there is no diversification, working-capital buildout, or reinvestment in the software business discussed.
- **The real scarcity is regulatory/ratings acceptance, not capital or coin supply.** Saylor's own list of "headwinds" is entirely about Basel capital rules, credit-rating-agency treatment of Bitcoin as collateral, and educating "banks, insurance companies, credit rating agencies" — not about running out of money or Bitcoin to buy.
- **International expansion is the next investment vector.** Plans for CAD-, EUR-, and other-currency-denominated preferred products are being built specifically to avoid forcing foreign investors to take currency risk — a real, funded expansion, not just talk (Le: "we're pretty far along").

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: Bitcoin compounds 30%/year for 20 years, leverage goes to zero.** Action: they're actually reducing convertible debt reliance and letting it equitize to zero by 2029, and did raise the Stretch dividend four times to defend its price — real, funded follow-through on the deleveraging thesis.
- **Belief: institutional (pension/insurance) capital will eventually flow once rated.** Action so far is limited to "conversations" — Le admits "it's not going to be an avalanche like tomorrow" — this is a stated expectation well ahead of any funded commitment.
- **Gap to flag:** management claims "leverage is going to zero" as almost a present-tense fact, while the balance sheet still carries $8.2B in convertible notes and Saylor states current leverage at 11% with 21% amplification in the same breath — the rhetoric is ahead of the actual capital structure.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No commitment on M&A of distressed Bitcoin treasury peers**, despite a direct question about accretive roll-ups — Saylor calls it a possible "option" but states the team is "not the ones" pursuing it, citing distraction risk and technology-M&A due-diligence hazards.
- **No commitment on buybacks below 1x mNAV** — an open authorization exists (last used ~2018) but Le calls it "not our primary strategy," leaving it purely optionally.
- **No specific mechanism disclosed for the last ~$2B needed to hit the 30% BTC yield target**, despite direct analyst pressure with only two months left in the year.
