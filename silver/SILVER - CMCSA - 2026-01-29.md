# CMCSA — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-29)

**The key idea:** This is a company mid-reset: a new co-CEO structure and a brand-new Connectivity & Platforms chief (Steve Crony, on his first call) are unwinding years of promo-driven broadband pricing in favor of simplicity and a free-wireless-line convergence play, while absorbing the first, most expensive year of a new NBA rights deal at Peacock. The bet is that near-term EBITDA pain (down 10% this quarter) buys a more durable, less promo-dependent subscriber base — but broadband still lost subscribers this quarter, and management wouldn't commit to a date when that turns around.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Broadband is still shrinking during the "fix."** Despite the biggest go-to-market overhaul in company history — four simplified speed tiers, all-in pricing, a five-year price guarantee — Comcast Corporation still lost 181,000 broadband subscribers in the quarter. Management frames early signals (lower voluntary churn, gig-plus mix improving to ~40% of base) as encouraging, but the net number moved the wrong way.

2. **Wireless is the real growth lever, funded by giving broadband away.** Comcast Corporation added 1.5 million wireless lines in 2025 (best year ever) largely via a twelve-month free-line promotion — nearly half of postpaid connects in the back half of the year were free lines. This is explicitly a customer-acquisition subsidy: "Our free line strategy is a logical and, importantly, a rational competitive approach for us," said Michael Cavanagh, betting the conversion to paid after twelve months makes the subsidy worth it. That conversion hasn't happened yet — it's the whole 2026 thesis.

3. **The "biggest investment year" is a relabeled discount, not new capex.** CFO Jason Armstrong was explicit: 2026 capital spending will be "relatively similar to 2025." The "investment" language covers rate giveback (lower everyday pricing, free wireless lines) and opex (marketing, customer service headcount) — a materially softer claim than the "largest broadband investment year in our history" framing implies.

4. **Peacock is absorbing NBA rights costs up front.** Straight-line amortization of NBA rights front-loads the expense hit — Q1 2026 will see "peak EBITDA dilution" with ~50% of NBA games played in that quarter alone. Peacock lost $552M in Q4 alone even as full-year losses improved ~$700M year-over-year. The bet is that ad growth (170+ new NBA advertisers, 20% net-new) and affiliate renewals from 2025-2028 eventually offset the rights cost — a multi-year payback, not a near-term one.

5. **Theme parks are the unambiguous bright spot.** Epic Universe drove theme park EBITDA over $1B in a single quarter for the first time, with Orlando hotel ADR up 20% and occupancy up 3%. Unlike broadband and Peacock, this is a completed investment (construction done) now converting straight to margin — "we will be, I think, fully ramped up in that park" by end of 2026, per Cavanagh.

## Economy & consumer

- **No macro read offered.** The call is entirely about industry-specific competitive dynamics (fiber overbuild, fixed wireless, mobile price wars), not consumer spending health broadly — notable by omission for a company touching tens of millions of households.
- **Promo fatigue is the implicit consumer insight.** The entire broadband repositioning — moving "away from short-term promotions toward a clear, transparent value proposition" — is an admission that the old rotating-discount model was alienating customers, evidenced in "voluntary churn continues to trend lower" once the new approach launched.
- **Fiber competition intensified further; fixed wireless plateaued.** Steve Crony noted fiber competitive intensity increased into Q4 and "that remains," while fixed wireless "stayed pretty consistent" — implying fiber, not FWA, is now the sharper broadband threat.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Working: wireless convergence.** Over 9 million total wireless lines, ~15% penetration of the residential broadband base, with 90% of Xfinity mobile traffic offloaded onto Comcast Corporation's own network — a real structural cost advantage versus MVNO-only competitors.
- **Working: theme parks.** Revenue +22%, EBITDA +24% in Q4, crossing $1B EBITDA in a single quarter for the first time, driven by Epic Universe lifting the entire Orlando resort's attendance and per-cap spend.
- **Not working: broadband subscriber losses.** -181K net adds despite the pricing reset; ARPU growth decelerated to just 1.1% and is expected to face "further ARPU pressure for the next couple of quarters."
- **Mixed: Comcast Business.** SMB is merely "stabilizing" amid fixed-wireless competitive pressure, while enterprise/mid-market is the genuine growth engine — a bifurcated segment where scale advantages only show up at the high end.
- **Not yet working: Peacock economics.** $552M quarterly loss with NBA rights now baked in; management projects "another year of meaningful EBITDA improvement" in 2026 but stopped short of a breakeven timeline.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Capex is flat, not growing** — ~$14.4B total company capital spending in 2025 (down 5%), guided "relatively similar" for 2026, split across Connectivity & Platforms (~$10.5B) and Content & Experiences (~$3.6B, down 17% now that Epic Universe construction is done).
- **The real spend is customer economics, not infrastructure.** Free wireless lines, everyday pricing cuts, and customer-service headcount are the actual 2026 investment — a bet on retention and lifetime value, not network build.
- **Network upgrade nearly complete.** ~60% of the footprint now on mid-split spectrum/virtualized architecture, delivering measurable ops gains (20% fewer trouble calls, 35% fewer repair minutes where FDX is deployed) — this is the one place hard infrastructure investment is paying off directly.
- **Scarcity is customer trust and internal focus, not capital.** With balance sheet leverage at 2.3x and $19.2B free cash flow in 2025 (helped by a one-time $2B tax benefit that won't recur), Comcast Corporation isn't capital-constrained — its binding constraint is winning back churn-prone, promo-conditioned broadband customers and executing a major internal reorg without losing momentum.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief: pricing simplification stabilizes and eventually grows broadband.** Action: migrating the "majority" of the base to new pricing by year-end 2026 — but the subscriber count is still falling now, so this is a forward bet not yet validated by results.
- **Belief: free wireless lines convert to a durable paid revenue stream.** Action: doubling down on the free-line promo through 2026, with management projecting "the vast majority" convert to paid — real money (foregone wireless revenue) is riding on a conversion rate they can't yet prove.
- **Belief: Peacock reaches sustainable profitability via price increases, ad growth, and affiliate renewals through 2028.** Action: absorbing NBA rights costs now (peak dilution in Q1 2026) while continuing to invest in content (Taylor Sheridan deal, expanded sports) — a genuinely multi-year, low-visibility payback with no stated breakeven date.
- **Gap flagged directly:** the "biggest broadband investment year" claim is not matched by capex, which is guided flat — the actual commitment is smaller and more reversible (pricing/opex) than the rhetoric suggests.
