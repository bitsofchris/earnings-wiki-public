# CMCSA — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** Comcast is deliberately sacrificing broadband ARPU and Connectivity EBITDA now — via lower everyday pricing, free wireless lines, and bundled gateways — to buy retention and convergence share in a broadband market management believes is permanently bifurcating into fiber vs. capacity-limited alternatives. The bet pays off only if a critical mass of "free" wireless lines convert to paying relationships in the second half of 2026; until then, the story is costs realized today against benefits promised for next year.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The pivot's cost just became visible.** This was the first quarter the pricing/CX reset showed up in the P&L: broadband ARPU growth decelerated to 2.6%, and C&P EBITDA fell 3.7%, with management guiding "we expect this decline to build slightly over the next several quarters."
2. **Wireless is the real growth engine, subsidized aggressively.** Wireless net adds hit a record 414K, with nearly half of new postpaid phone connects coming from a free-line-for-a-year offer — Jason Armstrong called this a deliberate strategy to overcome an "awareness issue," acknowledging today's ARPU drag is tomorrow's convergence tailwind once lines convert to paid in 2H 2026.
3. **Leadership transition lands mid-pivot.** Dave Watson moves to Vice Chairman and Steve Croney becomes C&P CEO in 2026, with Croney credited as the architect of the reorganization (centralized marketing/data science/CX, reduced management layers) already underway — continuity is emphasized, but a CEO change during a self-inflicted margin dip adds execution risk.
4. **Media is the bright spot funding the story.** Peacock losses narrowed by ~$220M YoY, advertising posted its best result of the year (up 2.6%, or 3% ex-Olympics) on Sunday Night Football's record season, and Epic Universe drove Parks revenue up 19% — but NBA rights amortization is about to introduce fresh dilution starting this quarter.
5. **M&A signaling was more open than expected.** Cavanagh reiterated a "very high" bar for deals but explicitly said Comcast would look at "streaming assets and studio assets" complementary to the post-VERSANT NBC Media business, adding "more things are viable than maybe some of the public commentary" — a notable crack in the door given ongoing Warner Bros. Discovery speculation.

## Economy & consumer
- **Broadband market structurally splitting.** Roberts's team frames the end state as two multi-gig symmetrical providers per address plus capacity-limited fixed-wireless competitors — "the broadband environment remains intensely competitive, which we do not expect to change anytime soon."
- **Usage keeps rising regardless of price sensitivity.** Broadband-only data usage hit 800 GB/month, up 9% YoY, even as the company concluded customers needed lower everyday pricing and 5-year rate locks to stay — usage growth and price resistance are coexisting.
- **Price-sensitive segment ceded to fixed wireless.** Management explicitly describes fixed wireless as "a durable competitor, serving price-sensitive segments with moderate performance needs," effectively conceding the low end of the market rather than fighting for it directly.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Video and churn are genuine wins.** Video subscriber losses improved by over 100,000 YoY, the best result in nearly 5 years, with churn at record lows.
- **Broadband subscriber losses persist.** Broadband lost 104,000 subscribers in the quarter; back-to-school seasonality and early pivot traction were "more than offset by the continued intense competitive environment."
- **Business Services is the quiet, steady grower.** Revenue up 6%, EBITDA up nearly 5%, with Enterprise Solutions singled out as the strongest growth driver even as SMB faces elevated fixed-wireless competition.
- **Parks is scaling but throughput-constrained.** Epic Universe drove Parks revenue up 19% and EBITDA up 13%, but Comcast is deliberately "holding back a little" on ride capacity to get the experience right before scaling to full throughput.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex shifting toward network and CPE.** Total capex was $3.1B, with increased spending in Connectivity to pass more homes and deploy the new XB10 gateway — now bundled free into every broadband package — faster.
- **Cost rationalization funding reinvestment.** Armstrong noted the team has been "fairly aggressive in cost rationalization" via divisional realignment and layer cuts, with savings explicitly redirected into the pricing/CX pivot rather than dropped to the bottom line.
- **The scarce resource is competitive share, not capacity.** Unlike compute- or power-constrained sectors, Comcast's binding constraint is customer retention against fiber and fixed-wireless rivals — hence the willingness to burn EBITDA now to lock in relationships.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: ARPU growth returns once the transition laps.** Watson: "we're real confident that we can get back to ARPU growth as we migrate through and manage through the transition," pointing to industry-wide fiber price increases as a favorable long-term backdrop.
- **Action: explicitly declining a broadband rate increase in early 2026.** This is a concrete, money-on-the-table commitment against near-term revenue, not just rhetoric — a real gap between the "we'll get back to growth" belief and the near-term action of forgoing pricing power.
- **Belief: free-line wireless converts into durable convergence revenue.** Armstrong: "what is a headwind right now in the form of wireless to our broadband ARPU becomes a tailwind" once free lines convert in 2H 2026.
- **Action: no guardrail metrics disclosed.** Watson emphasized quality-segment targeting and experience investment but gave "no specifics in terms of the actual migration amounts" — the belief in conversion quality isn't yet backed by a disclosed, checkable commitment.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No conversion-rate guidance.** Management repeatedly declined to quantify what share of free wireless lines will convert to paying relationships or when exactly ARPU inflects, despite this being the crux of the entire investment thesis.
- **M&A stance walks both sides.** Cavanagh maintained a "very high" bar for M&A while simultaneously implying more deals are "viable" than public speculation suggests — a deliberately ambiguous signal rather than a real answer on Warner Bros. Discovery.
- **Buyback pacing trimmed without a firm reason given beyond "near-term headwinds."** Quarterly repurchases were reduced to $1.5B citing broadband EBITDA pressure, NBA rights onboarding, and the VERSANT spin, without specifying magnitude or duration of the pullback.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **ARPU and OpEx trajectory into 2026 dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pressed on the pace of EBITDA pressure, cost investment breakdown (CPE, sales, marketing, customer service), and when the pivot reaches "the other side."
- **Free-line-to-paid conversion quality was a recurring anxiety**, with analysts probing whether Comcast is repeating a pattern seen elsewhere in the industry and what guardrails prevent low-quality subscriber growth.
- **Warner Bros. Discovery/M&A and the Verizon leadership change (Dan Schulman) drew direct questions**, both answered carefully — Comcast confirmed no MVNO relationship concerns with Verizon and left M&A optionality deliberately vague.
- **The compressed worry:** will next year's promised ARPU and convergence inflection actually arrive, or is Comcast trading a real, measurable EBITDA hit today for a conversion story that management can't yet quantify?
