# LIN — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-01)

**The key idea:** Linde delivered a "solid quarter against a challenging economic backdrop" that owes as much to geopolitical dislocation — the Iran/Strait of Hormuz helium shock and European feedstock disadvantage pushing volume to Americas — as to genuine demand recovery. Management is candid that this is a lopsided cycle: Americas running hot, EMEA still contracting, and the company is holding back on both raising full-year guidance and helium spot sales until the picture clarifies.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Helium went from glut to shortage overnight.** Through 2025 Linde was managing helium oversupply; the Iran conflict and Qatari supply disruption flipped that to "acute global shortages" mid-quarter. CFO Matt White: "I don't anticipate significant spot sales this year since we're focused on securing long-term agreements" — a deliberate choice to sacrifice near-term pricing upside for durable contract share.
2. **Americas is doing the heavy lifting, and it's a feedstock story, not a demand story.** +2% volume growth (best since Q3 2022) is explicitly attributed to production relocating from Europe and the Middle East toward "more feedstock-advantaged assets" — Gulf Coast refining, Latin American upstream, U.S. reshoring/PMI strength — rather than a broad-based industrial upturn.
3. **EMEA remains the missing cylinder.** White's own framing: "we have an engine here with a few cylinders... we're not running on all three." EMEA onsite chemicals/energy volumes declined year-over-year as customers shifted production out of continental Europe; management sees no path to improvement without a policy catalyst (import restrictions or IRA-style stimulus actually reaching the ground).
4. **Guidance discipline over optimism.** Full-year EPS range raised from the bottom only ($0.20) while the top was explicitly held flat — "it's still early to signal increased optimism... a lot of things happening in the world right now." Notably, guidance embeds zero helium upside, meaning any pricing/volume gains there are pure incremental beat potential.

## Economy & consumer
- **No economic improvement, still.** Guidance assumes flat macro at the midpoint for what one analyst noted is roughly the third or fourth straight year of no industrial improvement; management's own bar for calling a recovery is base volumes "rounding to positive" globally — not yet met.
- **Consumer proxy is healthcare, and it's soft.** Home care in the U.S. is flat, not growing with demographics as normal, due to a late-2025 policy change cutting reimbursed services for a specific device category — an effect White says "will continue for the next several quarters."
- **Food & beverage is the steady bright spot**, up 5% on broad-based U.S. beverage and bottling/freezing strength, framed as a reliable mid-to-high-single-digit grower unrelated to the macro noise elsewhere.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Margins expanding via management action, not volume.** Operating margin held at 30% with 50bps sequential improvement "driven by management actions in pricing and cost productivity" offsetting seasonal volume declines — full-year margin expansion guided to the "upper end or even above" the typical 40-60bps range.
- **APAC margin dip was a mix artifact.** A one-off equipment sale tied to long-term electronics merchant contracts (~0.5% of sales growth) came in at lower margin; White expects APAC back to ~29% margins as that normalizes.
- **EMEA is where the business is genuinely struggling** — weak chemicals/energy volumes, direct and indirect Middle East conflict impacts, and management admits: "we're not happy with the performance."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Electronics backlog scaling fast.** Sale-of-gas project backlog sits just over $7.1B; White expects it to exceed $7B further and "could potentially have an $8 handle on it" by year-end from pending electronics contract signings — directly tied to AI chip fab investment.
- **Woodside project has slipped.** Nitrogen startup remains on track this quarter, but the ATR/TNS (carbon sequestration) phase has slipped from year-end into Q1 next year due to U.S. Gulf Coast "construction and subcontractor" delays — a concrete example of execution friction even in a well-funded project.
- **Helium is now the binding constraint**, not capacity or capital — Linde is choosing which customers get supply rather than chasing all available demand, prioritizing existing contracts first.
- **Commercial space optionality is growing but explicitly unquantified.** Management raised the disclosure threshold discussion (5% of sales, ~$1.7B) without committing to a firm timeline: "never give a number and a year."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** chemicals/energy strength in Americas continues, comps get easier through the year, and helium pricing keeps climbing. **Action:** they're not raising the top of guidance and are declining spot helium sales — actual commitments lag the stated optimism, a deliberate hedge against volatility rather than a demand signal.
- **Belief:** electronics/AI investment continues accelerating with "a high degree of confidence" in new backlog additions. **Action:** already over $1B committed to ultra-high-purity plants — this is one area where money is actually following the words.
- **Belief:** Europe could improve with a policy catalyst (IRA-style stimulus, import restrictions). **Action:** none — management is explicitly waiting for external catalysts rather than deploying capital into EMEA today.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to raise the top of full-year guidance**, holding it at $17.90 despite beating the top end of Q1 guidance, wanting "a few more months" of clarity on geopolitical events.
- **No view taken on how the helium and Russia/China situations resolve** — guidance intentionally excludes any helium upside "either way," and management wouldn't quantify how much incremental helium volume/pricing could ultimately be captured.
- **Declined to give a firm commercial space revenue timeline**, walking back a prior "~$1B by end of decade" framing without committing to a new number.
- **No structural European price increase yet** — treating energy cost inflation as "surcharges" (reversible) rather than committing to embedding it into base price, pending evidence the cost increase is sustained rather than volatile.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Skepticism about whether Americas strength is a real cycle turn** or war-driven relocation noise — one analyst directly asked if this constitutes a genuine cyclical upswing; White declined to confirm, citing EMEA's continued weakness.
- **Repeated probing on Europe's lack of a catalyst** — energy security, de-globalization, IRA stimulus effectiveness — with management consistently pointing to the need for external policy action rather than internal levers.
