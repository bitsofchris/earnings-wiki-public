# MRK — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-03)

**The key idea:** This is a bridge-year call. KEYTRUDA is still doing the heavy lifting (79% of oncology, still growing 5%), but every question and half of management's prepared remarks are about proving the $70B "post-LOE" pipeline is real before the 2028 patent cliff arrives. Two acquisitions in four months (Verona, Sidera) and a $9B one-time charge signal urgency; the guidance for 2026 (1-3% revenue growth) is deliberately unglamorous because the real pitch is 2027-2030s, not this year.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The pipeline number keeps inflating, and management leans on that trend as proof.** CEO Robert Davis repeatedly cites "$70 billion... $20 billion more than just a year ago and more than double consensus 2028 peak KEYTRUDA revenue of $35 billion" — the growth-of-the-estimate is being used as evidence of derisking, which is a softer claim than actual derisking.
2. **Merck bought its way into a flu franchise mid-flu-season.** The Sidera acquisition (MK1406, a long-acting antiviral) closed just as "the scale of the ongoing seasonal flu outbreak in the Northern Hemisphere reinforces the threat" — convenient timing that management leaned into for a >$5B revenue-potential pitch, funded by a $9B one-time non-cash charge that guts 2026 GAAP-adjacent EPS.
3. **Gardasil's China/Japan collapse (-35%) is now a permanent feature, not a blip**, being offset — not solved — by Keytruda combination growth and new launches (WinRevair, Otovir, Capvaxive) that are real but small relative to what Gardasil used to contribute.
4. **The IP-defense answer to a direct patent question reveals more optimism than the guidance admits.** CFO models 2028 KEYTRUDA LOE (compound patent) as the "conservative" planning assumption, but CEO Davis volunteers that confidence in defending two additional patents (extending protection to May/November 2029) "has grown" — guidance is intentionally more bearish than management's private read.
5. **WinRevair is being positioned as a platform, not a product** — Dean Li draws an explicit analogy to ACE inhibitors reshaping heart-failure standard of care, and investigators are independently probing WinRevair for connective-tissue and pulmonary-fibrosis-adjacent indications, which management hasn't committed to trials for yet.

## Economy & consumer
No macro or consumer-spending commentary — pharma demand here is discussed purely in terms of prescription volumes, patent status, and payer/IRA dynamics, insulated from the broader economic cycle discussed on other 2026 calls.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **KEYTRUDA still carries the quarter** — Keytruda family sales **"increased 5% to $8.4 billion,"** driven by earlier-stage cancer uptake and women's cancers, with a **$200 million US headwind purely from purchase timing** (not demand).
- **New launches are real but early**: WinRevair **"global sales were $467 million"** with over 1,500 new US patients in the quarter and >27,000 total prescriptions; Otovir (COPD, post-Verona) at $178M; Capvaxive (pneumococcal) at $279M.
- **Gardasil is the clear casualty** — sales **"$1 billion, a decrease of 35% driven by lower demand in China and Japan,"** with US growth "largely due to price," not volume — a warning sign on underlying demand.
- **Inflonsia (RSV) badly underperforming** — sales of just $21M, explicitly blamed on **"lower-than-expected infant immunization rate coupled with high levels of total RSV monoclonal antibody inventory in the market"** — a market-saturation admission, not just a slow launch.
- **Animal health is the quiet strong performer**, +6% with livestock +9% "driven by higher demand across all species," while companion animal was flat on fewer vet visits.

## Investing & scarcity
- **2026 opex guided to $35.9-36.9B, including "a one-time charge of approximately $9 billion related to the acquisition of Sidera"** — a huge one-time hit that drags headline EPS from a $9.03 underlying midpoint down to $5.08 reported.
- **BD appetite explicitly still open**: CEO reiterates **"up to $15 billion as our sweet spot, but... for the right scientific deal, we would go bigger"** — after already closing Verona and Sidera in quick succession.
- **Capital discipline maintained on paper**: ~$3B buybacks planned, dividend growth commitment retained, "not having excess cash build on the balance sheet" — modest relative to the M&A pace.
- **Scarcity is time, not capital**: the whole pipeline story is gated on **"substantially all of that [the $70B] we are going to derisk... by the time we get to 2027"** — a two-year proof window self-imposed by management.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: KEYTRUDA LOE won't be the growth cliff the Street fears, because the >20-program pipeline plus animal health plus BD gets Merck to sustainable "4% to 7%... roughly 5% to 8%" underlying growth once one-time LOE headwinds roll off.
- **Action backing it**: two acquisitions completed within four months, a $9B charge taken proactively, 21 new phase 3 trials initiated in 2025 alone, and QLEX pricing engineered to pull IV KEYTRUDA volume ahead of biosimilar entry.
- **Gap**: none of the ten programs management says will be "substantially clinically derisked" over the next two years have reported pivotal data yet — the CEO's confidence ("as high as it has ever been") is running ahead of the actual trial readouts, which is exactly what several analysts pushed on.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No interim-analysis disclosure timeline for MK1406/ANCHOR**, despite a severe flu season plausibly accelerating the event-driven trial — Dean Li: **"at this time, we have not spoken about communication plans following IA."**
- **Refused to engage on M&A rumors**: **"we do not comment or speculate on market rumors,"** even when asked directly to reconcile a large rumored deal against Merck's historical $5B-for-$10B BD pattern.
- **Declined to detail TL1A combination strategy**: Dean Li explicitly said the combinability/bispecific roadmap **"is well in line. But that is probably something that I do not want to say in a public call at this time"** — an unusually candid admission of withheld information.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Derisking skepticism dominated**: questions probed whether the $70B pipeline number is provably real (patent-life modeling, Trope-2 ADC competitive positioning versus AstraZeneca/Daiichi, TL1A combinability) rather than an expanding but still-speculative estimate.
- **Growth-sustainability pushback**: one analyst directly asked if Merck is "a company that grows modestly in good times and significantly pressured in less good times" — management's answer leaned on ex-LOE underlying growth math (5-8%) rather than disputing the framing on reported numbers.
- **BD sizing questions** given the gap between Merck's historical small-deal discipline and a rumored large transaction, met with a flat non-answer.
- Compressed worry: **is the post-KEYTRUDA pipeline actually being derisked on the timeline management claims, or is the growing dollar figure doing rhetorical work that the trial data hasn't caught up to yet?**
