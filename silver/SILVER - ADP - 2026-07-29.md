# ADP — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** ADP is positioning itself as the trusted execution layer for the AI-driven workforce redesign rather than a company AI will disrupt — the pitch is that as jobs get restructured at the task level, compliance and payroll complexity only grow, and ADP's data, distribution, and "human judgment" moat make it the beneficiary, not the casualty.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **ADP Assist is now nearly universal**, live across almost all 1.1M clients, with 3.1M unique users generating 12M conversations and surfacing 45,000 compliance insights since a January launch — the company is treating agentic AI as embedded infrastructure, not a bolt-on feature, and just extended it onto the ADP Marketplace via API.
- **Lyric (the next-gen HCM platform) is the growth engine**, with live clients up 94% year-over-year and pipeline up 50%, 70% of it new logos. Notably, "every single one" of Q4's Lyric wins was "anchored in an AI discussion," per Maria Black — the platform is selling as an AI story, not a payroll upgrade.
- **The Zone (internal AI service platform) blew past its own target**, reaching 48% of the service population versus a 10% start-of-year base and a stated goal that was already exceeded; management now wants it broadly deployed across sales and U.S. service through fiscal 2027.
- **A wage-premium research partnership with Stanford's Digital Economy Lab** is being built into a differentiated data product — mapping which tasks (not job titles) command rising or falling pay, e.g., early IT-worker data shows design/development/analysis commanding premiums over testing/documentation/monitoring. This is a genuine moat argument: no competitor has payroll data at ADP's scale to run this.
- **Leaning harder into advisory/consulting-adjacent work**, citing a global auto manufacturer's AI-driven, 13-country labor restructuring as an example — CFO Peter Hadley confirmed this shows up as discrete professional-services revenue plus blueprinting fees baked into enterprise implementations.

## What's changing
- **AI monetization is diffuse, not a line item** — CEO and CFO both concede it shows up "in the fabric" via bookings, retention, and margin rather than as a distinct SKU, with only Marketplace/API Central carved out as discrete consumption pricing. That's a tell that direct AI monetization is still immature.
- **Headcount has flattened at 67,000** for a second straight year (was growing post-pandemic), with hiring redirected into sales/product while service and operations shrink via AI tools — a real, if modest, admission that AI is displacing roles internally even as the external message insists jobs are being "redesigned, not eliminated."
- **Service contacts per client fell 4%** as The Zone scaled — a genuine productivity signal, though management is careful to frame it as freeing staff for higher-value work rather than headcount reduction.
- **Pricing contribution ran ahead of plan** — ES price contributed just over 130 basis points to revenue growth in fiscal 2026 versus the ~100 bps guided at last year's Investor Day, and fiscal 2027 assumes similar levels — a quiet, steady price hike embedded in "value-based pricing."
- **Float income keeps doing heavy lifting**: client funds interest revenue is guided up from $1.35B to $1.54-1.56B in fiscal 2027 on a rising average yield (3.4% → 3.7%), even as underlying balance growth decelerates from 7% to 3-4% — margin expansion next year leans more on rates than on organic client fund growth.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Core ES revenue growth (ex-float, ex-FX) is guided lower** — an analyst flagged the underlying 4-5% implied growth as below the Street and below fiscal 2026's 6%, and Hadley's answer pointed mainly to the loss of last year's WorkForce Software acquisition anniversary tailwind and FX — a real deceleration dressed as a comparison-base issue.
- **ES retention guidance calls for a 10-30 bps decline** from a record 92.1%, with Hadley citing "potential for out-of-business rates to increase in the down market" — a rare acknowledgment of small-business fragility sitting underneath an otherwise upbeat narrative.
- **PEO margins contracted for a second straight year** (110 bps for fiscal 2026, more contraction guided for 2027), pinned on faster-growing zero-margin pass-through revenue and elevated workers' comp/selling costs — a segment management is quietly letting shrink in margin terms while touting its bookings and retention.
- Management **would not disclose win-rate figures** for Lyric or other products when asked directly, twice deflecting to "we're winning more" without numbers — a conspicuous refusal to quantify a metric they clearly track closely.
- **US pays-per-control growth is guided flat to 1%** for fiscal 2027, unchanged from a soft 1% in fiscal 2026 — ADP's own labor-market bellwether data show tepid hiring, a data point somewhat at odds with the "AI is reshaping work, not shrinking it" message repeated throughout the call.
- No mention of layoff-related severance or restructuring charges, unlike some peers navigating AI transitions — a conspicuous absence given how much the call leans on the "workforce redesign" narrative.
