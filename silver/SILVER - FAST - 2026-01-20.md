# FAST — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-20)

**The key idea:** Fastenal is compounding through a leadership transition (CEO- and CFO-elect both on their first calls, dialed in from three different cities) by doubling down on its core playbook — chase big accounts, embed vending/digital devices deeper, control SG&A — while the industrial economy underneath it stays stubbornly flat. The tension: growth is real and share gains are real, but management is candid that pricing has been a year of guessing wrong, gross margin got dinged by one-time noise, and the tailwind that propped up 2025 margins (the fastener expansion project) rolls off in Q1 2026.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Growth is share-gain, not macro-recovery.** Daily sales +11% for a second straight quarter came "despite a sluggish industrial economy" — PMI averaged low-48s and CEO Dan Florness flatly contradicted his own new CFO's suggestion of an industrial-production uptick: "in all candor, we haven't seen it in our numbers."
2. **The digital flywheel is now the majority of the business.** FMI vending plus e-business hit 62.1% of Q4 sales, up from a steady multi-year climb; nearly half of all sales are dispensed or managed through vending hardware alone — a genuine structural moat (stickiness, data visibility) that's directly analogous to instrumentation/observability lock-in.
3. **Tariff pass-through is uneven and it's costing them.** Fastener pricing is clean because Fastenal buys direct from manufacturers with full visibility; non-fastener branded products (cutting tools, etc.) are opaque, and suppliers are blending tariff costs "across the landscape" rather than by true geographic source — Florness described "push back like crazy" as the active countermeasure.
4. **A one-time margin prop is expiring.** The fastener-expansion project was 2025's single largest gross-margin contributor and offset the ongoing dilution from the mix shift to lower-margin large accounts; it anniversaries after Q1 2026, and management is explicitly counting on SG&A leverage to absorb the resulting gross-margin step-down rather than any new margin driver.
5. **Supplier rebates went quiet — read it as a demand signal.** Year-end "deals we normally can't say no to" from suppliers trying to hit their numbers simply didn't show up in Q4; Florness's read: suppliers either gave up on hitting targets or are "keeping their gunpowder dry for next year" — a soft, anecdotal but notable data point on supplier-side confidence.

## Economy & consumer
- **Industrial backdrop: flat, not recovering.** Q4 PMI in the low 48s, industrial production "close to flat," and heavy manufacturing customers described by President Jeffery Watts as "not seeing any real decline... but we're also not seeing any declines" — a static, not improving, environment.
- **No end-consumer exposure** — Fastenal sells B2B into industrial, construction, and government accounts, so a consumer answer doesn't apply; omitted from frontmatter for that reason.
- **Holiday shutdowns skewed December, not demand.** New Year's week saw shutdown facility counts roughly double (10%→20% of customers) versus prior years due to midweek holiday timing, but January "completely normalized" back to low single digits — management insists November/December sequentials are noise: "thirty years of experience... has told me November and December don't matter."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Key accounts are the growth engine.** 50k+ spend sites up 14% y/y and now over half of revenue; total contract customers up just over 7% for the year; heavy/other manufacturing customers grew double-digit, "far outpacing general industrial production."
- **Deliberately bleeding the long tail.** Under-$5k/month sites declined, with 94% of that decline concentrated in sub-$500/month accounts — a conscious strategic choice to reallocate sales talent toward large accounts rather than chase small transactional business.
- **Gross margin dinged by timing, not structure.** Full-year gross margin fell 50bps, driven mostly by a supplier-rebate negative true-up (last year was a positive true-up) plus inventory cost-flow timing — CFO Poneglyph: "these effects do not indicate a change in our underlying cost structure," expects it to "completely normalize going forward."
- **SG&A discipline is doing real work.** SG&A fell to 25.4% of sales from 25.9%, more than offsetting a bonus-comp reload; Florness's own accounting shows the company shared 29% of 2025's true earnings growth via incentive comp (versus only 55%-shared compression in the down year of 2024) — a useful "shock absorber" framing for how reported earnings understate underlying operating leverage.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex stepping up.** 2026 capex guided to ~3.5% of sales, up from 2.8% in 2025, aimed at hub/distribution capacity and throughput, more FMI device purchases, and IT — explicitly "getting distribution capacity and throughput ready for our future, which is here."
- **The binding constraint is supplier tariff opacity, not capital or headcount.** In fasteners Fastenal has full visibility to source-of-supply and manages tariffs cleanly; in non-fastener branded categories it doesn't, and that gap directly compressed Q4 non-fastener margins — the company's stated countermeasures are supplier pushback, transparent customer conversations, and product substitution.
- **Capital return remains conventional and ample.** ~$1B in dividends (≈80% of net income) for 2025, ROIC up 90bps to ~31%, balance sheet "capitalized" with buybacks kept opportunistic rather than programmatic.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: double-digit 2026 growth, "not guidance."** Florness repeatedly insisted the 2026 double-digit sales outlook is "momentum," not formal guidance, while in the same breath giving analysts a specific incremental-margin range ("high 20s... your number doesn't make me uncomfortable") — a distinction management leans on but that functions like guidance in substance.
- **Belief: pricing will "normalize" through 2026.** Action: CFO declined to quantify carryover pricing impact beyond "mathematically" implying a positive compare, and explicitly said further price increases are still being pursued but sizing is unknowable given "so many moving parts."
- **Belief: e-commerce reacceleration is coming, "especially in the latter half of '26."** Action: a relaunched website and tighter integration between e-business and FMI/solutions — but Florness undercut the significance himself, noting the pure e-commerce (non-EDI) slice "is actually a pretty small piece of our business" with little power to move gross margin either way.
- **Gap flagged explicitly by management itself:** the fastener-expansion margin tailwind ends after Q1 2026 with no replacement driver named — the plan is to absorb the resulting gross-margin contraction via SG&A efficiency, a bet on cost control rather than a new margin lever.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Pricing size, repeatedly.** "I'm not going to get too deep into the pricing piece, Dave. Because we've done such an excellent job of being wrong every time we talk about it" — Florness explicitly citing a track record of bad predictions as the reason to stop making them.
- **Quantifying price given up for volume.** Asked directly whether Fastenal sacrificed price to support share gains, both CEO and CFO punted: "we'll have to punt on that a little bit... No need to get aggressive in that area."
- **New CFO's capital-allocation stance.** Poneglyph, two months into the role, declined to signal any change in framework: "you heard me exactly correct. There's not an adjustment in our thinking" — deferring any real answer to future quarters.

- **Pricing mechanics and cadence dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pressed on 2026 price contribution, rebate-timing math, and price-versus-volume tradeoffs; management deflected specifics on all three, citing 2025's poor forecasting track record.
- **Macro read-through**: analysts wanted to know if January felt better or worse than six months ago; Florness's answer was unambiguous — "sideways" — undercutting any "green shoots" narrative he'd floated earlier in the call.
