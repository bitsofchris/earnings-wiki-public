# VRTX — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-03)

**The key idea:** Vertex is mid-transition from a single-franchise CF company to a four-pillar platform (CF, CASGEVY, pain, renal), and this call is about proving the newer three legs are real: CASGEVY's treatment funnel is finally showing depth at every stage, JOURNAVX's prescription growth is accelerating even as access lags, and povetacicept just completed the fastest Phase 3 enrollment in contemporary IgAN history, setting up an accelerated-approval filing before year-end. The tension is that all three newer businesses are gated by processes Vertex doesn't fully control — payer contracting, transplant-center onboarding, and FDA review — rather than by manufacturing or demand.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **CASGEVY's funnel is thickening, not just widening.** Nearly 300 patients referred, 160+ had first cell collection (110 in the first nine months of 2025 alone, double all of 2024), and 39 infused — the story shifted from "will ATCs adopt this" to visible progress at every stage of a known-duration treatment journey.
2. **JOURNAVX prescriptions are compounding while access lags behind.** Quarterly prescriptions went 10K → 90K → 170K, but the company still lacks formal coverage from the third of three major national PBMs, and the NOPAIN Act's coverage list — expected October 31 — was delayed by the government shutdown with no new timeline given.
3. **Povetacicept enrolled the fastest contemporary Phase 3 in IgAN (~600 patients in ~15 months)**, and with breakthrough therapy designation, rolling review, and a priority review voucher in hand, management commits to filing for accelerated U.S. approval before year-end — a genuinely aggressive regulatory timeline for a first-in-class dual BAFF/APRIL fusion protein.
4. **Vertex is quietly building a fifth commercial franchise (renal) from scratch**, with four kidney disease programs in or nearing pivotal stage (VX-407/ADPKD, inaxaplin/AMKD, pove/IgAN, pove/pMN) — CEO Reshma Kewalramani, a nephrologist by training, is personally driving this as the company's next growth leg.
5. **A pivotal type 1 diabetes trial (zimislecel) was quietly paused mid-dosing** for an "internal manufacturing analysis," with management declining to give any further detail "to maintain study integrity" — the one soft spot on an otherwise clean pipeline update.

## Economy & consumer
- Not applicable in the traditional sense: Vertex's growth this quarter is entirely a function of regulatory approvals, payer negotiations, and treatment-center capacity — CFO Charlie Wagner attributed U.S. growth to "ongoing patient demand and favorable net pricing," not macro conditions.
- The closest proxy for "consumer" friction is insurance access: JOURNAVX's patient support program exists specifically to bridge patients whose plans don't yet cover the drug, and management flagged this as a 2025-into-2026 program, not a short-term stopgap.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** CF franchise delivered "strong double-digit growth" on ALYFTREK uptake (nearly $0.5B in sales in its first 10 months) plus expansion into new geographies (Brazil, Turkey); CASGEVY has "clear line of sight to over $100 million" in 2025 revenue with "significant growth in 2026."
- **What's not:** Gross-to-net on JOURNAVX remains "elevated" for now, driven mainly by the patient support program rather than payer or wholesaler discounts — Wagner explicitly declined to project where it lands in 2026, saying "it would be early to say anything about that for next year."
- **A quiet flag:** the zimislecel (T1D) pivotal trial had dosing "temporarily postponed" for a manufacturing analysis mid-study — management gave no detail and no timeline, which is a bigger tell than the brief mention suggests.

## Investing & scarcity
- Non-GAAP opex guidance was raised for the second consecutive disclosure window (now $5.0-5.1B, up from $4.9-5.0B), attributed directly to "the acceleration in pove development programs across multiple indications and increased investment in commercial and marketing activities" for JOURNAVX.
- Headcount investment is explicit and dated: 150 additional pain sales reps arrive in Q1 2026, tied directly to a stated correlation between rep call frequency and prescription depth.
- The binding constraint isn't cash ($12B on hand, still buying back stock) — it's institutional throughput: ATC onboarding pace for CASGEVY, PBM contracting cycles for JOURNAVX, and FDA review clocks for pove.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** the majority of global TRIKAFTA patients will eventually move to ALYFTREK given its superior sweat-chloride reduction and once-daily dosing. **Acting:** no formal multi-year transition guidance given despite being asked directly — "it would be early" energy extends to this question too, even as CCO Duncan McKechnie says the pace is already "steady."
- **Believe:** pove has "best-in-class potential" in IgAN and membranous nephropathy based on preclinical tissue-penetration engineering and early RUBY-3 clinical signals. **Acting:** filing for accelerated approval before year-end, already enrolling a second indication (pMN) in parallel — a real bet, not just talk, backed by a priority review voucher purchase.
- **Believe:** renal medicine broadly is "experiencing a renaissance" and represents Vertex's "fourth vertical" of growth. **Acting:** four separate pivotal-stage or pivotal-approaching programs and a newly built-out renal commercial organization — this is the most capital- and headcount-backed of the forward beliefs on the call.
