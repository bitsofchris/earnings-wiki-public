# UNH — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-28)

**The key idea:** This is a self-diagnosed turnaround call: new CEO Stephen Hemsley and brand-new CFO Wayne DeVeydt are narrating a multi-year unwind of mispriced risk and an overextended Optum Health, trading 2026 growth for a promised "higher and sustainable double-digit growth beginning in 2027." The tension is that management asks the Street to trust a multi-year bridge to margin recovery while withholding both 2026 guidance and hard numbers on how much the fix actually costs.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Optum Health is being shrunk on purpose.** Management is walking back years of expansion — narrowing networks, cutting ~40% of the PPO footprint, and expecting VBC membership to fall ~10% in 2026 — because, per Patrick Conway, "the provider network grew too large" and risk was written in "products and services less suited for a clinically oriented value-based model."
2. **Pricing repair is the whole 2026 story.** UHC has repriced "the vast majority" of Medicare Advantage, commercial fully-insured, and ACA books; ACA rate filings average over 25% higher, even as that pushes ACA enrollment down roughly two-thirds — margin recovery is being bought with volume.
3. **Capital return stays frozen for another year.** Buybacks and M&A remain paused while debt-to-capital (44.1%) works back toward a ~40% target, with the CFO only committing to "may be in a position to reinstate our historical capital deployment practices later in the year" for 2026.
4. **Medicare Advantage margin math trades members for basis points.** UNH expects to lose ~1 million MA members (600K from deliberate exits, the rest from group/individual pricing discipline and competitor dislocation) in exchange for pushing MA margin into "the upper half of our 2% to 4% targeted range."
5. **A real but unquantified restructuring charge is coming.** UNH flagged a "low single-digit billion-dollar" non-GAAP, largely noncash charge tied to international exits, Optum Health consolidation, and Optum Financial Services realignment — details deferred to the Q4 call.

## Economy & consumer
- **Policy, not demand, is the macro driver here.** The dominant environmental force cited is government funding — "the third year of nearly $50 billion in industry-wide Medicare cuts by the previous administration" plus Medicaid states that "have not funded in line with actual cost trends."
- **Affordability pressure is visible across every consumer segment.** Commercial medical trend is running ~11%, MA members face benefit cuts and plan exits, and ACA members are being priced and geographically reduced out of the market — Dan Schumacher noted employers find "health care affordability is top of mind... even a little bit louder given the trends."
- **No commentary on the broader economy or labor market** — UNH's "consumer" is entirely refracted through health-plan enrollment and utilization, not spending power or employment.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **UnitedHealthcare's core servicing metrics remain strong.** Tim Noel cited 85% digital inquiry resolution, 90% of calls answered within 30 seconds, 95% first-call resolution, and 95% of claims auto-processed — the chassis is intact even as pricing wasn't.
- **Medical cost ratio deteriorated sharply year-over-year** — 89.9% in Q3 vs. 85.2% a year prior — with Medicare margins expected at breakeven for full-year 2025 and Medicaid margins negative amid unresolved rate adequacy.
- **Optum Health is explicitly underperforming its design.** Full-year 2025 Optum Health margin is guided to "just under 3%," with value-based-care margin under 1% — well below the long-term 6-8% target — and management named three specific causes: over-large network, weak integration of affiliated physicians, and risk taken in unsuitable products.
- **Optum Rx is the bright spot.** Double-digit pharmacy revenue growth, strong retention, and industry-leading rebate pass-through adoption (85% now, targeting 95% by 2027, 100% by 2028) — a rare unambiguous win on this call.
- **Optum Insight is "solid but not at potential."** New AI products (Optum Real, Optum Integrity One claiming 73%/23% productivity gains, Crimson AI at a claimed 13:1 ROI) are early-stage; new leader Sandeep Dadlani is only four weeks into the role.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Investment is concentrated in Optum Health/Insight fixes and AI.** Of the $450M+ Q3 discretionary step-up, roughly a third was a one-time UnitedHealth Foundation catch-up funding; the CFO characterized the remainder — people and AI investment in Optum — as "recurring in nature, part of our core business."
- **The real constraint is balance-sheet, self-imposed.** Debt-to-capital at 44.1% versus a ~40% target is why buybacks, M&A, and full reinvestment are all on hold — a capital-allocation choice, not a supply or demand constraint.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management expects "higher and sustainable double-digit growth beginning in 2027 and advancing from there," with 2026 positioned as a stabilization/recovery year.
- **Action backing it up:** repricing nearly all major risk books, completing ~90% of VBC payer recontracting to offset half the 2026 V28 headwind, and executing hard exits (600K+ MA lives, 40%+ of Optum Health's PPO footprint) — these are real, quantified commitments, not aspirational language.
- **Gap:** the 2027 growth claim rests on Optum Health and Optum Insight investments whose scope and payoff timing management explicitly declined to quantify ("I don't think we can achieve the level of precision you might be looking for" — Hemsley), meaning the biggest lever behind the multi-year story is the least measurable one on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 guidance.** Hemsley pointed to "current analyst consensus" as a "likely stepping off point" rather than issuing numbers, deferring formal guidance to January.
- **No breakdown of Optum Health turnaround spend.** Asked directly to split investment across risk exit, fee-for-service fixes, and operating-model integration, Hemsley said precision "like that" wasn't available — Krista Nelson gave only qualitative sequencing ("likely more back half weighted").
- **Medicaid margin recovery timeline stayed vague** — "over time about an 18- to 24-month period" to return to ~2% margin, with no rate-certainty commitment given ongoing state funding disputes.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Optum Health unit economics dominated the Q&A** — revenue mix (VBC vs. fee-for-service vs. payer/employer services), the V28 headwind offset mechanics, and portfolio rationalization criteria were asked from multiple angles and never got a precise dollar bridge.
- **MA membership erosion mechanics** (individual vs. group vs. duals) and CMS's posture toward the program were probed, with Bobby Hunter conceding growth in 2026 will look "more in line with" muted 2025 levels rather than any acceleration.
- **The discretionary-opex step-up drew a direct ask for one-time vs. run-rate**, the rare question that got a clean numeric answer (roughly a third one-time foundation funding).
- **Compressed worry:** is 2026 genuinely the trough of a real turnaround, or is management again pricing and guiding into another year of surprises — and how much of the "2027 double-digit growth" promise is actually funded versus aspirational?
