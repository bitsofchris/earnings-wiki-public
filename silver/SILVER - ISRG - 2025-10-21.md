# ISRG — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-21)

**The key idea:** ISRG delivered its strongest procedure growth print in years (20% worldwide) on the back of a full quarter of da Vinci V availability, and management is selling it as durable share-of-surgery expansion — new benign general surgery categories, upgrade-driven utilization, refurbished Xi as a new access tier. But underneath the headline, growth had real one-time tailwinds (anecdotal elective pull-forward ahead of ACA/Medicare policy noise, a one-point holiday-timing benefit OUS) that management itself flags as unverified, while bariatrics (GLP-1 drag) and China/Japan remain structurally soft.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The 20% procedure growth number has an asterisk.** CEO David Rosa acknowledged "anecdotal indications" of accelerated elective procedures in July/August tied to Medicare funding and ACA premium uncertainty, but noted "we have looked at third-party data, which obviously lags... it's not clear that that's supported by the third-party data." That's a company flagging a possible pull-forward it can't rule out.
2. **da Vinci V is doing exactly what it was designed to do — pull upgrades, not just new placements.** Trade-in transactions jumped to 141 in Q3 from 38 a year ago, "primarily driven by US customers upgrading to da Vinci V," and V's own utilization is "already outpacing Xi" in its first eighteen months. Refurbished Xi (20 sold so far) is a new segmentation lever aimed at cost-sensitive sites, but pricing is not yet disclosed.
3. **Net placements is a metric management is visibly steering away from.** When asked directly about declining net placements, CFO Jamie Samath pivoted: "we actually look first and foremost at procedure growth as our primary metric of success." That's a real redefinition of what counts as success, occurring in the same quarter placements optics are soft.
4. **Bariatrics remains an unresolved GLP-1 problem with no visibility on the bottom.** Six straight quarters of mid-to-high-single-digit declines, and after meeting ~50 surgeons across two sessions, "none of the surgeons we engage with are yet at a point where predicting a change in trajectory."
5. **China is a structurally worse market, not a temporary one.** "Tenders are slow. Competition is pretty healthy... there are many local preference provinces that want the local player to win," with ongoing price pressure on both capital and consumables — a genuine competitive/political headwind, not just a cyclical dip.

## Economy & consumer
- **Elective procedure timing may reflect policy anxiety, not organic demand.** Management surfaced but couldn't confirm a July/August pull-forward tied to ACA premium changes and Medicare funding noise — an unusually candid admission of a metric they can't fully explain.
- **GLP-1s are reshaping a durable slice of surgical demand.** Bariatric procedures are down mid-to-high-single-digits for six consecutive quarters and now under 3% of da Vinci volume, with no surgeon consensus on when new-patient starts will offset attrition.
- **No broader macro/consumer commentary** — this is a hospital-capex and physician-adoption business, not consumer-facing; healthcare policy (Medicare/ACA) is the closest proxy to "the economy" on this call.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broad-based strength:** 20% worldwide procedure growth, 23% revenue growth, 30% pro forma EPS growth; SP procedures up 91%, ION up 52%; US da Vinci procedure growth accelerated to 16% from 13-14% in H1.
- **Utilization is genuinely improving, not just installed-base growth:** OUS system utilization grew 8% in Q3 vs 6% in H1 and 4% in 2024; US da Vinci utilization hit 2% growth vs flat in H1.
- **Japan, China, UK are the persistent soft spots.** Japan procedure growth "was a little lower than our expectations, reflecting lower capital placements over the last several quarters"; UK cites "government budget challenges"; China faces tender slowdowns and local-preference dynamics.
- **Gross margin ticked down year-over-year (68% vs 69.1%)** on tariffs (90bp), facility costs, and a richer mix of lower-margin V/ION revenue — even as full-year guidance was raised, the underlying margin mix is getting less favorable per-unit.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is flowing into the V rollout, refurbished Xi as a new tier, cardiac R&D, and a go-direct conversion in three European countries** (Italy, Spain, Portugal — ~250 employees transferring from distributors in H1 2026, expected "slightly accretive" to EPS by eliminating distributor margin).
- **The binding constraint is evidence and clearances, not capacity.** Force-feedback adoption is gated on proving forces-to-outcomes links procedure-by-procedure (prostatectomy nerve function, colorectal bowel recovery); SP's US expansion is gated on additional 510(k) clearances beyond the current colorectal/thoracic base.
- **ASC expansion is capital- and reimbursement-constrained, not instrument-constrained:** "the greater constraint is on the capital side," and ASC reimbursement is "a fraction of" HOPD reimbursement — a real structural barrier to site-of-care shift, especially for IDN-owned ASCs. Sterilization/single-use instrumentation is explicitly not a blocker.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** da Vinci V will make robotic surgery "ubiquitous" by lowering the barrier for benign general surgery and enabling new categories (cardiac). **Action:** raised FY25 procedure-growth guidance to 17-17.5%, continued V clearances (Japan/Europe first placements), and R&D into cardiac-specific instrumentation — money and headcount are actually behind this.
- **Belief:** force feedback will improve clinical outcomes. **Action:** still in "limited launch," with outcome studies only "underway" and no committed timeline for broader release — this is the clearest talk-vs-action gap: strong conviction stated, but no capital commitment beyond ongoing studies until data lands.
- **Belief:** refurbished Xi opens meaningful new access/greenfield. **Action:** only 20 units sold to date and pricing still undisclosed — early-stage optionality, not a funded initiative yet.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **2026 gross margin guidance flatly deferred:** "we're going to wait till January to provide color on what the outlook is there."
- **Refurbished Xi pricing undisclosed:** "we're not ready to describe what the range of that might be because we're early."
- **No prediction on bariatric trough timing**, despite direct surgeon engagement — a genuine "we don't know" rather than a dodge dressed as guidance.
- **US long-term utilization ceiling as V mix rises left open:** CFO says "I think we'll see because it's so early with respect to the da Vinci V launch" rather than offering a target.

- **Refurbished Xi as an underappreciated channel:** analysts flagged that "not many street models have XI refurbished units in them," pushing for sizing that management wouldn't give.
- **Margin trajectory and net placements optics:** questions on whether Q4's implied sub-67% gross margin sets up 2026 headwinds, and why net placements (a metric investors watch) look soft — met with a redirect to procedure growth as the "real" success metric.
- **New disease-state ambition (cardiac) and the AI/data "hub" story:** genuine curiosity about where Intuitive's compute/imaging/AI stack goes next, met with enthusiastic but non-specific answers ("look forward to updating you more in the future").
