# TXN — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-27)

**The key idea:** TI is guiding to its first above-seasonal Q1 in roughly fifteen years, and management's entire case rests on real-time order strength rather than pricing or a durable macro signal. The tension: they built a multi-year, multi-billion-dollar bet on capacity and inventory specifically to catch this kind of turn, but they still can't tell if the order pickup is the real cycle or another head-fake like early 2025's.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The slowest recovery on record, finally maybe turning.** Haviv Ilan called this "one of the slowest, if not the slowest recovery ever" in TI's history by unit volume, even as industrial revenue sits ~25% below its 2022 peak — meaning the sequential Q1 uptick is happening off a still-depressed base, not a full-cycle top.
2. **Data center has quietly become a real growth leg.** Grew ~70% y/y in 2025 to $1.5B (9% of revenue, up from a rounding error a few years ago), now growing for seven consecutive quarters and exiting the year at a ~$450M/quarter run rate — TI reorganized its end-market reporting specifically to surface this.
3. **Inventory and capacity as a deliberate weapon, not a problem.** With inventory days at 222 (up 7 sequentially) and a six-year CapEx cycle winding down, management frames the buildup explicitly as readiness for "real-time just-in-time demand" and "turns business" — they want to be the supplier who can ship immediately when a customer's bill of materials finally completes.
4. **Pricing is not the growth story — deliberately positioned against peers.** Asked directly whether competitor price hikes explain TI's strong guide, Haviv said flatly "the answer is no," reiterating an expected low-single-digit price decline in 2026, same as 2025's realized decline. This is a pointed divergence from what analysts say other analog/memory suppliers are doing.
5. **Management still won't call the turn.** Despite bullish language on orders and bookings, both Ilan and Rafael Lizardi repeatedly hedge — declining to give book-to-bill, declining to signal loading direction, and explicitly recalling that a similar order pickup in early 2025 "calmed down."

## Economy & consumer
- **Industrial and auto in a genuine but incomplete recovery.** Industrial grew ~18% y/y in Q4 and 12% for 2025, but Ilan noted revenue is still ~25% below the 2022 peak — "there's still a lot of room to go," which he treats as an easy compare rather than a ceiling.
- **China described as normal, not a wildcard.** Q4 China revenue was down ~7% sequentially (in line with the corporate average) and up ~16% y/y; management expects the usual Chinese New Year seasonal dip in auto orders in Q1, nothing unusual.
- **Personal electronics still the weak link.** Declined mid-teens sequentially and upper-teens y/y in Q4, with home theater/TV weakest and mobile phones the relative standout — management links this partly to expiring China subsidies for appliances/TVs.
- **No direct consumer-spending commentary** — TI sells into device makers, not consumers directly, so end-demand signal here is inferred through personal-electronics sell-through, which read soft.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Analog and embedded both grew, but embedded is riding an insourcing tailwind.** Analog +14% y/y, embedded +8% y/y; embedded growth is partly mechanical — TI completed its 65nm foundry-to-Lehigh transition in 2025 and is now moving 45nm automotive radar production in-house, "yielding at the same level as they used to in the foundries."
- **Gross margin beat came from a mix of small wins, not one driver.** Rafael Lizardi: Q4 outperformance was "a combination of multiple things" — revenue, end-market mix, loadings, and OpEx all a little better than expected.
- **A one-time charge dented EPS.** Net income of $1.2B ($1.27/share) included a 6¢ non-cash goodwill impairment in the "other" segment plus tax items — the "other" segment also declined y/y for the year, the only segment that did.
- **Free cash flow nearly doubled.** 2025 FCF was $2.9B (17% of revenue), up 96% from 2024, aided by a $670M CHIPS Act cash benefit — a meaningful non-operating prop worth flagging even though management frames it as validation of the capacity strategy.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx cliff by design.** 2025 CapEx was $4.6B; 2026 guided to just $2-3B as the "six-year elevated CapEx cycle" ends. Long-term framework: CapEx intensity ≈ 1.2x revenue growth rate gross, but effectively "back to one to one" net of ITC benefits — meaning growth funds itself once tax credits are counted.
- **Depreciation still climbing.** 2026 depreciation guided up to $2.2-2.4B, with "upward pressure" continuing into 2027 "at a slower rate of increase" — the cost of the CapEx cycle lands after the spending does.
- **Sherman and Lehigh both ahead of plan.** Sherman fab 1 clean room already has production lines running, ramping "ahead of schedule, high yield," with the factory turning out "more capable" than originally hoped; Sherman capacity is explicitly positioned to serve data-center power sockets (VCORE/VRM) via TI's BCD process, now sampling.
- **Government incentives are a real, quantified lever.** ITC rose to 35% effective 2026 on all new CapEx; up to $1.6B of direct CHIPS funding remains to be captured on milestone completion — this materially changes the net cost of the remaining buildout.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: orders are strengthening, durability unknown.** Ilan: "we are seeing a little bit of strengthening in the orders right now in Q1... we'll see how long-lived it will be. The market has been jittery in the last twelve months." Despite this uncertainty, the Q1 revenue guide itself already bakes in the stronger booking trend.
- **Action lags the belief, deliberately.** Rafael Lizardi declined to say loadings are being raised in response to demand strength: "we are not making any disclosure right now on which way the loadings are going... nothing significant versus where we've been running." Capacity is being held in reserve rather than committed to the upturn.
- **Data center is the one place belief and action are aligned.** Management both states data center will keep growing ("I expect this market to continue to grow in Q1... I see the opportunity as an attractive one") and backs it with concrete engineering commitment — BCD process technology built specifically in Sherman for high-power VCORE rails, now sampling.
- **Auto: secular belief, cautious near-term framing.** Ilan expects auto content growth "for the next five years" but flags a seasonal Chinese New Year dip in Q1 — belief in the multi-year trend doesn't translate into an aggressive near-term call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No book-to-bill disclosure.** Asked directly for the metric, Mike Beckman said "I don't have a number specifically to provide you," despite repeatedly citing "improving bookings" as the basis for guidance — a case of citing a data point as justification while withholding the data point itself.
- **No commitment on loading direction.** Rafael Lizardi explicitly refused to characterize whether utilization/loadings are rising or falling beyond "nothing significant" change from Q4.
- **No forward CapEx floor confirmed.** Pressed on whether CapEx could fall toward ~$1B (a figure raised from a prior conference comment), Ilan reframed the number as "maintenance CapEx" rather than a hard target, avoiding a specific commitment.
- **No pricing response promised.** On whether TI would match competitors' 2026 price increases, Ilan said only "if anything changes with pricing... Texas Instruments will respond" — an explicitly conditional, non-committal stance.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Cluster 1: What's actually behind the above-seasonal guide?** Multiple questions probed whether pricing, end-market mix, or genuine order strength explains the unusual Q1 guidance — management consistently pointed to orders/bookings and explicitly ruled out pricing.
- **Cluster 2: CapEx and loading trajectory.** Repeated, granular questions on where CapEx and factory loadings go next, including a specific attempt to get a CapEx floor — met with the maintenance-CapEx reframe and no loading commitment.
- **Cluster 3: Inventory strategy and duration.** Whether TI intends to hold, build, or draw down its elevated inventory position — answered consistently as a strategic asset to be maintained, not adjusted for its own sake.
- **Cluster 4: Memory shortage spillover.** Whether rising memory prices are dampening consumer/auto/PC demand or driving defensive TI ordering — management said no clear read yet, only speculation that it could be prompting last-minute completion-of-BOM orders.
- **Dodged:** book-to-bill ratio and explicit loading direction, both declined outright.
- **Compressed worry:** is the Q1 order pickup a real cyclical turn TI's capacity buildout was built for, or another false start like early 2025's — and management itself admits it doesn't know yet.
