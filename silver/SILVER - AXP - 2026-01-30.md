# AXP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-30)

**The key idea:** American Express had its best card-fee year ever ($10B, up 18%) on the back of a US Platinum relaunch, but did it by deliberately shrinking the pool of new cardholders and pouring marketing into fewer, richer relationships. The tension for 2026 is whether that "acquire less, monetize more" trade — dressed up as return discipline — can keep generating mid-teens EPS growth once the Platinum "new card smell" wears off and rewards costs (VCE) settle at a permanently higher ~44% of revenue.

## Economy & consumer
- **Consumer engagement, not just spend, is the tell.** Squeri: restaurant spend up double digits, Resy engagement up sharply, travel bookings up 30% in Q4 tied directly to the new Platinum app — "all of the metrics that we look at speak to the fact that this was a wildly successful product launch."
- **Younger cohorts are now the core of US consumer spend** — millennials and Gen Z are the largest and fastest-growing share, with average new-cardholder age of 33 (Platinum) and 29 (Gold), which management frames as multi-decade relationship runway.
- **SMB is bifurcated:** small business "really, really strong" but middle-market decelerating — echoed industry-wide, not AXP-specific, and increasingly contested by Capital One/Brex and Ramp entering with balance-sheet-backed software plays.
- **International remains the fastest-growing segment** (spend up 12% FX-adjusted), broad-based across consumer and business.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** Record $10B card fees (up 18% for the year), NII outgrowing loan balances (up 12%), high-yield savings deposits up 8% with "less than 10%" of US consumer cardholders penetrated — a long runway management explicitly flagged.
- **What's not:** Net cards acquired declined sequentially in Q4 as the company chose fewer, richer sign-ups over volume — a deliberate trade-off, but one that drew the most analyst pushback of the call.
- **Operating leverage is real:** OpEx-to-revenue down four points since 2022 even as tech spend rose 11% for the year — expense discipline outside of VCE/marketing is funding the investment step-up.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital deployment:** $6.3B marketing spend (up 75% since 2019), $5B/year tech spend, both growing 20%+ over the last two years — with the ability to "dynamically reallocate resources" mid-quarter, as they did by redirecting marketing to Platinum late in the year.
- **The scarce resource is headroom in VCE, not capital or credit.** With credit losses near their floor and VCE guided to ~44% of revenue, the lever left to pull is whether rewards costs plateau as the Platinum cohort "stabilizes" (Squeri's word) rather than keeps climbing.
- **M&A is defensive infrastructure, not growth-by-acquisition:** Center (expense management) and the Resy/Tock combination are being folded in to build owned capability rather than partner with third parties, directly in response to Capital One's acquisition of Brex.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Card fee growth "picks up as the year progresses," exiting 2026 in the high teens, as more Platinum members hit their renewal anniversary at the new price point.
- **Action backing it:** Nothing structural yet beyond the already-completed refresh — this is a belief that the existing cohort will simply age into higher fees, not a new initiative. That's a relatively passive growth driver dressed as guidance conviction.
- **Belief:** GenAI/agentic tools on the new data platform will drive further marketing and fraud efficiency.
- **Action backing it:** Concrete — $5B/year tech budget, cloud migration targeted for 100% completion by 2027, GenAI tools already deployed to "nearly all" colleagues worldwide. This is one of the more credibly funded forward bets on the call.
- **Gap:** Management insists the "cost to grow" isn't rising in real terms, but simultaneously guides VCE-to-revenue up to ~44% and flags marketing at record dollar levels — the qualitative reassurance and the quantitative guide are in mild tension.
