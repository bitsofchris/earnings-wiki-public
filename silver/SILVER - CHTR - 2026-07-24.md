# CHTR — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-24)

**The key idea:** Charter is a broadband company losing broadband customers, betting its future on a delayed mega-merger (Cox) and a capex cliff that's supposed to unlock enormous free cash flow — while management quietly walked back an aggressive retention play that backfired and cut its own EBITDA guide. The subplot: everyone wants to know if Charter's network becomes wholesale infrastructure for AI/wireless newcomers (Starlink, EV fleets), and management keeps saying "it depends" without saying no.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **The Cox deal is the whole story now.** Closing "mid to late August" after a California-driven delay, it roughly doubles Charter to 37 million customers, 70 million passings, and $67 billion revenue — and management now expects transaction synergies to grow from $800 million to **"a billion"** run-rate, not including any operating or capex synergies.
- **Post-Cox, leverage target dropped to a flat 3.5x**, achieved within three years — a direct concession to "equity and debt investor preference for lower leverage," paired with a $20 billion capped bond exchange offer to cut debt principal ahead of close. Buybacks are **paused through Q3** to fund the deal, restarting in Q4.
- **Charter is quietly repositioning itself as AI infrastructure, not just a cable company:** post-network-evolution it will have "over 250 MW of available capacity without additional investment" at its edge data centers, and Winfrey explicitly claims Charter provides "the mission-critical AI infrastructure that will ultimately demand our superior speed, reliability, and low latency" — a notable framing shift for a subscriber-shrinking cable operator to volunteer unprompted.
- **The capex story is the actual investment thesis:** total capex should fall from ~$12.1 billion (LTM) to under $8 billion by 2028, which Fischer says is worth **"over $30 of free cash flow per share"** — and at consensus 2026 FCF with 2028 capex substituted in, implies "a bit over two times" FCF multiple and "nearly 50%" FCF yield at the current price. That's management doing the market's job of arguing its own stock is mispriced.
- **Nick Jeffery joins as COO September 1**, brought in explicitly to fix go-to-market and NPS — Winfrey was unusually direct that he doesn't want current pressure to "hamstring" Jeffery's mandate to accelerate growth.
- **New wholesale optionality is being probed but not committed:** BrightIQ (seamless authentication platform) already powers deals like Amazon's delivery fleet offload, and Winfrey floats EV fleets and even other MNOs as future wholesale customers, alongside a non-denial on Starlink partnership talk ("we talk to many industry players... not the case today").

## What's changing

- **Broadband customer losses got worse, not better:** 172,000 internet customers lost in Q2, "higher than a year ago" — driven entirely by weak gross adds (top-of-funnel softness from fixed wireless, fiber overlap, mobile substitution), with churn "essentially flat."
- **Video is the one product actually improving:** losses shrank to 21,000 from 80,000 a year ago, credited to app-inclusion bundling (55% of eligible video customers now activate apps) and new pricing/packaging — a rare bright spot management leans on hard.
- **Mobile offload economics inverted mid-quarter for a good reason:** Wi-Fi/CBRS offload had been climbing toward 89%, matching peer disclosures (Comcast cited 90%), but a deliberate product change (speed pass-through above certain caps) pushed more traffic onto 5G, **dropping offload back to 87%** — framed as a one-time, customer-experience-positive move, not a cost problem.
- **Programmer-app cost reallocation is scrambling the reported numbers:** $251 million of costs shifted into video revenue this quarter versus $67 million a year ago, making residential revenue (-3.5%) and programming cost (-9.7%) declines look far more dramatic than the underlying business — Fischer repeatedly has to restate figures "excluding" this allocation to show flat-ish reality.
- **Cash tax and debt-repurchase gains are propping up net income:** $1.3 billion net income was "essentially flat" only because a **gain on debt extinguishment** from open-market bond buybacks offset a real 4.3% EBITDA decline (3.2% excluding Cox transition costs).

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **Management admits its own Q1/Q2 retention pricing bet failed.** Winfrey, unprompted: "The pressure that we had inside of Q1, which carried through Q2, really was a bet at the time that you could get a substantial lift... It had some impact, but not enough to really merit what we did. We pulled back. **I own that.**" This directly caused the weaker broadband ARPU and contributed to the full-year EBITDA guide cut.
- **Full-year 2026 standalone EBITDA guidance was lowered** to roughly -1% year-over-year (excluding transition costs), attributed to the failed retention offers plus uncontrollable cost inflation in fuel and medical — a genuine miss dressed as "an update," with Winfrey immediately insisting they're "targeting to do better."
- **Cox transition expenses are running "a bit higher than expected"** — blamed partly on the closing delay itself and partly on a mix shift from capex into opex, though management claims the combined total will still land at or better than plan.
- **Conspicuous absence: no mention of fixed wireless competitors by name, no update on video's structural decline story beyond the app-bundling tactic, and no discussion of what happens to Cox's own weaker subscriber/revenue trends** (confirmed "a couple clicks lower" than Charter's) once folded into consolidated reporting — pro forma comps get murkier, not clearer, starting Q3.
- **Starlink and rural satellite competition is being downplayed** ("we haven't observed meaningful share loss... but we continue to monitor it closely") even as the same call entertains a hypothetical Starlink partnership — an inconsistency in how seriously satellite is treated as competitor versus potential partner.
