# CCEP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-17)

**The key idea:** CCEP delivered a record 2025 (revenue, profit, free cash flow, returns) built on a genuine mix recovery — away-from-home, coolers, energy, premiumization — that offset real soft spots in Germany, France, and Indonesia. The tension for 2026: guidance of 3-4% revenue sits below the 4% midterm algo, explained away almost entirely as portfolio-transition noise (Suntory exit), while the underlying story is that volume needs to do more of the work than it did in 2025's mix-driven year.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Mix, not just pricing, drove the beat.** Over a third of 2.9% revenue-per-case growth came from brand/pack mix — coolers, immediate-consumption single-serve, and Monster — the first year in a while mix "came back," per CEO Damian Gammell, a healthier growth composition than pure price/promo.
2. **Germany is a cautionary tale on promo discipline.** Customers pushed promotional prices "above levels that maybe for the consumer... proved a bit more challenging," and CCEP had to reinvest extra value in H2 to recover — a live example of how mispriced promos show up as reduced purchase frequency, not just lost share.
3. **Energy remains the crown jewel and the capacity bottleneck.** Monster volumes grew ~19-20% with 200+bps share gains; CFO Ed Walker notes about half the growth is from core, half from innovation — a "healthy" split — and CapEx has gone into "more accounting lines" (capacity) just to stay ahead of demand.
4. **Indonesia and Suntory exit are the two real headwinds baked into 2026 guidance**, worth roughly 0.5-1 point of revenue growth combined; strip those out and CCEP would be "bang in line" with its 4% midterm target — a framing management leans on repeatedly to defend guidance that looks soft at first glance.
5. **Capital return continues uninterrupted**: a fresh €1B buyback on top of the dividend, with leverage (2.7x net debt/EBITDA) comfortably inside the 2.5-3x target range and management content to let it drift toward the low end rather than force it lower.

## Economy & consumer
- **Value still matters, but excitement sells.** Gammell: "value has been a key need for some of our consumers... but we also know there's lots of consumers who want to engage with brands... that give them something a bit more."
- **Germany's promo overreach exposed price elasticity.** Higher promo price points caused hesitation — "I'm going to wait until the price comes down again" or reduced purchase frequency — a live case study in the ceiling on price pass-through even for a habitual category.
- **France absorbed a sugar-tax hike better than modeled**, with Coke Classic volumes still dragged but the brand "performed stronger than I would have expected" given the tax size — evidence of real (if imperfect) brand pricing power.
- **Indonesia's macro slowdown hit the category broadly**, with NARTD volumes ex-water down double digits and both local and international brands affected — a genuine demand-side shock, not CCEP-specific execution failure.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record year across every headline metric**: revenue €20.9B (+2.8%), operating profit €2.8B (+7.1%, margin +~50bps), EPS €4.11 (+6.2%), free cash flow just over €1.8B.
- **Away-from-home and coolers were the growth engine**: 75,000+ new coolers placed, GB won major accounts (Arsenal, Fullers, Jet2), and channel mix recovery is explicitly credited for margin-accretive revenue.
- **Philippines and GB were standouts**; Philippines grew revenue 3% with record 77% sparkling share and margin up ~150bps, on track for its 10% EBIT target. GB revenue grew almost 6% with broad-based share gains.
- **Suntory exit is a self-inflicted near-term drag** the company is choosing to absorb: "this creates a near-term headwind, it is the right decision for the long term," trading revenue now for a more integrated RTD tea/alcohol platform later.

## Investing & scarcity
- **~€1B CapEx** funded aseptic capacity, a new Queensland canning line, coolers, digital/AI/SAP S/4HANA, and groundbreaking on CCEP's largest-ever infrastructure investment — a new plant in Tarlac outside Manila.
- **Manila shared service center** is being built as a second global hub (alongside Bulgaria) explicitly to diversify operational risk, not just to serve APS — over 100 people hired in 8 months.
- **The real constraint is capacity/distribution for energy and promo-spend efficiency, not capital.** CEO frames energy capacity investment as "a nice problem to have"; on promo spend, "it's more about promo effectiveness rather than quantity" — CCEP is fully funded, the binding constraint is analytical/targeting sophistication, not budget.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believes 4% midterm revenue growth is durable** and is acting on it with continued ~€1B/year CapEx and unchanged capital allocation framework — genuine follow-through, not just talk.
- **Believes revenue growth mix will normalize to 1/3 volume, 1/3 price, 1/3 mix in 2026** (vs. mix-heavy 2025) — but the "acting" side is thinner here: no specific new volume-driving initiative beyond continued marketing/innovation calendar (World Cup, EPL, product launches), which is largely more-of-the-same rather than a new lever.
- **Believes M&A optionality remains open** ("we've got the balance sheet to do it") but explicitly pushed back when an analyst inferred a more bullish stance — a case of management walking back a inferred forward-looking claim rather than one they made themselves.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **CFO declined to give firmer free cash flow guidance**, setting "at least €1.7B" deliberately low versus 2025's €1.8B despite 7% profit growth guidance, explicitly to avoid "constrain[ing] our ability to invest by quoting a number too high."
- **CEO would not commit Indonesia's improvement to the full-year guidance number**, wanting "a good few quarters under our belt" before reflecting upside — an explicit statement that the guide is conservative on a market management is privately more optimistic about.
- **Declined to specify M&A targets, scale, or geography** when directly asked, reiterating only that the balance sheet supports "value-accretive" opportunities "should" they arise.
