# INTU — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-05-20)

**The key idea:** Intuit's growth engines (Assisted Tax, Money, mid-market) are compounding at 30%+ and funding a bullish AI-platform pivot, but the company just admitted it "lost on price" with its cheapest DIY tax customers and cut 17% of its workforce on the same day — forcing analysts to ask whether this is a fixable pricing mistake or the first visible dent from AI disintermediation that management is carefully declining to call by name.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A 17% layoff announced alongside raised guidance is an unusual pairing.** Revenue guidance went up and EPS growth was reaffirmed at "north of 15%" the same day management cut headcount — Sasan frames it as removing "management layers" and "coordination-heavy roles," explicitly not AI substitution, but the timing invites the opposite read.
2. **The DIY tax miss is being reframed as a pricing problem, not a demand problem.** "We lost on price" among sub-$50K filers, with the fix being a shift from "complexity-based to value-based" SKUs plus monetizing beyond tax — a durable version of the one-time-offer tactics used in 2023-2024, this time backed by real cross-sell data (Credit Karma + TurboTax ARPU up >30%).
3. **Assisted Tax is the actual growth story and it's still early.** TurboTax Live revenue growing 36% (vs. a 15-20% long-term algorithm) and now over half of TurboTax revenue is the clearest signal that Intuit's "buy confidence, not code" thesis is working — customers pay 7x more for human-plus-AI than software alone.
4. **Mailchimp is being quietly demoted, not sold.** Management argues its "revised cash flow profile will generate more value to Intuit than a third party is likely to pay" in the current software M&A environment — a claim that's convenient but unfalsifiable from outside, and the segment gets cost cuts rather than investment.
5. **The IRS-filer decline is doing real narrative work.** A 2-million-unit, ~30bps drop in total filers — the worst since post-COVID — lets management partially externalize the DIY miss onto macro filing behavior even while conceding a company-specific pricing failure in the same breath.

## Economy & consumer
- **Filer contraction was the worst since COVID.** "Total IRS filers are expected to decline by approximately 30 basis points this season... the most significant industry-wide contraction since the post-COVID tax season" — about 2 million units below expectations, concentrated among manual (non-e-file) filers who simply didn't file.
- **Price-sensitive consumers will tolerate a worse product for a lower price.** Sasan: "They're actually willing to have experiences that are far worse for them, as long as the price is right" — a blunt admission about the bottom of the DIY market.
- **The cross-sell consumer is healthier and more valuable.** ARPU is "approximately 30% higher for customers using both TurboTax and Credit Karma," and over 35% of TurboTax customers now adopt fast-money offerings — the platform's best answer to the low end's price sensitivity.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Three big bets are all growing 30%+:** Assisted Tax, Money portfolio, and mid-market — Sasan calls the priority now "scaling these growth engines with even greater speed and impact."
- **DIY is the conceded weak spot.** "I'm constructively dissatisfied with our performance" in a $5B TAM segment where Intuit still aims only to hold revenue share, not grow it.
- **Mid-market and payments are compounding fast.** QBO Advanced/Intuit Enterprise Suite online ecosystem revenue grew ~38%, with IES contracts up 37% quarter-over-quarter; total online payment volume (incl. Bill Pay) grew 30%.
- **Mailchimp is shrinking.** Revenue "down slightly versus a year ago," now explicitly being right-sized rather than turned around.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is flowing to sales capacity and embedded finance.** Direct sales team scaled ~30% for mid-market; buy-now-pay-later and a new Intuit Business Credit Card embedded directly in QuickBooks.
- **August platform launch is the next big lever.** A "sweeping expansion" turning the product into an autonomous-execution "control tower," paired with new consumption-based pricing for AI/human-intelligence services and price increases at the high end.
- **The binding constraint is organizational, not technical.** Management explicitly names "management layers" and "coordination-heavy roles" (PMO, bus ops, redundant product/design roles from the TurboTax-Credit Karma integration) as what's slowing them down — addressed by cutting headcount, not by adding compute or engineers.
- **Buybacks accelerating alongside layoffs.** $1.6B repurchased in Q3, "more than double" last year, with buybacks up over 60% year-to-date — capital return continuing even as the company restructures.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Assisted Tax and mid-market can keep compounding at 30%+ for years given ~90% of the tax TAM and a ~$90B mid-market TAM are barely penetrated. **Action:** scaling sales headcount, launching consumption pricing, and funding an August platform overhaul — genuine money behind the belief.
- **Belief:** DIY's price-sensitive segment is fixable with a durable SKU/pricing redesign rather than one-time offers. **Action:** no concrete new pricing structure or SKU detail was given yet — this is a stated intention without a disclosed mechanism, a gap analysts should watch next quarter.
- **Belief:** AI is not disintermediating the core tax/accounting business because customers "buy confidence, not code." **Action:** simultaneously investing heavily in AI agents and an LLM-adjacent funnel strategy (QuickBooks reachable within Anthropic/OpenAI interfaces) to capture "high-intent" customers before they'd otherwise use a competing AI tool — the action reveals more concern about AI-native entry points than the rhetoric admits.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No dollar or percentage split disclosed** between restructuring savings directed to margin expansion versus reinvestment in growth engines beyond "a big chunk" versus "a smaller part."
- **Declined to link the restructuring to DIY tax weakness at all** — "I would not connect the workforce reductions at all to DIY tax. They're discretely different choices and decisions" — a clean separation that's asserted rather than demonstrated.
- **No specific new DIY pricing/SKU structure disclosed**, only the directional shift from "complexity-based to value-based" tiers.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Multiple questions (Morgan Stanley, Wolfe, Evercore) probed the same core anxiety from different angles: is the DIY/tax miss a repeat of the fixable 2023-2024 pricing problem, or does GenAI represent a structural threat to the category — management repeatedly said "none of this has anything to do with AI" while also crediting AI investment for margin gains and the growth engines, a tension the analysts kept pressing on without a fully separable answer.
- Restructuring rationale (Deutsche Bank) and Mailchimp's fate (Evercore) were the other clusters — both answered with "we're running it for cash flow, not selling it" logic that wasn't independently verifiable on the call.
- Compressed worry: **is the assisted-tax and mid-market growth durable enough to keep outrunning a DIY tax base that's losing its cheapest customers, or is this quarter the first visible edge of AI-driven disintermediation that management is declining to name?**
