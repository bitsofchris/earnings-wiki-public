# MSTR — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-30)

**The key idea:** This call is not about Bitcoin accumulation — it's a rescue operation for STRC, Strategy's flagship preferred, which broke below par in May-June and hasn't recovered. Every capital decision this quarter (rebuilding the USD reserve, buying back converts, small Bitcoin sales, an active repurchase program) was reverse-engineered from "how do we get STRC back to $99-100," with Michael Saylor conceding the company had underweighted dollars and overweighted Bitcoin purity for years. The bet: digital credit, not Bitcoin per share, is now the thing that makes or breaks the trillion-dollar ambition.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **STRC is broken and management knows it.** Trading at ~$89.50 against a $99-100 target, down from a hyper-growth $10.5B notional base. Saylor: "We are certain that we will return STRC to par... The big question mark is how extensive will the buyback be." No date, no dollar cap — just an open-ended commitment.
2. **The maximalist playbook just got reversed.** After running the USD reserve down to $871M (0.5 years of dividend coverage) to fund a convert buyback, Strategy scrambled it back to an all-time-high $3.75B within two months. Saylor: "We saw that when we went 98% or 99% BTC, 1% USD, the Bitcoin investors didn't like it, the equity investors didn't like it, the credit investors didn't like it... I don't think it'll be 100% in the future."
3. **Bitcoin sales are now a tool, not a taboo.** Strategy sold small tranches (0.4% of holdings) explicitly to fund dividends and the dollar reserve — a first for a company whose entire identity was "never sell." Framed as immaterial to price (0.1% of trading volume) but management admits sentiment impact is possible.
4. **The credit engine is the real thesis now, not BPS growth.** BTC Yield collapsed to 4.5% year-to-date vs 22.8% in 2025 (bear-market math), so the pitch shifted to STRC's tax-equivalent 21.6% yield and its new #1 ranking in the three largest preferred-stock indexes (BlackRock PFF, Virtus PFFA, VanEck PFXF).
5. **Principle-over-opportunity signaling is deliberate.** Saylor repeatedly refused "easy" money — selling volatility/covered calls, borrowing against BTC, letting STRC float — arguing that preserving trust with market-makers and credit investors is worth more than any single quarter's capital raise.

## Economy & consumer
- No consumer-facing business exists here; omitted deliberately, not an oversight.
- **Macro is reframed as five specific headwinds to Bitcoin**, not a general economy read: AI-datacenter capital suction (~$1T+), trade/tariff wars, a Gulf War disruption, a "more restrictive" Fed (with rumors of a rate hike and three dissenting governors voting to raise), and delayed regulatory clarity (the Clarity bill). Saylor frames all five as cyclical, not structural: "Any one of them could become a tailwind or a catalyst."
- Sentiment metric cited directly: the Fear and Greed Index is "near a cycle low" even as Bitcoin sits near its 200-week moving average — used to argue the market is pricing fear, not fundamentals.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** Bitcoin holdings up 11% quarter-over-quarter to 846,000 BTC; Bitcoin per share up 5% in Q2 (201,170 → 210,824 sats); convert debt cut from $8.2B to $6.7B via an 8%-discount repurchase; STRC is the #1 holding across the three largest publicly traded preferred-stock indexes.
- **What's failing:** Q2 booked a non-cash $8.3B unrealized fair-value loss on Bitcoin, driving an $8.6B net loss and -$24.45 EPS — an accounting artifact of the mark-to-market model, not an operating failure, but a stark number regardless.
- **The real wound is STRC**, down from a hyper-growth trajectory to trading ~10% below par, with the USD reserve whipsawed from $2.25B to $871M and back to $3.75B inside two quarters — a self-inflicted volatility event Phong Le calls the "biggest lesson learned so far from 2026."

## Investing & scarcity
- Capital allocation flipped from "100% of new capital into Bitcoin" to an explicit three-way split: fund the USD reserve (up to $1.25B), fund $1.76B annual dividend/interest obligations, and fund up to $2B of buybacks (STRC and MSTR).
- **The actual scarce resource is trust/credibility in STRC's peg to par**, not Bitcoin or cash — Saylor: "If we can't create it such that it trades at par... we're not going to sell it at all... it's a matter of principle and credibility."
- Notably, the digital credit product line is being *consolidated*, not expanded — management explicitly rejects issuing more instrument types (STRK, STRF, STRD, converts) going forward, prioritizing single-product liquidity depth in STRC over diversification.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Stated belief:** STRC returns to par within ~70 trading days of breaking range (implying ~September 8), by analogy to its post-IPO recovery timeline.
- **Actual action:** An open-ended $1B (of which $975M remains) buyback authorization with no committed pace, no committed total spend, and explicit language that they're "calibrating day by day" — a real commitment of capital, but the size and speed remain fully discretionary. This is the clearest talk/action gap on the call: strong conviction language, vague operational mechanics.
- **Stated belief:** Bitcoin dominance (~two-thirds of non-stablecoin crypto market cap) means "there is no serious discussion of a flippening" — treated as a solved, foundational certainty.
- **Actual action:** Despite that conviction, Strategy is actively de-risking its own Bitcoin-only posture by holding more cash and selling small BTC tranches — a hedge against its own thesis that undercuts the "Bitcoin always up and to the right" framing used elsewhere on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to raise the USD reserve minimum** above one year of coverage despite holding over two years currently — Le: "One year is a good spot," keeping formal policy looser than practiced behavior.
- **Ruled out borrowing against Bitcoin from a bank counterparty**, even for a "fortress balance sheet" partner, calling bank BTC-lending terms uncompetitive and not worth the counterparty-risk optics: "It's not something we've considered doing."
- **Refused to give a dollar or time cap on the STRC rescue** — Saylor: "I can't guarantee you exactly how many dollars it takes... or how many days."
- **Declined to sell volatility (covered calls, warrants) against Bitcoin or MSTR equity**, framed less as inability and more as deliberately leaving that trade for professional derivatives desks to preserve trust with market-makers.

## The street — what analysts asked
- One thread: **could Strategy borrow against Bitcoin (via a bank) to shore up the USD reserve faster** — flatly declined, called uncompetitive and complication-prone.
- A second thread, asked two different ways: **would Strategy ever let STRC float below par, or change the pref-dividend rate to force it back** — both declined; Saylor calls the no-float rule "a matter of principle," not a negotiable lever.
- A third thread probed **the amplification/leverage target and whether Strategy would act countercyclically** (buying dollars at BTC premiums, buying Bitcoin at discounts) rather than mechanically issuing whatever the market will buy — management conceded this is new thinking they're still calibrating, an implicit admission the old playbook was too procyclical.
- Compressed worry: **can a company whose entire brand is "never sell, always buy Bitcoin" convince credit investors it will reliably prioritize dollar-stability over Bitcoin maximalism the next time the two conflict?**
