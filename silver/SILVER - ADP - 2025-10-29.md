# ADP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** ADP delivered record Q1 bookings and satisfaction scores while simultaneously cutting its core organic growth proxy — pays-per-control — to a flat 0% for the year. The tension of the call is bookings momentum (a sales/product story ADP controls) versus a frozen U.S. hiring market (a macro story it doesn't), with management working hard to frame the latter as noise rather than trend.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Pays-per-control is quietly eroding, one basis point at a time.** ADP rounded client headcount growth down to 0% for Q1 and now guides flat for the full year — the second straight quarter of guiding to "the lower end of the range." CFO Peter Hadley calls each cut immaterial ("tens of basis points"), but the direction across quarters is consistently downward, not stable.
2. **Bookings strength is real and product-driven, not macro-driven.** Record Q1 new-business bookings, HR Outsourcing reacceleration, and Lyric HCM beating expectations reflect genuine competitive wins (Workforce Now Next-Gen at 80% of new mid-market sales) rather than a strengthening hiring environment.
3. **A frozen labor market, not a deteriorating one.** "Very low levels of layoffs" alongside near-zero hiring — Hadley explicitly separates this from a recessionary read: clients aren't cutting staff, they're just not adding it. That's a distinct macro signal worth tracking beyond ADP.
4. **Client funds float is picking up slack pays-per-control drops.** A $10M raise to client funds interest revenue guidance (driven by stronger balance growth from wage growth, not yield) is directly offsetting the pays-per-control cut — a financial engineering offset that masks the underlying volume softness in the reported numbers.
5. **AI investment is real but not yet quantified.** The "Zone" sales AI tool, ADP Assist (5.5M client conversations over the trailing year), and a stated roadmap of simple → advanced → autonomous agents show a coherent AI strategy, but management deflects on hard rollout percentages ("north of 40%") and productivity metrics beyond anecdote.

## Economy & consumer
- **Stable-but-static hiring, no consumer read.** ADP doesn't touch end consumers directly; the best proxy is employer behavior — "relatively stable" demand with clients "remaining cautious around adding headcount."
- **Benefits enrollment hit a four-year high** in the PEO's July enrollment cycle, suggesting employers in higher-wage industries are still competing on total comp even as they freeze headcount growth.
- **Amazon-style layoff headlines dismissed as non-material**: Hadley notes ADP pays "a very small fraction" of Amazon's workforce and that the base of 1.1M clients / 26M U.S. workers absorbs such news without moving guidance.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: bookings, satisfaction, Lyric, Next-Gen.** Record Q1 sales volume, an all-time-high Q1 client satisfaction score, Lyric HCM bookings "exceeded our expectations," and Next-Gen driving "faster time to implementation" and lower client-contact volumes (a stated future profitability lever).
- **Not working: pays-per-control and retention drag.** ES retention "declined slightly," pays-per-control rounded to 0%, and ES margin fell 50bp on WorkForce Software integration costs — the segment carrying both the acquisition drag and the volume softness.
- **PEO margin pressure from one-timers.** A 140bp PEO margin decline driven by selling expense timing, SUI cost timing, and retroactive ERTC filing-deadline costs — management frames these as non-recurring, worth watching next quarter to confirm.
- **International "lumpy, not choppy."** A softer Q1 internationally is characterized as normal large-deal lumpiness following an "incredible" Q4, not a demand problem.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Pequity acquisition**: compensation management software, announced same-day as earnings, explicitly "not meaningful in the context of ADP for this fiscal year" — a capability tuck-in, not a growth bet.
- **WorkForce Software integration** continues (won a time-and-attendance deal in student transportation) but its cost drag on ES margin anniversaries this quarter, removing a headwind going forward.
- **The real constraint is client hiring, not ADP capacity.** Every guidance nuance this call routes back to pays-per-control; ADP has ample sales, product, and international capacity — what's scarce is client-side headcount growth to sell into.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: labor market stays flat, no further deterioration.** Action: guidance narrowed to the low end but not cut further, and pricing assumptions unchanged — a bet that 0% pays-per-control is the floor, not a step toward negative territory.
- **Belief: embedded payroll (Fiserv) is a "game changer."** Action: only just rolled out to Fiserv's back book in October — Black concedes "the bulk of the opportunity is still in front of us" and it "really doesn't contribute thus far in the numbers." The enthusiasm is well ahead of the revenue.
- **Belief: AI will be a genuine competitive moat in sales productivity.** Action: teased unreleased next-gen "Zone" capabilities as "unbelievable" and "ahead of a lot of the tools and technology vendors we even leverage," but declined to share rollout percentage or a productivity metric — talk is running ahead of disclosed action here too.
- **Belief: Next-Gen migration will lift mid-market margins.** Action: concretely extending Next-Gen distribution beyond the 50-150 employee segment this fiscal year — one of the few belief/action pairs that's tightly coupled.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No target for Next-Gen's ultimate mid-market ceiling** — Black: "I don't know that we will get to 100%," attributing the remaining 20% to structural "knockouts" (multi-location/M&A clients), not a solvable product gap.
- **No specifics on AI sales-tool rollout percentage** beyond "north of" the 40% cited at Investor Day — declined to give a hard update, saying she didn't want to make it "a quarterly update."
- **Stablecoin payroll rails: preparation, not commitment.** Black frames it entirely as a regulatory waiting game — "preparing for all possibilities" — with no capital or timeline attached.
- **Fiserv partnership risk deflected with reassurance, not detail.** Asked directly whether Fiserv's internal restructuring threatens targets, Black reaffirms commitment rhetorically without addressing whether Fiserv's own turmoil could delay the integration ADP depends on.
