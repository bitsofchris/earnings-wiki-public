# TSM — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-16)

**The key idea:** TSMC delivered a beat-and-raise quarter almost entirely on AI/HPC demand, then broke its own playbook — adding capacity to a mature node (3nm) it normally would have let run down — and pushed 2026 CapEx to the top of an already-raised range. The tension: management insists pricing and capacity decisions are driven purely by "customer partnership," not by the fact that they're sitting on the most under-supplied asset in the AI stack, with margins and returns to prove it.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **AI demand is reshaping the revenue mix in real time.** HPC jumped to 61% of revenue (+20% q/q) while smartphone fell to 26% (-11%); C.C. Wei ties this explicitly to agentic AI's shift from "query mode" to "command and action mode... leading to another step up in the amount of tokens being consumed."
2. **TSMC is breaking its own capacity discipline for the first time in the AI era.** "Historically, we do not add additional capacity to a node once it's reached its target capacity" — yet three new 3nm fabs (Tainan, Arizona, Japan) are being built specifically because AI demand for a node that entered volume production in 2022 hasn't slowed.
3. **CapEx guidance was pulled to the top of range mid-cycle, and next-3-years CapEx will dwarf the last three.** Wendell Huang: the 2021-2024 three-year total was $101B; 2026 alone is guided toward $56B, and "CapEx in the next few years... will be significantly higher than the past three years" — with no specific multi-year number offered.
4. **Margins are already at record highs, but management frames further upside as unlikely by choice, not by scarcity.** N3 margin will cross the corporate average in 2H26; long-term gross margin target was already raised to "56% and higher through the cycle" — yet CC Wei repeatedly redirects pricing questions to "customer is our partner," declining to claim more of the value TSMC is clearly capturing.
5. **Every competitive question (Tesla's Terafab, Samsung's LPU win) got the identical answer.** "It takes 2-3 years to build a new fab... no shortcuts" — a deflection that is also a real structural moat, since a rival's chip diversification takes years to bear fruit regardless of intent.

## Economy & consumer

- **Middle East conflict is a live but unquantified risk.** Wendell Huang: chemical/gas prices "likely to increase," Taiwan's LNG supply is secured "through at least May" only, and "it is too early to quantify the impact" on profitability.
- **Consumer bifurcation, not a downturn.** CC Wei: "memory price hike definitely has some impact to price sensitive end market, especially in PC and smartphone market. We did see a little bit softer market" — but "all the high-end smartphone continue to do better, and this is to TSMC's advantage."
- **No mention of broader macro (rates, employment, general consumer spending)** — TSMC's economic lens is entirely component-cost and AI-token-driven, unsurprising for a pure foundry.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Revenue and margin both beat guidance.** $35.9B revenue (+6.4% q/q, above guide); gross margin 66.2% (+390bps q/q, 120bps above the top of prior guidance) on "higher-than-expected overall capacity utilization rate and better cost improvement efforts."
- **2Q guide implies continued acceleration:** $39.0-40.2B, "a 10% sequential increase or a 32% year-over-year increase at the midpoint."
- **Inventory days rose 6 days to 80,** explicitly "reflecting the ramp-up of our 2nm technology and strong demand for our 3nm technology" — a working-capital cost of the AI ramp, not a demand-softness signal.
- **N2 already in high-volume manufacturing with good yield** across two Taiwan sites (Hsinchu, Kaohsiung); management expects N2/N2P/A16 to be "another large and long-lasting node."

## Investing & scarcity

- **CapEx raised to the high end of $52-56B**, explicitly to chase AI demand: "demand are very robust... we try very hard to speed it up and pull in all the equipment as we can. Still, our supply is very tight."
- **Wafer capacity is the binding constraint,** with a hard physical floor: "it takes 2-3 years to build a new fab... it takes another 1-2 years to ramp it up." CC Wei declined to forecast supply/demand balance before 2027.
- **Secondary constraints stacking up:** advanced packaging (CoWoS) "is very tight also," clean-room space is being built out ahead of schedule, and equipment lead times (ASML EUV, Applied Materials, Lam Research) are being managed as partnerships rather than pure procurement.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** "AI related demand continued to be extremely robust" and the "multi-year AI megatrend remain high" — full-year revenue guide raised to above 30% growth.
- **Action:** CapEx pulled to guidance's high end, three new 3nm fabs committed (Tainan 1H27, Arizona 2H27, Japan 2028), a CoPoS pilot line built for large-reticle packaging. These are real capital commitments, not just rhetoric — the CapEx-to-belief gap that shows up at many companies is largely absent here.
- **Gap that does exist:** management claims pricing decisions are demand-driven, not value-capture-driven ("we don't change our pricing dramatically"), while simultaneously guiding margin and ROE targets upward for two straight years — the stated humility on pricing doesn't fully square with the trajectory of the numbers.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No multi-year CapEx number**, despite being asked directly to repeat the 2021-style three-year guide: "We don't have a number to share with you."
- **No 3nm gross margin figure**, only that it "will reach and cross the corporate gross margin level" in 2H26.
- **No quantification of Middle East cost impact** — "too early to quantify."
- **Declined to give the precise above-30% revenue upside for 2026**, deferring to the July call.
- **Declined to comment on CPU inclusion in AI-accelerator revenue definitions**, citing inability to attribute CPU end-use ("we are not able to identify which CPU goes to where") — a real measurement limitation, not just evasion.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Profitability ceiling:** repeated, insistent questioning (Goldman, Arete) on whether TSMC's raised targets (56%+ gross margin, high-20s ROE) still undersell its structural pricing power given AI-supply-chain "supernormal returns" elsewhere — met with the "customer as partner" answer both times, and Wendell's only concession was "long-term planning is ongoing... we will update you when there is a change."
- **Competitive leakage:** Tesla's Terafab initiative and a customer's LPU business reportedly at Samsung Foundry both drew direct "will you win this back" questions; CC Wei's answer leaned on build-time physics ("2-3 years... no shortcuts") rather than any specific counter-offer.
- **Capacity timeline:** multiple analysts (JPMorgan, UBS) pressed on when wafer supply catches demand — CC Wei's clearest number given: 2027 at the earliest, with a formal update expected "when we enter 2027."
- **Compressed worry:** is the AI boom's biggest winner actually supply-constrained enough to justify raising CapEx to unprecedented levels, or is TSMC deliberately leaving pricing power on the table for the sake of customer relationships — and which of those two stories will still be true once its competitors' fabs come online in 2027-2028?
