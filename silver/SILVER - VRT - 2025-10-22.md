# VRT — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-22)

**The key idea:** Vertiv delivered a quarter that beat every guided metric by a wide margin — yet the stock sold off, prompting an unusually candid on-air complaint from the Executive Chairman. The real tension on the call isn't the current numbers, which are excellent, but the gap between eye-popping industry-level AI infrastructure announcements (Oracle's backlog, OpenAI's NVIDIA gigawatt deal) and the company's own maniacally strict definition of "backlog" as only legally-binding POs — meaning the street is pricing in a wave of demand that hasn't actually landed as orders yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Results were unambiguously strong but the market didn't reward them.** Organic sales +28%, adjusted OP +43%, EPS +63% YoY, with Cote noting drily: "I wonder what would have happened if we hadn't blown the doors off of every single metric."
2. **Orders are outrunning even this hot market.** Q3 orders +60% YoY, +20% sequentially, trailing-twelve-month organic orders +21%, book-to-bill 1.4x — management frames this as share gain, not just market beta, "through superior technology and execution."
3. **Backlog composition, not size, is the real signal.** CEO Albertazzi stressed the $9.5B backlog's phasing "mimics the way and the speed at which deployments occur" and hasn't stretched out — implying the giant hyperscaler mega-deals in the news are still mostly ahead of Vertiv, not already booked.
4. **EMEA is the one visible weak spot**, down 4% organically on power availability and regulatory drag, prompting a restructuring program — but management is betting on a 2026 H2 recovery driven by data-sovereignty-driven local AI inference buildout.
5. **Leadership transition mid-momentum**: CFO David Fallon is retiring after roughly 13 quarters on these calls, replaced by Craig Chamberlain, right as the company reaffirms its 2029 margin targets and steps up investment intensity.

## Economy & consumer
- No traditional consumer segment; the "customer" is hyperscaler/enterprise AI infrastructure buyers, whose capex intentions are described as "remarkable strength... driven by accelerating AI adoption globally."
- The one caveat is region-specific, not demand-specific: EMEA's weakness is attributed to structural power/regulatory constraints, not softer AI appetite — "vacancy rates are extremely, extremely low."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working**: Americas +43%, APAC +21%, margin expansion of 400bps in Americas despite tariffs, and services described as an accelerating "flywheel" now that equipment growth is so fast it's outpacing service revenue (a lag they expect to close).
- **What's not**: EMEA margins hit "a low point" in Q3 from deleverage on lower sales plus continued fixed-cost investment; Q4 guidance was cut ~100bps versus prior guide, half from fresh tariffs (Section 232) and half from deliberately pulled-forward fixed-cost investment.
- Tariffs remain an active drag through Q4 2025, with management targeting "materially offset" impact by the time they exit Q1 2026.

## Investing & scarcity
- Capacity expansion is deliberately ahead of demand: "our capacity expansion strategy keeps us six months, twelve months ahead of demand curves" — mostly via expanding existing sites rather than greenfield, because it's faster to bring online and reuses trained teams.
- R&D spend growing 20%+ in 2026 specifically to stay "multiple GPU generations ahead," funding an 800V DC platform aligned to NVIDIA's 2027 Rubin Ultra roadmap — a direct, named coupling of Vertiv's product roadmap to a specific chip vendor's cadence (relevant to anyone tracking AI-infra capex cycles).
- Field service headcount (~4,400 engineers) is being scaled via its own SIOP process alongside manufacturing capacity — services treated as a "distributed factory," not a cost center.
- M&A pipeline stays active for both bolt-ons (WeiLay for predictive analytics, Great Lakes for IT systems/white space) and larger strategic deals, funded by a balance sheet at 0.5x net leverage (guided to 0.2x by year-end).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: 2026 brings continued strong growth, EMEA reacceleration in H2, and tariffs flipping from headwind to tailwind post-Q1.
- **Action matching the belief**: EMEA restructuring already underway (~$30M cost / ~$20M annualized benefit), accelerated capacity investment specifically pulled forward into Q4 2025 rather than waiting for confirmation, and 20%+ R&D growth committed for 2026 regardless of near-term tariff noise.
- **Gap worth flagging**: management is confident the mega-deal headlines (Oracle, OpenAI/NVIDIA) will eventually convert to Vertiv orders, but by their own account almost none of that has hit backlog yet — the conviction is running ahead of the booked evidence, even as capacity is being built to meet it.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Refused to give any 2026 CapEx figure ("we will not be explicit when it comes to CapEx in 2026").
- Declined to size 2026 incremental margins, calling it "premature," despite reaffirming the 30-35% long-term incremental range and 25% margin target for 2029.
- Would not detail the restructuring program's specifics "for obvious reasons."
- Would not directly quantify how much of the hyperscaler mega-deal wave has yet reached Vertiv's own bookings, other than describing the relationship qualitatively ("two sides of the same very positive coin... but they beat to a slightly different drum").

## The street — what analysts asked
- Two dominant anxiety clusters: (1) whether headline hyperscaler AI capex announcements are actually flowing into Vertiv's orders yet, and on what lag; and (2) whether emerging cooling/power technologies from rivals and customers (AWS rear-door heat exchangers, Microsoft microfluidics, 800V DC removing PSUs) erode Vertiv's per-megawatt content or competitive moat.
- Secondary cluster on margins: repeated, granular pressure on Q4's 100bp guidance cut, the split between tariff and fixed-cost-investment causes, and whether 30-35% incrementals hold in 2026 — management gave direction but consistently declined hard numbers.
- One analyst also asked point-blank whether customer price negotiations have gotten harder given the innovation cadence — Albertazzi's answer notably reframed pricing power as contingent on continuous innovation delivery, not a static advantage.
- Compressed worry: **is Vertiv's confidence about capturing the AI infrastructure wave outrunning the actual evidence of bookings, and could a technology shift (DC power, microfluidics) erode its content-per-megawatt before that wave even arrives?**
