# ADI — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-05-20)

**The key idea:** ADI printed record revenue, margin, and EPS on broad-based demand, but the real story is a bet that the AI buildout's next bottleneck is chip-level power delivery — and ADI is buying its way to the front of that line with Empower Semiconductor rather than building it in-house, even though its own executives concede Linear/Maxim/Volterra should have covered this ground. Management is explicit that the current environment is calm, not tight — until 2027, when they expect the industry-wide demand ramp to test that resilience.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **This was a volume beat, not a price beat.** CFO Puccio was unusually direct: "Everything was above the midpoint of our guide was actually due to volume, not incremental price. The pricing played out as we expected." Full-year 2026 pricing actions add "a couple points" to growth — modest, not the driver.
2. **Data center is now the fastest-growing, highest-quality revenue stream.** Data center is >75% of the communications segment and grew "up more than 90% year-over-year," split roughly evenly across optical and power portfolios, and comms overall runs "an above corporate average" gross margin — a favorable mix shift that's doing real work on the P&L.
3. **Auto's strength arrived a quarter earlier than ADI itself predicted.** Management had guided a second straight below-seasonal auto quarter on tariff-driven pull-in digestion; instead auto hit a record on "a material pickup in China," with BMS returning to y/y growth for the first time in two years. That's a guidance miss ADI is happy to own.
4. **Power density at the XPU/GPU is named as AI's actual constraint.** Roche: "The biggest bottleneck that AI is creating for us today is we've got to solve for power density and delivery efficiency... down at the XPU, the GPU, the CPU." The Empower acquisition — IVR plus silicon capacitors, cutting data-center compute power draw an estimated 10-15% — is a direct, dollars-behind response to that bottleneck.
5. **Gross margin is being described as near a near-term ceiling.** CFO agreed with an analyst's framing that Q3's guided margin is "probably... the right way to think about" a local peak given maxed-out utilization; further upside would require either mix shift or more (presumably lower-margin) outsourcing.

## Economy & consumer
- **Calm despite the headlines.** Roche: demand is record-level "despite the quarter's heightened geopolitical tensions and ongoing macroeconomic challenges," and customer conversations are marked by "general calmness," with memory supply the main stress point, concentrated on consumer-facing customers.
- **Consumer stays resilient but is cooling into guide.** Consumer revenue was flat sequentially, up 23% y/y, but management explicitly guides it down single digits next quarter, citing "consumer sentiment and some of the inflationary pressures."
- **2027 is where the calm gets tested.** Roche flagged "concern that at the steepness of the demand ramp across the industry" — implying today's ease may not hold as the whole industry ramps into next year.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broad-based record quarter.** $3.62B revenue (+15% seq, +37% y/y), gross margin 73% (+180bps seq, +360bps y/y), operating margin 49% (above guide), EPS $3.09 (+67% y/y) — all fresh highs.
- **Industrial is the profit engine.** 50% of revenue, up 20% seq/56% y/y, led by aerospace & defense (new revenue high, "accelerating an already strong multiyear growth path" on national-sovereignty spending), ATE, ETM, and broad market — segments Roche says have "grown more than 40% in the first half" and remain "well below their prior cycle highs."
- **Auto beat on China, not restocking.** Auto was +8% seq/+2% y/y with record performance in Europe and Japan; management was explicit it is "not seeing" a broad inventory rebuild at Tier 1s/OEMs yet — the upside was demand, not channel-fill.
- **Design-in stickiness is the moat, by their own account.** Roche: once ADI wins a design slot, "substitution is effectively zero" given 15-20 year industrial product lifecycles — a structural claim worth weighing against any bear case on competitive share loss.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Empower Semiconductor acquisition** targets IVR and silicon-capacitor IP for "true vertical power delivery," shrinking customer power footprint "by up to four times" and cutting data-center compute power consumption an estimated 10-15% — framed as filling a gap Roche admits existing Maxim/LTC/Volterra IP didn't cover, bought rather than built because "time is of the essence."
- **Capacity build continues on both fronts.** Internal capacity has "more than doubled" since pre-COVID; external sourcing has added geographic optionality. Inventory (die bank and finished goods) rose $81M sequentially to build buffers against the demand surge.
- **CapEx discipline holds even amid the ramp.** Management reaffirmed CapEx staying within the 4-6%-of-revenue long-term model — capacity investment is not (yet) requiring the model to be reset upward.
- **The constraint is chip-level power, not raw wafer supply.** The clearest "scarcity" answer on the call wasn't fab capacity but power delivery density at the XPU/GPU — a distinctly AI-infrastructure-specific bottleneck, directly relevant to anyone tracking datacenter power as the next AI chokepoint after compute and memory.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** data center and ATE growth confidence "into 2027 is increasing," and the $20B 2030 revenue framework holds. **Action:** capacity doubling already executed, Empower acquisition committed now specifically to be ready for the 2027 demand surge — a rare case where stated belief and capital deployment line up cleanly rather than diverging.
- **Belief:** auto will grow through 2026 with above-seasonal Q3. **Action:** backed by "record bookings, positive book-to-bill" — concrete order-book evidence, not just tone.
- **Gap worth flagging:** management believes power-density bottlenecks matter enormously for AI infrastructure, yet is candid that Empower brings only "a fairly small amount of revenue" today and material contribution isn't expected until 2027 — the conviction is well ahead of the near-term financial commitment showing up in numbers.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No numeric split on internal vs. external capacity.** Asked directly to quantify how much revenue each channel could support, Puccio declined specifics beyond reaffirming the $20B capacity ceiling and "strong relationships" externally.
- **Won't promise gross margin expansion beyond Q3.** Agreed the guided level is likely a near-term ceiling absent a "significant mix shift," explicitly capping expectations rather than talking up further upside.
- **No detail on Empower design-win pipeline size or specific customers**, only that there are "a lot of design-ins in train."

## The street — what analysts asked
- Questions clustered around three anxieties: **can supply keep pace with the demand ramp** (capacity, external sourcing, competitor price letters), **is the margin story sustainable** (gross margin ceiling logic, utilization vs. mix vs. pricing), and **why Empower, why now** (rationale, revenue timeline, integration path) given ADI already owns a large power-analog portfolio.
- Auto inventory dynamics got specific scrutiny — whether Tier 1s are quietly restocking — and management's answer (not yet, still lean) pushed back on the restocking narrative directly.
- Compressed worry: **can ADI keep converting record bookings into shipped, margin-accretive revenue without hitting a supply or power-delivery wall before 2027 arrives.**
