# MCD — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-07)

**The key idea:** McDonald's turned in a clean top-line quarter (3.8% global comp growth, share gains in nearly all top-10 markets) built on its "three for three" playbook of value, marketing, menu — but the P&L reveals a widening split between franchisee-run restaurants, which are performing well, and company-operated U.S. stores, whose margins management flatly called "not acceptable." Value discipline is working almost everywhere except France, and beef inflation plus a looming remodel cycle are quietly raising the cost of staying in that position.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Company-operated margins are the actual story, not comps.** Despite solid U.S. top-line growth, Kempczinski said U.S. McOpCo underperformance came from "investing in labor, additional labor at the same time that they were probably being even more restrained around pricing" — a self-inflicted margin problem, not a demand problem — and the company is "revisiting the optimal franchisee versus company ownership balance."
2. **Value strategy is now a single global template, minus one holdout.** The under-$3 everyday-affordable menu plus meal-deal bundles is now standard everywhere except France, which Ian Borden said lacked "as strong a program as we needed to... on both dimensions," and just launched a fix.
3. **April was a real air pocket, not a trend break.** Comps in the U.S. and IOM went "slightly negative" in April purely from lapping last year's Minecraft promotion, and management is explicit that Q2 will show a "meaningful deceleration" from Q1's 3.9% — guidance investors should not read as demand cracking.
4. **Beef inflation is reshaping the menu mix in real time.** With beef prices at "historic highs," Kempczinski conceded chicken "becomes a much more attractive value opportunity relative to beef" and the company is leaning into a category it says is growing twice as fast as beef globally with much lower share (high teens vs. mid-40s in beef).
5. **A capital-intensive remodel cycle is coming into view for Investor Day.** Roughly a decade after the last Experience of the Future remodel wave, management flagged a new one is starting in the U.S. and IOM, alongside re-underwriting new-unit construction costs — both framed as September 23 Investor Day topics rather than answered now.

## Economy & consumer
- **Sentiment is drifting down, not stabilizing:** "it's certainly not improving, and it may be getting a little bit worse," per Kempczinski — a direct, unhedged admission the macro backdrop is deteriorating.
- **Bifurcation persists but has narrowed:** high-income spend is "very resilient" and McDonald's is gaining share there, while low-income spend is "absolutely still declining," albeit less severely than the high-single-digit drops seen 6-12 months prior.
- **Gas prices are the specific transmission mechanism** hurting lower-income consumers right now — both executives flagged elevated gas prices as disproportionately squeezing that cohort, with pressure expected to continue.
- No mention of employment/wage trends specifically — the read is entirely gas-price and sentiment-driven, not labor-market driven.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** value scores have swung sharply positive — Kempczinski said that 18 months ago the leadership gap on value perception was narrowing, but "over the last six months or so, we've seen a significant improvement... to the point where I think we're in great shape on value and affordability."
- **What's working:** the U.K. turnaround, attributed to leadership change and rebalancing "overemphasizing traffic at the expense of franchisee profitability" toward a model where franchisees "have a clearer line of sight to how we're also gonna be growing franchisee profitability."
- **What's not working:** U.S. McOpCo margins, explicitly called "not acceptable," driven by added labor without corresponding pricing discipline.
- **What's not working:** France, singled out by name as the one major market where the two-part value formula (meal deals + entry price points) wasn't in place, forcing a fresh relaunch mid-quarter.
- **Franchisee cash flow is strained system-wide:** "there's certainly concern around franchisee profitability, not just in the U.S., but in IOM as well," with beef inflation "particularly pronounced in Europe."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Beverages are the active investment lever:** McCafé refreshers/crafted sodas launched nationwide in the U.S., with Red Bull energy drinks delayed slightly for operational readiness with the partner rather than pulled — "there were some things that we needed to do in partnership with Red Bull... that didn't line up perfectly with this May launch."
- **A remodel cycle is starting** roughly ten years after the last major refresh; management is evaluating restaurant-of-the-future formats given the growth of digital and delivery, but deferred details on capital-sharing to Investor Day.
- **Beef cost is the binding constraint on franchisee economics**, explicitly linked to both margin pressure and a category shift toward chicken.
- **Capital is not the constraint on unit growth** — "we have a tremendous amount of financial firepower... we've got plenty of capital to spend if we need it" — the binding constraint is return thresholds on new sites given rising construction costs.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** share gains will continue through May/June and beyond, driven by the "locked in" McValue platform, beverage lineup, and FIFA World Cup marketing tied to North American hosting.
- **Action backing that belief:** unanimous franchisee votes to relaunch McValue, simultaneous beverage launches in three markets (U.S., Germany, Canada), and an active FIFA marketing calendar across U.S./Canada/Mexico teams.
- **Gap:** management admits it can't say whether share gains will outpace a possibly slowing industry — "I don't know if there's enough data at this point to really give you a definitive answer" — meaning the confident "momentum continues" framing rests on relative, not absolute, performance.
- **Belief:** long-term cost inflation risk is rising into late 2026/2027 from Middle East-linked supply chain disruption, yet the only committed action disclosed is existing hedging and supplier relationships — no new capital commitment or reformulated sourcing strategy was described.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No commitment on McOpCo ownership changes:** "If we're gonna make any changes on that would be a topic we would talk about at Investor Day" — a direct punt on the most consequential structural question raised on the call.
- **No specifics on remodel capital support:** Kempczinski said partnering on remodels is atypical but "if there's specific sales driving things... we would take a look," with any real detail deferred to September.
- **No quantification of industry growth vs. company share gains** for Q2, despite confidently predicting continued share gains.
- **No detail on the U.S. marketing calendar** for competitive reasons, despite a direct question about balancing value messaging against premium/beverage messaging.
