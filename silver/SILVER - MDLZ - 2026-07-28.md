# MDLZ — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** Mondelēz is running the same playbook everywhere at once — chase distribution into channels it historically ignored (value, convenience, away-from-home), lean hard into a handful of "big bet" innovations (Biscoff above all), and use productivity/AI-driven overhead cuts to fund it — while betting that cocoa's 2024-style crisis dynamics won't repeat even as prices run up again. The subtext of nearly every answer is: don't worry about this quarter's noise, 2027 earnings are "insulated" and will be strong.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **Distribution is still the growth engine, even in "mature" North America.** Management frames value channels, convenience, and away-from-home as historically deprioritized — not saturated — and is now building direct broker relationships and co-development pipelines (McDonald's-style QSR partnerships, cafeteria programs) to capture them: "I would focus a little bit more on what it could represent for us than why we are not yet as developed as we should be."
- **The Biscoff collaboration is being scaled into a real second brand platform.** Three layers now (co-branded chocolate tablets, licensed Biscoff biscuits in emerging markets, ice cream), with a fourth being explored (Biscoff-filled Oreo, croissants). CEO explicitly sizes it at **$500 million to $1 billion** over the coming years — a rare hard number on this call, and notable for a company otherwise allergic to specifics.
- **Emerging-market expansion is treated as structural, not cyclical**: another 100,000 stores added in India, Brazil crossing one million stores, continued build-out in China and Southeast Asia — framed as multi-year runway rather than a one-time catch-up.
- **AI shows up as a cost lever, not a product story** — mentioned three separate times (supply chain, overhead, and "creative media" efficiency in advertising spend) but never elaborated beyond generic "efficiency" language. Notable for someone watching how CPGs talk about AI: it's purely internal productivity, zero customer-facing ambition articulated.
- **A full Oreo relaunch is being teased for 2027** as a specific volume-mix driver in North America, though no detail was given on what "full relaunch" means.
- **Portfolio is being deliberately shifted away from cocoa intensity** — pushing "portfolio solutions that are less cocoa-intense" as a structural hedge against commodity volatility, alongside pricing and productivity levers.

## What's changing

- **A new CFO (Amit Banati) is on his first call**, weeks into the role, and his commentary is almost entirely affirming — "my initial observations reinforce my thesis on Mondelēz" — with no daylight visible yet between him and the outgoing narrative.
- **Cocoa's fundamentals have flipped from the 2024 crisis** despite recent price spikes: management points to a projected **half-million-metric-ton surplus (~10% of global demand)**, industry coverage now at 10 months versus 7 months in 2024, and net-short speculative positioning — arguing the current rally is technical (short squeeze, weak mid-crop pod counts, El Niño fear) rather than a repeat supply shock.
- **Europe is inflecting from pricing-driven to volume-driven growth** as prior-year price increases lap out, though Q2 volume mix came in "a little bit below" expectations because the company deliberately held back trade stock during an unprecedented heat wave hitting chocolate consumption.
- **North America's turnaround is credited to tighter promotional discipline plus real innovation traction** (RITZ Drizzled, Sour Patch Kids Chews, Oreo, Give & Go) rather than price increases — gross profit dollars grew ~3% on holding chocolate pricing amid what they call a "rational" competitive environment.
- **A&C (advertising & consumer) spend is being reallocated, not just increased** — double-digit growth in North America, with explicit new internal guidelines on media mix (communication/digital/point-of-sale split) and a push toward cohort-level targeting.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **China is openly called "softer"** with only a vague expectation it will "gradually improve" — no specifics offered, unlike the granular color given on India, Brazil, and Mexico.
- **Middle East conflict costs are a recurring, unquantified drag** — mentioned three times as hitting both revenue and (more so) the bottom line, "digested" into guidance, but never given a dollar figure across the whole call.
- **Full-year EPS guidance was held flat even as the top-line guide was raised** — the CFO is explicit that any top-line upside gets reinvested rather than flowing through, which is a deliberate choice to prioritize growth investment over margin flow-through, but also means the "beat" is somewhat manufactured to not show up in EPS.
- **Q3 EPS is flagged as "pressured" due to "phasing" — cocoa cost timing and lapping prior-year interest/tax items** — management repeatedly insists this is "purely mechanical" and "nothing structural," the kind of preemptive reassurance that invites skepticism about how clean the Q3 print will actually look.
- **Management moved away from guiding gross margin percentage entirely**, now guiding only gross profit dollars — a redefinition of the metric investors were used to tracking, executed without much acknowledgment that it's a change in disclosure practice.
- **Volume-mix guidance for the back half was conspicuously vague** — repeatedly declined to give a number ("I stay disciplined in not guiding too many variables"), even after two separate analysts pushed for it.
