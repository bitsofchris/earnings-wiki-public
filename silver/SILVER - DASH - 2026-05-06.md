# DASH — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-06)

**The key idea:** DoorDash's entire pitch this quarter is a bet that owning a proprietary, physical-world data catalog — every out-of-stock item, every shelf position, every restaurant's in-store-only offerings — is the moat that survives the agentic-AI shift, even as top-of-funnel traffic potentially routes through third-party AI assistants. Underneath that story sits a real financing tension: management is running three tech stacks simultaneously to build a unified platform, funding a ~$50M/quarter gas-rewards program by pushing other H1 investments into H2, all while insisting full-year EBITDA guidance hasn't moved.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Agentic AI is treated as a top-of-funnel risk, not an existential one — backed by a specific historical precedent.** Xu leans on Google Food Ordering (an 8-year experiment that drove traffic but never retention) and early-2010s Amazon product search to argue "customers ultimately don't care about any top-of-funnel... What they care about is did they get the order that they wanted." It's a confident argument, but it's also the only defense offered — no discussion of why agentic shopping might behave differently than a search results page.
2. **Grocery is framed as structurally underbuilt, not underdemanded.** Despite record volume share, Xu says flatly "we are miles to go" and that the fundamental blocker is that "the online delivery experience is just not yet good enough compared to the offline experience" — inventory systems and supply chains simply don't expose accurate real-time stock, so DashMart Fulfillment Services exists to solve that from the inside rather than layer another app on top.
3. **The re-platforming is running triple-cost during the transition.** Ravi Inukonda confirms DoorDash is "going to run all three tech stacks in parallel" through most of 2026, bleeding into early 2027 — a direct, quantifiable cost overhang management frames as temporary and on-plan, but one that keeps the cost base elevated for at least another year.
4. **Gas rewards program extension was funded by borrowing from H1, not new money.** The ~$50M Q1 program was extended into Q2 at the same cost, funded by pushing "some investments" out of H1 into H2 — a real trade-off against product/feature spend that management didn't specify further.
5. **AI is already rewriting internal headcount logic, ahead of DoorDash knowing what to do with it.** Xu states roughly "two-thirds of our code is written by AI today" and admits the company hasn't yet figured out how team structures should change in response — a candid admission that productivity gains are outpacing organizational redesign.

## Economy & consumer
- **Weather was the only macro drag cited.** Winter storms cost roughly 1 point of YoY GOV growth in Q1; beyond that, no macro or consumer-spending softness was mentioned.
- **Member growth accelerated, not decelerated.** DashPass/subscription hit "a record quarter... across the board" with accelerating sign-ups and improving retention — used explicitly to rebut the premise that Q1's order growth deceleration signals weakening demand.
- **DoorDash claims to be driving the industry's MAU growth**, per Inukonda: "if you actually look at the overall MAU growth in the industry, majority of that is being driven by DoorDash."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Restaurant delivery, the oldest segment, is still accelerating** — Xu says it's "growing at above historical highs," attributed to continuous selection/accuracy/speed improvements rather than any single initiative.
- **Grocery hit record share but is explicitly not good enough yet** — "the product isn't good enough yet" is Xu's own assessment despite becoming volume leader last winter; the core unsolved problem is real-time inventory visibility at partner stores.
- **New verticals (non-restaurant) are on track for gross-profit-positive in H2**, with basket sizes and order frequency improving in mature cohorts — Inukonda calls this "continued execution," not a structural fix.
- **Advertising is "never gone better"** — record highs, driven by cracking CPG advertiser demand alongside SMB, with management citing a deliberately lower ad load than competitors as a differentiator.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is going to the unified tech platform (several hundred million dollars) and DoorDash Dot autonomy**, both described as on track and on-cost versus plans set two quarters ago.
- **DashMart Fulfillment Services remains capped at a handful of partners on purpose** — Xu frames this as protecting quality before scaling into a model that disrupts decades-old grocer supply-chain habits.
- **The real scarcity is proprietary physical-world data, not capital or compute** — DoorDash's stated advantage is a catalog "much of which is actually about the physical world that does not exist in any digital repository that cannot be scraped," positioned as the durable moat against AI-agent intermediation.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: grocery should eventually rival or exceed restaurant delivery in size.** Action: incremental fulfillment partnerships (a handful of retailers) rather than a broad rollout — the gap between the ambition and the current commitment is wide and explicitly acknowledged ("there's a long ways to go").
- **Belief: AI agents won't disintermediate DoorDash.** Action: building agentic ordering tools directly into DoorDash's own product, betting the proprietary catalog keeps customers on-platform — but no external commitment (e.g., data-sharing terms, API strategy) toward or against third-party AI platforms was offered.
- **Belief: productivity gains from AI will eventually reshape org structure.** Action: none committed yet — Xu says explicitly "we'll figure it out," with only the tech-stack unification prioritized concretely today.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Gas rewards beyond Q2**: "we've not made any decision... we'll monitor the situation very closely" — a deliberate non-answer preserving optionality on a ~$50M/quarter spend.
- **AV/Dot market sizing**: Inukonda and Xu declined to quantify what percentage of U.S. delivery is addressable by autonomous vehicles, describing the program as still building toward "battle-tested at scale" rather than offering any share estimate.
- **Third-party agentic-platform ad economics**: on whether DoorDash could earn ad revenue on other companies' AI shopping agents, Xu deflected — "I think you'll have to ask them."

## The street — what analysts asked
Analyst questions clustered tightly around three anxieties: whether agentic AI assistants become a disintermediating layer between consumers and delivery platforms (asked from multiple angles — product strategy, competitive risk, ad-revenue implications); the true cost and timeline of running three tech stacks simultaneously plus the gas-rewards spend, and whether EBITDA guidance is actually intact; and whether Q1's order growth deceleration was weather or a genuine demand signal. Notably absent: no one pushed on Dot/AV unit economics or cost-to-serve, despite Ravi's own comments flagging it as an open question. The compressed worry: is DoorDash's proprietary-catalog moat actually strong enough to survive an agentic-commerce shift it can't fully control, and is management under-disclosing the true cost of running everything at once to get there.
