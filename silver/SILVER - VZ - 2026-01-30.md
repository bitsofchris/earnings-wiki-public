# VZ — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-30)

**The key idea:** A brand-new CEO (100 days in) is repositioning Verizon's chronic churn and share loss as self-inflicted — the product of "empty price increases" — and betting that reversing that behavior, closing Frontier, and cutting $5B of OpEx can deliver record subscriber volumes on a *lower* CapEx budget. The tension: 2026 guidance embeds a flat-to-down wireless revenue year as the price for fixing churn, asking investors to trust a turnaround story before the new value proposition (still undisclosed) even launches.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Record volumes were bought without raising prices, but not without cost.** Q4 delivered 616,000 postpaid phone net adds (best in ~5-6 years) and 1M+ combined mobility/broadband adds via promotions, not price cuts on the base — but churn "remained elevated," which management concedes is a legacy problem from prior years' price hikes.
2. **The CapEx cut is a portfolio-pruning story, not an efficiency miracle.** 2026 CapEx guided to $16-16.5B (down ~$4B combined) while raising the fiber passings target to 40-50M — reconciled by exiting businesses "losing $1 billion to $1.5 billion a year in margin" (business wireline, legacy copper/voice, wholesale) rather than doing more with less on the core network.
3. **Churn reduction, not gross adds, is the entire 2026 subscriber thesis.** CFO math: each 1bp of churn improvement equals ~90,000 net adds; 5bps of improvement gets them halfway to the full-year target of 750K-1M postpaid phone net adds — a low bar management calls "only about 10% to 15% of the net new to the industry."
4. **Frontier's economics improved faster than expected.** Synergy target doubled to $1B+ run-rate by 2028 (from an original ~$500M-equivalent estimate), and despite guiding Frontier to be free-cash-flow dilutive in 2026, overall FCF is still guided up ~7%+ to $21.5B — the highest since 2020.

## Economy & consumer
- **No broad macro read given** — the only external shock cited is the federal government shutdown, which depressed public sector wireless volumes; management expects "further improvements in public sector wireless volumes across the first half of 2026" as disconnects lap.
- **Consumer demand responded strongly to promotions**: consumer postpaid phone net adds of 551,000 were the highest in 5 years, driven by "new to Verizon" sales (the company's highest-LTV cohort) during the holiday season.
- **Churn is consumer-driven and self-inflicted per management**: CEO names four churn drivers — price increases without value, process friction (onboarding/billing/service), price perception, and competitive intensity — and says the company will "stop doing that" on pricing rather than expecting the environment to change.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: broadband/convergence.** Fixed wireless net adds of 319,000 and Fios' best Q4 since 2020 (67,000 net adds); bundled mobility+broadband customers show a **40% reduction in churn versus stand-alone mobility** — the clearest quantified proof point on the call.
- **Working: prepaid.** Sixth consecutive quarter of positive prepaid net adds (109,000 in Q4), described as consistent share-taking.
- **Not working: wireless service revenue growth.** Guided to roughly flat in 2026 (vs. 2% full-year 2025 growth) as the company laps last year's price increases (~180bps of headwind) and absorbs continuing promotional amortization.
- **Not working (structurally): a chunk of the legacy portfolio.** Management explicitly flags "$1 billion to $1.5 billion a year in margin" being lost across areas slated for sunset, retirement, or divestiture — an admission of long-tolerated underperformance.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx guided down to $16-16.5B for 2026** even with Frontier folded in, justified by C-Band build being "substantially complete," legacy-area rationalization (copper, voice platforms, business wireline, wholesale), and CFO's claim of finding "unit cost efficiency" per fiber prem passed.
- **Fiber target raised to 40-50M passings** (from 35-40M), funded via at least 2M organic builds/year plus the Tillman Infrastructure partnership and the Starry acquisition (helps with MDU passings) — inorganic fiber M&A remains "something we're always looking at."
- **The real scarce resource is trust/retention, not capital or spectrum** — CEO repeatedly frames churn reduction, not network investment or new spectrum, as the highest-leverage lever available, and says the company has "plenty of capacity and flexibility on our balance sheet" for further M&A if wanted.
- **Buyback authorization ($25B/3 years) and accelerated dividend increase** signal capital return is not supply-constrained; the constraint is entirely on the demand/retention side of the business.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management is confident 2027 marks the inflection where the 180bps pricing-lapping headwind disappears and volume-based revenue "comes in," compounding with continued OpEx efficiency (more savings beyond the $5B already identified) and buybacks to produce sustained EPS/FCF acceleration.
- **Action backing it up:** concrete 2026 commitments include $5B OpEx savings (headcount cuts already executed — 13,000 reduced in Q4), a narrowed CapEx envelope, doubled Frontier synergy target, and capital return increases — real dollar commitments, not just aspiration.
- **The gap:** the entire subscriber growth and revenue re-acceleration thesis depends on a "new value proposition" launching in H1 2026 that management will not describe, and on a churn improvement that has not yet shown up in the numbers (churn was still "elevated" in the reported quarter) — the guidance is described as "appropriately conservative," but it's built on a lever (churn) that has moved the wrong direction for three straight years before this quarter.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No details on the new value proposition** ahead of its H1 2026 launch, despite claiming to already have "conjoint analysis" data showing positive customer feedback — "we are not going to show our hand until the day we launch."
- **No terms disclosed on the renewed Comcast/Charter MVNO agreement**, beyond asserting it's "accretive" and "on very solid footing financially, operationally and strategically."
- **CEO hedges on the CapEx number itself**: "Honestly, I don't know if we need all $16 billion to $16.5 billion for our CapEx projects, but I think it's a responsible number to have there" — guidance framed as a ceiling with built-in slack, not a bottoms-up requirement.
- **No adjusted EBITDA guidance given** for 2026, only a qualitative "faster than EPS growth" — leaving the single biggest profitability metric without a hard number.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Subscriber quality and LTV sustainability**: whether record volumes come with weaker customer lifetime value given the more promotional posture — management insists new-to-Verizon adds (highest LTV) drove the quarter and that churn reduction, not promo intensity, will sustain it.
- **CapEx efficiency sourcing**: multiple questions pressed on where the ~$2-4B of CapEx reduction actually comes from and whether it risks network quality or growth — answered with a list of completed/near-complete programs (C-Band, 5G SA) and legacy-area cuts rather than a genuine productivity breakthrough.
- **Service revenue bridge mechanics**: detailed requests for the puts and takes behind flat 2026 wireless revenue (price lapping, promo amortization, perks/step-ups, broadband/FWA growth, prepaid) — a sign the flat guide itself is viewed skeptically enough to need decomposition.
- **Multi-year cost runway**: probing whether $5B of 2026 savings is a one-time reset or the start of a longer 2027-2028 efficiency program — management confirmed "billions more" ahead but offered no specific figures.
- **Compressed worry**: can a brand-new CEO's promise to stop "empty price increases" actually reverse three years of rising churn fast enough to hit 750K-1M net adds, without the still-secret new value proposition disappointing on launch?
