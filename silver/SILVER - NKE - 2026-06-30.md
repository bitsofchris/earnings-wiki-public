# NKE — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-06-30)

**The key idea:** Nike's turnaround is real but narrow — Running, Basketball, and Football are compounding under the new "Sport Offense" org structure while Sportswear and Jordan streetwear, roughly half of revenue, keep declining and just got a worse near-term outlook. The quarter's headline profitability is largely an accounting mirage: without a one-time $986 million tariff-recovery benefit, EPS would have been $0.20, not $0.72, and the CFO who architected the recovery is leaving mid-transition.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **The Sport Offense reorganization** moved roughly 8,000 employees into vertical sport-specific teams (running, basketball, football, training, ACG) replacing a more generalist structure — management credits it directly for five straight quarters of double-digit Running growth (~$1B added) and a football-led World Cup campaign that hit 1.5 billion story views in week one.
- **Sportswear and Jordan get a dedicated rescue plan but not until 2027**: more than a dozen new footwear styles land in the second half of fiscal 2027, explicitly framed as new product rather than "going back to the vault and doing old retro shoes" — an implicit admission that recent Jordan strategy leaned too hard on retros.
- **China gets a "local for local" bet**: a newly staffed Greater China product-creation team will ship products designed, developed, and manufactured in China — but not until holiday 2027, an 18-month lag that leaves the current China reset dependent entirely on marketplace cleanup and retail elevation in the interim.
- **Marketplace premiumization is the throughline across every geography**: aggressive promotion cuts (EMEA off-price down over 50%, China digital discounts falling) are deliberately trading revenue for full-price mix — CFO Matt Friend: "That's going to create a comp issue in Q2 of next year," said in advance, about their own strategy.
- **Supply chain consolidation** (fewer facilities, smaller workforce, changed factory-to-retail flow) cost nearly $400 million in severance through fiscal 2026 but is pitched as the structural lever — not demand recovery — that gets margins back toward double digits in fiscal 2027.
- Elliott Hill reiterated Win Now (the interim turnaround program) will **sunset by end of calendar 2026**, handing off to the Sport Offense as the "next decade" operating model, with specifics reserved for a November 16–17 Investor Day.

## What's changing
- **Foot Locker relationship inflected positive** for the first time in four years (revenue and retail-sales comp both up) — a concrete signal the wholesale-repair strategy is working somewhere real.
- **North America wholesale "grew" 10%, but CFO undercut his own number**: the growth was mostly lower returns, cancellations, and discounts rather than actual higher sell-in — a healthier-quality business, but not the volume story the headline suggests.
- **Guidance shifted from growth to margin**: management now expects revenue down low-to-mid single digits (worse than prior guide) but gross margin expansion starting a quarter earlier (Q1 instead of Q2) — a explicit trade of top line for profitability via tighter buys and reduced sell-in.
- Retail momentum **decelerated sharply mid-quarter**: a strong start in March (especially North America) gave way to a "deceleration in retail sales trends" by mid-April that management attributes to consumer pressure on traffic and discretionary spend, only partly offset by a World Cup-driven bounce into June.
- China's China's digital and store discounting is easing and full-price realization is recovering, but revenue still declined 17% and management explicitly told investors to expect "revenue trends in Greater China over the near term to be in line with recent performance" — i.e., don't expect a China inflection soon.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **The $986 million tariff windfall is doing almost all the earnings work this quarter**: reported gross margin was "up 890 basis points," but excluding the one-time IEEPA tariff recovery, underlying gross margin was actually down 10 basis points year over year. Reported EPS of $0.72 becomes $0.20 without it — a more than 3x difference the company is happy to let flow through the reported headline.
- **Sportswear and Jordan streetwear are openly declining and guided to stay negative all of fiscal 2027**, with improvement pushed to "the back half" — management's own framing: "the results aren't there yet... particularly in Nike Sportswear and Jordan streetwear, where sell-through remains challenged."
- **CFO Matt Friend is departing after nearly 18 years**, mid-turnaround, mid-tariff-dispute, with only a vague commitment to "a seamless transition in the coming weeks and months" — no named successor, no timeline given on the call.
- **EMEA remains broadly troubled**: NIKE Direct down 16%, digital down 24%, with "disruption in the Middle East" cited as an ongoing headwind and a heavier Sportswear mix than other geographies dragging the whole region.
- Management **declined to give a specific optimal performance-vs-sportswear mix**, deflecting to "the consumer's going to decide" — a non-answer to a direct analyst question about portfolio strategy that reads as genuine uncertainty rather than a hidden plan.
- **Tariff cost exposure is unresolved and escalating**: guidance assumes a 10% incremental tariff rate through July, rising to 15% after — a real embedded cost headwind sitting right next to a one-time tariff *refund* being used to flatter this quarter's results.
