# GS — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-13)

**The key idea:** Goldman posted its second-best quarter ever by leaning harder into balance sheet — record equities financing, record private wealth lending, expanded Asia prime brokerage — funded by a 180bp CET1 drawdown, record buybacks, and aggressively grown deposits. Management spent most of the call defending that this is durable, diversified growth rather than late-cycle leverage into a private-credit and volatility storm that hasn't actually broken yet.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **A record quarter built on deploying capital, not just trading well.** GBM revenue hit a record $12.7B and equities hit record $5.3B, but Coleman is explicit the growth came from "significantly expanded" activities in equities financing and lending — CET1 fell 180bp to 12.5% (110bp cushion) as capital moved into prime financing, acquisition financing, and higher market-risk RWA from volatility, alongside a record $5B buyback.

2. **FICC's weak spot got buried in strong headline numbers.** Rates and mortgages intermediation were "significantly lower" YoY on a "tougher market-making backdrop," offset by currencies/commodities strength from Brent Crude's record monthly move and a 60% European gas spike. Solomon frames it as still the "10th best FICC quarter ever," but the underlying mix shift — a business he says "used to" be more single-line-dependent — is doing real work to smooth what would otherwise read as a soft quarter.

3. **Private credit defense is rehearsed and pointed, but not a clean bill of health.** Solomon distinguishes broad private credit ($3.5T) from direct lending ($1.6-1.7T) from retail-exposed direct lending (~$230B NAV, "the thing that's been getting a lot of focus"), and Coleman states FICC financing's life-to-date realized losses are zero excluding some CRE. But Solomon also concedes this has been an unusually long credit cycle without a real recession test, and "when credit cycles go on longer... spreads get tighter, market participants get more aggressive" — an admission that today's comfort is priced for a cycle that hasn't turned.

4. **Sponsor/PE monetization stayed stalled, and Goldman is candid about why.** Private equity enterprise value across the whole industry is "$4 trillion... less than one NVIDIA," per Solomon, and GPs are exercising the "optionality to wait" on marks set during the 2020-2021 markup cycle rather than sell into current conditions — even as the underlying M&A and IPO backlog stays at a four-year high.

5. **AI shows up as cost center now, growth thesis later.** Non-comp expense growth ($750M YoY, $650M of it transaction-based) is explicitly tied to accelerating cloud migration and data infrastructure investment ahead of AI deployment under One GS 3.0 — Solomon is "hugely forward-leaning" on AI as a multi-year accelerant, but the near-term P&L impact is expense, not yet efficiency.

## Economy & consumer

- **Optimism curdled into volatility mid-quarter.** Solomon: the year "began with a degree of optimism... markets hit record highs," then volatility rose "amid concerns around AI-driven disruption in sectors like software, heightened uncertainty in parts of private credit, and the conflict in the Middle East."
- **Energy-to-inflation pass-through is the watch item, not yet realized.** "People did not see that really translating through in the first quarter, but that doesn't mean that people aren't extremely cautious about whether or not it will translate through in the second quarter," particularly if the Middle East conflict drags into Q2/Q3.
- **No direct retail-consumer segment**, but CEOs are described as watching commodity-price pass-through to consumer demand closely — a proxy read on households from the corporate side.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Record M&A advisory (+89% YoY) with a durable backlog.** Deals cited: Unilever's $43B food-business merger with McCormick, Sysco's $29B Jetro Restaurant Depot acquisition, Coterra's $26B sale to Devon Energy — backlog stayed near a four-year high even after "extraordinary accruals," because replenishment kept pace.
- **Equities financing nearly doubled YoY (+59%) on Asia strength**, hitting record average prime balances; FICC+equities financing combined rose 36% YoY to $3.7B, now ~40% of total FICC/equities revenue — a structural shift toward steadier, balance-sheet-backed income.
- **Private banking and lending revenue was squeezed by NIM compression** even as ultra-high-net-worth lending hit a record $46B — Coleman expects the deposit-cost headwind to "persist... for much of 2026" before durable-revenue growth resumes in 2027.
- **Provisions for credit losses rose to $315M** on a mix of loan growth, "several very small" single-name impairments Coleman calls "not particularly thematic," and a reserve adjustment for the broader operating outlook — explicitly *not* tied to private credit or FICC financing exposure per Coleman's direct denial.
- **Platform Solutions revenue declined YoY** on the Apple portfolio moving to held-for-sale, expected to "run lower" the rest of the year in line with seasonality — a legacy wind-down, not new weakness.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Capital flowed to equities financing, Asia, acquisition financing, and UHNW lending** — all named in the year-end strategic update as deployment priorities post-capital-rule changes, and Coleman says November's leverage-ratio changes specifically freed up capacity for acquisition financing.
- **CET1 cushion (110bp) is the live constraint** being actively traded off against three uses: client activity, buybacks, and regulatory-proposal uncertainty (Basel III finalization, G-SIB surcharge re-proposals) — Coleman calls 12.5% "basically... the wide end" of the typical operating range.
- **Deposit funding via Marcus is the strategic lever behind the lending build**, but competitive deposit-rate pressure is an accepted cost, not a surprise, "a strategic source of funding for us that we continue to grow."
- **Alternatives AUM at $429B, gross fundraising $26B** (incl. $10B private credit), on track toward the 2030 target of $750B AUM and $75-100B annual fundraising — diversified across corporate equity, credit, real estate, and hedge fund strategies so no single channel bears the growth load.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief: sponsor/PE activity "will turn on" because LPs, not GPs, own the capital and will eventually force distributions.** Action: no new commitment beyond staying "very well-positioned" — this is a wait-for-the-market call, not a funded bet.
- **Belief: AI is a multi-year accelerant that will let Goldman "more aggressively invest in growth in areas... more constrained" over the last five years.** Action: concrete near-term spend is on cloud migration and data quality/completeness infrastructure — the foundational plumbing, not yet AI products themselves; Solomon frames the productivity payoff as future, not current.
- **Belief: private credit's institutional base is resilient even through a cycle turn.** Action: continuing to scale toward the $300B credit platform target and grow institutional subscriptions (40% of Q1 GS Credit BDC subscriptions were institutions, "many... first-time investors") — a genuine funded bet, not just talk, though sized to "patiently and selectively invest capital" per Solomon.
- **Belief: Asia competitive gap in FICC/equities is closing.** Action: Coleman confirms capital was "moved quickly" into Asia in Q1 with results showing in record prime balances — talk and action aligned here, one of the cleaner matches on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Sponsor share of IB revenue: "not a number we've disclosed"** — repeated when pressed a second time by the same analyst later in the call.
- **No specific timeline for when sponsor/PE monetization actually accelerates** — "it will turn on," but tied to LP pressure dynamics with no date attached.
- **Credit-cycle loss framing is conditional, not a guarantee**: Solomon volunteers that a real recession would bring "higher losses across the space than... a shorter cycle," deliberately not extending the zero-loss FICC-financing track record as a forward promise.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Private credit risk dominated, asked from three angles**: sector-level contagion (Poonawala), loss-rate durability under a cycle turn (Najarian), and PCL build attribution (Mayo, Cassidy) — management's answer was consistent (institutional base, zero realized FICC-financing losses) but never fully closed the recession-scenario question.
- **Capital consumption and CET1 trajectory** drew repeated follow-ups (Schorr, McGratty) on whether RWA growth and buybacks at record pace are sustainable against the cushion — Coleman's answer leaned on "attractive returns" without a specific ROE floor disclosed for the new lending.
- **Durability of the equities/FICC-financing surge** was asked directly (Martinez): what's structural versus what reverses if markets or client leverage turn — Coleman conceded the flip side is real ("if clients were looking for a lot less... financing... those activity levels would reverse") without offering a hedge against that scenario.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts are asking, in different words, whether Goldman's best-ever quarter is a durable structural re-rating of a more diversified franchise, or leveraged beta into an equities/credit cycle that has not yet turned — and management's answer is confident but explicitly untested.
