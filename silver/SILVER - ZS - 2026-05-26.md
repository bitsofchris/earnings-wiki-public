# ZS — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-05-26)

**The key idea:** Zscaler posted a strong quarter — 25% ARR growth, record operating margin — but spent most of the call managing two separate shocks: the abrupt departure of two sales leaders under the CRO, and rising hardware component costs forcing a CapEx pull-forward. Layered on top is Jay Chaudhry's aggressive framing of frontier AI models (particularly "Mythos") as both a terrifying new attack vector and a large forthcoming tailwind — a bet the company is making operationally (Symmetry Systems acquisition, AI Protect) well ahead of it showing up in guided numbers.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Sales leadership turnover is muddying an otherwise clean quarter.** Two sales leaders under CRO Mike Rich departed at quarter-end; CFO Kevin Rubin repeatedly used the word "prudent" to describe guidance, and FY2027 net-new ARR (ex-Red Canary) is guided essentially flat.
2. **The AI-threat narrative is doing a lot of guidance-avoidance work.** Chaudhry says inbound demand tied to frontier models is unprecedented ("we've never seen so many inbound calls come in so quickly") but the company explicitly excludes any Q4 benefit and gives no FY2027 quantification — a real signal is being described in words, not numbers.
3. **Component cost inflation from the AI buildout is now hitting a pure-play security vendor.** Zscaler already raised branch-appliance prices once this year and is pulling FY2027 data-center purchases into Q4 to lock in prices; FY2027 CapEx/revenue is guided up to 200bps higher, attributed directly to AI data center demand consuming memory/storage/processor supply.
4. **New logo acquisition, not upsell, is the confirmed soft spot.** Net retention has held steady near 115% for several quarters; the deceleration is concentrated in new customer wins, and management laid out a four-part remediation plan (lower-market coverage, VAR incentives, GSI programs, major-account focus) for FY2027 — an admission the current motion isn't working, not a macro problem.
5. **Symmetry Systems is a small, forward-positioned bet on agent identity.** Immaterial ARR ("low single digits"), acquired purely for access-graph technology to plug into the Zero Trust Exchange for AI agents — Chaudhry frames it as solving a problem "most companies aren't thinking about," deliberately avoiding a play for core identity (ceded to hyperscalers).

## Economy & consumer
- No macro or consumer commentary — Zscaler is enterprise/public-sector infrastructure and doesn't touch end-consumer demand; the closest proxy is enterprise IT/security budget behavior, which management describes as accelerating due to AI-driven fear rather than budget constraint.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record margins amid growth:** Non-GAAP operating margin hit an all-time high of 23% (up 140bps y/y) with revenue up 25% to $850M, "exceeding the high end of our guidance."
- **Zero Trust Everywhere land-grab is working:** exited Q3 with more than 700 "Zero Trust Everywhere" enterprises (up from over 550 in Q2), with Zero Trust Branch ARR "approximately tripled year-over-year" and a record eight-figure, 2,000-site branch deal displacing both a firewall and VPN incumbent.
- **Data security crossed $500M ARR, up over 30% y/y**, as customers consolidate DLP/classification modules onto the platform instead of stitching together point products.
- **What's not working:** new logo growth ("an area where I think we can do better" — Rubin), and now leadership stability on the sales side, with two departures under the CRO cited as a reason for conservative Q4/FY2027 guidance.
- Free cash flow margin fell to 16% this quarter (from 18% a year ago) on collection timing, though it's 29% year-to-date.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx is being pulled forward and raised** — FY2026 CapEx guided to high-single-digits of revenue (up from prior mid-single-digit guidance), and FY2027 CapEx/revenue seen rising up to 200bps further, explicitly to "lock in today's prices ahead of potential increases."
- **The binding constraint is physical component supply**, not compute access per se: "This situation... is created by all these AI data centers that's causing such a shortage of so many parts" (Chaudhry) — memory, storage, and processor pricing/availability for branch appliances and data-center gear.
- **M&A is small and capability-driven, not revenue-driven:** Symmetry Systems (agent identity/access-graph) and SquareX are both "incredibly immaterial" in ARR — bought for technology and talent, not near-term financial contribution.
- Cloud marketplace TCV reached ~$900M year-to-date, "more than doubled year-over-year" — a notable go-to-market/procurement shift rather than a product investment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Chaudhry calls this a "forward-like moment" for Zero Trust, arguing frontier models like Mythos are multiplying unpatched enterprise vulnerabilities "by as much as 10x" and that AI agents will soon be cybersecurity's weakest link, moving "from discovery to data theft in minutes."
- **Action so far is modest relative to the rhetoric:** the concrete moves are one small acquisition (Symmetry), a product bundle (AI Protect, ~$100M in bookings over 12 months), and partnership programs (Project Glasswing with Anthropic, OpenAI's Daybreak/TAC, Project AI Guardian with GSIs) — not yet a step-change in R&D spend, headcount, or guided revenue.
- **The gap is explicit and self-acknowledged:** management "aren't really factoring in any meaningful impact of the new opportunities for Q4" and only expects impact to show up "in fiscal 2027" — the conviction is stated well ahead of any commitment showing up in the model.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Declined to quantify how much the eight-figure federal deal was pure upsell vs. new bookings, deflecting with "it is TCV" and "a large part of that deal was renewals."
- Declined to give FY2027 growth assumptions for core ZIA/ZPA products specifically, calling it "a fairly granular element" not worth disclosing this early.
- Declined to commit to another branch-appliance price increase next year: "I don't know that I would want to sit here today and say... there will be another price change."
- Would not quantify the sales-productivity/hiring plan behind the FY2027 guide, calling it "a bit early to provide quantification."

## The street — what analysts asked
- The dominant theme across multiple questioners was the sales leadership departures and whether they alone explain the guidance deceleration, or something deeper — management's answer never went beyond "prudent" and "disruptive," without naming a root cause.
- A second cluster probed new-logo softness specifically — is it leadership disruption, product-market coverage in the mid-market, or demand — management pinned it on coverage gaps and incentive structure, not weakening demand.
- A third cluster tried to get Mythos/frontier-model tailwinds sized in dollars and timing — management gave vivid qualitative color (unprecedented inbound volume) but explicitly declined to quantify pipeline or revenue impact.
- Compressed worry: is Zscaler's slowing net-new ARR guide a temporary leadership-transition blip, or the first sign that new-logo growth and core product deceleration are becoming structural even as the AI-threat narrative provides cover?
