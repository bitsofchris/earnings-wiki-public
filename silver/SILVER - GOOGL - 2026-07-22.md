# GOOGL — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-22)

**The key idea:** Alphabet raised CapEx guidance again while admitting it's still supply-constrained enough to rent third-party capacity at a margin cost — the tension is between "demand outpaces investment" bullishness and the mechanics of an infrastructure buildout that's now bleeding into free cash flow. Cloud's margin expansion this quarter is real, but management is pre-warning that TPU bridging deals and Wiz integration will pressure it right back down in the second half.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Gemini 4 is the actual bet**: "we have started our most ambitious pre-training run yet," with Pichai saying larger base models are required to stay competitive at the next frontier tier — an implicit admission that Flash-tier iteration alone won't hold the line.
- **TPUs are becoming a standalone product line, not just internal infrastructure**: the company began recognizing revenue from TPU system sales delivered to customer data centers for the first time this quarter, with the "vast majority" of those contracted revenues pushed to 2027.
- **Third-party capacity as an explicit bridge**: rather than turn away large cloud customers during the compute crunch, Alphabet is renting external capacity short-term to capture multi-year deals it judges "highly ROI positive" over the deal's lifetime — a bet that near-term margin pain buys long-term customer lock-in.
- **Agentic commerce infrastructure**: Universal Commerce Protocol (open-sourced with the retail industry) and Universal Cart aim to make Google the transaction layer for agent-driven shopping, with Target and Steve Madden already live.
- **Antigravity as an internal force multiplier**: a Chrome team is "on track to accelerate delivery by 8x, compressing a two-year timeline into three months through model-driven refactoring" — offered as proof the coding agent tooling is already changing how Google itself ships software.

## What's changing
- **CapEx guidance raised again**, this time to $195–205 billion for the year, up from the prior $180–190 billion range — the second consecutive quarter of upward revision, driven by "acceleration in the delivery of capacity," not new demand signals per se.
- **Free cash flow went negative** for the quarter (-$5.9 billion) for the first time in this stretch, purely a function of CapEx timing rather than operating weakness — operating cash flow itself stayed strong.
- **Cloud backlog jumped again**, up more than $50 billion sequentially to $514 billion, with management guiding just over half of it recognized as revenue over the next 24 months — leaving a large, long-duration tail.
- **Debt load roughly sextupled year-over-year**, from about $16 billion to roughly $100 billion, alongside a new equity raise — a capital-structure shift Ashkenazi framed as building "resilience," not just funding growth.
- **Net income was inflated by a non-operating windfall**: other income and expense hit $98 billion, "primarily due to unrealized gains in our equity securities portfolio" — worth flagging plainly since it drove the headline earnings number more than operations did.
- **Token throughput nearly 40% higher quarter-over-quarter** (22 billion tokens/minute, up from 16 billion), described as being met by continued supply constraints rather than excess capacity.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Cloud operating margin is guided down, not up**, despite this quarter's jump to 35.6% — third-party bridging capacity and ongoing Wiz integration costs are both flagged as near-term headwinds for the back half of 2026.
- **Coding is an admitted weak spot**: Pichai directly conceded "coding and agentic coding is an example" of an area where Google isn't yet at the frontier, pointing to iterative Flash improvements rather than claiming parity today.
- **Model release cadence questions went half-answered**: asked why Google doesn't ship as frequently or loudly as rivals, Pichai promised a move toward "almost monthly cadence" once Gemini 4 lands — an implicit admission the current pace has been slower than the competition's.
- **Waymo structure question was deflected**: asked directly what conditions would justify spinning Waymo out of Alphabet, Pichai declined to engage with the premise at all, pivoting to "we're focused on scaling" — a non-answer to a direct governance question.
- **No mention of margin dilution timeline specifics**: management repeatedly declined to break out TPU-specific margins ("we don't break out margins for any specific products"), leaving investors to infer the P&L impact rather than see it.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Whether 2027 CapEx returns will match the discipline of prior cycles given supply-chain inflation · how much of Cloud's margin gain is sustainable once third-party bridging and Wiz costs bite · whether Gemini can defend frontier-model status without matching rivals' release cadence · what the real TPU margin profile looks like once system sales scale · optimal capital structure now that debt has grown sixfold and equity issuance has resumed · whether Waymo's growing scale eventually forces a structural change management won't yet discuss. The underlying worry: Alphabet is asking investors to trust that an accelerating, increasingly external and debt-funded buildout will pay back on the same attractive ROIC curve as the smaller, self-funded one that preceded it.
