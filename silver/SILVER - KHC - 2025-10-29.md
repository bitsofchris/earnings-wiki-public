# KHC — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** Kraft Heinz is trying to execute a two-year corporate breakup against the backdrop of what CEO Carlos Abrams-Rivera calls "one of the worst consumer sentiments we have seen in decades." Management cut full-year guidance not because it's overspending to defend the business, but because consumption is worse than expected and its own promotional dollars aren't generating the lift they used to — a tension the company itself surfaces rather than papers over.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The guidance cut is a demand story, not a spending story.** CFO Andre Maciel was explicit: "The profit revision is not linked to incremental investments beyond what we had previously communicated" — it's lower U.S. consumption, a slower Taste Elevation recovery, unpriced Meat/Coffee inflation, and Indonesia-specific supply chain one-offs.
2. **Promotions are working less well than they used to, and management admits it on the call.** Maciel: "The ROIs of those are not good, to be honest, the lifts are low" — a rare, direct concession that ~$300M in incremental U.S. promo and ~$80M in media isn't translating into the volume scanner data would show, because higher industry-wide promotional intensity dilutes everyone's lift.
3. **Retailers and distributors are destocking ahead of the consumer, amplifying the pain.** Beyond soft underlying demand, customers are "adjusting their own level of inventory" heading into the holidays, and the same dynamic — an aggressive inventory pullback by a single distributor — is compounding a genuine ~10-point consumer sentiment collapse in Indonesia.
4. **The split narrative is being actively defended against comparison to a peer that pivoted.** Asked directly whether KHC might change course the way a comparable CPG spin did, Carlos held the line on perimeter and leadership while Maciel left an explicit door open ("if at any moment, we think it might create more value... we will") — commitment with a hedge attached.
5. **Two future companies, two very different jobs.** Global Taste Elevation's mandate is return-to-growth in 2026 (already showing low-single-digit improvement and 70% share gains); North American Grocery's mandate is stabilizing cash flow first, growth second — a real divergence in what "success" means for each spinco in year one.

## Economy & consumer
- **Consumer sentiment is the headline drag, not a KHC-specific failure.** Carlos: "we have now one of the worst consumer sentiments we have seen in decades," with the U.S. industry decelerating through Q3 and softening further into October independent of hurricane noise.
- **Indonesia is a sentiment shock, not a competitive loss.** A ~10-point year-over-year drop in consumer sentiment triggered sell-out declines and a distributor disruption on a ~$300M (12% of Emerging Markets) business — market share there is reportedly fine, distribution execution is not.
- **Emerging Markets ex-Indonesia is genuinely strong and accelerating** — 9.2% growth, with Heinz up 13% YTD, a bright spot management leans on to argue the core brand-investment playbook works outside the U.S. malaise.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Taste Elevation is the recovery story:** 70% of that portfolio's revenue is gaining share, trending into low-single-digit growth, with September share gains in 70% of the U.S. Taste Elevation business.
- **Meat, Coffee, and Cheese are the structural weak spot** — roughly 40% of North American Grocery sales — where Carlos previously conceded (per an analyst callback to CAGNY commentary) a "much lower right to win," and where inflation went unpriced due to competitive dynamics, directly denting Q3 margins.
- **Both future companies declined low-single-digits pro forma in Q3**, with North American Grocery declining more than Global Taste Elevation — the improvement from 1H25 is real but the two businesses remain in overall contraction.
- **Renovated Lunchables and Capri Sun returned to growth** off a concentrated back-to-school execution push, evidence the Brand Growth System playbook can work when fully resourced — but it hasn't yet been extended with the same intensity to Meat/Coffee/Cheese.

## Investing & scarcity
- Incremental 2025 investment is quantified precisely: ~$300M more U.S. promotion, ~$80M more media, plus R&D and commercial headcount — all concentrated in 2H25 and explicitly not being increased further, because "we don't think that adding more price at this moment will yield results."
- Capital allocation priorities are unchanged and stated plainly: organic investment first, then holding net debt at/near 3x, with both post-split entities targeted at investment grade (net debt below 4x) — cash discipline is being preserved even as EBITDA declines, with cash flow up year-over-year.
- **The real scarcity is demand, not resources.** Distribution/inventory levers (Indonesia distributor transition, U.S. retailer destocking) are actively working against the company faster than marketing dollars can offset.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Taste Elevation returns to growth in 2026 and Indonesia's P&L recovers by 2H26. **Action:** resourcing that belief mainly through continued marketing/ABC brand investment and a distributor swap — not through faster price action or larger emergency spend.
- **Belief:** current cyclical consumer weakness will pass and KHC should "come out of it with a much stronger portfolio." **Action:** concentrated, testable promotional experiments (cross-merchandising, spreading spend beyond holiday peaks) rather than a broad reflex to spend more — a deliberate, patient bet that brand renovation beats blanket discounting.
- **Gap worth flagging:** management says returns from stepped-up promotion are already "not good," yet is doubling down on promotional intensity into the holiday quarter specifically because that's traditionally KHC's highest-share period — a bet that concentration, not the underlying tactic, is what's been suboptimal.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- No incremental price or marketing beyond what's already been announced — explicitly ruled out for now, with future increases left conditional on "brand assessments."
- No estimate given for one-time separation/restructuring costs, deferred with "a lot of work in motion right now."
- No firm commitment that the announced business perimeter or leadership structure survives untouched — Maciel left explicit optionality to adjust "if... it might create more value to shareholders."

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: (1) is the profit cut actually disguised overspending — answered directly, no; (2) will KHC's spin plan itself wobble the way a comparable peer's did — answered with reaffirmed commitment plus a deliberately open door on perimeter/balance sheet; (3) why doesn't scanner/syndicated promo data show the investment KHC claims to be making — answered with a candid admission that ROIs and lifts are simply weaker this cycle.
- One question (on the "much lower right to win" in commoditized Meat/Coffee/Cheese categories) was cut off by a technical outage before management could respond, leaving the Brand Growth System's applicability to the weakest third of the North American Grocery portfolio unaddressed on this call.
- Compressed worry: **is Kraft Heinz spending real money to defend brands in a consumer environment that's deteriorating faster than management's own promotional playbook can compensate for, right as it tries to split the company in two?**
