# DXCM — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-30)

**The key idea:** Dexcom delivered a clean beat — 15% revenue growth, gross margin up 600bps year-over-year, EBITDA margin up 840bps — but nearly every analyst question circled back to one soft spot: U.S. new-patient starts have not hit a record in four quarters, even as management keeps pivoting the narrative to "global" records and future coverage catalysts (Prime Therapeutics, CMS) that haven't landed yet. The bet is that Type 2 non-insulin coverage expansion — not current demand — is what reaccelerates the U.S. business.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Margin story is real and underappreciated.** Gross margin hit 63.5%, "up significantly on a year-over-year basis and flat compared to the fourth quarter, despite our typical Q1 seasonality," per CFO Jereme Sylvain — a genuine operational inflection from manufacturing efficiency and freight normalization, not just G7 15-Day mix.
2. **The U.S. new-patient-record question won't die.** Four consecutive quarters of "close to a record" in the U.S. specifically, even as the company posted a global record. When pressed on whether the low end of guidance (no global record) is now the base case, Sylvain pivoted to "we did have a record new patient quarter this quarter globally" rather than address the U.S. gap directly.
3. **CMS coverage is the whole ballgame and still unscheduled.** Roughly half of the Type 2 non-insulin population sits in Medicare; CEO Jake Leach calls it "only a matter of time" three separate times across the call without offering a date, and notably suggests the RCT "may not be required" — undercutting the company's own emphasis on the upcoming ADA data readout as pivotal evidence.
4. **Cash pile is now explicitly a strategic question, not just a balance-sheet fact.** $2.4B in cash, up over $400M in the quarter, with management flagging "exploring new opportunities" language in the release and punting specifics to the upcoming Investor Day — tuck-in M&A and buybacks both on the table.
5. **Gross margin guidance held flat despite the beat** — a rare instance of a company sandbagging optics on purpose, citing 50-100bps of fuel/resin risk tied to "the current geopolitical environment" and oil-linked shipping costs.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read at all.** This call has zero discussion of consumer spending power, discretionary trade-down, or employment — CGM demand here is a function of insurance coverage and clinical awareness, not economic cycle.
- **Penetration framing stands in for consumer health.** Leach: "about 30% penetration into the covered lives... that other 2/3 is out there today" — the addressable gap is coverage-driven, not demand-driven.
- **Type 2 non-insulin is the growth frontier and it's a coverage story, not an affordability one:** only "25% of the people that see their physician that have Type 2 diabetes have coverage" today per Sylvain.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: G7 15-Day launch.** Broad U.S. rollout drove "an outstanding launch," improved field reliability, and management now expects **nearly 50% of the base converted by year-end** — a faster-than-typical product transition.
- **Working: margin execution.** Operating margin hit 22.2% of revenue vs. 13.8% a year ago; EBITDA margin hit 30.6% vs. 22.2% — full-year opex guide raised on the strength of one quarter, which Sylvain called out as unusual: "we typically don't like to raise... guidance after one quarter."
- **Not working / stagnant: U.S. new-patient growth.** Retention and utilization were described as "fairly consistent within a band" rather than improving — a plateau metric management is trying to reframe as an opportunity ("one of our goals is to improve it").
- **Prime Therapeutics win is real but modest.** Adds ~1 million non-insulin lives, pushing total Type 2 non-insulin commercial coverage past 7 million by year-end — Sylvain was explicit this "wasn't necessarily counted on [as] a major contributor to the original guide."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Ireland manufacturing ramp is the biggest committed capital item**, turned on in Q4 2025 and now ramping through Q2/Q3 2026 — flagged as the reason R&D/opex won't stay flat despite Q1's unusually disciplined spend.
- **Smart Basal is still in pilot**, deliberately not scaled: "We're not... validating the algorithm... it's more around how does it fit into the clinical workflow" — a workflow-integration constraint, not a technology one.
- **The real scarcity is regulatory/payer, not physical.** No supply chain, chip, or component constraints were mentioned (contrast with typical hardware/semis calls) — the binding constraint the entire call orbits is CMS and commercial payer decisions.
- **New patch/adhesive technology** (FDA-cleared) begins shipping "in the coming weeks" to improve sensor survivability — an incremental reliability investment, not a new platform.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** CMS coverage for Type 2 non-insulin is inevitable ("when not if"). **Action:** Completing an RCT for ADA — even though Leach simultaneously says CMS "may not" require RCT data at all, which weakens the stated rationale for running the trial as a coverage-unlock mechanism versus a marketing/evidence asset.
- **Belief:** International growth will stay roughly balanced with U.S. for the full year. **Action:** No structural investment shift toward international was described beyond localized product launches (Stelo, a new international CGM system) — the balance claim rests on comp dynamics (easier prior-year international comps late in the year) rather than a new spending commitment.
- **Belief:** Cash pile enables "multiple opportunities" including M&A. **Action:** No concrete M&A targets or committed capital — this is optionality being deferred to Investor Day, not a stated plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to raise gross margin guidance** despite a beat, citing unquantified fuel/resin geopolitical risk of 50-100bps — an explicit sandbagging move Sylvain acknowledged directly.
- **Would not give a CMS coverage timeline**, repeating "hard to estimate exactly when this coverage is gonna come" across multiple questions.
- **Declined to break out lifetime patient value by cohort** in detail, deferring the longer-term financial framework to the upcoming Investor Day.
- **Would not confirm whether the low end of guidance (no global patient record) is now more likely** — Robbie Marcus asked this directly and got a reframe toward the Q1 global record instead of a direct answer.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The dominant theme was U.S. new-patient stagnation** — three separate analysts (Goldman, JPMorgan, BofA) pressed on the four-quarter "close to a record" pattern in the U.S. specifically, clearly unconvinced by the "global record" substitution.
- **CMS timing and structure** was the second cluster — whether coverage requires an RCT, whether it comes with restrictive stipulations (e.g., requiring metformin use), and whether it's a binary event.
- **Margin durability and capital allocation** rounded out the set — why guidance wasn't raised further given the Q1 beat, and what the swelling cash balance is actually for.
- **Dodged directly:** the U.S.-specific record-quarter question was never answered head-on; management consistently pivoted to the global figure. The compressed worry: *investors are watching for evidence that U.S. growth is decelerating structurally, and management's answer is "wait for coverage catalysts we can't date yet."*
