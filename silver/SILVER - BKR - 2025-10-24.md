# BKR — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-24)

**The key idea:** Baker Hughes is running two businesses on opposite trajectories — an IET (gas technology, power, digital) segment riding a genuine structural boom in LNG, AI-driven power demand and record backlog, funding a bet-the-portfolio $8.1B Chart Industries acquisition — while OFSE (oilfield services) quietly absorbs a second straight year of declining upstream spend through cost-cutting rather than growth. Management's "age of gas" framing is doing a lot of work to paper over that divergence, and a freshly announced but unspecified "comprehensive evaluation" of capital allocation and cost structure suggests bigger portfolio moves are still to come.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **IET is now unambiguously the growth story.** Segment revenue grew 15% y/y to $3.4B, EBITDA up 20% to $635M, margins up 90bps to 18.8%, and backlog hit a record $32.1B — management is targeting **20% IET margins next year** and reiterated a **$40B+ three-year IET order target** that explicitly excludes any Chart contribution.
2. **The Chart acquisition is the real capital allocation story**, not a bolt-on. Shareholders approved it October 6, an integration office with 14 workstreams is already running pre-close to chase the full **$325M in cost synergies**, and management wants **net debt/EBITDA down to 1-1.5x within 24 months** post-close — signaling real balance-sheet strain is expected from the deal.
3. **AI and data centers are explicitly credited as a macro tailwind**, not just a BKR-specific opportunity: management cited outside estimates that AI investment drives **30-40% of U.S. GDP growth this year**, and BKR's own data-center power orders (NovaLT) hit ~$700M+ year-to-date, on track to beat a 3-year, $1.5B target early.
4. **OFSE margin resilience is real but the underlying demand is not** — margins held up mainly through cost-out initiatives against an 8% revenue decline, while Q4 guidance explicitly cites "E&P budget constraints affecting U.S. land" and softness in Saudi Arabia, Argentina, and the North Sea.
5. **A vague "comprehensive evaluation" of capital allocation, costs, and operations** was announced in early October and repeatedly deflected on this call — CEO Lorenzo Simonelli said only "we won't speculate today" — leaving the door open to further restructuring, divestitures, or capital returns beyond what's already disclosed.

## Economy & consumer
- **No direct consumer exposure** — BKR sells to oil & gas operators, industrial customers, and increasingly data-center/AI infrastructure builders, so the "consumer" lens doesn't apply; omitted from frontmatter.
- **Macro framed as AI-subsidized resilience:** "This wave of investment is unlocking new growth vectors... serving as a broad stimulus for the global economy," with AI-driven investment cited as 30-40% of U.S. GDP growth this year — an unusually explicit macro claim for an oilfield services company to make on its own earnings call.
- **Oil demand described as genuinely uncertain:** "concerns around softer demand and rising OPEC+ production" on one side, geopolitical risk propping up prices on the other — management is not projecting confidence in oil, only in gas.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working — IET orders and backlog:** $4.1B of IET orders this quarter (record Cordant Solutions orders, $800M+ LNG equipment), backlog up 3% sequentially to a record $32.1B, book-to-bill of 1.2x.
- **Working — SSPS (subsea) had a record quarter**, including a return to the Brazilian subsea tree market via a Petrobras frame agreement for up to 50 trees "following an extended absence" — notable re-entry into a market BKR had ceded share in.
- **Not working — OFSE softness in specific geographies:** revenue declined 1% sequentially internationally, "where declines in Saudi Arabia, Argentina and the North Sea were largely offset by growth in Asia Pacific and Middle East."
- **Full-year guide raised modestly:** total company adjusted EBITDA guidance raised to exceed $4.7B (from prior framing), driven entirely by IET upgrades — OFSE EBITDA midpoint held "relatively unchanged."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Chart Industries acquisition ($8.1B, approved, closing mid-2026)** is the dominant capital deployment, adding thermal management and air/gas handling to IET, explicitly expected to be **accretive to the 20% margin target** post-integration.
- **Portfolio pruning continues in parallel:** Continental Disc Corp acquisition closed; PSI (precision sensors) sale and Cactus surface pressure control JV expected to close early 2026, together cutting ~$150M of annual EBITDA but generating ~$1.4B gross cash proceeds — funding Chart deleveraging.
- **Binding constraint is turbine/manufacturing capacity, not demand:** NovaLT delivery slots are booked "well into '28 and beyond," and management flagged "the impact of any aeroderivative supply chain tightness" as a live risk to IET guidance — demand is outrunning BKR's own build capacity.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** LNG installed capacity target raised from 800 MTPA (2030) to ~950 MTPA (2035), with natural gas demand growth "over 20%" and LNG growth "at least 75%" by 2040 — a genuinely bullish multi-decade structural call.
- **Action backing it up:** Targeting $40B+ IET orders through 2028, 20% total-company margins by 2028, 20% IET margins in 2026 specifically, and at least $1B in additional divestiture proceeds to fund the Chart deleveraging — these are dollar-and-timeline commitments, not just talking points.
- **Gap:** On oil, the belief ("longer term, the outlook is more positive") is not backed by any comparable OFSE investment commitment — instead the stated 2026 plan for OFSE is cost discipline and "closing the gap with peers," i.e., defense, not offense. The bullishness is concentrated entirely in gas/power/industrial, and capital is following that split precisely.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- The **"comprehensive evaluation" of capital allocation, cost structure, and operations** announced in early October got no specifics: "We won't speculate today, but we'll keep working through the evaluation" — a live, unresolved process that could include further divestitures, cost actions, or capital returns.
- **2026 upstream spending guidance is deliberately soft** — "possibly leading to another year of global upstream spending decline" — management is hedging on magnitude while still committing to the direction.
- Horizon Two targets ($40B IET orders, 20% margins by 2028) are explicitly stated to **exclude any Chart acquisition benefit**, preserving optionality to point to Chart as incremental upside later rather than baking it into the headline commitment now.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts probed **power generation's breadth beyond data centers** (distributed power in oil patch, geothermal, offshore), the **Horizon Two margin bridge and $40B order confidence**, the **vague capital allocation evaluation**, **Chart pre-close integration progress**, **OFSE's surprising margin resilience amid declining upstream spend**, and **NovaLT order cadence/lead times**.
- No pushback or skepticism surfaced on the AI-GDP framing or the LNG capacity upgrade — questions stayed constructive and mechanics-focused rather than adversarial.
- The compressed worry: **can IET's structural gas/power boom keep growing fast enough to outrun a second straight year of OFSE decline, and what is the still-undisclosed "comprehensive evaluation" actually going to change?**
