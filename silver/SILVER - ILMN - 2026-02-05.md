# ILMN — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-05)

**The key idea:** Illumina closed out a recovery year with clinical sequencing running hot (20% ex-China growth, NovaSeq X's second-best placement quarter ever) while research/academic demand stays anesthetized by NIH funding uncertainty. Management is using the clinical cash engine to fund a pivot into multiomics and AI-driven drug discovery (Somalogic, BioInsight, Billion Cell Atlas) — but is guiding 2026 conservatively enough that the strong Q4 exit rate reads more like a ceiling than a floor.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Clinical is doing all the work.** Ex-China clinical consumables grew 20% in Q4 and are guided to "double-digit to mid-teens" for 2026, driven by more sequencing-intensive tests (whole exome→whole genome conversions, larger MRD/therapy-selection panels) — CEO Jacob Thaysen called this rising "intensity" the "main growth driver going forward," not just new test launches.
2. **Research is flat-lining, not recovering.** Academic/research consumables were "roughly flat" in Q4, still below historical levels, and guided down mid-to-high-single-digits for 2026 despite "welcome" NIH budget news — Thaysen conceded "there's still a lot of uncertainties for the institute for how grants are being distributed."
3. **The multiomics/AI pivot is real money, not slideware.** The $350M Somalogic close, PacBio IP acquisition, and BioInsight's Billion Cell Atlas (with AstraZeneca, Merck, Eli Lilly as initial partners) represent Illumina building a third customer base in pharma — but management is explicit it won't be "meaningful" until 2027.
4. **China remains a capped, not growing, market.** Down to <5% of revenue with instruments off 55% YoY on export restrictions; guidance assumes "little or no step-up" in H1 2026, contingent on regulatory relationship progress that isn't guaranteed.
5. **Guidance is deliberately conservative relative to exit momentum.** CFO Ankur Dhingra explicitly built 2026 guidance off the H2 2025 average (blending a weak Q3 with a strong Q4) rather than extrapolating the 7% ex-China Q4 growth rate — a tell that they don't fully trust the momentum to hold.

## Economy & consumer
- **No traditional consumer exposure** — customers are clinical labs, academic core labs, and pharma/biotech.
- **Policy, not macro cycle, is the swing factor.** Research demand hinges on "clarity around US policy and the funding environment," with NIH budget announcements framed as a "welcome development" but not yet resolved at the grant-distribution level.
- **Clinical reimbursement expansion is an independent tailwind**, insulated from the funding uncertainty hitting academia — driven by MRD and early cancer detection coverage growth.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Clinical consumables +20% ex-China; over 100 NovaSeq X placements in Q4 (second-highest ever), >60% to clinical customers; non-GAAP operating margin expanded 400bp YoY in Q4 to 23.7%; full-year EPS grew 16% to $4.84, beating original guidance.
- **Not working:** Research/applied consumables flat, still below historical run-rate; China instruments down 55%; gross margin down 40bp YoY in Q4, driven by 205bp of tariff impact.
- **Mid throughput (NovaSeq 6K) is a managed decline, not a cliff** — customers keep validated assays on 6K while building new assays on X, so pull-through is "holding up a lot better than expected," per an analyst's framing that management didn't dispute.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Somalogic ($350M cash, closed Jan 30) anchors the proteomics push** — described as "the frontline in multiomics" — plus Illumina Connected Multiomics software to unify genomics/proteomics/single-cell/epigenomics analysis.
- **Spatial transcriptomics and Constellation MAP Reads are slated for 2026 launch**, with five-base sequencing and other new assays expected to contribute just 1-2 points of growth starting in 2027 — a multi-year payoff, explicitly flagged as such.
- **Binding constraints are regulatory/institutional, not physical:** NIH grant-flow ambiguity gates research spend; China's export-control list gates instrument sales into a market Illumina says it has "good line of sight" on for 2026 but no resolution.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** Core sequencing business returns to "high single-digit growth" and 26% operating margin by 2027; research "can return to healthy growth" longer term but is modeled flat through 2026.
- **Acting:** Guiding only 130bp of 2026 margin expansion (vs. 200bp achieved in 2025) with an explicit 100bp Somalogic drag — margin ambition is being spent on acquisition dilution, not banked as upside.
- **Gap:** Management touts "high confidence" the 2027 targets remain intact post-Somalogic, but CFO's language — "we will get as close to the 26% operating margin as possible" — is softer than a hard commitment, and synergies are still "working through," not quantified.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to size BioInsight revenue** beyond "not going to be meaningful" in 2026, pushing all quantification to 2027-plus.
- **Wouldn't break out oncology vs. genetic-disease growth rates** when asked directly, only that oncology "will be the main growth driver."
- **Explicitly rejected extrapolating Q4's 7% ex-China growth** into 2026 guidance — CFO said "Q4 just one quarter didn't feel like using as a base for a full-year guide."
- **No firm timeline or dollar figure on China instrument sales** returning — contingent on ongoing government engagement with no committed outcome.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Guidance-vs-exit-rate skepticism dominated:** multiple analysts pushed on why 2026 guidance (2-4% ex-China) sits well below the Q4 exit rate (7%), and whether any one-time pull-forward inflated Q4 — management denied any pull-forward but conceded the guide leans on a blended, more conservative base.
- **Competitive pricing threat ahead of AGBT:** analysts flagged rival "$100 genome" pricing headlines; Thaysen reframed the conversation toward "total cost of workflow" rather than per-gigabase price, a message he all but admitted is a defensive repositioning ahead of the conference.
- **Capital deployment and M&A appetite:** with a clean balance sheet and ~$1B free cash flow, one analyst probed M&A criteria — answer stayed at the level of "opportunities for synergies," no explicit framework given.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts don't doubt the clinical story — they doubt whether management's own guidance discipline is masking either sandbagging or genuine uncertainty about whether Q4's momentum is durable.
