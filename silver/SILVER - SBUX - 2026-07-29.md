# SBUX — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Starbucks is claiming vindication for "Back to Starbucks" — a fourth straight quarter of positive comps and a second straight quarter of margin expansion, driven almost entirely by operational fixes (staffing model, supply chain, store remodels) rather than price. The unresolved tension: management is now quietly admitting the prior growth strategy was broken — years of bad store siting and "difficult remodels" — and is shifting from opening stores to closing and fixing the ones it has.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Green Apron Service, one year in, is being treated as the platform, not a program** — the CEO called it "the operating foundation of Back to Starbucks," with two-thirds of North America company stores now hitting 4+ shot ratings, up more than 40 points since October launch.
- **Store "uplifts" (cheap refreshes, not full remodels) are the highest-conviction capital bet**: over 650 completed this quarter alone at roughly $150,000 each, done overnight so stores never go dark, with management citing transaction lift "across access points, day parts, formats, and customer segments" and accelerating the target to at least 1,500 by fiscal 2026-end.
- **International is being restructured almost entirely into a licensing model** — with China now moved to a joint venture, about 90% of the international portfolio is license-based, explicitly framed as "a capital-efficient way to build our brand around the world."
- **Afternoon daypart is the next growth lever after "winning the morning"** — Refreshers (double-digit U.S. growth), a new "Spritzers" sparkling test, matcha, and food attach (a Q3 record) are the tools; digital menu boards (soon 80-90% store penetration) are being day-parted to merchandise afternoon occasions.
- **Delivery is scaling with "no trade-off on margins" and no cannibalization observed so far**, with a coming in-app white-label delivery integration that will finally let delivery orders earn Stars — currently delivery customers don't participate in the loyalty program at all.
- **Fiscal 2027 is billed as a tech-modernization year**: new inventory, staffing/scheduling, and point-of-sale systems, plus a push toward 24-hour supply chain replenishment (not 24-hour store hours, which management explicitly downplayed as "not a focus area").

## What's changing
- **North America operating margin grew year-over-year for the first time since Q1 fiscal 2024** — management's clearest single proof point that the turnaround has legs.
- **The growth algorithm has flipped from "open more stores" to "fix and close the ones we have"** — the CEO admitted "we did not have a great development strategy" two-to-three years ago, with "the wrong store in the wrong place," and the CFO now flagged potential North America closures from "deeper visibility into some underperforming coffee houses."
- **Consolidated operating margin expanded 430 basis points, but roughly half the story is a one-time tariff-refund timing effect** — Q3 refunds "largely offset" tariffs incurred over the prior three quarters, and management itself steered analysts to use the year-to-date COGS rate (32.3%) rather than the Q3 print (30.3%) as the "more normalized" figure. Even backing that out, margin still expanded — a genuine signal, but the headline number is inflated.
- **EPS grew 70% year-over-year to $0.85**, aided by a lower effective tax rate (21.8%) from favorable catch-up adjustments — flagged as non-recurring, with Q4 tax expected back in the "mid-20s."
- **Full-year guidance raised across revenue, margin (now >11%), and EPS ($2.55-$2.65)** — the guide-up itself functions as management's strongest claim that Q3 wasn't a one-off.
- **Debt paydown from China JV proceeds**: ~$1.8 billion repaid, leverage down to 2.9x — balance-sheet repair riding on the one-time China deconsolidation cash rather than organic FCF.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **New unit growth in North America is being quietly throttled, not expanded**, even as the company touts its best comps in years — a tacit admission that recent-vintage stores were poorly sited or over-invested in remodels, and that fixing legacy mistakes now takes priority over new openings.
- **China's contribution is still tiny and the framing is aspirational**: just $53 million of Q3 international net revenue attributable to China, at "operating margin above 100%" (a JV-accounting artifact, not real profitability), against a stated ambition of "up to 20,000 coffee houses over time" — a huge gap between the number disclosed today and the number being sold.
- **Labor/staffing efficiency questions were deflected rather than answered** — asked directly whether Green Apron Service labor hours were "nailed" or could get more efficient, the CEO gave a qualitative answer about "great job managing the schedule" without any hours-per-transaction or productivity metric.
- **Marketing spend as a share of sales was likewise dodged with a vague figure** ("a little bit more than 2% of sales") and no forward commitment beyond "will obviously continue to grow with the business."
- **Notably absent: any mention of AI, automation, or digital ordering technology as a lever** — for a company explicitly investing in a fiscal 2027 tech-modernization wave (new POS, inventory, staffing systems), there was zero discussion of AI-assisted operations, demand forecasting, or automation — a conspicuous gap for a large-scale retail operator in 2026.
- **Licensed-store North America revenue was roughly flat only because of net closures** — the "flat despite closures" framing is presented as resilience, but is also a tacit admission the licensed footprint is shrinking.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
How long the comp momentum can be sustained above the long-term 3% target and whether the current run rate is durable or lapping easy comparisons · skepticism about "Starbucks Refreshers" as a moat given competitors are now advertising into the same category · why store closures are accelerating now, right as the business is supposedly turning around, rather than earlier · whether licensed-store performance is converging with company-operated stores or still lagging · pressure-testing whether tariff refunds are propping up the margin beat and whether more refunds are coming in Q4 (management says mostly no) · digital menu board penetration and whether afternoon daypart economics can really rival the morning · the underlying worry: that the reported acceleration is a mix of one-time and lapping effects (tariff refunds, favorable Japan comp, tax catch-up) layered on top of a real-but-modest operational improvement, and the Street wants to know how much of the headline beat is repeatable.
