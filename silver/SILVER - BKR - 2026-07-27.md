# BKR — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-27)

**The key idea:** Baker Hughes just closed the Chart Industries acquisition and posted a record IET orders quarter ($7.1B, doubling YoY) on the back of AI-driven data center power demand and an LNG infrastructure boom — while its legacy OFSE oilfield business quietly absorbed a Middle East conflict shock better than feared. The tension: orders and backlog ($37.1B RPO) are surging faster than management is willing to pull revenue and margin guidance forward, because gas turbine cycle times mean much of today's bookings won't convert until after 2027.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Power is the new bottleneck, not compute.** Management explicitly reframed the AI infrastructure story: "access to reliable, scalable power increasingly becoming the primary constraint," with hyperscaler capex expected to nearly double from ~$370B (2025) to ~$750B (2028) and data center power demand growing 18%/year through 2030. This is Baker Hughes' central growth thesis, not a side note.
2. **Record orders, deferred revenue.** IET orders hit $7.1B (2.2x book-to-bill), pushing RPO to an all-time-high $37.1B, yet full-year IET revenue guidance midpoint was held at $13.5B — barely moved despite $12B of H1 orders "significantly exceeding" the original outlook, because GTE cycle times push a meaningful chunk of the order mix past 2027.
3. **Chart closes but arrives without guidance.** The acquisition completed day one, becomes a third reporting segment, and management laid out $325M of run-rate cost synergies by year three plus commercial synergies (data centers pairing power+cooling, gas infrastructure spanning hydrogen/helium/CO2/nitrogen/oxygen) — but explicitly declined to guide Chart's numbers until Q3, and leverage will "temporarily increase" before a 24-month deleveraging path back to 1-1.5x net debt/EBITDA.
4. **Middle East disruption absorbed, not resolved.** OFSE Middle East revenue fell 10% from Q4 2025, yet company-wide results beat guidance because product revenue outperformed even as service activity softened — at the cost of elevated logistics/freight costs compressing regional margin. Guidance assumes the region stays "broadly unchanged" through year-end, an assumption management flagged as a genuine swing factor either direction.
5. **Capacity investment is deliberately conservative.** New gas turbine/generator capacity (doubling by end-2028) targets paybacks "below two years" and reuses existing manufacturing footprint rather than greenfield — a disciplined, phased bet rather than a land-grab, even as demand signals are described as historically strong.

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro downgraded, energy-security upgraded.** World Bank's 2026 global growth estimate moved to 2.5% following the Middle East conflict, but Simonelli frames this as reinforcing rather than undermining demand: "these events have further elevated energy security as a strategic priority for governments, customers, and economies globally."
- No direct consumer-facing exposure — BKR sells to energy/industrial operators and hyperscalers, not end consumers, so this key is omitted from frontmatter.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **IET is the growth engine.** Orders doubled YoY to a record $7.1B; EBITDA rose 16% YoY to $678M with margin up 280bp to 20.6%, "driven by favorable backlog pricing and ongoing execution of the Baker Hughes business system."
- **OFSE outperformed despite the war.** Revenue of $3.45B beat guidance (+7% sequentially); non-Middle East international OFS revenue grew double digits sequentially. But margin pressure showed up specifically from Middle East logistics costs and broader inflation.
- **GTS backlog catch-up is fading.** Growth "led by GTS as we worked through the overdue aero-derivative backlog," but management explicitly warned this catch-up contribution "levels off in the second half" — a known step-down analysts should model, not a surprise miss later.
- **Divestitures are a deliberate drag on comps.** PSI divestiture and SPC joint-venture formation created a 2% aggregate revenue headwind for IET and pressured EPS comparisons — portfolio-shaping, not weakness.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Power Systems capacity bet, quantified.** New capacity online by 2029 implies "nearly $5 billion in annual power systems revenue opportunity at full utilization," a 3-4x increase over 2025's ~$1B — split roughly half gas turbines, a quarter Brush (generators), balance across turbo expanders/gearboxes/synchronous condensers. Capex is "moderate," phased 2026-2028, leveraging existing roofline and a make-buy supplier strategy rather than greenfield buildout.
- **Turbine delivery slots are sold out into 2027-2028.** Management noted 2027 delivery slots for Frame 5 turbines are still available but NovaLT capacity additions don't land until late 2028 — a real physical constraint on how fast bookings convert to revenue, independent of demand.
- **LNG scarcity reinforced, not resolved, by recent disruption.** "It will take time for LNG markets to fully normalize given the complexity of restoring liquefaction capacity, rebalancing trade flows, and rebuilding inventories" — yet the long-term nameplate capacity path (800 MTPA by 2030, ~950 MTPA by 2035) is unchanged.
- **Chart brings a second scarcity axis: cryogenic/thermal management** for data-center cooling, which management frames as the natural complement to BKR's power-generation constraint story.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Power generation is "in the early stages of a multi-year growth cycle" tied to AI capex durability. **Action:** doubling gas turbine capacity by 2028, raising Horizon Two IET orders target past $45B, and closing Chart to add thermal/cooling capability — money and integration headcount (18 workstreams, 300 identified synergy initiatives) are actually behind this belief, not just rhetoric.
- **Gap flagged explicitly by management itself:** despite record H1 orders, they held full-year IET revenue guidance roughly flat — a deliberate refusal to let bookings momentum imply near-term revenue acceleration, because GTE cycle times push conversion past 2027. This is a rare instance of management under-translating good news into guidance, worth noting as credibility-building rather than sandbagging.
- **Belief on Middle East:** conditions "remain fluid." **Action:** guidance simply assumes current disruption levels persist unchanged through year-end — a static assumption, not a stated mitigation plan, which is itself a hedge (see below).

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No Chart segment guidance yet:** "Given the recent close, we are not providing Chart segment guidance today. We will provide updated Baker Hughes and Chart guidance ahead of the third quarter earnings call" — a full quarter of opacity on the newly closed, second-largest acquisition in company history.
- **No 2027 formal guidance**, despite repeated analyst probing on margin/backlog visibility — Ahmed Moghal: "it's still early and we're not providing formal guidance," offering only qualitative confidence on pricing/mix.
- **Middle East assumption is a flat-line, not a forecast:** guidance explicitly "assumes current activity levels in the Middle East remain broadly unchanged through year-end" — management is not predicting resolution or further deterioration, just declining to bet either way.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Questions clustered around three anxieties: (1) how real and how fast is the Power Systems capacity payoff (mix, pricing basis, capex discipline), (2) what's actually monetizable near-term from Chart versus longer-dated "underappreciated" synergies (space, geothermal, mining), and (3) why guidance raises look muted relative to the scale of the H1 orders and OFSE beat.
- On the last point, one analyst directly noted the full-year guide rose "by less than the first half beat," pushing management to explain the GTE conversion lag and flat free-cash-flow conversion rate despite a strong H1 — a polite but pointed challenge to whether the order boom is being under-communicated to the P&L on purpose.
- Compressed worry: *is Baker Hughes' record backlog a genuine multi-year earnings ramp, or a bookings sugar high that management is already quietly discounting in its own guidance?*
