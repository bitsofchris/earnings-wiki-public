# TTD — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-06)

**The key idea:** TTD posted solid growth (18%, 22% ex-political) but spent the call re-litigating its own narrative after a rocky 2025 — a full C-suite rebuild (new COO, CFO, CRO), a defensive lecture on why Amazon and Google "aren't really competitors," and a Q4 guide that decelerates despite an easier year-ago comp. The pitch is that the open internet is entering a structural buyer's market that only an "objective," non-conflicted DSP can fully monetize — but the tell is how much energy went into explaining away deceleration and defining competitors out of existence rather than just showing the numbers.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A full leadership reset just happened, mid-flight.** New COO (March), new CFO (this quarter), new CRO (literally started the week of the call) — CEO frames it as "foundational improvement," but three C-suite seats turning over in eight months, replacing execs who'd each been there "10 years or more," reads as a company that recognized its go-to-market and operating discipline had broken down.
2. **The Amazon/Google non-competitor argument is doing a lot of work.** Jeff Green spent a multi-minute answer arguing Amazon's DSP is trivial ("low single digits" of its ad business) and Google will "back away from the open internet" post-antitrust — a comforting story for TTD's moat, but it's built on "back of the napkin," "in my view," and "our market triangulation," not disclosed data.
3. **Kokai's performance numbers are real but the guide doesn't reflect acceleration.** Kokai adoption is at 85% and delivering 26% better CPA, but Q4 guide of ~18.5% ex-political growth is a *deceleration* from Q3's 22% ex-political — on a comp that's 7 points easier. Management didn't offer a crisp explanation beyond "trends we've observed."
4. **CPMs falling ~43% is framed as a feature, not a symptom.** Attributed to "IC-level accountability" and account-planning rigor rather than pricing pressure — plausible, but a number that big deserves more scrutiny than a passing mention.
5. **International and JBPs are the actual growth engines.** International (13% of revenue) is outgrowing North America, and JBPs (now ~half the business) are outgrowing non-JBP accounts — this is the real story under the macro rhetoric, not the open-internet philosophy.

## Economy & consumer
- **"Tale of 2 cities":** CPG and parts of retail are "still feeling pressure from factors like tariffs and inflation," clinging to "cheap reach of user-generated content" and even "cheap reach of linear TV," while financial services, health care, insurance, and auto lean into data-driven marketing and are winning share of TTD's business.
- **CMOs are under CFO pressure to justify walled-garden metrics:** "Walled gardens give easy answers that don't satisfy CMOs nearly as easily as they once did" — a genuine signal of budget scrutiny tightening, not just competitive spin.
- **Consumer attention/budget gap:** consumers spend "2/3 of their digital time on the open Internet," but "most budgets today still go through Facebook, Google and TikTok" — management bets this closes but offers no timeline.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Growth held up:** 18% YoY revenue (22% ex-political), adjusted EBITDA ~43% of revenue — solid, not spectacular, and decelerating into Q4 guide.
- **CTV/video is the engine:** ~50% of mix, "consistently growing at a faster rate than the overall business."
- **JBPs are compounding:** ~180 live joint business plans last quarter, another 80 in pipeline "worth billions," now roughly half of total business — growing "significantly faster than non-JBP accounts."
- **What's not working:** an admitted history of "too many people servicing the exact same accounts" and underinvestment "in brand relationships" — explicit acknowledgment that go-to-market execution was a real weakness, not hypothetical.
- **DSOs ticked up** (92 days, +3 YoY) alongside DPOs (+3 YoY) — working capital drifting slightly worse, not flagged as a concern by management.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Product investment concentrated in supply-chain infrastructure:** OpenPath (grown "many hundreds of percentage points"), OpenAds (just launched, already 20+ publisher commitments), Pubdesk (built on the acquired Sincera data), Deal Desk (deals "performing about 35% better" than legacy).
- **Capital return kept pace:** $310M in buybacks this quarter, ~$2B cumulative since 2023, new $500M authorization approved in October — no debt on the balance sheet, $1.4B cash.
- **Scarcity is trust/objectivity, not resources:** the entire competitive pitch rests on being the only non-conflicted large-scale buy-side platform — an intangible moat argument rather than a hard constraint like compute or headcount.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** "decision CTV will become the default buying model in the years ahead" and biddable/decisioned buying displaces programmatic guaranteed and IO models.
- **Action:** shipped OpenPath, OpenAds, Pubdesk, Deal Desk this year specifically to industrialize that transition — genuine follow-through, not just rhetoric.
- **Belief:** Google will "back away from the open internet" after the antitrust ruling closing arguments (Nov 17).
- **Action gap:** no concrete TTD commitment tied to this belief beyond continuing current product roadmap — it's presented as a tailwind TTD will passively benefit from, not something they're building specifically to capture.
- **Belief:** AI-driven search fragmentation will create "search-like inventory" opportunities resembling CTV's fragmented, competitive structure.
- **Action gap:** purely speculative — "I anticipate," no product or resourcing commitment cited for this specific opportunity yet.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No quantified 2026 guidance:** repeatedly deflected to qualitative framing ("tale of 2 cities") rather than segment- or growth-rate-specific 2026 commitments.
- **Avoided direct competitive engagement with Amazon/Google's DSP ambitions:** defined both out of the "real DSP" category by asserting their ad revenue is almost entirely owned-and-operated — a framing choice, not a data disclosure.
- **CFO's Q4 deceleration explanation stayed vague:** "grounded in the trends we've observed so far in October and November" — no specific vertical, geography, or account-level driver named for the growth-rate step-down.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: **(1) competitive threat from Amazon/Google DSPs racing to zero-fee pricing**, **(2) why Q4 growth guidance decelerates despite an easier political comp**, and **(3) agentic AI/search's effect on publisher ad inventory supply**.
- The Q4 deceleration question was asked pointedly (explicitly noting the "7-point easier comp") and got a qualitative, trend-based answer rather than a specific driver — the closest thing to a dodge on the call.

- **Compressed worry:** is TTD's growth genuinely re-accelerating on the back of a real leadership and product upgrade, or is the C-suite turnover and Q4 deceleration a sign that 2025's "tale of 2 cities" macro softness is starting to bite even TTD's most defensible categories?
