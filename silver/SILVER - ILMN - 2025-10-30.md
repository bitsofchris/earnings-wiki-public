# ILMN — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** Illumina just cleared the hardest part of its NovaSeq X transition — clinical consumables now growing double-digit even as pricing resets — and used that momentum to raise full-year guidance. But management would not extend that confidence into a 2026 number, hedging on three unresolved swing factors simultaneously: China export policy, NIH grant predictability, and residual tariff drag on gross margin.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The X transition inflection has arrived.** High-throughput volume on NovaSeq X jumped from 44% to 51% of revenue in a single quarter, and clinical consumables still grew double-digit ex-China through that shift — evidence the "volume offsets price" thesis is playing out, not just a promise. CFO Ankur Dhingra: "For this group of customers, volume offset price in year 1 and then revenue and volume both accelerated in year 2."
2. **China is a live regulatory risk being managed, not solved.** Illumina won approval to let OEM partners locally manufacture instruments for China, but CEO Jacob Thaysen was blunt: "we have not yet reached a long-term resolution related to our operations in China." Guidance is being built with zero 2026 China contribution assumed.
3. **Research funding is "flowing but not spending."** NIH grants are being awarded again, but Thaysen noted no catch-up spending has materialized yet — researchers need multi-quarter *predictability*, not a single grant, before they resume normal cadence. This decouples "funding restored" headlines from actual demand recovery.
4. **Cost discipline is real and still has runway.** Non-GAAP opex fell ~6% YoY (R&D dipped below $230M for the first time since 2021) while op margin expanded 190bps despite flat revenue — and both executives flagged more margin levers still unrealized (manufacturing footprint, Singapore/India centers of excellence, tariff mitigation).
5. **Illumina is building a second growth vector beyond core sequencing.** The new BioInsight unit (population sequencing, data partnerships, AI-enabled services) and the SomaLogic-driven multiomics push (Protein Prep, 5-base methylation) are explicit bets that revenue growth beyond 2027 needs to come from data/software, not just gigabases.

## Economy & consumer
- **No end-consumer exposure** — Illumina sells to clinical labs, biopharma, and academic/government researchers, so macro reads through funding and healthcare capital cycles rather than consumer spending.
- **Ex-China growth resumed** at +2% YoY, described as "an important milestone" by the CFO, after several quarters of decline — but 2026 planning assumes the environment looks like "second half of 2025," not a rebound.
- **Government funding is the swing macro variable**: CEO says NIH grants are flowing again but stresses budget approval (or at least a CR) and *regular* grant flow are still-needed checkpoints before research demand normalizes.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: clinical.** Double-digit consumables growth ex-China, driven by "broader adoption of comprehensive genomic profiling and growing use of sequencing-intensive applications like MRD," plus 55+ NovaSeq X placements in-quarter, over half going to clinical customers.
- **Working: cost structure.** Opex down ~6% YoY, op margin up 190bps to 24.5%, both above guide — CFO attributes it to multiyear cost programs finally showing up in the P&L, with "several plays that are yet to reflect the full benefit," especially in gross margin.
- **Not working: research/applied.** Consumables down high-single-digits ex-China; CEO calls the environment "stabilizing, but still soft."
- **Not working: China instruments and services.** China instrument revenue down ~54% YoY on export restrictions; sequencing service/other revenue also fell ~3% YoY on "timing of certain strategic partnership revenues" — a soft explanation for a miss versus expectations.
- **Margin drag: tariffs.** Gross margin fell 130bps YoY, with ~220bps of that from tariffs alone (meaning underlying gross margin was actually up ~90bps) — a metric-unpacking moment worth flagging since the headline number understates core margin health.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Multiomics buildout**: SomaLogic acquisition (via Standard BioTools) still expected to close 1H26; Illumina Protein Prep (up to 9,500 proteins/sample) launched in Q3; a new "5-base" solution combining variant and methylation reads launched at ASHG — all aimed at extending beyond core sequencing into adjacent biological insight layers.
- **Binding constraint #1: China regulatory access.** Only a "measured step forward" (local OEM manufacturing approval) achieved; full resolution still pending and treated as unpredictable enough to exclude entirely from 2026 planning.
- **Binding constraint #2: research funding predictability**, not grant dollars themselves — researchers won't resume normal spend cadence until they can count on multiple future grants, not just the one in hand.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** clinical will keep driving growth as X volume outpaces conversion pricing headwinds, and research will "gradually" recover as pricing headwinds ease now that 91% of research volume has already moved to X.
- **Doing:** raised full-year 2025 guidance across revenue, margin, and EPS; held NovaSeq X placement pace at 50-60/quarter with no signal of pulling back; kept opex cuts running while still funding multiomics launches and the new BioInsight unit.
- **Gap:** management is confident enough to raise guidance and commit capital to multiomics/BioInsight now, but explicitly refuses to commit to any 2026 number — even the previously-stated "500bps margin expansion" and "double-digit EPS growth" targets were reaffirmed only in vague directional terms ("we believe we can continue to do so"), not reconfirmed as hard commitments.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **2026 guidance categorically declined**, repeated across nearly every question: "it will be too early for us to go in and give you a view on '26."
- **China's 2026 contribution excluded entirely** from planning frameworks: "I wouldn't take China into the consideration at this point of time... That's too early to see where this is going."
- **NovaSeq X placement cadence for 2026** not committed to specifics despite analyst prompting for a number: "too early at this point to actually give you specifics on exactly how we think about that range."
- **Instrument revenue step-up in Q4 explicitly downplayed as budget flush**: CFO clarified the forecast doesn't assume repeating last year's 91-placement Q4 spike.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Dominant theme: reverse-engineering 2026 guidance** from partial disclosures — multiple analysts built explicit bottom-up models (clinical growth rate + research trend + China headwind) and asked management to confirm or correct the math; management consistently declined to validate the frameworks numerically while agreeing directionally.
- **Competitive threat (Roche) probed directly** and dismissed by the CEO as one-dimensional competition against Illumina's multidimensional value prop — worth noting as a claim un-stress-tested by data on this call.
- **Margin durability questioned** given a prior "500bps expansion" target set at a much lower margin base — CFO reaffirmed the goal but declined to specify pacing.
- **NIH/funding mechanics scrutinized closely** — analysts wanted to know if the Q3 beat included any pull-forward or catch-up NIH spending; management said no, framing the recovery as still ahead rather than already banked.
- **Compressed worry:** can Illumina keep beating and raising once the three unresolved 2026 variables — China resolution, tariff drag, and NIH funding predictability — stop being tailwinds-by-absence and start actually showing up in the numbers?
