# PG — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-01-22)

**The key idea:** P&G's flat quarter was engineered to look worse than the underlying business by a one-time US comp (port-strike pantry loading a year ago), and management is using that cover to launch a much bigger story: a "reinvention" built on the same superiority playbook that already worked outside the US, now layered with AI-enabled data platforms and a multiyear restructuring. The tension is that the near-term acceleration case leans heavily on comp math and unproven-in-the-US interventions, while the long-term "CPG company of the future" story is admittedly 12-18 months from being "evenly distributed" — a lot of confidence being asked for on a flat quarter with declining market share.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The quarter was a controlled demolition, not real weakness.** Organic sales were flat only because Family Care (-10%), Baby, and Fem Care lapped hurricane/port-strike pantry loading; ex-Family Care, organic sales grew 1%, and everywhere outside the US grew or accelerated (Latin America +8%, Europe +3%, China +3%, AMEA +2%).
2. **The US is structurally the problem, and management admits it started interventions there late.** Schulten: "The US slowdown was really a little bit delayed versus the balance of the markets... That same playbook is being executed in The US" — meaning the fixes that already worked in China, Mexico and Brazil are only now rolling out domestically, with global share down 20bps and 25 of 50 top category-country combos holding/gaining share.
3. **"Reinvention" is mostly about connecting data assets P&G already built, not new spend.** Schulten was explicit that "the heavy investment is already done" on the data lake, ERP, and AI platforms — the ask now is organizational integration, not incremental capital, which is a notably capital-light framing of a "next important phase of constructive disruption."
4. **Value strategy is proposition strengthening, not price cuts.** Both executives repeatedly ruled out promotional depth ("not market constructive") in favor of performance upgrades at the same price point (Tide Liquid relaunch called "the biggest laundry liquid upgrade in twenty years") — a bet that product superiority converts to volume before competitors erode share further.
5. **Grooming is the one segment genuinely deteriorating**, organic sales flat with volume inflecting negative and margins down ~300bps — attributed to initiative timing/phasing rather than demand, but it's the most concrete miss on the call.

## Economy & consumer
- **Category growth has settled near 2% value globally**, with the US read at roughly 1-2% including some inventory noise per the O&D data — "you could say the US structurally could be growing at two to three points. But we have to see where that goes" (Schulten), a hedge dressed as optimism.
- **Household usage volume is flat**, not growing: "Usage volume growth is slow, to honestly flat if you look at the front half of the year" in both the US and Europe — a direct signal that past growth was price/mix-driven and the consumer isn't absorbing more product.
- **Inflation and value-consciousness are reshaping purchase behavior** across food, energy, and healthcare per Jejurikar's framing, but P&G frames this as an execution opportunity ("strengthen propositions") rather than a demand risk requiring price cuts.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: China baby care and Mexico fabric care**, both cited as proof-of-concept for the innovation playbook — Pampers Prestige (silk-based) drove Greater China Baby Care to sustained double-digit growth and ~3pts of share over 18 months; Downy Intense drove double-digit growth and 2pts of value share in Mexico.
- **Working: enterprise markets broadly** — Latin America +8%, Europe enterprise +6%, funding itself without margin dilution because "the margin structure... allows consistent investment... it's not dilutive" (Schulten).
- **Not working: US share and Grooming.** US organic sales -2% (volume -3pts, ~2pts of which is base-period noise); Grooming organic sales flat with margin down ~300bps on volume deleverage, tied to Braun/female-grooming initiative timing.
- **Gross/operating margin compressed** (gross -50bps, operating -70bps, currency-neutral operating -80bps) despite strong 270bps of productivity — the company chose to reinvest essentially all of the productivity gain into innovation and demand creation rather than let it flow to the bottom line.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex is rising, but for capacity with built-in automation/digitization**, not a step-change in cost structure — Schulten was clear this is "building capacity in a different way, but not fundamentally more expensive."
- **The real constraint is organizational, not financial.** Jejurikar: platforms exist (petabyte-scale data lake, AI capabilities, autonomous supply chain tools) but "the future is here. It's just a little uneven" — the bottleneck is integrating tools the company already built, plus a stated need for "internal cultural change" that "is not a change that you can just do overnight."
- **Restructuring (announced June, two-year program) is the vehicle for freeing capacity** — management expects it to fund the transformation without a second wave of restructuring, and expects future growth to come from higher sales-per-head rather than added headcount.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management expects the US to reaccelerate in H2 purely from lapping base-period comps plus rolling out already-proven interventions (Tide Boost, Olay relaunch, Tide Evo later in H2).
- **Action:** concrete moves back this up — full US distribution of Tide Boost as of December, new Olay campaign/architecture, second wave of Baby Care innovation, restructuring execution — so this is one of the more credible say/do alignments on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused back-half margin guidance outright**: "At the risk of disappointing you I will not give you margin guidance for the back half... margin will be an outcome" (Schulten) — margin is explicitly subordinated to whatever top-line investment they judge necessary.
- **Would not commit to a full return-to-algorithm timeline**, deferring to "the next two quarters" before saying anything about fiscal '27.
- **Declined to quantify the "reinvention" investment size**, insisting the heavy lift is already sunk into existing platforms — convenient for guidance optics but not independently verifiable from the call.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Multiple analysts pushed on how much of "acceleration" is real vs. comp math** — the most persistent skepticism of the call, with Schulten repeatedly pointing to ex-US growth as the "real" evidence while acknowledging US share still needs to be recovered, not just comp-assisted.
- **Amazon/e-commerce concentration and its threat from small/DTC brands** drew a direct question on media efficiency; management's answer leaned on "stronger core, bigger more" content and premium-price innovation rather than a defensive playbook against smaller competitors.
- **Promotional intensity data (up 200-300bps) directly contradicted management's stated preference for value-over-discounting** — Schulten's explanation (gift cards/layered couponing distort the read) reframed the data rather than disputing the underlying trend.
- **Grooming's deceleration and margin contraction** got a pointed follow-up; the answer attributed it to initiative phasing, not demand weakness — plausible but unverifiable within the call.
