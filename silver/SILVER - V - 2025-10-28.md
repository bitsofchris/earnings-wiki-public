# V — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2025-10-28)

**The key idea:** Visa closed a strong fiscal 2025 (net revenue +11%, EPS +14%) and used the call to reframe itself as a payments "hyperscaler" — a four-layer stack (foundation/services/solutions/access) that other players build on. The real tension: management is pouring investment into three unproven frontiers — agentic commerce, stablecoins, and a rebuilt VisaNet — while explicitly declining to say how big or how soon any of them pay off, all while insisting the core consumer-spend business remains untouched by the "choppiness" competitors have flagged.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Visa is pre-positioning for disintermediation risk, not just growth.** The entire "Visa as a Service stack" framing, the Trusted Agent Protocol launched two weeks before the call, and the stablecoin buildout are defensive as much as offensive — Visa wants to be the standards-setter before someone else (Stripe was named directly by an analyst) locks in agentic checkout rails.
3. **Management pushed back hard on the "choppy consumer" narrative.** Asked directly about peers citing trade-down behavior, Suh cited consistent growth across all spend bands, with the top band growing fastest — a rebuttal delivered with data, not just reassurance language.
4. **Every agentic-commerce answer stayed at the standards/framework level, never volume or revenue.** Three separate questions pressed for a timeline or TAM estimate on agentic transactions; Ryan McInerney answered each with "early days" and process description (discovery → integrated buy → autonomous purchase) rather than any number.
5. **Stablecoins have flipped from perceived threat to stated opportunity.** Stablecoin-linked card spend "quadrupled versus a year ago" and Visa is now minting/burning on banks' behalf via its Tokenized Asset Platform — a notable reversal of the market's original disintermediation fear.

## Economy & consumer
- **Spend-band resilience is the load-bearing data point.** "Growth across our spend bands has remained quite consistent all year... with higher spending cardholders driving more of the growth" — used explicitly to counter competitor claims of discretionary trade-down.
- **Cross-border and travel both above pre-COVID trend**, with e-commerce now ~40% of cross-border volume (vs. ~1/3 pre-COVID) — the mix shift itself is inflating headline cross-border growth.
- **Latin America decelerated on Argentina's disinflation**, a genuine regional soft spot rather than the U.S. consumer weakening.
- **Asia Pacific acceleration (~2.5pts) attributed partly to timing effects**, not a clean structural improvement — management flagged this itself rather than overselling it.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Data processing yield jumped to 17% growth vs. 10% transaction growth**, driven by new FY25 pricing and richer cross-border mix — a real yield expansion, not just volume.
- **Commercial and money-movement (CMS) revenue +14%,** helped by lapping a prior-year one-time adjustment — worth noting the comp benefit rather than treating it as pure organic acceleration.
- **Opex grew 13% in Q4, above plan**, though management attributed the miss largely to FX and an EPS-neutral deferred-comp mark-to-market — a technical rather than operational overshoot.
- **Tax rate benefit from one-time items is fading:** guided to 18.5–19% for FY26, up from below-18% actuals in FY24/25 "primarily due to the absence of onetime benefits" — a real headwind to EPS growth optics next year.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Stablecoin infrastructure spend spans issuance, settlement, and cross-border money movement** simultaneously — Visa is hedging across multiple stablecoin use cases rather than betting on one.
- **The stated constraint is ecosystem collaboration, not capital or compute** — repeatedly, McInerney framed agentic commerce progress as gated by getting merchants, platforms, and other networks to converge on shared standards, not by Visa's own resources.
- **FIFA World Cup 2026 and Milano Cortina Olympics are treated as investment events**, not just marketing — opex cadence is explicitly guided higher in Q2/Q3 FY26 for these.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** agentic commerce is a "considerable opportunity," likely additive to (not substitutive of) e-commerce TAM. **Action:** shipped Trusted Agent Protocol, merchant agent toolkit, and MCP server access — real releases, but volume through them is unquantified, so the belief is running ahead of measurable proof.
- **Belief:** stablecoins are now emerging-market and cross-border opportunities rather than threats. **Action:** concrete deployment — Tokenized Asset Platform minting/burning, Visa Direct prefunding pilot, 130+ stablecoin card programs — this is one of the few areas where action volume (quadrupled spend, $2.5B annualized settlement run-rate) roughly matches the confidence of the belief.
- **Belief:** the consumer is resilient and macro is stable. **Action:** guidance assumes "no material change" from Q4 levels — a static assumption, explicitly not a forecast ("we are not economic forecasters"), meaning the guide is a bet on the status quo persisting, not a read on where things are headed.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No margin target, ever:** "we don't manage our company to a margin target, at least not in the classical sense" — opex/revenue will move together by design, denying analysts a lever to model incremental margin.
- **No agentic commerce volume or revenue timeline** despite three direct asks — answered with maturity-curve narrative (discovery → integrated buy → autonomous) instead of numbers.
- **No specific answer on whether agentic commerce cannibalizes or expands e-commerce** beyond "base case is acceleration" with a hand-waved "upside case."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Agentic commerce dominated the Q&A** — TAM impact, timing to material volume, and direct competitive comparison to Stripe's Agentic Commerce Protocol — reflecting real investor anxiety that Visa's rails could be bypassed by a new checkout layer.
- **Stablecoins and RPO/VAS growth composition** were the second cluster, probing whether the "value-in-kind" incentive mechanics inflating RPO are sustainable or an accounting artifact.
- **Opex-versus-revenue growth parity** drew a pointed question about whether flat incremental margins reflect opportunism or a structural cost of running an increasingly layered stack — management didn't fully resolve which.
- **Compressed worry:** is Visa's stack narrative a genuine moat against agentic/stablecoin disintermediation, or a well-funded defensive story that can't yet point to material dollars?
