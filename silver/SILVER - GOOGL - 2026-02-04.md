# GOOGL — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-04)

**The key idea:** Alphabet posted an unambiguously strong quarter — Search and Cloud both accelerated, Cloud backlog more than doubled year-over-year to $240B — and used that strength to justify nearly doubling 2026 CapEx to $175-185B. The tension is that management describes itself as supply-constrained even after $91B of 2025 spend, meaning the entire bull case now rests on turning a bigger capital commitment into capacity fast enough to keep pace with demand it says it can't currently fill.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Cloud is the standout, and the margin story is real, not just top-line.** Cloud revenue grew 48% to $17.7B with operating margin more than doubling from 17.5% to 30.1%, and backlog jumped 55% sequentially to $240B — driven by "the number of deals in 2025 over $1 billion surpassed the previous three years combined" (Sundar Pichai). This isn't just AI hype pricing; existing customers are "outpacing their initial commitments by over 30%."

2. **The CapEx guide is the story, and it comes with an explicit admission of being under-supplied.** 2026 CapEx of $175-185B is roughly double actual 2025 CapEx ($91.4B). Pichai was direct: "we've been supply constrained even as we've been ramping up our capacity," and expects to "go through the year in a supply constrained way" because today's capacity reflects investment decisions made years ago, not this year's spend.

3. **Efficiency gains are being used to fund the flywheel, not to slow spend.** Gemini serving unit costs fell 78% over 2025 and ~50% of Google's code is now agent-written (reviewed by engineers). Anat Ashkenazi was explicit that this isn't cost discipline for its own sake — freed capital gets reinvested: "the more capital we can free up within the organization to invest the better we can turn this flywheel."

4. **YouTube ads growth (9%) is the one soft spot, explained away as a mix shift.** Philip Schindler attributes underperformance to lapping US election ad spend and users migrating from ad-supported to YouTube Premium/Music subscriptions, calling the shift "a slightly negative impact on YouTube ads revenues, but a positive impact on our business." Plausible, but it's the only metric on the call not accompanied by an acceleration story.

5. **Gemini app growth is genuinely startling and management insists there's no search cannibalization.** MAUs jumped from 650M to 750M in a single quarter, engagement per user rose sharply post-Gemini 3, and Pichai says usage "creates an expansionary moment" rather than shifting share from Search — an assertion not yet tested by hard usage-overlap data.

## Economy & consumer

- **No macro softness anywhere on the call.** Search accelerated to 17% growth "led by broad strength across all major verticals, with retail particularly strong" (Anat Ashkenazi); retail, finance, and health specifically cited as the biggest contributors to the Q4 acceleration.
- **Consumer behavior is shifting toward conversational, longer-session AI usage.** "Queries in AI mode are three times longer than traditional searches," daily AI mode queries per US user have doubled since launch, and "nearly one in six AI mode queries are now non-text" (Pichai).
- **Subscription economy scaling fast.** 325M+ paid subscriptions across Google One/YouTube Premium, YouTube TV adding sports-tier and genre packages, NFL Sunday Ticket hit its "highest paid subscriber number ever."

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Search and Cloud both re-accelerated simultaneously** — Search +17%, Cloud +48% — a rare combination that Pichai frames as validation of the "full stack" AI thesis (infrastructure → models → products).
- **Cloud's product mix is diversifying, not just riding infrastructure demand:** revenue from products built on Google's generative AI models "grew nearly 400% year over year," and AI-using Cloud customers "use 1.8 times as many products" than non-AI customers — a real cross-sell flywheel.
- **YouTube ads underperformed** at 9% vs. Search's 17%, with network advertising down 2%; management's explanation (election lapping + subscription substitution) is plausible but unverified against a genuine demand check.

## Investing & scarcity

- **CapEx guide nearly doubles**, from $91.4B actual (2025) to $175-185B (2026 guide), with "investments ramping over the course of the year" — a genuinely aggressive step function even against an already-elevated base.
- **The binding constraint is admitted openly: power, land, and supply chain, not capital or demand.** "Some of the time horizons are increasing in the supply chain" (Pichai) — meaning even with capital committed, physical build-out lag is the real gate.
- **Capital allocation shifting toward Cloud:** "just over half of our ML compute is expected to go towards the cloud business" in 2026, per Ashkenazi — a shift from prior years where DeepMind/internal model training likely took a larger share.
- **Depreciation is about to bite the P&L harder:** 2025 depreciation rose 38% to $21.1B, and management explicitly warns 2026 growth "will accelerate in Q1 and meaningfully increase for the full year" — a headwind to margins even as revenue accelerates.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** 2026 will be the year agentic commerce becomes real for consumers via the Universal Commerce Protocol. **Action:** the protocol shipped with named retail partners at NRF in January, and checkout-in-AI-mode/Gemini is coming "from select merchants" — a concrete, funded rollout, not just a roadmap slide.
- **Belief:** demand for AI compute will outstrip supply through 2026. **Action:** CapEx guide nearly doubled — real money behind the belief, though management is candid that supply gaps this year are a function of investment decisions made in *prior* years, meaning the 2026 spend won't fully close 2026's gap.
- **Belief:** efficiency gains can fund reinvestment without new external capital. **Action:** coding agents now write ~50% of code, treasury and invoice-reconciliation agents are live internally — a real, quantified internal AI deployment, not just a marketing claim.
- **Gap worth flagging:** management is confident Gemini enterprise adoption strengthens SaaS partners' pricing power rather than undermining it, but offered no data to counter the "seat cannibalization" thesis analysts raised — just an assertion that "successful companies" are "incorporating Gemini deeply in critical workflows."

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No timeline or specifics on Gemini app ads monetization.** Philip Schindler: "we're not rushing anything here" — despite the app hitting 750M MAU and being framed elsewhere on the call as a major growth vector.
- **No detail on Apple/Siri partnership economics** when directly asked how revenue alignment works given the shift from link-based referral value to on-platform AI utility — the question specifically probed whether the old DOJ-disclosed revenue-share model still applies, and it went unanswered.
- **No granular CapEx-versus-return targets disclosed** beyond a qualitative "rigorous framework" description from Ashkenazi — no explicit ROIC or payback benchmark given despite a direct question about the investment/performance relationship.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **SaaS pricing power anxiety:** the market's "DeepSeek moment"-style fear reframed — are Gemini-native agents cannibalizing seat-based software economics? Management's answer was reassurance without data.
- **Supply-demand gap mechanics:** multiple questions probed how the CapEx step-up actually closes the compute gap and over what timeframe — Pichai's answer conceded the gap persists through 2026 regardless of this year's spend, given supply-chain lead times.
- **"What keeps you up at night" drew a direct, unhedged answer:** capacity constraints — power, land, supply chain — not competition, not model commoditization, not regulatory risk.
- **Compressed worry:** Alphabet is spending at a nearly unprecedented rate to catch up to demand it admits it can't fully satisfy this year either, and the market's real question is whether that gap ever closes or simply gets more expensive to chase.
