# FANG — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-04)

**The key idea:** Diamondback is explicitly refusing to be provoked into growth by peers who are reportedly loosening capital discipline, instead doubling down on a "yellow light" framework — hold production flat, keep reinvestment rates near record lows, and let free cash flow per share do the compounding. The unresolved tension is gas: the company is sitting on hundreds of millions of dollars a year in lost value from Waha exposure and is only slowly building egress, admitting the fix "over the next 12 months" isn't great even as the long-term pipeline and power deals look promising.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A third consecutive "yellow light" quarter, but with conviction, not paralysis.** CEO Kaes Van't Hof: "We spent a lot of time, I think more time than ever this year on the macro... it's a debate on the supply side." Rather than freezing, management is running a 36-37% reinvestment ratio at ~$63 oil and calling that "a very solid place to be."
2. **Slide 8 is the load-bearing artifact of the call.** Management repeatedly steers analysts to it as proof that Endeavor-merged co-development (drilling more wells per section AND improving per-well returns) is compounding, not just adding scale — "not only did we get bigger, but we got better."
3. **Gas monetization is the quiet multi-hundred-million-dollar problem.** ~70% of gas still sells into weak Waha pricing; management admits "$500 million a year is pretty meaningful for you for every $1 change in gas price, and you're kind of giving it away right now" — the fix (new pipelines, a power-plant supply deal) doesn't land meaningfully until 2026-2029.
4. **Efficiency gains are absorbing an external cost shock.** Well costs fell in 2025 despite steel tariffs adding roughly 20% to steel costs — credited to continuous pumping (targeting ~20% more lateral footage/day) and drilling consistency (1-in-10 wells now under 5 days, versus 1-2 per quarter previously).
5. **Capital priority stack is dividend, then buybacks (≥1% of public float/quarter), then debt paydown — M&A is explicitly secondary.** Diamondback sold $1.5B of non-E&P assets above its own trading multiple and says it has "exhausted the majority" of non-core sale opportunities, positioning the balance sheet for "whatever the next couple of quarters may hold."

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro read is supply-side, not demand-side.** "Overall, demand looks strong and supply is the hot debate now" — a notable reversal from earlier in the year when management feared a Liberation-Day-driven demand shock that "didn't end up happening."
- No consumer-facing exposure; omitted as not applicable to an upstream E&P.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: capital efficiency under cost pressure.** Free cash flow per share up 15% even as oil prices fell 14% year-over-year, attributed to a leaner cost structure and steady execution, not price tailwinds.
- **Working: development-style differentiation.** Co-developing all zones in the Midland Basin (versus single-well optimization) is credited as the source of the ~20% PV-per-well uplift promised at the time of the Endeavor merger.
- **Not working / structurally lagging: gas realizations.** Legacy midstream dedications from acquired acreage have locked much of Diamondback's gas into Waha pricing, and management concedes the drag persists through most of 2026 despite hedges.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx normalizing to a maintenance band, not a growth budget.** Q4 CapEx of ~$925M/quarter is framed as the "bogey" for holding ~505-510 mbo/d flat into 2026, down from an original $4B 2025 budget cut by $500M after Liberation Day.
- **Binding constraint is takeaway capacity, not rock or capital.** New pipeline capacity (Blackcomb, Hugh Brinson via Energy Transfer/WTG) and a first power-generation gas commitment (Competitive Power Ventures, 50 MMcf/d, online 2029) are the levers to cut Waha exposure from ~70% to ~40% by year-end 2026.
- **Emerging capital allocation shift: the PDP tail.** Management is testing acidization, oxidation, and downhole stimulation chemistries on older wells, explicitly framed as a way to eventually lower the reinvestment rate by shifting dollars from drilling to workovers — "never underestimate the American engineer."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: a "green light" (~$70-80 oil) is achievable but not imminent** ("maybe it takes until '27"), and when it comes, capital deployed then will earn a higher return off a shrunk balance sheet and share count.
- **Action taken now doesn't presuppose that outcome:** the company is holding production flat, not staging incremental capacity, and is explicit that "adding crude to a market that is clearly oversupplied... is not a prudent decision today" — a real gap between long-run optimism and near-term restraint.
- **Belief: gas realizations should improve materially by 2026.** Action: locked in specific pipeline capacity and one power off-take, but management admits realizations stay weak through most of the next 12 months regardless — "it's not great over the next 12 months. We protected that with hedges... but that time is coming."

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to hard-guide 2026 CapEx**, instead offering a range ($875M-$975M/quarter) explicitly caveated by unknown steel prices and efficiency gains: "we have no visibility into today."
- **Declined to commit to monetizing the 30%-owned Deep Blue water JV**, calling it a business "creating a lot of value in the shadows" that "at some point... will monetize or look different," without a timeline.
- **Declined to size or commit to big M&A**, saying only that Diamondback will be "more selective" and noting competitors have done deals "without our name on it."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Traffic-light framework dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pushed on where the red/yellow/green thresholds actually sit; management gave a specific red-light trigger (consecutive months near $50 oil) but kept green-light timing vague ("maybe it takes until '27").
- **Slide 8 durability was interrogated repeatedly** — is the productivity edge geology, acreage quality, or genuine development-style skill, and does it survive as Endeavor acreage and lower-tier zones get folded in.
- **Gas realization and Waha exposure got sustained, skeptical follow-up**, with one analyst noting the mismatch bluntly ("you're kind of giving it away right now"), pushing management past its high-level pipeline slide toward a real accountability moment.
- **Compressed worry:** are Diamondback's "best-in-class" reinvestment metrics durable capital discipline, or a story that only holds together as long as oil stays exactly where it is and peers don't force its hand by growing anyway?
