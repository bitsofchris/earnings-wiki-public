# CSX — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-22)

**The key idea:** CSX got more demand than it could smoothly handle — volume up 6%, revenue hit a new quarterly record — and the story is whether a new CEO's productivity/pricing discipline can convert a truck-market tightening tailwind into durable margin gains without the service network cracking under the strain. Management is explicit that market share for its own sake is not the goal; profitable, returns-driven growth is.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Pricing as a built muscle, not a lever pulled.** New CEO Steve Angel described a deliberate build-out of "price management capability," calling out AI-assisted pricing tools explicitly: **"This is an area where AI can really do some good with good price analytical tools."** Pricing decisions are framed as "surgical," market-by-market, based on customer value and next-best-alternative — not a blanket percentage.
- **Intermodal conversion is the growth engine**, riding the Howard Street Tunnel capacity unlock and the CPKC SMX partnership — both **"building upon itself,"** with week-over-week growth cited in both. Intermodal revenue was up 26% on 9% higher volume, more than double the growth rate of the rest of the business, with a tighter truck market (regulatory enforcement pulling capacity out) accelerating the value proposition.
- **Cost discipline reframed as an "outside-in" review**: management said they "looked outward first" — scrutinizing every dollar paid to contractors — before insourcing work back to CSX employees, with more of that pipeline still to come.
- **A full 2027 efficiency plan already underway mid-2026**, described as starting "probably a lot earlier than we normally would in any other year," with an explicit goal of "creating the muscle" — accountability and common goals pushed throughout the organization, not just top-down cost cuts.
- **Data center and power-infrastructure buildout showing up directly in freight**: management tied demand in domestic coal, frac sand, and heavy equipment to power infrastructure and data center construction — a concrete rail-side signal of the AI capex cycle spilling into physical industrial demand.
- **Full-year guidance raised**: revenue growth now guided to mid-to-high single digits, operating margin expansion raised to greater than 350 basis points, and free cash flow growth guided above 80% — capex held flat at under $2.4 billion, meaning the raise is coming from operating leverage, not incremental investment.

## What's changing
- **Truck-to-rail conversion is accelerating in real time**, most visibly in forest products, where supply (not demand) improved and conversions picked up "on tighter truck capacity and higher fuel costs" — a sharper inflection than seen in Q1.
- **Incremental margins are strong when isolated from fuel**: non-fuel expenses fell 2% even as volume grew 6%, and CFO Kevin Boone pushed back directly on a suggestion that incrementals were weak: **"our incremental margins were very strong when you ex-fuel."**
- **Domestic intermodal spot pricing and recent contract renewals are turning up first**, ahead of the broader same-store pricing acceleration management reaffirmed for the year — but international intermodal is explicitly called out as "not as highly coordinated to the truck market," being long-term contracted and concentrated.
- **A modest, targeted headcount increase in train & engine crews** is coming after CSX ran leaner into a demand surge that landed squarely in vacation-heavy summer months — a timing mismatch management characterizes as transient, not structural.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Service metrics degraded even as the network handled record volume.** Dwell increased and trip-plan performance missed target; COO Mike Cory conceded directly: **"Our service metrics aren't where we want them to be, particularly terminal dwell and trip plan performance."** The explanation offered — crew tightness from seasonal vacation timing colliding with a demand surge — is plausible but also exactly the kind of excuse a company gives when it doesn't want to admit understaffing.
- **The 350bps margin guidance still includes non-operating gains** (real estate/property sale gains, insurance recoveries) — an analyst had to explicitly ask whether the target "includes the gain on sale," and Boone confirmed it does, while downplaying the quarterly amount. PS&O tailwinds from these items are also flagged to fade in H2 (fewer property gains, higher locomotive overhaul costs).
- **Pricing questions were dodged repeatedly.** Asked multiple times for actual merchandise and intermodal pricing ranges or 2027 color, Maryclare Kenney declined each time ("we're not going to put out a number," "too early to get into 2027") — CEO Angel later joked she fielded "about 18 pricing questions" without materially answering any with a number.
- **Automotive and chemicals softness flagged for H2**: automotive demand "really hasn't improved" (North American light-vehicle production still guided down ~2% for the year) despite a stronger Q2, and chemicals/plastics volume is expected to moderate after pull-forward activity tied to Iran-conflict-driven inventory building — a geopolitical shock propping up a segment that's now expected to give it back.
- **No mention of autonomous/driverless trucking** as a competitive threat to the intermodal-conversion thesis, despite it being the most cited long-term risk to rail's truck-diversion pitch — a conspicuous absence given how central "tight truck capacity" is to the entire quarter's growth narrative.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Whether pricing gains are real or all fuel/mix illusion, with analysts pressing repeatedly for a specific merchandise or intermodal pricing number and getting only "same-store better than last year" · whether the softening service metrics (dwell, trip plan) are a genuine early-stage capacity constraint that CapEx (held flat) won't fix, or just a one-quarter blip from vacation timing · how much of the 350bps margin target is real operating leverage versus property gains and insurance recoveries that fade in H2 · whether the current unit growth pace (~6.5% quarter-to-date) is sustainable against flagged auto and chemicals deceleration · and beneath it all, whether this is a genuine structural share shift from truck to rail or a temporary window opened by regulatory enforcement and geopolitical pull-forward that closes once truck capacity normalizes.
