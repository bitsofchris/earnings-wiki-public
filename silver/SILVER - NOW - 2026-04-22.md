# NOW — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-22)

**The key idea:** ServiceNow beat every guided metric and unveiled a security/identity acquisition spree (Armis, Veza) atop an "AI native, no sidecar" repositioning — but the stock fell 12% after hours because investors couldn't tell how much of the beat was organic versus acquired, and the full-year guide barely moved once Armis and FX were stripped out. Management's answer was to dangle a bigger number ($1.5B vs. $1B AI revenue target) and tell everyone to wait two weeks for Financial Analyst Day.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A beat that didn't convince the market.** Subscription revenue grew 19% cc (above guide), cRPO 21% cc, margins beat by 50bps — yet shares fell 12% after hours because, as one analyst put it bluntly, "the full year number doesn't really move at all" excluding Armis and currency. CFO Gina Mastantuono had to walk through Veza/Pyramid contributions line by line to prove the quarter wasn't acquisition-inflated.
2. **AI monetization number moved up hard, mid-call, unscripted.** Bill McDermott dropped that the 2026 Now Assist AI revenue target — previously $1B — is now tracking to "$1.5 billion... on a run," a number he admitted was supposed to be saved for the Financial Analyst Day: "Gina will be mad at me because it's something we were hoping for FAD."
3. **Security become the second growth leg via M&A, not builds.** Armis (asset visibility) plus Veza (identity governance) plus ServiceNow's own CMDB are being bundled into a "unified end-to-end security stack" — McDermott calls Armis "our Instagram" and pitches a single-agent-breach cost of $5-10M as the urgency hook, while insisting these were bought for technology/talent, not revenue ("we didn't buy anything with a whole bunch of revenue").
4. **Middle East on-prem deal slippage is the one real, quantified headwind.** ~75bps of subscription revenue guidance impact from delayed sovereign-cloud on-prem deals tied to the Iran conflict — notable because on-prem deals there recognize as lump-sum, non-linear revenue, so timing slips hit disproportionately hard.

## Economy & consumer
- **Geopolitical volatility acknowledged but ring-fenced.** Management repeatedly frames global demand as strong outside of a narrow, quantified Middle East disruption, calling the broader environment merely "hectic" and "chaotic" rhetorically without operational impact elsewhere.
- **No consumer-facing exposure** — this is a pure enterprise-software call; "the consumer" is the enterprise buyer (CIO/CISO), and all demand commentary is B2B budget reallocation, not household spending.
- **Budget source is reallocation, not net-new spend expansion:** Gina notes AI budget "is coming from a lot of different places... labor budgets are coming down," and technology spend is shifting from point solutions into platform consolidation — a defensive rather than expansionary budget story.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: large-deal momentum and AI cross-sell.** 16 deals >$5M in net-new ACV, 5 deals >$10M, customers spending $1M+ grew over 130% YoY, deals with 3+ Now Assist products up ~70% YoY.
- **Working: Moveworks integration executed fast.** Folded into Employee Center Pro in "under three weeks," rebranded EmployeeWorks, and grew 5x YoY in its first quarter inside ServiceNow — "closed seven-figure deals in Q1... more deals than they did the entire year last year."
- **Not working / soft spot: on-prem/sovereign cloud timing risk.** The Middle East slippage exposes that a meaningful slice of revenue is lumpy, non-ratable on-prem recognition — a structural fragility the CFO downplayed as "not a bad thing" since "we want hosted."
- **Not working: messaging control.** Management had to spend the entire Q&A defending the *quality* of the beat rather than discussing new business — a sign the market doesn't trust headline growth numbers without acquisition-adjusted detail.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$2B accelerated buyback**, 20.2M shares repurchased — double all of 2025's repurchase volume — alongside three acquisitions closed inside one quarter (Armis, Veza, Pyramid), an aggressive capital-deployment posture on both the return-to-shareholders and M&A fronts simultaneously.
- **Scarcity is customer readiness, not ServiceNow capacity:** the constrained resource is enterprise organizational bandwidth to move from AI "experimentation" to production deployment — Bill McDermott: customers "hadn't yet deployed all the AI they have... their company wasn't ready for it yet."
- **Margin absorption of M&A treated as a KPI itself:** Armis's margin drag was guided down from 100bps to 50bps "thanks to Gina's efficiency," with an explicit commitment to zero impact by year-end — margin protection during acquisition integration is being marketed as proof of platform efficiency.

- **Gap:** management asserts confidently that acquisitions aren't "plugging a revenue gap," yet simultaneously needed to justify why full-year organic guidance was essentially flat — the rhetoric of pure strategic upside sits uneasily next to a guide that only moved because of Armis.
