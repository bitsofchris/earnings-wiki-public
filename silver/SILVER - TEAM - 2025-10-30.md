# TEAM — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-10-30)

**The key idea:** Atlassian set a hard end-of-life date (March 2029) for its on-prem Data Center product, converting a slow-burn cloud migration into a forced, multiyear deadline — and framed the resulting revenue-recognition mechanics (ratable cloud vs. upfront DC, lower marketplace take rates) as pure good news even though they mechanically shave 50bps off the non-migration organic growth guide. Underneath, the more interesting story is Atlassian's bet that AI-driven code generation expands rather than cannibalizes its business: customers using GitHub Copilot/Cursor are adding Jira seats 5% faster and running 20%+ more projects than those who aren't.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The EOL date is a demand-forcing mechanism, not a technical trigger.** CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes admitted "I don't think the playbook has necessarily changed" — what changed is that DC customers no longer have a "if" conversation, only a "when," now anchored to a fixed 2029 deadline that CFO Joe Binz expects to concentrate migrations in FY28-29.
2. **Guidance math obscures a real trade-off.** Full-year cloud revenue guidance rose 1.5pts to 22.5% purely on migration outperformance, while non-migration organic growth guidance was cut 50bps — because ratable cloud recognition and lower marketplace take rates on cloud apps both drag near-term reported revenue relative to DC. Binz maintained "the same conservative and risk-adjusted outlook" on every other driver, meaning the raise is 100% migration-timing, not broader momentum, despite Cannon-Brookes repeatedly calling the whole picture "incredibly positive."
3. **AI coding tools are read as a Jira tailwind, not a threat.** A customer cohort using third-party cogen tools (Copilot, Cursor, explicitly excluding Rovo Dev to avoid self-serving bias) expanded paid Jira seats 5% faster and ran 20%+ more projects than non-users — the clearest data point management has offered that AI-written code increases, rather than reduces, demand for work-tracking software.
4. **Two very different acquisitions signal where Atlassian thinks the AI interface layer is going.** The closed Browser Company deal bets that AI reshapes where knowledge workers do their work (an AI-native browser), while the pending DX deal bets that AI-driven code volume creates demand for developer-productivity measurement — both premised on the coding wave being a net expansion in enterprise software surface area.
5. **CFO transition adds execution risk during the migration push.** Joe Binz announced his departure (timing later, undetermined) right as the company leans hardest into the EOL-driven migration wave — a leadership discontinuity during the most consequential revenue-recognition transition in the company's history.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Cloud and RPO both accelerating:** cloud revenue grew 26% y/y to $998M and RPO grew 42% y/y to $3.3B, both cited as evidence the three strategic priorities (AI, enterprise, system of work) are "directly driving the results."
- **Migrated-seat volume doubled y/y** last quarter, which management attributes to five-plus years of investment in enterprise-grade cloud (FedRAMP Moderate, isolated cloud, multi-cloud) finally removing customers' technical objections.
- **Monetization mechanics are working against near-term optics:** cloud's ratable revenue recognition and lower marketplace take rate versus Data Center's upfront/higher-take model directly caused the 50bp cut to non-migration organic growth guidance — a real headwind, not just a comping artifact.
- **AI usage scaling fast internally:** 3.5M monthly active AI users, up 50% quarter-over-quarter; AI interactions up ~150% in six months; tokens processed tripled quarter-over-quarter — Rovo Dev also going GA this quarter, with over half of Atlassian's own internal security-incident findings now sourced from it.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Browser Company acquisition closed** this quarter — a bet that AI will shift the interface layer where knowledge workers operate, building an AI-native browser purpose-built for SaaS-heavy, enterprise-governed workflows that Cannon-Brookes says today's browsers (built pre-SaaS, pre-AI) weren't designed for.
- **DX acquisition pending** (not yet closed) — developer-productivity analytics aimed at helping enterprises measure ROI on their expanding AI-coding investment, explicitly framed as riding the same cogen wave as the Jira seat-expansion data.
- **Consumption-based pricing rolled out across the platform** (Rovo/AI credits, JSM agents and assets, Bitbucket pipelines, Rovo Dev, Forge/CDP) as the primary AI monetization lever, though Cannon-Brookes conceded customers are "cautious" about it even as they're "interested."
- **No scarcity in compute, talent, or capital was raised** — the binding constraint articulated throughout is customer trust/readiness to migrate, which the company is now managing on a fixed multiyear timeline (fast Shift program, partner network covering ~50% of revenue) rather than a resource bottleneck.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** migrations will keep accelerating and cluster heavily in FY28-29 as the 2029 EOL date approaches. **Action:** Ascend and Fast Shift partner programs are already running, and the EOL date itself is the forcing function — a real commitment (a published deadline that removes the "if"), not just a stated expectation.
- **Belief:** "there will be more developers in 5 years' time" and AI-written code expands rather than shrinks the addressable market for Jira. **Action:** consumption pricing SKUs and the DX acquisition are structured to capture that expansion directly — capital is following the stated belief.
- **Gap:** management calls the guidance revision unambiguously positive while simultaneously holding every non-migration growth driver flat/conservative — the "incredible confidence" language isn't backed by any actual raise to the underlying organic business, only to the migration-timing component.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **CFO Binz repeatedly declined to attribute the guidance raise to anything beyond migration timing**, explicitly restating that all other organic drivers remain "conservative and risk-adjusted" — a deliberate refusal to claim broader momentum even when directly asked ("is your fundamental growth outlook stronger, weaker or unchanged") — answered "stronger," but only cited bookings/CRPO tied to migration and Q1 outperformance, not a broadened set of drivers.
- **No specific cohort size was disclosed** for the AI-coding customer data (5% faster seat growth, 20%+ more projects) — Cannon-Brookes asserted it was "statistically relevant" without giving the underlying N when asked directly.
- **CFO succession timeline left open** — Binz confirmed the departure is "announced now but transition later," declining to give a date, tied to personal life events rather than business reasons stated.

## The street — what analysts asked
Analyst questions clustered almost entirely around reconciling the guidance mechanics — why raising cloud/migration guidance simultaneously lowered organic ex-migration growth by 50bps, and whether the AI-coding-cohort seat-expansion stat generalizes beyond a small sample. Secondary themes: partner-channel readiness for the migration wave, M&A rationale/philosophy (Browser Company, DX), consumption pricing penetration, and the CFO transition. Nothing was dodged outright, but the underlying-momentum question kept getting answered with migration-specific numbers rather than a broader organic reacceleration story. Compressed worry: is Atlassian's raised outlook real growth acceleration, or just faster recognition of migration revenue that was coming anyway?
