# REGN — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-29)

**The key idea:** Regeneron delivered a clean beat quarter — Dupixent still compounding at scale, EYLEA HD winning the anti-VEGF conversion battle — while facing three separate credibility tests: a stuck FDA manufacturing approval, an investor base skeptical the ~50-program pipeline will ever produce a second blockbuster, and a freshly signed MFN pricing deal with the US government whose long-run margin implications went almost entirely undiscussed. Management's posture throughout was confident bordering on defensive, repeatedly telling analysts their skepticism about pipeline productivity is a forecasting-model failure, not a company failure.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Dupixent's growth is still broadening, not slowing.** +31% cc to $4.9B ($5.6B in the US at +35%) with growth "across all nine indications," including brand-new launches (AFRS, pediatric CSU, COPD). Sanofi's development balance repayment completing by Q2-end means Regeneron's collaboration profit share steps up further starting Q3 — a real, quantifiable tailwind investors may be underpricing.
2. **EYLEA franchise transition is happening faster than the base business can absorb.** EYLEA HD +52% to $468M with 10% sequential demand growth, but legacy EYLEA -36% on cannibalization, competition, and "patient affordability issues," with management guiding EYLEA demand down "mid-to-high teens" next quarter ahead of new biosimilar launches. The prefilled syringe — the product's next competitive edge — is stuck on an FDA site re-inspection with no confirmed timeline beyond "this quarter."
3. **A manufacturing hiccup is quietly eating GAAP margin.** The Limerick, Ireland "temporary interruption in bulk manufacturing" pushed GAAP gross margin guidance down to 77%-78%, with continued Q2 impact before full production resumes. Framed as fully contained (no product availability impact), but it's the kind of operational miss that doesn't usually surface on an otherwise-clean beat quarter.
4. **The MFN pricing deal landed with almost no scrutiny.** Management touted the new Most-Favored-Nation agreement with the US government as achieving "shared goals" on affordability while "preserving innovation" — but no analyst asked about margin, list-price, or net-price implications. For a company this reliant on US branded pricing, that's a notable gap in the Q&A, not evidence the deal is margin-neutral.
5. **Leadership visibly bristled at the "where's the next blockbuster" question.** Asked directly which late-stage assets investors should have confidence in, Yancopoulos told the analyst that "past performance should be the strongest indicator of future performance" and that Regeneron's prior $10B+ blockbusters were things "you... never saw coming" — a pointed rebuttal that doubles as an admission the market doesn't currently believe in the pipeline's forward productivity.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro framing offered** — Regeneron doesn't talk economy/consumer in cyclical terms; the closest proxy is patient affordability, cited explicitly as a drag on legacy EYLEA sales.
- **Underutilization as a stated public-health gap**: Yancopoulos frames widespread underuse of lipid-lowering drugs as a systemic failure ("dramatically underutilized... causes incredible morbidity and death"), which the company is positioning its GLP-1/PCSK9 combo to exploit.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working**: Dupixent (all nine indications growing), EYLEA HD (+52%, broadest label in class), Libtayo (+54% to $438M on adjuvant CSCC launch plus lung share gains).
- **Not working**: legacy EYLEA (-36%, further "mid-to-high teens" demand decline guided for Q2 ahead of new biosimilars); GAAP gross margin dented by the Limerick manufacturing interruption; EYLEA HD PFS device approval delayed past its April PDUFA date.
- **linvoseltamab** (multiple myeloma bispecific) still in early "gradual uptake" phase, with management defending the drug-class infection-risk profile against Darzalex-based standard of care.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital return**: new $3B buyback authorization on top of $800M already repurchased this quarter, ~$3.4B total dry powder — a signal of confidence but also that management doesn't see a bigger internal use for the cash right now.
- **R&D breadth**: 11 phase III anticoagulation (Factor XI) trials, two new global phase III obesity programs for olatorepatide, first-in-human trials for CFB-siRNA, IL-13 antibody, and a genetically-identified lupus/Sjögren's/PBC target — spend is going wide, not narrow.
- **The real constraint is regulatory/manufacturing, not demand or capital**: EYLEA HD's prefilled syringe launch — the product's next growth lever — is gated on an FDA site re-inspection at a third-party filler (Catalent), with the FDA having already missed one PDUFA date. Management could only commit to "action on one or both applications during this quarter," not a specific approval.
- **New external partnerships**: Telix (radiopharmaceuticals) and TriNetX (real-world data access to 300M patients) — both capability extensions rather than pipeline-asset buys.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: Dupixent's addressable market keeps expanding (new indications, younger ages, mild-disease patients) and doesn't need a single "replacement" — next-gen IL-4/IL-13 assets are framed as expansion, not defense.
- **Action**: first-in-human IL-13 antibody trial starting mid-year; no dollar commitment or timeline disclosed beyond "expedited path to regulatory approvals" — a belief stated with more specificity than the action backing it.
- **Belief**: the olatorepatide + Praluent GLP-1/PCSK9 combo is a "no-brainer" that could become "the preferred obesity approach," pricing competitively against tirzepatide.
- **Action**: two global phase III programs starting later this year, first combination clinical study "initiating shortly" — genuine capital commitment, matching the confident rhetoric, making this the one belief/action pair that's actually aligned.
- **Belief**: pipeline breadth (48 programs) will inevitably produce the next blockbuster.
- **Action**: no specific near-term (18-month) blockbuster was named when directly asked — CEO Schleifer answered with a list of ongoing trials rather than a forecast, which is a gap between the confidence expressed and the specificity offered.
