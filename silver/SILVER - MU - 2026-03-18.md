# MU — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-03-18)

**The key idea:** Micron just posted the largest sequential revenue jump in its history and guided gross margin to 81%, and the entire call is management arguing this time really is different — that AI has permanently repriced memory as a "strategic asset" rather than a cyclical commodity. The tension: analysts keep asking what happens on the other side of the cycle, and management keeps declining to answer, hiding the downside protection (if any) inside confidential multiyear Strategic Customer Agreements.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Supply, not demand, is the whole story.** Revenue nearly tripled YoY and every business unit hit a record, yet key customers are still only getting "50% to two-thirds" of what they're asking for — this is a rationed market, not a demand-elastic one.
2. **The SCA is a new instrument built for a chronically tight world.** Micron signed its first five-year Strategic Customer Agreement — a step up from prior one-year LTAs — explicitly to give customers supply certainty and give Micron "confidence in future supply plans," but every specific (price mechanism, cancellation terms, downside margin floor) was withheld as confidential across four separate analyst attempts.
3. **Margin durability is the real debate, and management won't fully engage it.** Guided to 81% gross margin (up from a prior peak in "the low sixties"), CFO Mark Murphy argued this reflects durable structural repricing of memory's value to AI rather than a cyclical peak to be mean-reverted, but refused to guide even one quarter past the current one.
4. **Capital is chasing a scarcity problem, not a demand problem.** CapEx guided above $25B for fiscal 2026 and set to jump over $10B further in construction spend alone in fiscal 2027 — the constraint is cleanroom space and multi-year construction lead times, not chip demand or balance-sheet capacity (net cash hit a record $6.5B).
5. **Consumer memory is being pushed out to fund AI supply.** PC and smartphone units could fall low-double-digits in 2026 "due to supply constraints" even as on-device AI drives structurally higher memory content per device (32GB+ agentic PCs, ~80% of flagship phones now shipping 12GB+ DRAM) — the affordability squeeze and the content-growth story are happening simultaneously.

## Economy & consumer
- **Data center is eating the industry TAM.** Sanjay Mehrotra: AI demand is driving DRAM/NAND data center bits to exceed 50% of industry TAM "for the first time" in calendar 2026, with servers growing low-teens percent driven by both agentic AI workloads and broad-based refresh.
- **Consumer units may shrink even as content grows.** PC/smartphone units could decline low-double-digits in 2026 due to supply constraints and higher prices, while on-device AI (agentic PC apps, personal AI workstations at 128GB) is doubling recommended memory configs — a genuine tension between affordability and AI-driven content growth.
- **Automotive is inflecting on ADAS, not EVs.** AEBU revenue crossed $2B combined with industrial for the first time, driven by L2+ ADAS fleet rollout (16GB DRAM today vs. 300GB+ for L4 autonomy) — a structural, non-cyclical demand driver independent of AI hype.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Every metric was a record, including free cash flow.** Revenue $23.9B (+75% sequential, the largest dollar increase in company history), gross margin 75% (nearly doubled YoY), free cash flow $6.9B (beat the prior record by 77%).
- **Pricing, not volume, drove the quarter.** DRAM bit shipments were up only "mid-single digits" sequentially while DRAM prices rose "mid-sixties percent" — the entire margin story is price, not units, which is exactly what a supply-constrained market produces.
- **Data center NAND/SSD is the emerging profit engine.** Data center NAND revenue more than doubled sequentially to a record, SSD market share rose for a fourth straight year, and NAND demand is now "significantly in excess" of available supply — Micron is deliberately shifting NAND mix toward this higher-value tier.
- **What's not working: nothing disclosed as underperforming this quarter** — an unusually clean beat-and-raise with no segment weakness called out, which is itself worth flagging as a data point about how one-sided this cycle currently is.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx keeps ratcheting up mid-year.** Fiscal 2026 CapEx raised again to "above $25B," driven mainly by the Tongluo acquisition (closed early, ahead of schedule) and U.S. fab construction; fiscal 2027 construction spend alone will step up "over $10B" YoY, plus higher equipment spend and an R&D ramp — Sanjay explicitly flagged expanding R&D investment for fiscal 2027 on top of the CapEx increase.
- **The binding constraint is physical: cleanroom space and time.** Sanjay named it directly — "cleanroom constraints and long construction lead times, higher HBM trade ratio, higher HBM growth rates, and declining bits-per-wafer growth from node migration constrain bit supply growth." New NAND capacity is even being cannibalized: "some industry suppliers redirect cleanroom space for DRAM."
- **Global fab buildout accelerating on multiple fronts simultaneously**: Tongluo (Taiwan, DRAM, product shipments fiscal 2028), Idaho fab #1 (wafer output mid-2027), Idaho fab #2 (ground prep started), New York fab (ground broken, ahead of plan), Singapore NAND fab (wafer output 2028), Hiroshima cleanroom expansion, and a new India assembly/test facility now shipping commercially.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** tight supply-demand persists "beyond calendar 2026" for both DRAM and NAND, HBM4E ramps in 2027, robotics becomes "one of the largest product categories in the technology world" over a 20-year horizon, and enterprise AI deployment is still "very, very low" — huge runway implied.
- **Act:** locking in a first 5-year SCA and pursuing more with multiple customers, raising CapEx twice within two quarters, breaking ground on fabs ahead of schedule, ramping HBM4 for Vera Rubin, and raising the dividend 30% — capital and contracts are being committed now, not just talked about.
- **Gap worth flagging:** the CapEx build (fabs coming online 2027-2028) lags the stated demand runway by years — management is explicit that even this unprecedented buildout won't resolve the supply-demand gap by the time it lands, meaning the tight-supply/high-margin regime is somewhat self-reinforcing by design, not just by market accident.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No SCA specifics, at all.** Across four separate questions from different analysts, Sanjay repeatedly declined to disclose SCA pricing mechanisms, cancellation terms, downside margin protection, CapEx-forward requirements, or even customer type/segment breadth beyond "a large customer" and "across multiple markets" — citing confidentiality every time.
- **No Q4 gross margin guidance.** Mark Murphy explicitly declined to extend the margin guide past the immediate quarter: "we are not going to provide the fourth quarter gross margin guidance," despite repeated analyst attempts to get a directional read.
- **Stopped disclosing HBM share by quarter.** Having hit its 2025 HBM-share target (in line with DRAM share), management now declines to update the HBM TAM/CAGR figure or break out quarterly HBM mix, folding it into "managing the portfolio" language instead.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The dominant cluster was SCA mechanics** — duration, pricing negotiation, cancellation terms, downside margin floors, customer breadth, and whether CapEx commitments are tied to these agreements — asked from at least four different angles, every one deflected to "these agreements are confidential."
- **The second cluster was margin sustainability** — whether 81% gross margin reverts to a historical mean (management's prior peak was "low sixties"), and how customers might react to seeing these margin levels embedded in their AI silicon costs.
- **A smaller thread probed supply-allocation strategy and technical architecture risk** — how Micron balances big vs. small customers and data center vs. consumer allocation, and whether SRAM/LPU-based architectures (e.g., NVIDIA's Grok LPX) could divert demand away from HBM.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts don't doubt this quarter — they doubt whether Micron's newfound pricing power survives contact with the next down-cycle, and management's refusal to disclose SCA downside terms or forward margin guidance leaves that question completely unanswered.
