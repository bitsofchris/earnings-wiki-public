# CCEP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** CCEP delivered an in-line, guidance-reaffirming Q1 that leans heavily on innovation, mix, and hedging discipline to preempt a possible repeat of 2022-style inflation shock — management's central bet is that this time is different because supply is diversified and 85% of commodities are hedged, even though they concede a Middle East-driven energy/inflation shock could still hit consumer demand broadly across food and drink.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **"Don't see it as 2022"** is the load-bearing claim of the call. CEO Damian Gammell and CFO Ed Walker both explicitly contrast today's Middle East-driven cost pressure against the 2022 inflation shock, citing supply diversification and ~85% commodity hedging as the reason they expect a softer landing — but this is a forecast, not yet observed data ("we aren't currently seeing any material impact on consumers").
2. **Zero/Diet is cannibalizing, not just adding.** Original Taste Coke volumes fell roughly 3%, explicitly linked by management to consumers trading into Coke Zero and smaller packs — framed as "a very healthy dynamic" rather than a warning sign, since total sparkling category health holds up.
3. **Monster keeps compounding.** Volumes up 20%+ with multiple new launches (Rehab, Viking Berry) outperforming prior-year launches in CCEP's largest energy market (GB) — this remains the single strongest growth engine in the portfolio.
4. **AI is now touching planning, trade promo, and customer data, but CEO admits they're only "on the way to the middle"** of the journey — most mature in forecasting/inventory, least mature (and highest value pool) in trade promo optimization.
5. **The Pepsi Nordics bottling opening is a live but undecided opportunity.** Management confirmed balance-sheet readiness for M&A and framed it as core to their 10-year "become a bigger bottler" ambition, but gave zero specifics on intent for the Denmark/Finland territories, deferring entirely to future conversations with The Coca-Cola Company.

## Economy & consumer
- **Middle East crisis flagged as the swing factor** for the year, described as "increasingly uncertain," with planning based on a "temporary market disruption" assumption that management says it will revisit if conditions worsen.
- **No material consumer impact seen yet**, but CFO Walker concedes the risk is not soft-drink-specific — it's "a general challenge on inflation across all of food and drink" that could pressure demand broadly.
- **Value still matters to European shoppers** — up to 30-40% of retail volumes run on promo in some markets — while emerging markets (Philippines, Indonesia) lean on entry-level affordability (RGB, smaller sparkling packs) to build the category long-term.
- **No specific commentary on unemployment, wages, or discretionary spend shifts** beyond the promo/affordability lens — a gap for a company this consumer-facing, likely because Q1 is deliberately framed as too early to call.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Revenue grew 9.8% in Q1**, powered mostly by volume (extra consumption days contributed over 8 points) plus continued positive brand mix from coolers and Monster — a strong headline number, but partly an artifact of calendar timing (Easter, extra days) rather than pure demand acceleration.
- **Europe volumes +1.4%, APS volumes +1.9% on a comparable basis** — APS was called out as showing a "sequential volume improvement," driven by Australia share gains, Philippines strength, and a "successful Ramadan" in Indonesia around sparkling specifically (tea remains "work in progress").
- **Large PET format continues a multi-quarter decline**, while single-serve/smaller packs and the new half-liter "Superfan" can are gaining — management frames this as intentional RGM (revenue growth management) working as designed, not demand weakness.
- **Suntory alcohol-brand exit created a real headwind** — about 3% drag on APS revenue and 1% at group level — a clean, disclosed one-off rather than a hidden prop.
- **GB and Germany both delivered strong quarters**, but Gammell is careful to flag Germany's strength as largely Easter-timing-driven with "more work to do" on price-promo architecture to sustain volume growth into Q2 and beyond; France, by contrast, is recovering from prior sugar-tax-driven pricing disruption and is now through the worst of the cycling.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Hedging discipline is the primary lever against cost shocks**: ~85% of commodities hedged for the remainder of the year, plus a post-2022 shift toward directly helping suppliers hedge their own exposure so cost pressure doesn't cascade back to CCEP.
- **Capital continues into physical capacity** — the Philippines mega-plant outside Manila remains on track for a 2027 production start, explicitly tied to relieving supply-chain bottlenecks that were constraining the sales team's ability to sell.
- **Cooler placement is treated as a growth lever, not overhead** — ~40,000 added year-to-date with a named 1,000-unit commitment to Co-op convenience stores in GB, under the explicit logic "what is cold is sold."
- **AI investment is broad but still early-stage in the highest-value area** — deployed in forecasting/planning (mature), Copilot and partner-embedded AI in Salesforce/ServiceNow (adopted), and a new internal tool "Kira" for insights analysis — but trade promotion optimization, the biggest value pool, is explicitly called "the beginning of that journey."

This is a real gap: confidence is stated ahead of the balance-sheet actions that would prove it.
- **Belief:** Original Taste Coke's volume decline is a healthy structural shift, not erosion. **Action:** concrete investment behind Cherry, Coke Zero Zero, and Cherry-led FIFA activations — spend is following the stated belief, which is a coherent talk/action match.
- **Belief:** CCEP wants to grow via additional Coca-Cola bottling territories (Nordics opportunity specifically raised). **Action:** confirmed "capability" (balance sheet) but zero commitment of capital or even a stated intent toward Denmark/Finland — pure optionality language, despite direct prompting.
