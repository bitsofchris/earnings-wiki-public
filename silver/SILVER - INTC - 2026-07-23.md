# INTC — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-23)

**The key idea:** Intel's problem has flipped from "can we sell this" to "can we make enough of it" — demand across servers, client, and foundry is outrunning supply for the seventh straight beat-and-raise quarter, and the company is responding by pulling CapEx sharply higher on the bet that 18A/14A yields and external customer confidence are finally real. The tension: this is the same management that just wrote down stranded client inventory and still won't name a single external foundry customer, even while claiming growing "engagement."

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **CPU-GPU parity as the thesis**: Lip-Bu Tan argues agentic and multi-agent inference is pushing the CPU-to-GPU ratio toward parity and "could eventually even skew more to CPUs on a unit basis" — the entire capital program is being justified by this reframe of what AI infrastructure actually needs.
- **14A is the real foundry bet, not 18A**: PDK 0.5 is done, PDK 0.9 is on track for October, and defect density/transistor performance are "outpacing 18A development" — Intel fully committed to high-volume 14A ramp in 2028 during Q2, with external customer engagement described as accelerating but still unnamed.
- **CapEx raised meaningfully with 2027 "significantly above" that**: 2026 CapEx guided above $20B (tooling up 40% YoY), with Zinsner explicitly declining to give a 2027 number yet — "we need another quarter or two to really solidify" — while noting nearly $100B in cumulative US tool/space spending since 2021, more than any other semiconductor company.
- **ASIC/purpose-built silicon scaling fast off a small base**: run-rate is "approaching $2 billion," with management guiding to a $4B run rate "in the not too distant future" against a claimed >$100B TAM; the Fortinet security-ASIC win is the one concrete external logo cited.
- **Packaging (EMIB-T) as the quieter high-conviction bet**: growing backlog, yields hitting targets, positioned for 2027 customer ramps — Tan calls it a capability competitors can't match "with today's mainstream offerings."
- **CCPG renamed to Client Computing and Physical AI Group**, explicitly to stake a claim in edge/physical AI (robotics "brain and control" deployments, 130 Series 3 edge design wins) — a hedge against a client PC market management itself says is going to shrink.
- **Memory ambitions reopened**: hiring former SK hynix CEO Seok-Hee Lee and exploring compute-memory integration/stacking, framed as a response to memory becoming "the big supply constraint challenge" industry-wide.

## What's changing
- **Server CPU growth is the best on record**: Q2 server growth was the strongest ever recorded for Intel, with DCAI revenue up 24% sequentially and 59% year-over-year — the company is now "substantially increasing investments" specifically because this demand signal has firmed rather than softened.
- **18A cost curve is moving fast**: Panther Lake SKU cost is down roughly 50% year-to-date on Foundry, with another 20% cut targeted by year-end — the clearest evidence yet that 18A economics are converging toward viability rather than remaining a permanent drag.
- **Gross margin discipline paid off, barely**: management hit its stated goal of "comfortably into the 40%s" for two straight quarters (41.8% in Q2, 42% guided for Q3) — a number Zinsner says he was "a dog with a bone on."
- **Supply, not demand, is now the binding constraint across nearly every segment** — wafers, substrates, memory, and T-glass are all cited as choke points, with management saying linearity of supply growth "is more skewed towards the end of Q3 and into Q4," meaning the current quarter's guide is intentionally conservative.
- **Client strength was priced, not demanded**: the CCPG beat was "largely ASP," driven by mix-shift to higher end and deliberate price increases to offset input cost inflation — the underlying PC market is actually softening, guided down low double digits for the full year.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Stranded inventory write-down in CCPG**: products that "weren't fully completed from a match set perspective" were scrapped rather than finished, taking $173M out of segment operating profit — a quiet admission that supply-chain execution on client SKUs is still messy even as headline numbers beat.
- **Zero named external foundry customers, again**: despite two straight quarters of "confidence" language on external engagement, no logo, no volume commitment, no dollar figure was disclosed for any external 18A, 18A-P, or 14A customer — the entire foundry pitch remains internal-product-validation-by-proxy.
- **Foundry is still burning $2.1B a quarter**: the loss narrowed only modestly (by $348M) despite yield and cost improvements — Intel Foundry remains deeply unprofitable with no stated date for breakeven.
- **2027 CapEx number was explicitly withheld**: Zinsner declined to guide, saying only that it will be "significantly above" 2026 — an unusual amount of hedging for a company that just told investors to trust its supply/demand read.
- **No mention of AI accelerators/GPUs as a competitive answer to Nvidia** — the entire AI pitch stays anchored to CPUs, ASICs, and packaging; for a company positioning itself as central to "the next era of computing," the conspicuous absence of any training-class accelerator roadmap is notable.
- **Balance sheet caveat buried in the CapEx answer**: Zinsner admits that "if we're super successful... we may need to tap the capital markets" — a raise is being pre-signaled even as the company touts $30B cash and de-levering.
