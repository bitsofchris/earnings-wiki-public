# IBM — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-28)

**The key idea:** IBM closed out its best year in over a decade — 6% revenue growth, record free cash flow, software crossing 45% of the business — and is now guiding to double-digit software growth and another $1B of free cash flow growth in 2026. But underneath the celebration sits a quieter story: the 2026 guide leans heavily on productivity cuts pre-funded specifically to absorb Confluent's dilution, a hedge on Red Hat's actual growth rate, and the retirement of the one metric (Gen AI book of business) that had started to show deceleration risk.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Software did the heavy lifting, and management knows it.** Software hit 9% full-year growth — "our highest annual growth rate in history" — with data up 19% and automation up 14% in Q4. CFO Jim Kavanaugh contrasted this with "only about three years ago, we only had one growth factor. And that was Red Hat," underscoring that this is a genuinely diversified acceleration, not one segment's rebound.
2. **The Confluent dilution math is being pre-funded by layoffs, not disclosed as such upfront.** IBM "accelerated productivity initiatives in the fourth quarter" and took workforce-rebalancing charges specifically "to help mitigate 2026 dilution" from the pending Confluent deal — the same playbook used for HashiCorp. Kavanaugh only volunteered the ~$600M 2026 dilution figure after an analyst pushed on why it wasn't mentioned alongside the free-cash-flow guide.
3. **Red Hat guidance was deliberately vague.** Kavanaugh: "I would say the word choices were being prudent on Red Hat's guidance right now," tying it to federal-government-shutdown-driven bookings weakness. Yet the whole double-digit software story requires Red Hat to hit double digits — a load-bearing assumption stated without a number.
4. **Memory/DRAM pricing is a real, multi-year cost headwind IBM is talking its way around.** Arvind directly confirmed spot DRAM prices are "six times that of last year" due to HBM capacity diversion, and that "those pricing issues are going to be there through the year" and likely "at least a couple of years" — then pivoted immediately to argue AI server demand offsets it via CPU attach, without quantifying the net effect.
5. **A metric got retired right when it might have started decelerating.** The Gen AI book of business, tracked since 2023, is being discontinued as a standalone disclosure just as it crossed $12.5B — management's stated reason ("AI is now embedded across our business") is plausible, but it also removes a number analysts had used to gauge Gen AI momentum specifically.

## Economy & consumer
- **No direct consumer read** — IBM is a B2B enterprise seller; no end-consumer commentary. Its "economy" signal is entirely enterprise capex behavior.
- **Enterprise spend is bifurcating toward "must-have" infrastructure.** Arvind: clients are prioritizing "hybrid cloud, AI, and mission-critical infrastructure... no longer viewed as incremental tools but as platforms that fundamentally change how businesses scale, compete, and operate" — discretionary/BPO-style consulting spend is explicitly described as recovering only in the second half after an 18-month pullback.
- **Government spend is a visible drag.** The US federal shutdown was cited directly as the cause of "delays... related to the government shutdown" hitting Red Hat and consulting bookings in Q4 — a rare instance of IBM naming a specific macro/political event as a revenue drag.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Z mainframe had its best cycle in ~20 years, but the company itself guides it down next year.** IBM Z revenue rose 61% year-to-year in Q4 alone, yet 2026 infrastructure guidance calls for a low-single-digit decline as the Z17 cycle laps a record placement year — management frames this as normal cycle math, not weakness.
- **Consulting remains the weak link.** Full-year consulting only "inflected back to growth" in H2, finishing +1% in Q4 with signings down against a tough prior-year comp; 2026 guide is modest (low-to-mid-single-digit growth).
- **Transaction processing (TP) revenue is inflecting favorably** off Z placement — flat in 2025 versus a down-9% comp in the prior cycle, described by Kavanaugh as showing "the compounding effect of what's happening to TP."
- **OpenShift is a genuine standout inside Red Hat** — nearly $2B ARR, growing >30%, cited repeatedly as evidence Red Hat's broader deceleration (to 8%) isn't a full-portfolio problem.

## Investing & scarcity
- **M&A is now IBM's primary growth lever, not just a bolt-on.** Confluent (real-time data/streaming for agentic AI), following HashiCorp, Apptio, DataStax, and Software AG — $8.3B spent on acquisitions in 2025 alone. Confluent is expected to be "accretive to adjusted EBITDA within the first full year and to free cash flow in year two."
- **Scarcity is squarely on the input-cost side: HBM-driven DRAM pricing.** Arvind's own words: "spot memory DRAM prices are six times that of last year... as long as that dynamic is there, those pricing issues are going to be there through the year," with the underlying HBM-vs-DRAM capacity shift expected to persist for "at least a couple of years."
- **Productivity is being run as an internal capital source.** Full-year 2025 run-rate savings hit $4.5B (target was $2B by end-2024); 2026 target raised to $5.5B, an incremental $1B explicitly earmarked to offset both Z product-cycle mix headwinds and Confluent dilution.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: enterprise AI inference splits 50/50 (or even 80/90 by volume) toward private/on-prem over the next 3–5 years.** Arvind: "if I look out three to five years, 50% of the enterprise usage of AI [is] going to be in either a private cloud or... their own data centers." **Action matches belief here** — IBM is building sovereign-core offerings, embedding real-time inferencing directly into Z17 hardware, and pursuing Confluent for real-time data delivery to on-prem/agentic workloads.
- **Belief: Red Hat can hit double-digit growth to carry the software guide.** **Action doesn't match** — no specific commitment or number was given; the guide leans on it while hedging language ("prudent") explicitly signals lower confidence than the topline story implies.
- **Belief: memory/HBM cost pressure is temporary-ish but persistent (~2 years).** **Action is muted** — no specific pricing/hedging/supply strategy was described beyond an argument that AI demand offsets it; this is closer to optimism than a stated mitigation plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No firm Red Hat 2026 growth number** — deliberately non-quantified ("prudent") despite giving specific figures for automation, data, and TP.
- **Retired the Gen AI book-of-business disclosure** going forward, removing a metric analysts had used to track a possibly plateauing growth narrative, framed instead as maturity ("AI is now embedded across our business").
- **Declined to frame the FCF guide as anything but "our job is to beat it"** — Kavanaugh repeatedly deflected the "sandbagging" question rather than committing to a specific confidence level on upside.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Recurring themes: (1) can software really hit double digits given Red Hat's deceleration, (2) is the free-cash-flow guide conservative/"sandbagged" again like last year's, (3) how bad is DRAM/memory pricing for the server refresh cycle and Red Hat Linux, and (4) why guide infrastructure down after the best Z cycle in 20 years.
- Management's answers consistently redirected toward aggregate confidence (portfolio diversification, multiple double-digit growth engines) rather than the specific, harder sub-question asked (e.g., Red Hat's actual number, the net dollar impact of memory pricing).
- Compressed worry: **is IBM's 2026 guidance built on genuine organic acceleration, or is it propped up by cost-cutting timed to absorb M&A dilution and a hedge on the one segment (Red Hat) the whole software story depends on?**
