# SBUX — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** Starbucks posted its first positive comp in seven quarters and is treating it as proof that "Back to Starbucks" — more staffing hours, a new Green Apron Service standard, and a wave of store closures/renovations — is working. But the improvement rides almost entirely on the U.S. business turning positive in the last two weeks of the quarter, margins are still compressing hard (EPS down 34%), and management deferred every hard number (FY26 guidance, cost-savings targets, pricing plans) to a January Investor Day — leaving the "turnaround is taking hold" claim resting more on narrative and anecdote than disclosed math.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The inflection is real but extremely fresh.** Global comp went positive (+1%) and US comp hit flat after 6 straight negative quarters, but CEO Brian Niccol repeatedly notes the underlying driver — Green Apron Service — is "only 2 months in" and "8 or 9 weeks in," with September the first month of transaction-led growth continuing into October.
2. **Margin pain is the price of the turnaround, and it's steep.** Operating margin fell 500bps and EPS dropped 34% YoY in the same quarter management is calling a "turning point" — Niccol frames this deliberately: "we expect to grow the top line first and then earnings will follow."
3. **107 store closures were a financial cleanup, not a growth move.** CFO Cathy Smith said closed stores "didn't demonstrate a viable path to profitability," driven primarily by insufficient top-line volume rather than cost structure, and the closures are expected to be "slightly accretive" to margin — a tacit admission the prior footprint had unprofitable units baked in.
4. **China is being partially sold, not fixed and kept.** Management confirmed "very strong interest from multiple high-quality partners" and plans to "retain a meaningful stake" while collecting upfront investment and future royalties — a structural retreat dressed as a strategic partnership, even as China comps turned positive for a second straight quarter.
5. **Every hard number got punted to January.** Guidance, cost-savings targets, and the "financial algorithm" were all explicitly deferred to the Investor Day, meaning this call is a qualitative narrative update, not a quantified plan.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro framing offered.** Management never characterizes the broader economy; all commentary is company-specific execution (staffing, sequencing, menu).
- **Choiceful but responsive consumers.** On younger/under-35 cohorts, Niccol says customers "are going to be more choiceful with where they choose to spend their dollars," but reports transaction and sales growth improved across every generational cohort in the quarter.
- **Value perception up across the board**, attributed to Green Apron Service, restoring the condiment bar, simplifying pricing, and dropping the non-dairy milk surcharge — value framed as service quality, not discounting.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** US comps turned positive in September and held through October; morning daypart transactions outpaced overall recovery; non-Rewards customer transactions grew YoY for a second straight quarter; brand-affinity and "first choice" scores hit multi-year highs; delivery grew nearly 30% YoY, crossing $1B for the fiscal year.
- **What's not:** US company-operated transactions were still down 1% YoY even as sales turned positive — the recovery is ticket-led as much as traffic-led in aggregate; consolidated operating margin fell 500bps on coffee/tariff inflation plus labor investment; US licensed store revenue declined on grocery/retail channel weakness.
- **Metric redefinition worth flagging:** store-level reporting is being cut from "nearly 2 dozen metrics down to a scorecard of just 5 KPIs" — a genuine simplification, but also fewer external-facing signals of underlying store health going forward.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Green Apron Service is the flagship capital/labor bet:** additional staffing hours, expanded rosters, and a Smart Queue sequencing algorithm rolled out across the full US company-operated portfolio by August, with CEO and CFO both saying the initial labor investment is largely made and future labor spend will be "earned" via improved peak-hour performance rather than added.
- **Real estate reset:** ~70 coffee house "uplift" renovations completed, targeting 1,000+ by end of fiscal 2026; a new lower-build-cost, smaller-footprint prototype is being piloted (first converted from a pickup-only NYC location).
- **Binding constraint = input costs, not capacity.** Coffee commodity prices and tariffs remain elevated with no retreat yet; CFO expects the headwind to persist through most of fiscal 2026 with possible relief only in the back half.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management expects US comps to "build through the year" in fiscal 2026, with the whole Back to Starbucks framework (service, coffee houses, marketing/menu) hitting full stride for the first time this holiday season.
- **Action backing it:** ASM (assistant store manager) rollout, dividend raised for a 15th straight year (a costed signal of confidence despite EPS down 34%), 1,000+ planned uplifts, continued menu innovation pipeline (bake case, matcha, protein platform).
- **Gap:** the confidence is not matched by disclosed numbers — no FY26 revenue/margin guidance, no cost-savings target, and CFO's own hedge that "recoveries are not always linear" undercuts the "the plan is working" framing from the same call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No FY26 guidance or savings targets:** CFO explicitly deferred to the January Investor Day twice, at one point pre-empting the disappointment: "I'm going to, I'm sure, completely disappoint you in my response."
- **No broad-based pricing commitment:** Niccol said he doesn't "envision us just doing broadscale pricing across our menu," keeping pricing "very strategic, very targeted" and contingent on inflation and value-score tracking — optionality, not a plan.
- **No incremental Green Apron Service investment guaranteed:** management frames further staffing gains as something the business will "earn" through improved peak performance, not additional committed spend.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered around durability: is Green Apron Service a one-time base effect or a compounding flywheel, and how much of the improvement is really visible only 8-9 weeks in?
- Several asked for numbers — FY26 outlook, cost-savings buckets, pricing plans — and got explicitly deferred answers each time.
- Store closures and margin math got scrutiny (AUV vs. cost drivers, sales-transfer rates), as did the license business restructuring and rising competition from emerging beverage concepts, which Niccol answered mostly with a scale argument rather than data.
- Compressed worry: **is the "turn" a genuine structural recovery, or a low base plus a service-standard sugar high that management is unwilling to quantify until it can control the narrative in January?**
