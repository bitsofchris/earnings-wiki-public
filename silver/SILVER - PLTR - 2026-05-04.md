# PLTR — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-04)

**The key idea:** Palantir posted its first triple-digit US growth quarter (104% YoY) and its largest-ever full-year guidance raise, and management's entire narrative is that cheap tokens don't commoditize AI value — they explode demand for it (Jevons paradox), with Palantir's ontology positioned as the only governance layer that turns that demand into safe, auditable enterprise action ("no slop zone"). The tension: a company boasting near-limitless demand and a ~70-person sales force is simultaneously conceding it "cannot meet demand," meaning the bottleneck this year is delivery capacity, not the market.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **US growth crossed 100% for the first time as a public company** — 104% YoY, 19% QoQ, alongside total revenue growth accelerating for the 11th straight quarter to 85% YoY. Rule of 40 jumped 18 points QoQ to 145.
2. **Management's central bet is that inference deflation increases their moat, not erodes it.** Sankar: "As inference gets cheaper, the number of tasks that you can economically assign to AI grows exponentially... More tokens means more slop... That system is AIP." This is the load-bearing thesis of the whole call — cheap tokens create demand for the governance layer that prevents "slop."
3. **Guidance raise is unusually broad-based** — revenue, US commercial revenue, adjusted operating income, and adjusted free cash flow were all raised together, with the revenue raise described as the company's "largest ever full year revenue guidance raise."
4. **Karp explicitly frames capacity, not demand, as the constraint**: "Our biggest problem currently is demand in the U.S.... we just cannot meet demand." Sales headcount stayed unusually thin (~70 total, "seven people" who actually sell) even as bookings scaled — a claim worth stress-testing against hiring/expense data going forward.
5. **A commercial-to-government customer transition muddies the headline commercial number** — ex-transition, US commercial growth would have been 143% YoY (vs. 133% reported) and 22% QoQ (vs. 18%). Disclosed cleanly, but it means the reported figure understates organic commercial momentum while a chunk of "commercial" success is really migrating into the government book.

## Economy & consumer
- No macro or consumer commentary — Palantir doesn't touch an end consumer directly, and management gave zero cyclical/demand-environment framing outside their own AI-adoption narrative. The closest thing to a macro view is the token-deflation argument (Sankar): GPT-4-equivalent cost "$20 per million tokens in early 2023 is now approximately 1,000x cheaper."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US commercial is the standout**: 133% YoY, 18% QoQ, with $1.2B in TCV bookings marking a third consecutive quarter over $1B (45% YoY growth in that metric) and $4.7B trailing-12-month US commercial bookings, up 115% YoY.
- **Government reaccelerated too** — US government revenue grew 84% YoY, 21% QoQ, driven by Maven usage that "doubled in the past four months" and is "4x over the past 12 months."
- **Net dollar retention rose sharply** — 150%, up 1,100bps QoQ — but Glazer flags it still doesn't capture new-logo velocity from the past year, meaning the reported number understates rather than overstates momentum.
- **What's not discussed**: churn, competitive losses, or any deal that slipped — the call is unusually one-sided even by Palantir's promotional standards, with zero acknowledgment of execution misses.

## Investing & scarcity
- Adjusted expense grew 32% YoY (7% QoQ), explicitly tied to "technical hiring" and platform investment, with management stating they "expect expenses to ramp in 2026" while still holding to GAAP profitability every quarter.
- The binding constraint, per Karp, is go-to-market/delivery capacity, not compute, capital, or even talent broadly — though talent (specifically "the best talent in the world" and neurodivergent recruiting) gets significant airtime as an ongoing competitive battle rather than a current blocker.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe**: US growth of "100%" is sustainable this year and possibly repeatable next year; AI labs building enterprise tools will ultimately funnel customers back to Palantir ("mostly, they come home to Palantir... they're creating the market for us").
- **Acting**: Raised guidance across revenue, margin, and cash flow simultaneously; surged commercial engineering resources into defense manufacturing (ShipOS) and shipped a "next generation of Apollo" for vulnerability remediation in response to what Sankar calls a "Sputnik moment" in AI-discovered zero-days.
- **Gap worth flagging**: despite claiming demand exceeds capacity, management gave no specific hiring targets, sales headcount expansion plans, or delivery-capacity investment numbers — the acknowledged bottleneck has no matching disclosed action plan on this call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Continuing-resolution/appropriations risk to Maven/TITAN and defense revenue was acknowledged but not quantified: "History would suggest... we're gonna be in a CR" (Shyam), treated as background noise rather than a modeled risk.
- Strategic commercial contracts — a legacy overhang line item — guided down to "less than half a million dollars in each remaining quarter," effectively written off rather than promoted.
- No commitment on how large the addressable US commercial market truly is; Karp's "we can only cap... at our limit doing 100%" reads as capping ambition at delivery capacity rather than giving a market-size target.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Two live questions dominated: (1) how Palantir balances government vs. commercial deal prioritization amid demand exceeding supply, and (2) how the company defends against AI labs building their own enterprise/agentic tooling, plus talent competition against those labs.
- A written shareholder question probed the same competitive-threat theme directly — "AI is pressuring software companies and their capabilities" — which management answered by reframing cheap tokens as a tailwind rather than a threat.
- A defense-focused question pushed on how much 2026 growth depends on the presidential budget request being appropriated versus surviving a continuing resolution; management conceded CR risk exists but declined to quantify downside exposure.
- Compressed worry: **is 100%+ growth durable once easy compares fade and appropriations risk actually bites, or is Palantir currently capacity-constrained in a way that caps the upside management is promising?**
