# JNJ — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-14)

**The key idea:** J&J used a strong beat-and-raise quarter to launch a bigger structural story — spinning off Orthopaedics (DePuy Synthes) to concentrate MedTech on cardiovascular, surgery, and vision, while simultaneously telling Wall Street its 2026 models are too conservative. The tension: management wants credit for "accelerated growth the balance of the decade" while giving almost no hard numbers to back the 2026 claim until January.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **STELARA erosion is now a tailwind story, not a headwind one.** STELARA sales fell 42% (a 640-1070bps drag depending on the segment), yet total company growth still hit 5.4% — management's central "we told you we'd grow through this" victory lap, backed by 11 brands with double-digit growth.
2. **The ortho spin is a margin-and-focus play, not a distress sale.** Tim Schmid was blunt: "this is all about shrinking to grow faster for MedTech... you're not rewarding size, but really rather best-in-class performance." Hips and knees actually returned to growth (5.1%/5.6%) this quarter, undercutting any narrative that ortho is being cut loose because it's broken.
3. **Management pre-committed to beating Street's 2026 numbers before giving guidance.** Joe Wolk stated consensus 4.6% revenue growth is "lower than we project, which we believe in total will exceed 5%," with EPS upside "perhaps as much as $0.05" versus $11.39 consensus — an unusually specific tease with zero segment-level backup, deferred to the January call.
4. **MedTech's margin compression is the print's soft spot.** Despite accelerating sales (5.6%), MedTech adjusted margin fell from 24.1% to 21% on "macroeconomic factors" — a vague attribution management didn't unpack, sitting oddly next to the accelerated-growth narrative.
5. **The pipeline is doing real work covering the STELARA gap.** TREMFYA (+40%), CARVICTI (+81%), SPRAVATO (+61%), and the newly approved INLEXO (bladder cancer) are each pitched as future $5B+ assets — this is less "one blockbuster" risk than a broad multi-asset base, per Jennifer Taubert's framing.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro or consumer-demand commentary** — JNJ is a manufacturer/pharma-MedTech company selling through physicians and health systems, not a consumer-facing business; the closest analog is patient access, e.g. awaiting the Inlexo J-code in April 2026 to unlock normal buy-and-bill uptake.
- **Policy is the macro variable that matters here.** Effective tax rate jumped from 19.3% to 31.2% this quarter due to the One Big Beautiful Bill Act's deferred-tax remeasurement; Duato described "an open dialogue" with the administration on MFN and tariffs but offered no specifics: "I don't have anything to share today."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: broad-based double-digit growth across Innovative Medicine.** DARZALEX +20% (>50% share across all lines of multiple myeloma therapy), CARVICTI +81% (>8,500 patients treated, "most successful CAR T launch ever"), TREMFYA +40% on IBD indications.
- **Working: MedTech cardiovascular is the standout segment.** Shockwave +21%, Abiomed +16%, electrophysiology growth "doubling" in the U.S. this quarter per Schmid; Shockwave is set to become the 13th MedTech platform to cross $1B in sales by year-end.
- **Not working: MedTech margin degradation despite sales acceleration.** Adjusted MedTech income-before-tax margin fell to 21% from 24.1%, attributed only broadly to "macroeconomic factors and cost of products sold" — a real cost story management didn't quantify further.
- **Not working, quietly conceded: a one-time reserve adjustment flattered MedTech's quarter.** When pressed directly on whether a reserve adjustment (offset partly by an energy-segment go-to-market change) inflated the 5.6%/5.7% MedTech growth number, Wolk called it "moderate, certainly not material" but declined to quantify it.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The Orthopaedics spin is the quarter's defining capital-allocation move.** DePuy Synthes will separate as a standalone company (targeted spin-off, 18-24 months, tax-free intent) addressing a >CHF 50B ortho market, freeing MedTech to concentrate fully on cardiovascular, surgery, and vision.
- **U.S. manufacturing commitment reaffirmed and extended.** $55B over four years, including a new $2B, 160,000+ sq ft facility at FUJIFILM's Holly Springs, NC site (announced August), on top of the Wilson, NC groundbreaking in March — explicitly tied to tax-policy incentives ("U.S. tax policy has enabled Johnson & Johnson to increase our manufacturing footprint in The U.S.").
- **Explicit rejection of large M&A as a growth lever.** Wolk: "We rely on our thoughtful long-term approach to growing through any loss of exclusivity and won't carelessly deploy capital on speculative transactions out of desperation" — instead pointing to 60+ small/bolt-on deals in 18 months (Inlexo itself was sourced this way in 2019 for "a couple $100 million").
- **The binding constraint is strategic focus, not resources.** With ~$19B cash, $27B net debt (down from $32B last quarter), and $14B free cash flow YTD, JNJ isn't capital-constrained — the stated scarcity is management attention/portfolio focus, which is the explicit rationale for shedding ortho.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: 2026 growth will "exceed 5%" and beat consensus meaningfully** — but the only action behind this stated belief so far is reaffirming existing pipeline launches (Tremfya IBD, Ribrevant+Lazcluze, Inlexo, icotrokinra) already disclosed; no new incremental commitment was announced to justify the above-consensus call. Formal guidance is deferred to January 2026, so the claim currently rests entirely on rhetoric.
- **Belief: MedTech will become "the best-in-class MedTech group in the industry"** (Duato, repeated at least three times) — backed concretely by the ortho divestiture and continued electrophysiology/robotic-surgery (Ottava, FDA de novo filing targeted early 2026) investment, so this is one case where talk and action align.
- **Gap: management claims post-spin margin uplift will be durable, but its own CFO conceded the near-term number is soft.** The 75bps figure was walked back toward ~100bps on a trailing-2025 basis when an analyst pushed back, then re-hedged as "muted" over a multi-year horizon — a number that moved twice in one answer.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 formal guidance** — repeated refusal to quantify beyond directional commentary, deferred explicitly to the January earnings call, despite making specific consensus-beat claims.
- **Ortho separation structure left open.** Spin-off is the prioritized path "given it is the most resource-intensive and likely longest duration," but Wolk explicitly reserved the right to "consider other avenues that optimize shareholder value" — i.e., a sale isn't off the table despite the spin framing.
- **Declined to rule out further portfolio separations with full confidence.** Wolk said "this is not a precursor to anything else" but the answer leaned on current conviction in the remaining segments rather than a hard commitment never to divest again.
- **No quantification of the reserve-adjustment impact on MedTech's reported growth**, despite a direct analyst request for a number.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The ortho spin dominated the Q&A** — rationale, timing, whether it's a template for future breakups, and whether the stated 75bps margin benefit undersells the real opportunity (management conceded ~100bps on a trailing basis before re-hedging).
- **Policy exposure** (MFN, tariffs, drug pricing) drew a direct question; Duato's answer was entirely process-oriented ("ongoing... optimistic") with no substantive detail.
- **Whether 2026 consensus is under-modeling JNJ** recurred across multiple questioners, with management repeatedly asserting a "clear disconnect" versus Street models without providing the numbers to support it.
- **MedTech's underlying organic growth quality** was probed once directly (the reserve-adjustment question) and management minimized rather than quantified it.
- Compressed worry: **investors are being asked to underwrite an above-consensus 2026 and a bigger-margin post-spin MedTech on management's word alone, with the actual numbers withheld until January.**
