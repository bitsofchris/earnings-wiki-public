# KO — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-10)

**The key idea:** This is a leadership handoff call — James Quincey's last as CEO, Henrique Braun's first as CEO-elect — layered on top of a 2026 guide that quietly resets expectations lower on a comparable basis (7-8% EPS growth on a 3-point FX tailwind vs. 4% growth against a 5-point FX headwind in 2025) while insisting nothing structural has changed. The real tension: management wants credit for "all-weather" resilience (19 straight quarters of value share gains, North America hitting 30% margin) while asking investors to look past a genuinely soft China/India/ASEAN volume picture and a fresh Mexican excise-tax headwind.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Leadership transition dominates the tone, not the substance.** The entire call is bookended by tributes to Quincey and introductions of Braun; strategic detail is explicitly deferred to CAGNY the following week ("I'll elaborate further... next week at CAGNY" — John Murphy said this three separate times), meaning this call is thinner on new information than usual.
2. **2025 volume was flat, and management is proud of that.** Braun frames it as resilience: "Over the past 50 years, annual volume declined only once, and that was during the pandemic." That's a low bar being presented as a high one — flat volume in a non-recession year against 4% pricing is the real underlying story.
3. **The Q4 mix miss gets explained away as noise.** John Murphy called the negative 3-point mix drag "the first time... to have 3 of those types of effects hitting us in the same quarter," attributing it to emerging-market growth outpacing developed markets, lower-margin category mix, and marketing timing — a plausible explanation, but one that conveniently arrives on the retiring CEO's last call.
4. **North America crossed 30% operating margin for the first time**, prompting a direct question about whether the company is "over-earning." Murphy's answer — ~60bps/year expansion averaged over 8 years, still room to run — implies more margin expansion ahead rather than reinvestment, a notable margin-over-volume signal for a "volume is a key priority" 2026 guide.
5. **FX flipped from years-long headwind to engineered tailwind.** After being "stuck at around $2 comparable earnings per share" for years due to currency, KO has now locked hedges that turn emerging-market currency weakness (Latin America, South Africa) into a 3-point EPS tailwind for 2026 — a deliberate treasury decision, not a market gift.

## Economy & consumer
- **Low-income U.S. consumer still under pressure**, unchanged from prior quarters — Braun: North America growth came "despite continued macroeconomic pressure on lower-income consumers."
- **SNAP policy change framed as manageable, not benign** — Quincey: "consumers should be allowed to choose, but regulation is regulation," expecting spending to reallocate across cash vs. SNAP credits rather than disappear.
- **Mexico gets a fresh, self-inflicted headwind**: a new excise tax "already implemented" at the start of 2026, met with RGM (revenue growth management) playbook learned from a prior 2014 Mexican tax increase.
- **China and India consumer softness is real and multi-quarter**: China consumer sentiment and spend remain "below pre-pandemic days" per Braun; India hit by "industry dynamics, weather" in 2025.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **North America: broad-based strength.** Trademark Coca-Cola, Sprite Zero, Fresca, Dasani, fairlife, BODYARMOR, and Powerade all grew volume; margin hit 30% for the first time.
- **Asia Pacific is the clear laggard**: flat volume for the segment, but revenue and profit both *declined* in Q4 — "Volume growth in Japan was offset by declines elsewhere, driven primarily by softer consumer spending, weaker industry performance."
- **Latin America and EMEA gained value share and grew volume/revenue** despite a "challenging external environment," aided by refillable packaging and value price points.
- **Two new billion-dollar brands added** (innocent, Santa Clara/Mexico dairy), bringing the portfolio to 32 — but Braun admitted "our innovation today is not where it needs to be" and success rates, while improved, aren't yet where the company wants.

## Investing & scarcity
- **2026 capex ~$2.2B**, roughly a quarter directed to still-owned bottling franchises (Africa, India), the rest largely growth capacity: concentrate lines and finished-goods capacity (notably fairlife) plus India cold-chain and digital ordering (Coke Buddy platform, agentic AI for SKU recommendations, still only reaching ~1/4 of addressable outlets).
- **Concentrate manufacturing capacity is the stated binding constraint** in some markets — Murphy: investment is needed "to shore up capacity for our concentrate business as it continues to be challenged in some areas to supply market needs."
- **Capital return stays disciplined**: dividend raised for a 63rd consecutive year (73% of adjusted FCF payout in 2025, near the 75% long-term target); buybacks limited to offsetting option dilution, with explicit optionality preserved pending the IRS tax case outcome.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: volume/price mix normalizes to roughly 50-50 in 2026** as India, China, and weak ASEAN/European markets "build back" through the year. **Action:** continued "ahead of the curve" bottler investment in India (described as "unprecedented" new-line capacity) and stepped-up Mexico World Cup marketing from Jan 1 — real spend behind the belief, though the recovery timing itself is unverified and pushed to "through the year."
- **Belief: FX will be a durable tailwind, not just a 2026 event.** **Action:** hedges locked specifically for 2026 under G10 currencies; emerging-market hedging explicitly limited by economics the further out you go — meaning the tailwind is a one-year commitment, not a structural repricing, despite Quincey's framing of "catching up" from years of currency drag.
- **Belief: North America margin has more room ("still tremendous opportunity").** **Action:** no specific new reinvestment commitment disclosed for North America beyond continuing existing supply-chain/marketing levers — a case where the stated ambition (further margin expansion) has real execution behind it but the volume-reinvestment tradeoff wasn't addressed directly.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Capital allocation beyond the dividend was explicitly left open** for 2026 — Murphy said the priority is "as much optionality as possible" pending the IRS tax case milestone expected late 2026/early 2027, rather than committing to specific buyback or M&A scale.
- **No specific magnitude given for India digital/B2B platform investment** — Braun described the Coke Buddy platform and agentic AI deployment qualitatively but declined to quantify capital committed.
- **Long-run FX outlook beyond 2026 dodged** — asked directly whether currency would be a similar tailwind in 2027, Murphy stuck to "our going-in position for the year" without extending the view, consistent with hedge economics not extending that far.

- **Market-by-market recovery mechanics** (India, China, Mexico) were probed repeatedly — analysts wanted specifics on what "gets better" and when; answers stayed largely qualitative ("build back... through the year") without hard sequencing.
- **North America margin sustainability** was directly questioned as potential over-earning; management reframed it as durable structural improvement rather than a one-off, without addressing the volume-reinvestment tradeoff.
- Compressed worry: **is the "all-weather" 4-5% growth algorithm for 2026 real underlying strength, or a currency-and-mix-assisted number papering over stalled volume in China, India, and now Mexico?**
