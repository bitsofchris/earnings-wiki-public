# DASH — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-05)

**The key idea:** DoorDash just told the Street to expect "several hundred million dollars" of incremental 2026 spend — on a new AI-native global tech stack, an in-house autonomous delivery vehicle, and a retailer-inventory-fulfillment buildout — while insisting nothing about its investment discipline has changed. The tension: management repeatedly deflected requests to quantify or bucket the spend precisely, even as it acknowledged some of these bets (tech platform, autonomy) have payback periods well outside the company's historical playbook.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **DoorDash is rearchitecting for an agentic/AI development paradigm, not just adding AI features.** Tony Xu: "if we were starting a company over again today, I think we would write software pretty differently... there's a lot of work in order to architect how we set up the architecture, so we can manage both agent workflows as well as how you would deploy software, test software, write software, what the role of the engineer is in that new paradigm." This is a direct signal of AI reshaping internal engineering org design — relevant to anyone tracking how AI-native companies restructure dev workflows.
2. **The real scarcity is retailer inventory data, not delivery capability.** DashMart Fulfillment Services exists because "not every store... knows their inventory" — DoorDash is vertically integrating warehousing/fulfillment to fix a data problem, not a logistics one. Xu pegs current retail SKU penetration at "single-digit percentage" of available inventory.
3. **Growth is accelerating into the investment, not despite it.** Fourth consecutive quarter of accelerating growth, record DashPass/Wolt+ subscriber adds, and "incremental margins over the last 8-quarter average above 7%" — management is framing 2026 spend as reinvestment of outperformance, the same playbook used for ads and new verticals historically.
4. **Deliveroo is performing ahead of the underwriting case, which is being used to justify more spend, not less.** Ravi Inukonda: Deliveroo's ~$200M EBITDA contribution and double-digit growth are "in line with what we underwrote," but growth "exceeding our expectations... is giving us the ability to reinvest back in the business" — a self-reinforcing logic that never produces a ceiling on investment.
5. **Autonomy remains multi-year and deliberately hedged.** Xu frames 2026 as merely "the year where we're ready to commercialize some of these efforts," explicitly warning "this is not something that's going to happen overnight" — a long-duration bet with no near-term revenue attached yet.

## Economy & consumer
- **No explicit macro commentary** — unusual for a consumer discretionary name of this scale; the closest proxy is repeated emphasis on affordability (SNAP benefit continuation, "most aggressive on making sure that we are the most affordable platform").
- **Consumer cohorts show no fatigue.** Ravi Inukonda: "even existing cohorts, cohorts that are quite old, they're also continuing to engage with us even more," alongside record DashPass and Wolt+ subscriber quarters.
- **New-category pull is organic, not promotional.** Growth in pets, electronics (gifting), health/beauty, and home improvement ("thousands of pounds of mulch per day") is attributed to underlying search/demand signals, not marketing pushes.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **U.S. restaurant marketplace is at a multi-year high**, with four straight quarters of accelerating growth on a larger base and incremental margins holding above the 7% historical average.
- **New verticals are growing fastest but still unprofitable**: "unit economics continue to improve... sequentially quarter-on-quarter as well as year-on-year," but breakeven timing is tied to scale, not a specific date — a soft commitment dressed as confidence.
- **Deliveroo integration carries a real, quantified accounting cost**: expect a "$8 million to $10 million impact" moving from Roo's EBITDA definition to DoorDash's, on top of "extra tech investments" to keep Roo's product stable mid-migration.
- **Ads hit $1B annualized revenue faster than any ad business in company history**, but management explicitly declines to treat it as pure margin flow-through — "every dollar that we generate, our goal is to reinvest back in the business."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Three investment buckets for 2026**: a unified AI-native global tech platform (replacing three redundant market-specific stacks across DoorDash/Wolt/Deliveroo), new product commercialization (DashMart Fulfillment, autonomy, SevenRooms/Going Out), and Deliveroo integration costs.
- **The binding constraint is data quality on the merchant side, not logistics or Dasher supply** — Dasher supply is explicitly called "never been healthier."
- **Spend is deliberately not itemized**: CFO says investment "up and down the P&L" spans cost of sales, sales & marketing, and R&D, without a clean split — making the "several hundred million" figure hard to independently verify or model.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: unified tech stack will eventually cut costs and free engineering capacity.** Action: temporarily *increasing* costs in 2026 by running duplicate cloud infrastructure across old and new stacks simultaneously — a real near-term cash cost for a benefit management says arrives later, undated.
- **Belief: retail/grocery will become as profitable as restaurants at scale**, citing DoorDash's 2021 grocery trajectory as the template. Action: building owned fulfillment infrastructure (DashMart Fulfillment Centers) customized per retailer with "no one-size-fits-all solution" — a slower, more capital-intensive path than the software-only playbook that worked for restaurants.
- **Belief: autonomy is a multimodal, multi-year platform opportunity.** Action: building DoorDash Dot in-house while also pursuing third-party partnerships — hedging the build-vs-partner decision rather than committing to one path, appropriate for the stage but notably non-committal for a "several hundred million dollar" spend year.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 guidance beyond a single directional margin comment** ("up slightly" ex-Deliveroo) — CFO repeatedly punts precision to the Q4 call: "we'll give more precision as we give quarterly guidance."
- **No fixed-cost-vs-variable-cost breakdown of the investment**, despite being asked directly twice; answer both times was qualitative ("some... cost of sales... some... R&D").
- **No single operating model for DashMart Fulfillment Services** — Xu insists each retailer relationship is bespoke, avoiding a scalable, repeatable structure being pinned down this early.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **The dominant thread was investment-clarity fatigue**: at least five separate questions (Deepak Mathivanan, Michael Morton, Nikhil Devnani, Lee Horowitz, and a follow-up from Ross Sandler) tried different angles to get a clean dollar/margin/payback breakdown of the 2026 spend and were met with qualitative reassurance rather than numbers.
- **Secondary cluster on Deliveroo mechanics**: EBITDA definitional reconciliation (~$8-10M), synergy timing, and integration risk — analysts probing whether the deal thesis is intact given the added spend.
- **A smaller cluster on external/regulatory risk**: Amazon's entry into perishables, and NYC political change (Mamdani win) raising questions about Dasher classification and pay — both answered with confidence but no specifics on exposure.
- **Compressed worry**: is DoorDash's "several hundred million dollar" 2026 step-up a disciplined reinvestment of genuine outperformance, or a signal that the payback math on AI-platform and autonomy bets is getting harder to underwrite and easier to obscure behind qualitative language?
