# ON — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-04)

**The key idea:** onsemi is calling the bottom of its cyclical downturn while simultaneously pitching a structural re-rating story — AI data center power (doubling in 2026), Treo-driven automotive content gains, and three years of "fab right" cost cuts converging to push gross margin toward a stated 50%+ long-term target. The tension: the recovery signal (lead times stretching, orders coming in "inside lead time") is real but automotive — the largest segment — is only just now shipping to "natural demand," not yet showing the allocation-driven strength management says is coming.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI data center beat its own raised bar.** Revenue grew 30% sequentially against a guide of "high teens," and management now guides to doubling FY2026 vs FY2025 (from ~$250M base) — "the strength is starting already to come in," per Hassane El-Khoury, driven by breadth across the power tree (low-voltage GPU-side power through high-voltage rack/UPS) rather than a single customer or technology.
2. **Automotive turned positive after seven quarters of decline — but it's a content story, not a units story.** China auto revenue grew YoY even as China passenger vehicle sales fell 6%; El-Khoury: "we absolutely leverage more content than SAR." Global SAR is roughly flat; the growth is silicon carbide penetration (~55% share of EVs shown at the Beijing Auto Show) and new zonal/10BASE-T1S Ethernet content that "did not exist about a year ago."
3. **Gross margin expansion is being sold as multi-year and additive: >200bps from fab-right actions, >200bps from mix, plus a further step from the 2022 fab divestitures flowing through around 2027 — CFO Thad Trent: "when you start to stack it up, you can get over 50%."** Q1's 38.5% (third straight sequential expansion) was driven mostly by utilization (77%, up quickly within the quarter) rather than pricing, which hasn't hit the P&L yet.
4. **Non-core business exits are on a fixed glide path to zero by end of 2026** (~$50M Q1, $30-40M Q2, ~$300M annualized), unchanged from plan — a rare instance of management holding a self-imposed revenue headwind steady rather than softening it as the top line improves.
5. **Allocation and lead-time signals (23→26 weeks) are emerging but concentrated, not systemic** — El-Khoury: "I am starting to get calls. We are on allocation in certain technologies," while explicitly denying any channel or automotive inventory rebuild is underway.

## Economy & consumer
- **Cycle-bottom language, backed by leading indicators.** "Book to bill... order pattern, lead times, et cetera, all these are pointing in the right direction," and unlike last year, "second half [will] outgrow the first half" — a directional call management says is "not the sentiment that I personally had last year."
- **Lead times stretching for the first time in the cycle:** 23 weeks (Q4) → 26 weeks (Q1), alongside a rise in expedited and inside-lead-time orders — read as demand outrunning supply, not restocking.
- **No end-consumer read** — ON sells to OEMs/hyperscalers, not consumers; the closest proxy is China EV/auto strength, which management frames as export- and content-driven, not a domestic demand surge (China passenger vehicle volumes fell 6% YoY in Q1).

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **AI data center and Treo are the clear engines:** Treo revenue up 2.5x sequentially with wins spanning zonal architectures, ADAS, AI-client power, and humanoid position sensing; the GaN design funnel now exceeds $1.5B with 10 products sampling and revenue starting 2027.
- **Energy storage/microgrid is reaccelerating:** guided to >40% revenue growth in 2026 with share approaching 60%, "renewed growth... from China to North America," aided by falling battery prices and rising grid energy costs.
- **Industrial and AMG remain soft spots:** industrial revenue fell 6% sequentially (though ahead of expectations) and AMG segment revenue was down 5% YoY — the recovery is uneven, concentrated in AI/auto-content rather than broad industrial.
- **Manufacturing utilization jumped to 77%**, driven by management deliberately ramping fast to chase demand signals — a real-time capacity response rather than a slow reopening.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital discipline stays tight even as growth resumes:** CapEx guided at just 1.4% of revenue in Q1 ($22M) and capital intensity is expected to stay mid-single-digit % of revenue "for the foreseeable future" — management says they need revenue 25-30% above current levels before needing new capacity, since prior-cycle investment (e.g., East Fishkill for Treo) is only now being utilized.
- **Scarcity is narrow and technology-specific**, not company-wide: allocation is happening in select technologies while automotive — the biggest segment — is still described as "shipping to natural demand" with no recovery-driven allocation pressure yet.
- **Pricing is a live, asymmetric lever:** input costs (energy, commodities) are already hitting the P&L, but offsetting price increases haven't shown up yet — CFO: "we're already seeing the impact of input costs being higher in the P&L, although we're not seeing the impact of the higher pricing yet."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** H2 2026 will outgrow H1, AI data center revenue doubles for the year, and gross margin reaches a step-function higher trajectory in H2 as pricing catches up and utilization climbs.
- **Action backing it up:** utilization was raised quickly within Q1 to meet demand (not a passive wait-and-see), non-core exits are being executed on the original timeline (no slippage despite improving conditions), and buybacks are being sized to opportunistic dislocations ($346M at $60.54, ~160% of FCF) rather than a fixed program.
- **Gap worth flagging:** management is confident about a structural AI/Treo/margin re-rating story but is unwilling to quantify AI data center revenue by product line or customer type, and CapEx spending shows no acceleration despite claiming a "major player in power for AI data center" ambition — the balance-sheet commitment (capex, inventory) lags the verbal conviction.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to give H2 guidance framed as a seasonality percentage:** "I don't wanna guide the outlook from a seasonality perspective," offering only directional confidence that H2 outgrows H1.
- **Declined to break out AI data center revenue by technology (GaN vs SiC vs Treo) or by customer type (GPU/XPU vs hyperscaler):** "We're not breaking it out to that level by product family... It's across the board really."
- **Non-committal on inventory strategy going forward:** on the just-in-time vs just-in-case question, El-Khoury deflected to "that's all cool... it's irrelevant what model the industry is gonna end up landing at," declining to predict how OEMs (or ON itself) will resolve balance-sheet inventory risk.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Durability of the upturn dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pressed on whether 2026 repeats 2025's pattern of a strong first half fading into a soft second half; management pushed back firmly, distinguishing this cycle by pointing to concrete ramping programs (AI data center, China SiC, industrial recovery) rather than just sentiment.
- **AI data center sizing and scalability** was a recurring theme — analysts wanted absolute revenue context, technology mix, and whether ON needs M&A to compete; CEO pointed to 2025's Aurasemi and SiC JFET acquisitions as sufficient without committing to more deals.
- **Gross margin cadence** — analysts explicitly asked for "stair steps" vs "slow linear ramp"; CFO promised larger step functions in H2 but tied them to pricing catching up, an outcome not yet visible in the data.
- **Inventory anxiety and direct sourcing** — several questions probed whether OEMs are starting to hold their own inventory (bypassing tier ones) amid stretching lead times; management confirmed early signs (allocation calls, one OEM's shift to direct semiconductor sourcing) but said broad automotive inventory building hasn't started.
- **Compressed worry:** is this recovery's strength (lead times, allocation, pricing power) durable and broad enough to justify the margin re-rating story, or is it still concentrated enough in AI/China-EV content that a normal seasonal automotive-and-industrial world could undercut it in H2?
