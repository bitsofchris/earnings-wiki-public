# PLTR — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-04)

**The key idea:** This was less an earnings call than a victory lap — record growth, record margins, record bookings — wrapped in an unusually combative ideological framing from Karp positioning Palantir as the sole legitimate winner of enterprise AI while dismissing "analyst friends" wholesale. Underneath the theater, the actual substance is real: US commercial TCV up 6x on a duration-weighted basis, 121% commercial growth, and a genuine claim that AI FDE agents are letting the company scale delivery without proportional headcount growth.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The growth numbers are extraordinary and largely unchallenged.** 63% total revenue growth, 77% US growth, 121% US commercial growth, a record $2.8B TCV quarter, and a Rule of 40 of 114% (up 20 points sequentially) — none of these figures were seriously interrogated by analysts on the call.
2. **AI FDE is the internal proof point management leans on hardest.** Shyam Sankar's claim that "2 human FDEs spawned an army of AI FDEs to migrate a customer off their legacy data warehouse in 5 days, something that would have taken an army of SIs up to 2 years" is offered as evidence the labor-scarcity constraint on delivery is being solved by their own product — a claim worth watching for repeatability, not just one anecdote.
3. **Deal size and speed are compounding, not just deal count.** A "leading medical device manufacturer" expanded ACV eightfold just 5 months into an initial contract — management frames this land-and-expand acceleration, not new logo growth, as the real engine of US commercial's 121% growth.
4. **Karp's remarks were unusually adversarial toward the analyst community and explicitly political** (fentanyl, immigration, ICE, "anti-woke" positioning), which is notable context for anyone reading the numbers independently of the rhetoric — the ideological framing risks obscuring rather than clarifying the underlying unit economics.
5. **No 2026 guidance was given, deliberately.** Despite the triumphant tone, management drew a hard line at forward-year guidance, leaving the durability of the current growth rate an open question the call didn't attempt to answer.

## Economy & consumer
- **Bifurcated AI economy is the explicit macro frame.** Ryan Taylor: "organizations are embracing an undeniable truth. Real enterprise AI at scale requires Palantir" — companies without a "compounding AI leverage" platform are cast as structurally falling behind.
- **Europe is repeatedly cited as the drag / counterexample.** Karp: "look how poorly Europe is doing. Look how well America is doing" — international commercial grew only 10% YoY vs. 121% for US commercial, the widest geographic gap disclosed on the call.
- **No direct consumer read** — Palantir sells B2B/B2G; the closest consumer-adjacent claim is Karp's assertion that AI-driven enterprise gains are flowing to "the American worker," which is a political framing, not a measured outcome.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US commercial is the standout.** $397M revenue (+121% YoY, +29% Q/Q), $1.3B in TCV bookings alone (+342% YoY), surpassing $1B in a single quarter for the first time; customer count up 65% YoY to 530.
- **US government remains solid but comparatively pedestrian.** +52% YoY, +14% Q/Q — strong in absolute terms but the growth-rate gap versus commercial (121%) is now stark, and commercial has outgrown government for four consecutive quarters.
- **Net dollar retention rose to 134%** (+600bps Q/Q), though management flags this metric structurally lags the business — it excludes revenue from customers acquired in the past 12 months, understating recent velocity.
- **Headcount discipline claimed as a feature.** Sankar: "our headcount has grown roughly 10%, but revenue grew 63%" — attributed to internal AI FDE productivity gains rather than hiring freezes.
- **Adjusted expense still grew 29% YoY**, undercutting the "we need fewer people" narrative somewhat — the efficiency story is real on revenue-per-head but expenses are still climbing briskly alongside it.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Product investment concentrated in three areas**: AI FDE (agentic development inside AIP), AI Hivemind (multi-agent problem-solving, originally built for classified government use, now being extended to commercial supply-chain bottleneck-solving), and Edge Ontology (lightweight ontology for mobile/embedded devices — drones, robots).
- **Sales force is reportedly shrinking, not growing**, per Karp — an unusual claim for a company posting its highest-ever bookings quarter, offered as evidence that delivery/product quality is now doing the selling rather than headcount.
- **The scarce resource, by their own account, isn't compute or capital — it's skilled deployment capacity**, and AI FDE is positioned as directly manufacturing more of it: "we've made our FDEs wildly more productive... and we decided to give it to our customers."
- **No mention of GPU/compute supply constraints or cloud infrastructure costs** on this call — notably absent for a company this deep in enterprise AI deployment, suggesting Palantir's bottleneck is genuinely more about integration/delivery labor than raw compute, unlike hyperscaler or model-lab peers.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: enterprise AI is shifting from single use-cases to full-organization reorgs.** Ryan Taylor: customers are "coming to us saying, how do we deploy this across our entire organization, how do we reorganize our entire organization around Palantir and AIP."
- **Action backing it: raised full-year guidance sharply** — FY25 revenue guide raised to $4.396–4.400B (a $252M/8-point increase over last quarter's guide) and US commercial guidance raised to 104%+ growth (a 19-point jump from the prior quarter's guide) — real, near-term commitments consistent with the stated belief.
- **Belief: the US Army's shift to consolidate on Vantage signals broader government platform-standardization momentum.** Sankar frames the Army memo as "not merely a technical decision, but a cultural decision."
- **Action gap: no discussion of increased government-specific headcount, contract structure changes, or capital committed to capture this** — the Army/Golden Dome opportunity was discussed enthusiastically but the actual investment behind capturing it wasn't detailed, unlike the concrete commercial guidance raise.
- **Belief in continued compounding was explicit, but the year-ahead commitment was deliberately withheld** — the gap between "wildly enthusiastic" rhetoric and refusing 2026 guidance is itself the most visible talk-vs-action gap on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No FY2026 guidance whatsoever.** Karp: "we are obviously not going to forecast for next year" — a sharp contrast with the otherwise maximalist confidence of the call.
- **Golden Dome and other named government opportunities were acknowledged but not quantified.** Sankar: "I can't comment on all the opportunities you mentioned there" — enthusiasm without specifics.
- **Strategic commercial contracts (the smaller, arguably lower-quality revenue bucket) were flagged as shrinking and de-emphasized** — guided to just $2-4M in Q4 (down from $9.6M a year ago) and under 0.5% of full-year revenue, effectively conceding this line no longer matters to the growth story.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Only two questions were fielded, both from sell-side analysts, and both were essentially invitations for management to elaborate on the good news rather than probes for weakness — one on what's driving accelerated commercial sales-cycle compression, one combining a commercial follow-up with a Golden Dome/government upside question.
- **No questions touched competitive threats, valuation, margin sustainability, or the sales-force/headcount claims** — a notably soft Q&A session for a company reporting numbers this extreme.
- **The compressed worry, if there is one beneath the celebration**: can 121% commercial growth and a 114% Rule of 40 possibly be durable, and does government TCV (with its termination-for-convenience clauses explicitly flagged by the CFO) convert as reliably as the commercial narrative implies? No one asked it directly, but the deliberate refusal to guide 2026 leaves it hanging.
