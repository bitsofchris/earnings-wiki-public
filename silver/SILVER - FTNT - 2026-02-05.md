# FTNT — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-05)

**The key idea:** Fortinet is running a real-time pricing-power test: memory costs are spiking, and rather than absorb the hit, management is raising appliance prices 5-20% monthly on 30-day notice, betting that a 5-10x ASIC performance advantage and a unified single-OS platform (firewall + SD-WAN + SASE) makes customers price-inelastic. Unified SASE billings at +40% is doing the heavy lifting for both the quarter and the 2026 growth narrative, while SecOps quietly decelerated and got waved off as "noise."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Infrastructure/capex spend roughly triples on an annualized basis:** guided to $350-450M for full-year 2026 vs. $80-120M for Q1 alone — a meaningful step-up tied to FortiCloud build-out and AI data center security infrastructure.
- **Binding constraint is memory/component cost and lead time**, not compute, power, or headcount: "10% to 20% cost come from the memory," offset by ~6 months of inventory buffer and direct-manufacture relationships that bypass third-party distributors.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** service revenue growth reaccelerates in H2 2026 as 2025's product revenue strength flows through the ratable-recognition base, and secure networking will be "surpassed" as the primary category by "unified" security+networking convergence by end of 2026.
- **Action:** they're not waiting passively — raising prices now to protect margin ahead of the services recovery, expanding SASE PoP infrastructure, and pre-committing $350-450M of 2026 capex to AI/data-center security infrastructure. The gap: the pricing actions are justified by *current* memory costs, but management also frames them as durable margin support into a *future* services recovery that hasn't materialized yet — two different justifications for the same lever.
- **Capital return as a stated-confidence signal:** $1B increase in buyback authorization (to ~$1.4B remaining) approved in January, plus voluntary repayment of a $500M debt tranche at maturity rather than refinancing — both actions that reduce optionality in exchange for a clear signal of balance-sheet confidence.
