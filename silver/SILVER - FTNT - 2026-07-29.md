# FTNT — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Fortinet is rebranding its core business — merging Secure Networking and Unified SASE into a single "SASE firewall" category — to claim a TAM two to three times larger than cloud-only SASE, betting that AI-driven east-west traffic and data-sovereignty demands favor its on-premise/ASIC-heavy architecture over cloud-native competitors. The numbers back the story so far: billings and product revenue are **accelerating for a second straight quarter**, not just holding steady.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- Ken Xie is coining a new category, the **"SASE firewall,"** explicitly comparing it to how UTM/next-gen firewall replaced traditional firewalls 20 years ago — a deliberate attempt to redefine the market Fortinet competes in rather than just win share in existing ones.
- The bet is that **cloud-only SASE has a ceiling**: "the cloud-only SASE solution cannot meet the customer requirement" for data privacy and local processing, and Fortinet claims to be the only vendor with all SASE components built in-house on one OS (FortiOS) plus on-prem "Sovereign SASE."
- Just-launched **FortiGate 1200G** targets exactly this seam — local enforcement plus cloud-delivered security — aimed at AI infrastructure and data-sovereignty buyers, especially concentrated in EMEA and public sector.
- AI data center wins are scaling: an **eight-figure deal** with a new GPU-cloud provider this quarter builds directly on a seven-figure win last quarter with the same customer — a concrete signal of AI infrastructure spend translating into security infrastructure spend.
- 26-year investment in custom **ASIC silicon** (via FortiASIC and a deepening Intel partnership) is positioned as the moat behind price/performance claims, with Xie noting paybacks that "may take 10 years" — a rare admission of how long this bet took to pay off.
- Management explicitly frames Fortinet as now competing as a **platform provider** alongside Palo Alto and CrowdStrike, but with a differentiated focus — network security-first (not endpoint-first) and organically built rather than acquired.

## What's changing
- **Machine-to-machine traffic now exceeds human traffic** on the internet per Xie — the stated root cause behind accelerating east-west/data-center demand, and the justification for merging Secure Networking and SASE into one narrative.
- Service revenue growth is inflecting: management says **Q1 2026 was the trough**, and service billings growth (26%) and deferred revenue growth (17%) point to renewed acceleration, attributed to bundling SD-WAN, SASE, and FortiGuard services together.
- **FortiSASE billings more than doubled year-over-year**, with installed-base penetration reaching 90% of large enterprises — fast even by the company's own accelerating standards.
- Non-GAAP operating margin hit a **second-quarter record of 38%**, up nearly 5 points, attributed partly to internal AI tooling reducing headcount growth needs in support functions.
- Pricing is being managed dynamically: **high-single-digit** price impact baked into H2 billings guidance, with Xie noting prices move both up and down with component costs (memory) in real time to preserve gross margin and avoid channel inventory buildup — a notably transparent mechanic.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- The **"SASE firewall" terminology itself is an admitted gray area** — Xie concedes deals are "difficult to categorize whether it's a SASE deal or it's a Secure Networking deal," meaning the reported pillar-level growth splits (34% vs 35%) may partly be an artifact of how bundled FortiGate deals get bucketed, not a clean read on demand by category.
- No mention of **endpoint or cloud-native (CNAPP) competition** dynamics beyond the brief CrowdStrike/Palo Alto comparison — the call stays narrowly on network security turf where Fortinet is strongest, sidestepping where it's weaker.
- Despite the AI-security framing throughout, there's **no concrete AI product revenue disclosure** — "20+ AI-enabled solutions" and "AI-driven SecOps" billings (25% growth, the slowest of the three pillars) are cited but not broken out from legacy SecOps consolidation upsell.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Durability of the acceleration and whether it's pull-forward or supply-driven · pricing power versus real demand, and how much of billings growth is price increases versus units · visibility into 2027 product-revenue comps against a 50%+ base this year · sizing of the OT/data-center attach opportunity · whether refresh cycles are simply shortening due to AI-driven ASIC obsolescence rather than net-new demand · underlying worry: is this a genuine multi-year architectural shift toward on-prem/sovereign security, or a cyclical AI-capex sugar high that reverses once hyperscaler build-outs plateau.
