# NOW — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-22)

**The key idea:** ServiceNow is repositioning itself mid-flight from a workflow/ITSM company into a cybersecurity company, using the Armis and Veza acquisitions plus an "AI Control Tower" narrative to argue it's the governance layer every enterprise agentic AI deployment needs. The numbers back the pitch — beats across every metric, AI ACV over a first-time threshold — but the CEO's answer to nearly every question, including ones not about security, was a pivot back to cyber, which is itself a tell about where the growth story now lives.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Cyber is being repositioned as the primary growth engine, not an accessory.** McDermott: "cybersecurity will be bigger than ServiceNow is in the next few years," and predicted the company moves from the "eighth-largest cybersecurity business" toward "contending for the top spot" — a striking claim for a company whose core has always been ITSM/workflow.
- **AI Control Tower is the wedge product tying agentic governance to the security narrative.** It combines CMDB heritage with Veza (identity across human/machine/AI) and Armis (device visibility) to give customers a "kill switch" for rogue agents — over 500 customers live in six months, explicitly framed as the reason enterprises won't build their own agent governance in-house ("5x to 10x" more expensive to DIY).
- **A new AI-native, ticketless product line is coming imminently.** A "conversational service desk experience, no tickets" aimed at the long tail ("Fortune 500,000") to "bypass the startups" — already in beta, described as "the first of many net new AI native products."
- **Voice is called out as a new, unexpected frontier.** A large airline is running 5 million annual voice calls through ServiceNow's voice AI CRM agents in production — used as proof the platform can absorb entirely new interaction channels, not just ticket-based workflows.
- **Labor arbitrage is now explicit in the sales pitch for L1 AI specialists**, not just an efficiency talking point. Zavery: agents resolve "80%-85% of service requests without any human interaction," and framed the value proposition partly as "we also get to monetize the labor cost, which employers have to pay the employees."

## What's changing
- **Pricing power is real but capped by design.** ProPlus SKU uplift is running "above 30%," new AI-native SKU uplift lands in the pre-announced 20%-30% band — management is choosing predictable hybrid seat+consumption pricing over outcome-based pricing explicitly to avoid what they frame as competitors' (implicitly Salesforce's) harder-to-manage metrics.
- **Contract durations are lengthening, not shortening**, with several marquee five-year deals (Experian, Tech Mahindra, Maybank) cited as evidence — management points to this directly in RPO/cRPO growth as a rebuttal to fears that AI pricing would compress deal terms.
- **Sales headcount growth outpaced last year's full-year total already**, attributed to a mix of M&A inheritance (Moveworks/Veza/Armis) and deliberate front-loading of quota-bearing reps ahead of the new pricing/packaging launch — margin discipline is asserted (headcount returning to pre-acquisition levels by 2027) but not yet demonstrated.
- **Gross margin is taking near-term pressure from two supposedly good problems**: faster-than-planned hyperscaler ramp and rising AI/token consumption, both framed as diluting near-term margin in exchange for scale benefits later — operating margin was still protected this quarter by pulling cost elsewhere (marketing spend timing).

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **The Q2 beat was flattered by a one-time timing shift, not fully organic.** Roughly half the cRPO/net-new-ACV outperformance came from U.S. federal on-prem revenue pulled forward from Q3 into Q2 — management called this out unprompted, which is honest, but it also means the full-year guide raise was smaller than the headline beat implied, and CFO Mastantuono explicitly cited "conservatism" for the back half.
- **No analyst asked, and management didn't volunteer, hard renewal-rate or churn detail on the newly acquired businesses (Armis, Veza, Moveworks)** — all commentary on those assets was strategic/qualitative, not unit-economic.
- **The obsessive redirection of every answer toward cybersecurity is itself notable.** Even when asked about deal-cycle elongation, sales headcount, or gross margin, McDermott routed back to the cyber narrative — a possible sign the "core" ITSM/ITOM story alone wasn't seen as compelling enough to carry the quarter on its own.
- **Competitive detail on the AI-native/ticketless product is thin.** It was announced as imminent with "several customers in beta" but no pricing, name, or customer count was given — a preview timed for the earnings call more than a shippable product update.
- **No mention of margin drag or integration costs from three acquisitions closing in rapid succession** beyond a broad promise to return headcount to pre-deal levels by 2027 — the actual integration friction (systems, GTM overlap, retention of acquired sales talent) went unaddressed.
