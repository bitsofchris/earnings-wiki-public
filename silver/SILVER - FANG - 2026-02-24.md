# FANG — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-24)

**The key idea:** This call was a coming-out party for Diamondback's Barnett/Woodford resource expansion — a ~900-location position built for free through leasing rather than M&A — but the subtext is cost discipline: at $1,000/ft the play doesn't compete with core Midland economics, and management spent the hour explaining the path to $800/ft rather than claiming victory. Underneath the new-play excitement, the company is still holding production flat on a "yellow light" macro stance, funding growth optionality (surfactants, Barnett) almost entirely out of efficiency gains rather than new capital.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The Barnett is a call option, not a 2026 growth driver.** It's getting nearly all the airtime but only $150M of a $3.75B budget; CEO Kaes Van't Hof calls the imbalance out himself: "we have a $3.75 billion budget and $150 million is allocated to the Barnett, but it's getting all the airtime."
2. **Cost curve, not resource quality, is the gating issue.** Al Barkmann: Barnett EURs are running ~75 BO/ft vs. ~50 BO/ft in core Midland — a 50% uplift — but well costs are roughly double core ($1,000/ft vs. $510-520/ft), so the entire investment case hinges on execution learning curve (multi-well pads, simul-frac, 15,000-ft laterals) rather than subsurface risk.
3. **Surfactants are a cheap, real production lever they're still learning to use.** A 60-well test run in six months (idea to execution) delivered roughly 100 bbl/d average uplift for ~$0.5M — "a high-returning project" — but management is explicit it's early ("we don't even know how much of the wellbore we're touching today") and not yet baked into new-well type curves.
4. **The macro stance is cautiously less bearish, not bullish.** Van't Hof's stoplight framing has shifted from closer to "red light" (cut production) six months ago toward the middle, driven by oversupply fears not materializing as fast as expected — but the company is explicitly not calling a "green light" (grow production) and 2026 CapEx guidance still assumes flat output.
5. **M&A is winding down in favor of organic resource capture.** With basin consolidation reducing the deal pipeline ("the RBC website continues to shrink in terms of the number of tickers"), Diamondback is pivoting spend toward extracting more from existing acreage — Barnett, surfactants, lateral-length growth — rather than buying the next operator.

## Economy & consumer
- **Oil macro cautiously de-risking.** Van't Hof: "there were periods of time over the last 6 months where we were all much closer to the red light scenario in terms of crude price," attributing the shift to oversupply fears not materializing as aggressively as forecast.
- **Still no green light.** Despite improved sentiment, 2026 CapEx guide is set toward the low end of the quarterly range and production is being held flat as the base case — the company won't commit to organic growth until the macro clears further.
- **No consumer exposure** — upstream E&P sells into commodity markets, not to end consumers.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Core Midland execution keeps compounding.** Leading-edge completed feet/day reached 4,500 (some fleets above 5,500) via continuous pumping across simul-frac fleets, and 2025 oil productivity per foot rose versus 2024 in "many basins" even as the industry broadly sees per-foot productivity degrade.
- **Barnett not yet economic at scale.** Current $1,000/ft well cost needs to fall to ~$800/ft before returns become "competitive"; full-field development doesn't start until H2 2026.
- **OpEx headwinds are modest but real.** 2026 LOE/GP&T guided slightly higher on unhedged power price increases in the basin (~$0.10-0.20/unit), rising workover/P&A spend, and CPI escalators on gas transport contracts — offset partly by the sale of the EDS system to Deep Blue.
- **Reserve write-down is a valuation-timing artifact, not an operating problem.** CEO attributes the impairment to marking the Endeavor deal (booked at $80 oil/$4 Henry Hub) against a ~$64 average realized price in 2025, insisting "there have not been meaningful changes to the reserve report" on a PDP performance basis.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Inventory duration is the real constraint, openly acknowledged.** "We're in a depleting business, right? And we think about inventory every day" — the company reports ~20 years of inventory at 2026 pace and grew average lateral length ~600 ft/well in 2025 without acquisitions or capital raises.
- **Capital allocation stays concentrated in the core.** The Barnett gets only ~4% of the 2026 budget; the bulk continues funding the proven Midland core, with cost-out initiatives (drilling cycle time, completion efficiency) treated as the primary lever for freeing up future capital.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: Permian gas takeaway (2027-2030) improves Barnett economics and gas realizations.** **Action:** building a dedicated gas marketing strategy and exploring hyperscaler/data-center power purchase agreements to capture "material uplift to nat gas pricing" — but nothing has been announced, and management says it won't discuss specifics "until it's completely binding."
- **Belief: Barnett will become a meaningful part of the development program.** **Action:** ramping from ~30 wells drilled in 2026 (only ~10 completed) to ~100 gross wells in 2027, with separate dedicated rig lines and upgraded rig packages already being sourced — a real capital commitment building ahead of the cost target being hit.
- **Belief: growth could eventually become "the output" of efficient capital allocation.** **Action:** none yet — CEO explicitly says "that day is not today," keeping 2026 CapEx flat/declining rather than growth-oriented.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 growth rate or long-term sustainable growth target** — Van't Hof calls it "highly dependent on the macro" and stays inside the yellow-light framing rather than giving a number.
- **No detail on hyperscaler/data-center deal economics** — "we're not going to announce anything until it's completely binding and we can talk to our investors about what it means for them."
- **Declined to claim surfactants are lifting new-well EURs** — on Viper (mineral) data specifically, Van't Hof says "I don't know if we're seeing enough yet... to make that distinction."
- **No commitment on whether CapEx will actually come down this year** even if Barnett costs and surfactant results trend favorably — called a "goal," not guidance, "given our history of conservatism."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Barnett/Woodford dominated nearly every question**: well costs and path to $800/ft, EUR uplift, working interest (lowest in the portfolio at 64%), rig strategy, geographic variability, and nomenclature/reservoir mechanics — a near-single-topic call.
- **Secondary cluster on capital discipline and inventory**: sustainable growth rate, inventory replenishment philosophy, DUC backlog, and reserve revisions (mostly price-driven, some PUD reshuffling from bolt-on deals).
- **Tariffs/input costs got one direct question**, with casing (OCTG) inflation flagged as sticky even amid tariff uncertainty, while tubing pricing has held.
- **Dodged/deferred**: growth rate guidance, hyperscaler deal terms, and whether cost savings will actually flow into lower 2026 CapEx.
- **Compressed worry:** can the Barnett actually get cheap enough, fast enough, to matter — or is this six years of "flavor of the day" optionality dressed up as resource expansion?
