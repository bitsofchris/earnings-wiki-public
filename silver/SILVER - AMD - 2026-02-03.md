# AMD — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-03)

**The key idea:** AMD closed out a record 2025 (revenue +34%, FCF nearly doubled) and is pivoting the entire narrative to 2H26, when MI450/Helios is supposed to convert years of customer engagement — anchored by the OpenAI six-gigawatt deal — into an AI-revenue inflection. The tension: nothing about the AI ramp shows up in hard numbers yet; it's all "on track," "going well," and OpEx that keeps outrunning its own guide.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Server CPUs, not GPUs, are 2026's most concrete growth driver.** Fifth-gen EPYC (Turin) already crossed half of server revenue, and Lisa Su said the CPU order book has "continued to strengthen... especially over the last sixty days," with CPU revenue guided up sequentially into a quarter that's seasonally down.
2. **The AI story is entirely forward-loaded to 2H26.** MI450/Helios revenue "will start in the third quarter, but it will ramp significant volume in the fourth quarter" — meaning the whole "tens of billions in 2027" thesis rests on execution that hasn't happened yet, just "deep co-development" and OEM (HPE, Lenovo) rack announcements.
3. **China is being deliberately underwritten, not modeled.** AMD booked $100M of MI308 China revenue for Q1 but explicitly won't forecast anything beyond that "just because it's a very dynamic situation" — a live regulatory/geopolitical variable management refuses to price in.
4. **OpEx discipline is a promise, not yet a pattern.** Stacy Rasgon called out that OpEx keeps "coming in even higher" than guided every quarter; Su's answer was that 2025's overspend was "for all the right reasons" and leverage should appear "especially as we get into the second half of the year" — the same half everything else depends on.
5. **Gaming/semi-custom is whipsawing guidance credibility.** 2025 semi-custom was guided flat and grew 50%; 2026 is now guided down "significant double-digit" in "the seventh year" of the console cycle — a reminder that AMD's segment-level guides have missed materially in both directions recently.

## Economy & consumer
- **AI-driven CPU demand is the macro tailwind AMD is leaning on hardest.** Su ties surging EPYC demand directly to agentic AI workloads needing head-node and parallel-task compute, not just GPU-adjacent scaling.
- **PC market is softening on cost, not demand.** "We're probably seeing the PC TAM down a bit just given some of the inflationary pressures of the commodities pricing, including memory," with 2H26 modeled subseasonal to 1H — a rare admission of macro drag rather than share dynamics.
- **No direct consumer-spending commentary** — AMD doesn't sell to end consumers directly; all consumer signal is inferred through PC OEM/channel sell-through, which stayed strong through Q4 holiday season despite the 2026 caution.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Data center segment hit a record $5.4B, +39% YoY,** with record server CPU sales to both cloud and enterprise and record share exit-rate; large on-prem EPYC deployments "more than doubled" in 2025.
- **Client revenue hit a record $3.1B, +34% YoY,** desktop CPU sales set records for a fourth straight quarter, and commercial notebook/desktop sell-through grew "more than 40% year over year" — enterprise, not just gaming/consumer, is now the growth engine.
- **Gaming's 50% YoY growth is a fragile, semi-custom-driven number** that management is guiding to reverse hard in 2026 as the console cycle ages into year seven.
- **Embedded stayed nearly flat (+3% YoY)** but design wins hit a record $17B for the year, "up nearly 20%," suggesting the segment's growth is queued but not yet landed — Jean Hu explicitly calls for "a return to growth" in 2026.
- **Gross margin got a real assist from a $306M inventory-reserve release** tied to previously written-down MI308 inventory; ex-that and ex-China revenue, underlying gross margin was ~55%, up a more modest 80bps YoY — the headline 57% overstates organic margin improvement.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is concentrated on MI450/Helios and Venice,** with MI500 (cDNA6, 2nm, HBM4e) already disclosed for a 2027 launch — AMD is telegraphing a full generation ahead, unusual candor about roadmap depth.
- **Supply chain is the explicit constraint, not demand.** AMD says it has "increased supply capacity capability for server CPUs" over the last several quarters and is doing "multiyear agreements" with suppliers "given the tightness of the supply chain" — an admission that wafer/HBM availability, not orders, has been gating growth.
- **Memory cost inflation is bleeding into client economics** — the same tightness helping AMD's AI upside is squeezing PC gross margins and TAM, a scarcity dynamic cutting both ways within the same company.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** >60% data-center revenue CAGR is "certainly possible in 2026," tens of billions in AI revenue by 2027. **Action:** the only hard commitments cited are the OpenAI six-gigawatt multi-generation deal and OEM rack partnerships (HPE, Lenovo) — real, but still pre-revenue infrastructure commitments, not shipped volume.
- **Belief:** OpEx will show leverage as AI revenue inflects in 2H26. **Action:** Q1 non-GAAP OpEx is guided to ~$3.05B, continuing the same aggressive-investment posture as 2025 — the leverage claim is asserted for a future quarter, not demonstrated in the guide being given today.
- **Belief:** the PC market will be soft in 2026. **Action:** AMD is still investing to grow PC share in enterprise and the premium tier even in a shrinking TAM, betting execution beats the macro rather than pulling back.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No China MI308 revenue forecast beyond the $100M already booked for Q1** — Su called it explicitly "a very dynamic situation" with MI325 export licenses still pending, refusing to bake any resolution into guidance.
- **No segment-level AI revenue disclosure.** When pressed for a full-year 2025 Instinct number, Su declined and instead pointed analysts to infer sequential growth trends themselves.
- **No firm gross-margin framework for the MI450 ramp** — Jean Hu repeatedly deferred detail ("we'll give you more color when we get there"), leaving the margin impact of the biggest 2026 catalyst unquantified.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **OpEx credibility** dominated (Stacy Rasgon): AMD has guided OpEx up and then beaten that guide higher every quarter, and the promised leverage is still a 2H26 story, not evidenced today.
- **Supply chain risk** came up from multiple angles — wafer/HBM procurement timelines, server CPU capacity, and rack-level bottleneck risk heading into the MI450 ramp; AMD insists it will not be supply-limited but offered no independent verification.
- **Competitive positioning** — x86 vs. ARM in agentic workloads, and Nvidia's move to sell ARM CPUs separately plus rumored SRAM/spatial-architecture deals — Su's answers were confident but largely reasserted AMD's existing triplet-architecture pitch rather than engaging with the specific competitive threats raised.
- **Gaming/semi-custom volatility** and China MI308 uncertainty were the two areas where guidance itself, not just execution, is genuinely unsettled.
- **Compressed worry:** the entire 2026-2027 AI growth story is underwritten by supply-chain execution and OpEx discipline that haven't yet been demonstrated — the street is asking AMD to prove a promise, and AMD is answering with more promises.
