# V — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** Visa posted its best net revenue growth since 2022, powered by value-added services and commercial/money-movement businesses both growing in the mid-20s%, but management's own CFO concedes a chunk of that beat is deal timing and one-off performance adjustments rather than durable trend. Layered on top is an increasingly elaborate story about Visa becoming the "hyperscaler of payments" for AI agents and stablecoins — long on strategic framing (network, security, trust), notably short on concrete unit economics or committed risk-sharing rules.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

2. **Trust, not compute, is the stated bottleneck for agentic commerce.** CEO Ryan McInerney explicitly contrasts Visa's framing with the industry's default answer: "there's a lot of talk around AI in general... people talk about compute, and they talk about power... I think the limiting factor for agentic commerce is trust." This is a company betting its network/brand moat matters more than infrastructure in the AI payments race.
3. **Fraud demand is a real, current signal, not just AI hype.** Management describes fraud as now a "top three, top four concern" for CEOs of issuer/acquirer/merchant clients — "that just wasn't the case... several years ago" — with Visa's own large transaction model claiming up to 5x improvement in fraud value capture, directly fueling VAS growth.
4. **CEMEA is the one crack in an otherwise resilient consumer.** The Middle East conflict knocked ~2.5 points off CEMEA payments volume growth and pressured cross-border travel most acutely in March; management repeatedly minimizes this ("about 6% of our total payments volume") while conceding it will persist into Q3.
5. **Pismo's Wells Fargo win validates the core-modernization thesis but stays vague on economics.** A major U.S. bank moving its core ledger to Pismo is a credibility marker for Visa's "bank-as-a-service" ambitions, but management wouldn't specify monetization beyond "it's considered VAS" and shows up in "other revenue."

## Economy & consumer
- **Broad-based U.S. spend acceleration, no low-end weakness.** U.S. credit (+10%, up 2+ points from Q1) and debit (+7%, up ~1 point) both improved, with "growth across consumer spend bands... incremental improvement from Q1" and the highest spend band growing fastest — CFO states plainly: "We do not see signs of the lower spend consumer weakening in our volumes."
- **Tax refunds cited as a tailwind**, alongside strong travel spend in both consumer and commercial credit.
- **Geopolitics is the swing factor internationally.** CEMEA payments volume growth stepped down ~2.5 points from Q1 "primarily due to the conflict in the Middle East," with cross-border travel impact "most pronounced in March," partly masked in headline cross-border data by Ramadan timing effects.
- **Cross-border e-commerce (+13%, a point above Q1) is outrunning cross-border travel (+10%)** — a structural mix shift the CFO leans on to argue overall cross-border resilience despite Middle East softness.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Net revenue +17% y/y, "strongest since 2022"** (ex-pandemic recovery/Visa Europe, strongest since 2013); EPS +20%, beating expectations on higher-than-forecast volatility, stronger VAS, and lower incentive payouts.
- **Value-added services (30% of net revenue) grew 27% constant-dollar**, with outperformance in AI-embedded products — Smarter Stand-In Processing, Visa Provisioning Intelligence, Visa Account Updater, Visa Consumer Authentication Service — described as the fastest-adopted category by clients.
- **Commercial and money movement revenue +24% constant dollar**, the highest in recent quarters, but CFO flags this was inflated by "adjustments and deal timing... in addition to pricing" that won't recur; Visa Direct transactions +23% y/y on 18B+ network endpoints.
- **Client incentives grew only 14%, below expectations**, "driven primarily by deal timing and performance adjustments" — a reduction in a cost line that flattered EPS this quarter but is guided to step up again in Q3.
- **Record capital return:** $7.9B in buybacks this quarter (highest ever) plus $1.3B in dividends; new $20B authorization brings total capacity to ~$33B — a strong signal of management's confidence in forward cash generation, even as guidance flags Q3 as the year's weakest revenue-growth quarter.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Stablecoin infrastructure buildout accelerating**: 160+ stablecoin card programs (volume up ~200% y/y), $7B annualized stablecoin settlement run rate (+50%+ q/q), and Visa now a blockchain validator (Tempo) and super-validator (Canton Network) — moving, in the CEO's words, "from a blockchain participant to an infrastructure leader."
- **M&A adding non-carded payment rails**: Prisma (issuer-processor) and Newpay (real-time bill pay/ATM network) acquisitions in Argentina, layering onto Pismo, which just signed Wells Fargo for core-ledger migration — Visa's clearest proof point yet that large legacy banks will outsource core modernization to it.
- **Scarcity reframed as trust, not hardware.** Unlike most AI-infra commentary (compute/power/data centers), Visa's stated constraint on agentic commerce growth is user and merchant trust in the payment credential — network scale (175M+ seller locations, 5B credentials, 14,500 FI clients), security (300B+ annual transactions feeding fraud models), and brand trust are positioned as the moat, not physical infrastructure.
- **Marketing/sponsorship spend timed to mega-events**: incremental operating expense growth (guided to low-double-to-low-teens for the year) is explicitly tied to FIFA World Cup and Olympics activations, which management frames as high-margin, client-demand-driven investment rather than discretionary marketing.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Agentic commerce and stablecoins will expand Visa's addressable market for "not just the next three to five years, but beyond," with agents creating "significantly more transactions" including agent-paid microtransactions.
- **Action:** Visa CLI (proof-of-concept for command-line/agent-driven payments) and Intelligent Commerce Connect are live but explicitly framed as early — "while it's early, we are seeing growth in agentic shopping" — real dollars are flowing into validator/infrastructure roles and settlement rails rather than into a finished consumer/agent product.
- **Gap:** When pressed directly on agentic-commerce fraud liability — how a four-party network handles agent transaction risk versus American Express's move to explicitly own that risk — Ryan McInerney gave no concrete commitment: "as all of this starts to mature and evolve, we'll evolve our rules with full buy-in from the entire ecosystem." Belief in the opportunity is far ahead of any committed operating model for it.
- **Belief vs. spend on VAS/CMS growth:** Despite VAS/CMS running well above the 16-18% combined medium-term target set at Investor Day, management declined to raise that guidance, telling analysts "we don't guide to growth pillars" — talking up the trend while stopping short of committing to it as a new baseline.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No unit economics for agentic/stablecoin transactions** beyond a hand-wavy assertion that "the economics of those products look just like our normal products" — no incremental margin, take-rate, or volume disclosure.
- **No formal fraud-liability framework for agent-initiated transactions**, unlike the American Express comparison raised directly by an analyst; management deferred to "evolving" rules over time.
- **No updated medium-term growth guidance for VAS/CMS** despite running roughly 8-10 points above the prior 16-18% combined target — explicitly declining to "recalibrate" the multi-year view when invited to.
- **No specifics on Pismo monetization or additional large-bank pipeline** beyond confirming Wells Fargo is booked as VAS/other revenue and expressing hope to "have more to share... over time."
