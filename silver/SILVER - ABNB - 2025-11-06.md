# ABNB — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-06)

**The key idea:** Airbnb delivered its best quarter ever on adjusted EBITDA and reaccelerated U.S. growth, but the reacceleration leans heavily on a single new lever — Reserve Now Pay Later — while the multi-year bets meant to diversify the platform (services/experiences, hotels, AI search) are explicitly described as 3-5 years from mattering financially. This is a "trust the process" call: strong current numbers, thin evidence yet that the new businesses are more than pilots.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Reserve Now Pay Later is doing a lot of quiet work.** Offered only to a subset of U.S. domestic bookers with flexible/moderate cancellation policies, it's taken up by "about 70%" of those offered it and is credited by both Chesky and Mertz as a primary driver of the U.S. acceleration from Q2 to Q3. Mertz concedes cancellations rose too but says the net is additive — an assertion, not a disclosed number.
2. **Chesky's founder thesis: AI push people back into the real world.** "A bet on Airbnb is a bet on AI because... the more AI proliferates the content we consume on devices, the more people are going to yearn for real connection with real people in the real world." It's a well-turned pitch, but it's also a hedge against Airbnb's absence from the AI-agent/travel-discovery land grab everyone else is racing into.
3. **Hotels and services/experiences are framed as multi-year, not near-term, revenue.** Chesky explicitly says services/experiences take "3 to 5 years" to become material and hotels are three-city pilots (LA, NYC, Madrid) chosen partly to backfill regulatory-constrained supply. Good storytelling, low current P&L impact.
4. **The company opted out of the ChatGPT app-integration launch** — notable given how aggressively booking/travel peers have raced into agentic-commerce surfaces — with an explanation (didn't want to be "a commodity" or "a data layer") that sits awkwardly next to Airbnb's own AI-search ambitions built on the same frontier models everyone else has API access to.
5. **Margin story is capital-return-funded, not yet new-business-funded.** $857M of buybacks in the quarter, 3/4 of trailing-12-month free cash flow returned to shareholders, while two new businesses were launched in parallel — the company is confident enough in current cash generation to do both at once.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read offered.** Neither Chesky nor Mertz frames results against a consumer spending or travel-demand backdrop; all growth commentary is anchored to product levers (payments, pricing tools, maps) rather than macro tailwinds/headwinds.
- **Payment flexibility as demand unlock, not price cuts.** RNPL and Brazil's installment plans are both cited as "huge boon to growth," suggesting the marginal U.S./LatAm traveler is liquidity-constrained rather than price-resistant — a consumer-health signal worth flagging.
- **The 9:1 hotel-to-home ratio is management's core demand thesis**: Chesky repeatedly cites that for every Airbnb stay, ~9 people stay in a hotel, and that surveyed hotel-stayers say affordability, quality, and supply gaps — not preference — keep them out of homes.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record profitability, accelerating volume.** Revenue +10% YoY (high end of guide), GBV +14%, nights/seats +9% (2-point sequential acceleration), adj. EBITDA >$2B — "our highest in any quarter ever" (Chesky).
- **International expansion outpacing core.** Expansion-market nights booked grew "at double the rate of our core markets" over the trailing 12 months; first-time bookers up over 20% in Japan and nearly 50% in India YoY — genuine share gains in immature markets, not just comps.
- **"No silver bullets" admission.** Chesky: growth comes from "hundreds and hundreds and hundreds of improvements," not one big fix — an honest but also slightly deflating answer when pressed on what's left to improve.
- **AI customer support already cutting cost-to-serve.** Reduced human-agent contact by 15% in its U.S. rollout; expanding to 50+ languages next year — a real, measurable operational win versus the more speculative AI-search roadmap.

## Investing & scarcity
- **2025 was a deliberate double-launch year** (services/experiences relaunch + hotels pilot) that required rebuilding "almost every part of the platform" — Mertz confirms this was unusually investment-heavy and that 2026 should carry less "first year of launch" cost even as spend continues.
- **New playbook: test many pilots in one city before scaling.** Chesky says Airbnb will "simultaneously testing half a dozen, maybe even a dozen new businesses at a time in one market" — a shift from historical global-first launches, explicitly to de-risk capital committed to unproven ideas.
- **Supply/regulatory scarcity is the stated reason for hotels.** NYC and Madrid are called out as markets "constrained from a regulatory standpoint," where hotel supply fills the gap Airbnb can't legally fill with more home listings.
- **Capital return remains aggressive alongside new-business investment**: $6.6B still authorized for buybacks after repurchasing $3.5B trailing-12-months (~75% of FCF), with share count down 8% since 2022 — investing in new businesses hasn't slowed the buyback.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: core homes business is "not even close to mature" anywhere and could reaccelerate meaningfully.** Action: no market-specific capital commitment disclosed beyond "more supply, better pricing tools, more quality" — this remains largely an assertion backed by anecdote (Italy example) rather than a funded initiative with numbers attached.
- **Belief: AI search and a unified AI concierge will differentiate Airbnb from commodity travel search.** Action: still in testing, phased rollout ("Phase 1... Phase 2") targeted for "next year" — real engineering investment (dozen-plus AI workstreams) but no committed launch date or KPI target given.
- **Belief: services/experiences and hotels could each become multibillion-dollar businesses.** Action: three-to-a-dozen-city pilots only, explicit 3-5 year timeline to material revenue — the gap between conviction language ("very, very bullish") and actual dollars committed (still described as small-scale pilots) is the widest divergence on the call.
- **Belief: specialization beats platform commoditization in AI-driven travel search.** Action: declined ChatGPT app-store integration and previously declined Google Hotel Finder — a real, costly-in-distribution decision made in service of that belief, so this is one case where stated conviction and action actually align.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 revenue or margin guidance** — explicitly deferred to the February call, despite repeated analyst attempts to pin down whether 2025's ~$200M incremental investment persists into next year.
- **Wouldn't name the next new billion-dollar business** beyond confirming a cadence ("you should expect every year to see new businesses") — "I probably shouldn't say too much more beyond that for next year."
- **Loyalty and advertising programs left open-ended**: "we are absolutely looking at both," but no launch timing, format, or investment level disclosed — advertising in particular is stated as intentionally paused until the AI-search paradigm is "nailed."
- **Ducked a firm answer on AI-search exclusivity vs. third-party syndication**: Chesky says Airbnb will be "open-minded" about appearing in other apps but commits to nothing, calling it "a fast-evolving landscape" to be handled "one app at a time."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **RNPL durability and hidden cost**: multiple threads on how much of the U.S. beat is attributable to the new payment feature and what cancellation rates look like — management gave directional reassurance ("net impact... is a lift") without hard numbers.
- **New-business economics**: repeated pressure on whether 2025's investment step-up rolls off or persists, and what the real payback timeline is for hotels/services/experiences — met with qualitative "should get more efficient over time" answers rather than unit economics.
- **Core-market maturity**: skepticism that homes growth has structurally slowed in Airbnb's largest markets, challenging the reacceleration narrative directly.
- **The ChatGPT snub**: a direct, pointed question on why Airbnb sat out a high-visibility AI distribution partnership other travel platforms joined — Chesky's answer (commoditization risk) invited a same-session follow-up on the broader AI-search strategy, suggesting the room wasn't fully convinced.
- **Compressed worry:** is this quarter's reacceleration a durable structural shift, or a payment-feature sugar high propping up a core business investors already suspect is maturing faster than management admits — while the genuinely new revenue streams stay pilot-sized for years to come?
