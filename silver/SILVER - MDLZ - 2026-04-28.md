# MDLZ — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** A strong, broad-based Q1 (emerging markets +6.3%, European chocolate share turning positive, U.S. biscuit share gains) collided with management's decision to reaffirm rather than raise full-year EPS guidance — not because the quarter was weak, but because Middle East conflict-driven cost inflation (oil, packaging, freight rerouting) is expected to eat the upside, and any further beat is earmarked for reinvestment rather than earnings. It's a company simultaneously bullish on execution and cautious on the macro backdrop.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Emerging markets are quietly carrying the company.** At 40% of revenue and +6.3% in Q1, EM strength (India double-digit, Brazil high-single-digit, China improving off a soft base, aided by India's GST change) is the growth engine while developed markets merely stabilize.
2. **The EPS reaffirm is a tell, not a guidance miss.** Zaramella was explicit: "if EPS upside materializes, we would like most likely to invest it back in the business" — management chose to absorb a Q1 beat into guidance cushion for Middle East cost risk rather than signal confidence with a raise.
3. **Cocoa has stopped being the story.** Zaramella flagged the industry has gone "a little bit longer" (~10 months of coverage, a multi-year high) which explains the price bounce off lows — not a demand shift — and he expects "another year of surplus," with $2,500 as "a fair representation" and possibly headed lower.
4. **North America is a share-gain plan, not a category-recovery plan.** Zaramella was blunt: "we don't see really the category improving much" — growth is coming from Ritz, Sour Patch Kids (projected double-digit for the year), and ventures (Tate's, Clif, Perfect Bar), not a rebounding U.S. snacker.
5. **A real supply-chain reinvestment is underway in North America biscuits.** Beyond the usual optimization talk, MDLZ is insourcing co-manufactured lines, building flexible multi-pack/club-format packaging capacity, and automating DSD distribution centers to cut inventory and cost — a genuine capital commitment, not just messaging.

## Economy & consumer
- **U.S. consumer confidence is low and expected to worsen**, with Dirk Van de Put stating plainly: "consumption in the U.S., for a number of reasons, will remain subdued in general," citing three years of flat shopping-basket dollar value despite rising per-item prices.
- **K-shaped bifurcation is explicit and named**: higher-income consumers trade up to premium, lower-income consumers hunt unit price and channel-shift to value/club/online — Walmart and Costco biscuit growth (+4%) outpaced the total U.S. biscuit category (+0.3%) by 13x.
- **Europe's consumer is "stable, but fragile,"** vigilant about Middle East-driven energy and inflation risk even though category penetration and snacking value growth currently "hold up quite well."
- **China is the one soft spot among the big four EM markets**, though confidence "improved versus the last quarter" and the business still grew mid-single-digit on a strong Chinese New Year.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **European chocolate share flipped positive**: "our base business, if I take Easter out, turned from a share loss into slightly positive over the last month," attributed to fixed price-point gaps and stepped-up working-media investment.
- **U.S. biscuit share gains are real but narrow** — Ritz-led, with Oreo underperforming due to a limited-time offer that "didn't perform as well as last year's"; management says fixes are already in plan.
- **Ventures brands (Perfect Bar, Hu, Tate's, Clif) are outgrowing the core** — Clif Kid Zbar "close to double digits," Tate's gaining share — suggesting the acquired/adjacent portfolio is doing more organic-growth work than the legacy core brands.
- **Gross margin held up better than a ~$350M inventory-phasing headwind implied** (down only ~270bps) thanks to positive volume/mix leverage, a highly profitable China quarter (+5%), and supply-chain productivity gains — genuine operational execution, not accounting.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Biscoff partnership is the single most-repeated growth bet on the call** — described as "really quite big for them and for us in the coming years," already sold out in India biscuit launch, expanding into chocolate (cream/crumb inclusions) across markets, with "a few more" collaborations expected.
- **North America biscuit supply-chain modernization** targets insourcing proven co-manufactured volume, building multi-pack/club-format flexibility, and automating DSD centers — Zaramella frames roughly 60% of the U.S. network as already "state-of-the-art," with the investment aimed at the remaining underperforming plants and inefficient DSD logistics.
- **The binding constraint is cost exposure, not raw material supply** — cocoa fundamentals are described as fine (surplus likely), but Middle East disruption is forcing "alternative routes to produce our brands and deliver our brands... coming at an extra cost," and regulated markets block hedging that cost into 2027.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **They believe** U.S. category growth will stay muted through the year, cocoa will settle near/below $2,500, and North America will still inflect positive on volume/revenue in H2 — a belief resting entirely on execution (share gains, new channels), not demand recovery.
- **They're acting on it** by reaffirming rather than raising EPS guidance, redirecting any Q1 upside into brand reinvestment (A&C spend) and innovation (Biscoff expansion, protein/well-being launches, premium chocolate range), and greenlighting a multi-year North America supply-chain capex program — concrete capital commitments that outpace the cautious guidance tone.
- **The gap**: guidance language is defensive ("cautiously optimistic," headwinds "we didn't have in our original forecast") while the investment posture (stepped-up media, packaging capex, DSD automation, more Biscoff collaborations) is offensive — management is spending like the momentum is durable while guiding like it might not be.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No EPS raise despite an explicit Q1 beat** — Zaramella: "we were ahead. We are ahead," yet guidance stayed unchanged, with upside reserved for reinvestment rather than shareholders.
- **No firm 2027 EPS number**, only "strong 2027 EPS growth" as a qualitative commitment when pressed for parameters.
- **No guidance uplift for U.S. category growth** — management explicitly separated "the category" (expected to stay weak) from "our business" (expected to improve via share gains), avoiding any commitment that the broader consumer environment will help them.
