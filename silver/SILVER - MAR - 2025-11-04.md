# MAR — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-04)

**The key idea:** Marriott's earnings beat isn't coming from RevPAR — that's stuck near flat globally and negative in the U.S. — it's coming from fee leverage, cost cuts, and a luxury/conversion-driven room count that keeps growing regardless of demand softness. The real swing factor for 2026 earnings, the co-brand credit card renegotiation with Amex and Chase, just opened up for the first time on this call, and management gave color but zero numbers.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Fee-based model is decoupling from RevPAR.** Global RevPAR rose just 0.5% in Q3, yet adjusted EBITDA grew 10% and EPS grew 9% — driven by 13% growth in co-brand card fees, record signings, and an 8-9% cut to G&A. The asset-light franchise model is doing exactly what it's designed to do: grow earnings faster than the underlying hotel economy.
2. **The credit card renewal is the story nobody can size yet.** CEO Tony Capuano confirmed active negotiations with Amex and Chase on deals last touched in 2017/2020, noting Bonvoy membership has more than doubled (110M to 260M) and system size has grown ~50% since then — implying real repricing upside — but both executives repeatedly declined specifics, with Oberg saying "it's definitely too early to talk about potential upside from these deals."
3. **K-shaped consumer, stated plainly.** Luxury RevPAR rose 4% while "performance weakened down the chain scales"; U.S. softness is concentrated in select-service brands, and SME business transient is notably weaker than large-corporate BT. Government transient RevPAR fell 14-15% on the shutdown.
4. **Development is conversion-led, not new-build-led.** Conversions are ~30% of both signings and openings YTD, and pipeline hit a record 596,000 rooms — but new-build construction starts remain "meaningfully below" 2019 levels because financing costs and construction/labor costs haven't come down enough, per CFO Leeny Oberg.
5. **Extended shoulder-season travel in Europe and Japan.** Capuano's anecdotal read from a multi-week trip (Rome, Milan, Venice, Florence, Tokyo) is that heat-driven rebookings and structural demand shifts are pushing "traditional peak season" into spring and fall — a soft but consistent signal echoing airline commentary.

## Economy & consumer

- **Global RevPAR growth stayed muted:** Q3 RevPAR +0.5% globally, with U.S. & Canada down 0.4% versus international up 2.6% — "ongoing global macroeconomic uncertainty" cited as the driver.
- **Government spend cratered:** Government transient RevPAR fell 14% (later cited as 15% ex-adjustments) amid the shutdown, dragging U.S. business transient.
- **High end holds, low end doesn't:** Luxury RevPAR +4% for a second straight quarter of leading performance, "weakening down the chain scales" — Capuano called it "a pretty powerful illustration of the strength and appetite of that luxury consumer."
- **SME weakness distinct from large-corporate strength:** Oberg noted large corporate BT showed "pretty encouraging strength" while SMEs showed "a bit of hesitancy... as they try to navigate the volatile economic environment," with greater impact on select-service brands.
- **China's operating environment stayed "challenged"** by weak macro conditions even as Marriott's market share continued to grow there.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Fee growth outran RevPAR:** Gross fee revenue +4% to $1.34B, led by co-brand card fees +13% (international cards +20%, driven by Japan and UAE).
- **Incentive management fees fell:** IMF down 7% y/y to $148M, due to U.S. hotels under renovation and a prior-year insurance-proceeds benefit in Florida that didn't repeat — a real, disclosed headwind rather than spin.
- **G&A cuts flattered margins:** G&A fell 15% in Q3 (partly a comp against a $19M reserve last year) and full-year G&A is guided down 8-9% to $975-985M, reflecting ~$90M of "enterprise-wide efficiency" savings.
- **Group RevPAR declined 3%** in Q3 on calendar timing, even as forward group pace for 2026 is up 7% globally (8% in the U.S.) — a disconnect between reported quarter and booked pipeline that management attributes entirely to timing.
- **Record development momentum:** YTD signings at a record level, pipeline at an all-time high of 596,000 rooms (250,000+ under construction), Bonvoy membership up 18% y/y to ~260M.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Investment spend crept to guidance's high end**, but Oberg attributed it to non-development items — tech transformation timing, owned/leased capex, asset-sale-related spend — explicitly denying any shift in key-money philosophy or development-related outlays.
- **The real constraint is financing, not demand or land:** new-build U.S. construction starts remain "meaningfully below" 2019 levels; Oberg described developers as "standing on the sidelines and waiting to go back in rather than not doing the deals at all," pending lower rates and moderating construction/labor costs.
- **Conversions are absorbing the gap:** ~30% of signings and openings YTD are conversions, explicitly pitched as a hedge against the weak new-build financing environment — and Marriott's expanding mid-scale platform (StudioRes, City Express, Four Points Flex — 150 U.S. pipeline hotels) is designed to capture exactly this dynamic.
- **Capital returns held at ~$4B for the year** while keeping leverage in the 3.0-3.5x net debt/EBITDA range — no scarcity of capital on Marriott's own balance sheet, unlike its developer partners.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** 2026 global RevPAR growth "could be similar" to 2025's 1.5-2.5%, with the World Cup adding 30-35bps concentrated in the U.S. **Action:** no incremental RevPAR-driving investment disclosed beyond the tech platform rollout — the World Cup bump is treated as a one-time calendar tailwind, not a reason to change strategy.
- **Belief:** Bonvoy's scale (260M members, ~50% more properties since 2017) justifies materially better card economics. **Action:** actual negotiations are underway with Amex and Chase, but management won't commit to numbers, growth-rate targets, or a hard close date — the gap between "we deserve more" rhetoric and "we can't tell you what we'll get" is the widest divergence on the call.
- **Belief:** AI/agentic platforms (ChatGPT-style search and booking) will become "a helpful new distribution channel." **Action:** limited to "optimizing content" across platforms for GenAI discoverability — no committed product, headcount, or spend disclosed, framed as still "in their infancy."
- **Belief:** Conversions and mid-scale will keep driving unit growth despite weak new-build economics. **Action:** concretely backed — 200+ mid-scale rooms open, 200+ more in pipeline, 150 U.S. mid-scale hotels in pipeline, reduced loyalty chargeback rates to lower owner affiliation costs.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Credit card deal specifics:** repeatedly declined size, structure, or precise timing — "too early to talk about potential upside" — offering only that a new deal could land "sometime next year."
- **M&A appetite:** Capuano gave a scripted non-commitment — no "burning need to chase M&A," would only act on a specific geographic gap or brand-architecture hole, citing citizenM as the bar for financial discipline.
- **New-build construction turnaround timing:** no date given for when rate cuts or cost moderation will unlock a "dramatic pickup" in new-build starts — framed as gradual, incremental improvement only.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Credit cards dominated the Q&A** — size of the program, renegotiation parameters versus 2017 terms, timing, and competitive threat from bank-issued premium travel cards (Amex/Chase's own new offerings) — with management giving context (membership growth, spend growth) but no hard numbers, the single biggest unresolved swing factor for 2026 guidance.
- **Development financing and pipeline health** were probed from multiple angles: investment spend mix, under-construction acceleration, China/APAC signings growth, and how low-end consumer weakness is affecting developer return assumptions for new-build versus conversion.
- **Business transient granularity:** analysts pushed to separate government-shutdown effects from underlying private-sector BT trends, getting a clean answer (ex-government BT +1% y/y) that somewhat de-risks the headline government-driven decline.
- **Compressed worry:** the Street is underwriting 2026 earnings partly on a credit-card renegotiation whose size, terms, and close date management will not disclose — leaving the single largest identified earnings lever for next year effectively unmodelable from this call.
