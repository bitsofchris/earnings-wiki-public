# CEG — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-11-07)

**The key idea:** Constellation is sitting on the most favorable supply/demand setup in its history — nuclear capacity factors near-perfect, power prices rising, and hyperscalers now sophisticated enough to close deals fast — yet the flagship hyperscaler transaction Dominguez has been "seventh-inning stretch"-ing for two quarters still hasn't signed. The tension of the call is between an unambiguously hot market and a company still visibly waiting on outside parties (interconnection queues, DOJ, Westinghouse cost certainty) before it can convert that heat into contracted, funded megawatts.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The hyperscaler deal is still not done, and the excuse has evolved.** Two calls ago it was "seventh-inning stretch"; now it's baseball games that "drag out" in "later innings." Dominguez commits only to "before our fourth quarter call" — a real deadline, but the third consecutive quarter of near-miss.
2. **Buyer sophistication, not buyer interest, is the actual unlock.** The company frames the bottleneck as no longer being customer hesitation but interconnection process speed — "the negotiations move much more quickly than ever before... we are often paced by the speed of interconnection."
3. **New nuclear construction is still all upside talk, no capital committed.** Despite a $80B federal-Westinghouse partnership announcement and visible excitement, Dominguez is explicit: "I have been cautious, and I remain cautious, and I will always be cautious because it's a lot of your money that we are talking about here."
4. **Stock-comp success is quietly eating this year's operating beat.** The >50% YTD rally triggered incentive-plan payouts that offset "much of the gross margin favorability this year" — a rare case of a company's own stock performance becoming a real cost line.
5. **Demand response is being positioned as a stealth nuclear plant.** 1,000MW of AI-enabled demand response, per Dominguez, has effective load-carrying capacity equivalent to "a new nuclear plant," versus a tenth that for the same nameplate in batteries — a capital-light way to monetize the interconnection queue problem.

## Economy & consumer
- **Data-center capex dwarfs the power sector itself.** Management cites ~$0.75T in projected data center capital deployment, "probably twice as large as the 3 largest publicly traded power companies in the United States" — the scale argument for why this cycle is durable, not speculative.
- **Buyer maturity has structurally shifted.** Early customers were "exploring options, kicking tires"; today's are sophisticated on "pricing and term... the accounting and the collateral needs... the interconnection process."
- **Forward power curves, not spot, are where the real signal is.** Per commercial chief Jim McHugh: "the power upside has been longer duration... it's really outperformed gas," attributed to load growth and interconnection reality rather than near-term gas moves.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Nuclear operations remain best-in-class.** 96.8% fleet capacity factor, ~4 points above industry average, "the equivalent to having another reactor's worth of power on a full year basis."
- **Earnings beat, but with an unusual offset.** GAAP EPS $2.97, adjusted $3.04, up from last year — but stock-comp payouts tied to the stock's own rally created "nonrecurring O&M headwinds" that erased much of the gross-margin beat.
- **Retail/commercial margins are healthy and getting better via sustainability products.** Retail margins sit at "the upper end of the range," with McHugh noting CFE and sustainability-linked products carry stronger margins "than pure true commodity margins."
- **One large low-margin C&I gas customer churned**, explaining the quarter's dip in gas renewal rate — framed as normal ebb-and-flow, not a demand signal.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Uprates are the highest-return, lowest-risk lever in the portfolio.** ~900–1,000MW identified (LaSalle, Limerick, Calvert Cliffs) with completed engineering; Dominguez stresses uprates add output "without adding people... without an O&M drag," making them cheaper on a lifecycle basis than headline capital cost comparisons to new-build suggest.
- **Three Mile Island (Crane) restart is de-risking, not adding risk.** "We're not seeing new challenges emerge... the condition of the plant is better" than anticipated — a rare positive surprise in a megaproject update.
- **Land, not just megawatts, is the scarce asset.** Dominguez argues Constellation's existing nuclear sites — with water, rail, community acceptance, and talent already in place — are "the most valuable asset that we have that presently isn't fully recognized," positioning the company as gatekeeper for where any new U.S. nuclear gets built.
- **Interconnection queue speed is the real bottleneck**, not generation capacity, financing, or even customer demand.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Nuclear's political and public-support tailwind is now overwhelming — Westinghouse's $80B federal partnership, NextEra's Duane Arnold restart, the Japan trade deal's nuclear investment centerpiece, and FERC being directed by Secretary Wright to build a standard fast-interconnection process for large loads. **Action:** Constellation has not committed capital to any new nuclear build; it's monetizing land and pursuing an operating-services-agreement structure so any future unit on its property is run by Constellation, not a partner — but funding stays gated behind a durable PPA and firm cost/schedule guarantees it says it doesn't have yet.
- **Belief:** Data-center demand response can substitute for new capacity at scale. **Action:** Only a Grid Beyond partnership and early contracting toward the 500MW-this-year target exist today — a real program, but nowhere near the scale of the "new nuclear plant"-equivalent framing.
- **Belief:** Deal pricing will hold or strengthen as scarcity value rises. **Action:** Management explicitly says it is "not quite there yet" on slowing sales or repricing more aggressively — talk of scarcity pricing is forward-looking, not yet operative.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No hyperscaler deal name, structure, or firm date** — only "soon," and "before our fourth quarter call."
- **Declined to discuss Limerick-area data center interconnection activity directly**, despite an analyst citing public disclosures — "I think I'm going to just kind of decide not to answer that question," a notable tell given how forthcoming Dominguez was on everything else.
- **No capital commitment to new-build nuclear** pending Westinghouse cost/schedule specifics and a durable PPA.
- **Combined Calpine guidance deferred to February**, with management explicitly declining to give numbers now beyond reaffirming the January 2025 framework still roughly holds.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Deal timing anxiety dominated**, with repeated variations on when the promised hyperscaler PPA actually lands, and whether BTM (behind-the-meter) deals from non-power entrants are structurally different (weaker) than CEG's front-of-meter contracts.
- **Competitive-threat questions clustered around BYOG and new entrants** — oil companies, gas players, and BTM deal-doers — probing whether CEG's pricing power or asset visibility erodes as more capacity gets promised into the market; Dominguez pushed back firmly on both.
- **Calpine mechanics** — asset-sale delay reports, DOJ timeline, and why guidance waits until February — got direct, unworried answers: management says it's deliberately not rushing divestitures because "the market is very supportive of sales of these assets right now."
- **Compressed worry:** analysts are less concerned CEG can find demand than whether the queue of competing power suppliers and unclosed deals means CEG's scarcity pricing edge erodes before its own transactions actually close.
