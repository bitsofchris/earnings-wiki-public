# CTSH — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** Cognizant is rebranding itself from "system integrator" to "AI builder" — funding platform and IP investment (Flowsource, Agent Foundry, a new Anthropic partnership) with productivity savings from AI-assisted coding (~30% of internal code now AI-generated), while betting that a second, larger "agentic" services wave lands on top of the first. The results this quarter are genuinely strong — beat-and-raise, five straight quarters of organic growth — but a sequential bookings decline and Belcan-driven margin dilution complicate the "nonlinear AI productivity" story management is trying to sell.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Beat-and-raise with real breadth.** All four segments grew organically for a fifth consecutive quarter; full-year revenue guidance rose to 6%-6.3% cc (from 4%-6%) and margin guidance moved to the top of the prior range (~15.7%). CEO Kumar called it "another podium finish to our peer group's Winner's Circle."
2. **Bookings lumpiness undercuts the narrative slightly.** After 18% y/y bookings growth in Q2, Q3 bookings fell ~5% y/y — management frames this as noise ("lumpiness") rather than a slowdown, and points to large-deal TCV up 40% YTD as the offsetting signal, but the two data points sit in tension.
3. **The "AI builder" pitch is a two-swim-lane bet.** Lane one is productivity/consolidation (linear, price-sensitive, funds the transition); lane two is "agentic innovation" (elastic, less price-sensitive, still early — AI engagements grew from 2,500 to 3,500 in one quarter, a 40% jump). Kumar's framing: "the money you save on the software cycles on productivity is going to be underwritten to innovation cycles."
4. **Frontier-model hedging via multi-partner strategy.** New Anthropic deal (Claude models + agentic tooling) sits alongside existing Google Gemini Enterprise launch-partner status and #1 GitHub Copilot partner ranking — CTSH is explicitly avoiding single-vendor dependency since "the machine actually belongs to the frontier model companies."
5. **India secondary listing floated as a valuation fix.** Management confirmed it's "assessing a potential primary offering and a secondary listing in India" to close a persistent valuation discount to peers — still "early phase," no commitment, but a notable disclosure on an unprompted analyst question.

## Economy & consumer
- **Macro described as uncertain, not deteriorating.** CFO Dalal: clients are "navigating elevated levels of uncertainty around trade policy" and showing "a lower pace of discretionary spending in certain areas like products and resources," even as cost pressure sustains demand for consolidation deals.
- **No end-consumer exposure** — CTSH's "consumer" is the enterprise IT buyer; the relevant demand signal is enterprise AI infrastructure spend translating (with a lag) into services spend, which Kumar argues is now compressing: "that cycle has shrunk now."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Financial Services is the standout vertical**, now on its fourth or fifth straight quarter of sequential growth, with insurance (previously lagging) starting to spend again.
- **BPO/BPaaS is the hidden growth engine**, up 10% for two straight quarters and "on track to reach $3 billion in annualized revenue," powered by the TriZetto health-plan platform (200M+ members).
- **Products and Resources and Communications/Media/Tech remain soft spots** — CMT grew only because large-deal wins offset "pockets of discretionary spending weakness," and Products and Resources growth is deferred to "new deal wins to ramp up more meaningfully in 2026."
- **Utilization hit 85% for a third straight quarter** (up from 84% a year ago) and voluntary attrition fell to 14.5%, a third consecutive sequential decline — both signal a tightening, more efficient delivery engine.

## Investing & scarcity
- **New-grad hiring roughly doubled year-over-year**, explicitly framed as a margin and AI-adoption lever, not just cost management — CFO Dalal calls freshers and AI "very complementary."
- **H-1B exposure is being actively wound down**: "Cognizant has significantly reduced the dependency on visas while increasing local hiring and our nearshore capacity," which management says shields it from the recent U.S. visa policy changes.
- **The real constraint is skills-mix, not compute or capital** — the entire "pyramid" strategy (more freshers, faster reskilling) is a response to needing a broader base of AI-fluent junior talent to support higher-value Vector 2/3 work.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: AI-generated code rises from ~30% to ~50%** "in the years ahead" — **action so far** is mostly cultural/tooling (Flowsource platform, GitHub Copilot partnership, an internal hackathon that set a Guinness World Record), not yet a disclosed roadmap or capex commitment tied to that specific target.
- **Belief: large/mega-deal momentum carries into 2026** — **action**: signed 6 more $100M+ deals this quarter (16 YTD, TCV +40% y/y), but management explicitly declines to translate that pipeline into 2026 guidance, leaving the belief unconfirmed by a hard commitment.
- **Belief: agentic/innovation spend will out-pace productivity spend as the next wave** — **action**: real dollars are moving (Anthropic partnership, Agent Foundry, 1,500+ agents built with clients, IP-on-the-edge with 400 platform deployments), a genuine gap between talk and commitment is smaller here than on 2026 guidance.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **2026 financial guidance flatly declined**: "difficult to call about... talk about '26 at this juncture. We will come back in January."
- **India secondary listing kept deliberately vague** — "still in its early phase," "no decision has been made," described as "a long-term project."
- **Q4 revenue guide given as a range with explicit downside/upside framing** rather than a point estimate, and management did not fully reconcile it against the ~4% exit rate flagged the prior quarter.
