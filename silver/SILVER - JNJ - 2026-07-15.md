# JNJ — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-15)

**The key idea:** JNJ is running two businesses on opposite trajectories — Innovative Medicine is compounding on a wave of new launches (ICOTYDE, INLEXZO, TREMFYA in IBD) that management insists will carry double-digit growth into 2027 and beyond, while MedTech's cardiovascular franchise took a real hit from a single U.K. clinical trial that spooked physicians away from Impella. The whole call is management working to convince the Street that the Abiomed slowdown is "behavioral, not structural" while the pharma side quietly does the heavy lifting.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **ICOTYDE (oral IL-23 blocker) is the must-win launch and it's landing**: 11,000 patients initiated, 6,000 unique prescribers, over half already covered by commercial payers within 90 days — "a little bit ahead of our projections." Direct-to-consumer ads started the day before the call, timed to World Cup and baseball audiences.
- **Bladder cancer platform (INLEXZO, erdafitinib) is being built out as a multi-drug franchise**, not a single product: INLEXZO already leads all novel agents in new-patient share (1 in 3 eligible patients, up from 1 in 4 last quarter), and management is explicit about sequencing SunRISe-1 (3,000 patients, approved) into SunRISe-5 (10-15K) and eventually SunRISe-3, the BCG-naive population (40-50K patients) — "before we even get to the erdafitinib piece."
- **Oncology ambition is quantified and aggressive**: targeting **#1 oncology company by 2030 with sales exceeding $50 billion**, layered onto a multiple myeloma franchise where 80% of patients receive a J&J medicine at some point in treatment.
- **Two robotic surgery launches this year, contingent on approval**: OTTAVA (table-integrated surgical robot, positioned as differentiated against "the existing incumbent" — an unmistakable Intuitive Surgical jab) and MONARCH for urology, described as "first of kind."
- **Firefly Bio acquisition** (KRAS-driven solid tumors) follows the Halda deal, but Wolk is careful to frame M&A as optional: "acquisitions are an enabler of growth, but they are not a requirement to deliver on our near and long-term objectives" — guidance excludes Firefly entirely pending Q3 close.
- **DePuy Synthes orthopedics separation stays on track for mid-2027**, with more detail promised at the December 8 investor day — a structural bet that the surgical/ortho business is worth more decoupled from cardiovascular's volatility.

## What's changing

- Company crossed **$25 billion in quarterly sales for the first time**, on pace to break **$100 billion annual revenue for the first time in the company's 140-year history**.
- Guidance raised again: operational sales growth range moved up, and adjusted EPS midpoint rose to a **7.3% year-on-year increase**, with management citing Q2 beat and launch uptake as the driver.
- **TREMFYA crossed $2 billion in quarterly sales for the first time**, growing 71%, now the clear IBD share leader (58% of ulcerative colitis induction share, 50%+ in Crohn's) — and new FUSION data gives it the first positive perianal fistulizing Crohn's result **in over 20 years**.
- **STELARA's decline is now steep and largely absorbed**: down 55.7% on biosimilar erosion, but shrunk to just 4% of the Innovative Medicine business — "if you exclude that, the 96% of our business actually grew over 14%," a framing management leans on hard to argue the headwind is now background noise rather than a drag.
- CARVYKTI, TECVAYLI, and TALVEY are all compounding off a low base at 48-63% growth, with TECVAYLI's U.S. sequential growth **accelerating to 46%** after a combination approval — this is a franchise still in the steep part of its curve, not leveling off.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **Abiomed/heart recovery declined outright (-2%)** after a neutral U.K. clinical trial made physicians "more selective" about Impella use — management repeatedly insists this is "behavioral... rather than anything structural," but a 300-patient U.K. study moving physician behavior against a device with 40,000+ patients of prior evidence suggests the clinical narrative is more fragile than the "temporary dynamic" framing admits. Recovery to double-digit growth isn't expected until **PROTECT IV data reads out in 2027** — over a year of drag being asked to be looked past.
- **Electrophysiology growth was quietly cut by ~400bps from a China inventory dynamic**, alongside unspecified "competitive PFA pressures" — the pulsed-field-ablation market that JNJ (VARIPULSE) is fighting for share in against newer entrants is getting tighter than the top-line 3.1% suggests.
- **Hospital procedure-volume anxiety is being pre-empted rather than confirmed absent**: Tim Schmid volunteers, unprompted, that JNJ is "acutely aware" of hospital operators' recent commentary on elective procedure softness and ACA subsidy expiration risk, then asserts JNJ isn't seeing it — a defensive answer to a question that hadn't fully been asked yet, worth watching next quarter for whether the reassurance holds.
- **milvexian (Factor XIa anticoagulant) readout timing remains genuinely unknown**: "these are event-driven studies... could be towards the end of this year, beginning of next year" — management is confident in dose/schedule from Phase II biomarkers but has zero control over when the trial actually reads out, and it wasn't even on the slide deck this quarter.
- **MedTech margin compressed** (22.2% → 22.0%) on tariffs, commercial investment, and R&D — a smaller, less-discussed drag sitting underneath the more dramatic Abiomed story.
