# AZN — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-10)

**The key idea:** AstraZeneca closed out a genuinely strong 2025 (revenue +8%, EPS +11%, 16 blockbusters) and used the call to pivot the story toward 2026-2030: absorbing the Farxiga patent cliff while betting heavily — via Phase III commitments in oral GLP-1, ADCs, bispecifics, and amyloidosis antibodies — on growth beyond 2030. The tension: management won't guarantee the pipeline hit rate that got them here continues, even as they lean on that same hit rate to justify a widening capital and R&D commitment.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Diversification is now the explicit pitch, not a caveat.** Soriot directly addressed years of "your pipeline is too complicated" criticism by pointing at peers who got hurt by concentration risk: "It's great to have 1 or 2 big, big products... but if you lose one of those... it really becomes very painful very quickly." No single therapy area is more than a fraction of the $57B+ base.
2. **Oncology is doing the heavy lifting.** Oncology revenue hit $25.6B (+14%, or +17% ex a one-time milestone), crossed $7B in a single quarter for the first time, and Enhertu grew 46% in Q4 — the clearest evidence of "what's working."
3. **CVRM is the acknowledged drag, and it gets worse before better.** Farxiga faces US LOE in April plus China VBP in Q1; management flags "more of this, unfortunately, in 2026" on CVRM patent erosion, while pointing to Europe (patent-protected to 2028) and baxdrostat as the offset.
4. **Capital allocation discipline is now framed around P&L absorption, not cash headroom.** Despite 1.2x net debt/EBITDA and "plenty of capacity," Soriot and CFO Sarin repeatedly steer toward early-stage BD over paying full price for de-risked Phase III assets, because absorbing large assets into the P&L — not raising the cash — is the real constraint.
5. **China BD prices are rising and management admits it.** Soriot: "the price of these BD deals is going up, right? And that's — I assume that would be the case... it's becoming more difficult because everybody is going there" — a rare candid acknowledgment of eroding deal economics in a market they've relied on for cheap early pipeline.

## Economy & consumer

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Oncology (+14%, 4 franchises now individually over $2.5B-$7B), rare disease Ultomiris (+15%, now past the Soliris-to-Ultomiris transition), and respiratory/immunology (Tezspire, Fasenra, Breztri) growing double-digit with China reimbursement listing wins.
- **Not working:** CVRM revenue was down 6% in Q4 as Brilinta continues to decline and Farxiga growth slows to 2% under generic pressure; V&I was down 33% in Q4, though that's a one-time comp against a 2024 Beyfortus milestone rather than a real trend.
- **Cost discipline:** SG&A fell from 28% to 26% of revenue even as R&D rose 12%, and management explicitly frames this as "operating leverage" funding pipeline investment rather than protecting margin for its own sake.
- **Guidance:** mid-to-high single-digit revenue growth and low-double-digit core EPS growth for 2026, with gross margin "broadly flat to slightly higher" once a one-off royalty buyout normalizes.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx up ~1/3 in 2026** to expand US and China manufacturing/R&D and the Singapore ADC facility — described as multiyear commitments, not one-time spend.
- **R&D rising to the upper end of low-20s% of revenue**, with 300+ active trials and 100+ in Phase III; CFO Sarin: a "substantial portion" of R&D "won't have any substantial revenue in 2030" — it's explicitly beyond-2030 spend.
- **The real constraint is P&L absorption capacity and deal-price inflation in China**, not cash or debt headroom (1.2x leverage, "plenty of capacity" per CFO) — management is choosier about *which* assets to acquire, not *whether* it can afford to.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** cardiometabolic disease (obesity, GLP-1/SGLT2 combinations) is "the biggest issue mankind is facing" and AstraZeneca can win despite a crowded field.
- **Action behind it:** moved elecoglipron (oral GLP-1) into Phase III off Phase IIb data, added a dual GLP-1/glucagon-amylin combination platform, and inked a long-acting-injectable deal with CSPC — real Phase III spend, not just talk. Soriot was direct that a company "would never move a product in Phase III and unleash the kind of spend we have" without confidence in a competitive profile.
- **Belief:** ATTR cardiomyopathy combination therapy (silencer + depleter) will become new standard of care.
- **Action behind it:** Wainua Phase III readout due H2 2026, and a Phase IIb combination trial with cliramitug (depleter) is being initiated ahead of Wainua's own data — committing to the combination strategy before knowing if the monotherapy works, a genuine bet.
- **Gap:** management touts 2026-2027 as ~$10B/year of risk-adjusted peak-sales-potential readouts and "increasing" confidence in the $80B 2030 ambition, but declined to say hit rates will match 2025's unusually strong run — the enthusiasm in the framing outruns the hedge in the actual risk language.
