## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Studio is unambiguously the strongest segment**, leading domestic, international, and global box office simultaneously, with Weapons and The Conjuring: Last Rites together grossing "more than $750 million."
- **Streaming has flipped from cash drain to profit engine**: "more than $1.3 billion in EBITDA" expected this year versus "losing $2.5 billion 3 years ago," with subscriber count set to exceed 150 million by end of 2026.
- **Linear is the soft spot**, with affiliate growth and subscriber decline both trailing peer disclosures (Charter's video sub trend cited as the comparison point); management calls it transitional, not structural.
- **Debt paydown continues**, net leverage down to 3.3x EBITDA after a $1 billion bridge-loan payment in Q3 — balance-sheet cleanup ahead of a possible transaction.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Two new stand-alone apps in development**: a U.S. sports streaming app (replacing HBO Max's sports role domestically post-spin) and a CNN news app built by a team recruited from the New York Times' digital operation under Mark Thompson — both explicitly built as low-incremental-cost "skins" on the existing HBO Max platform rather than separate stacks.
- **Deliberate IP under-monetization as strategy**: Zaslav says they forgo "another $1 billion or $2 billion" in near-term licensing revenue by keeping premium titles (Game of Thrones-caliber library) exclusive to HBO Max rather than selling them externally — trading current cash for long-term platform differentiation.
