# PEP — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-09)

**The key idea:** PepsiCo is threading a needle between an admitted, self-inflicted cost problem in North America (overbuilt Frito Lay capacity for demand that never arrived) and a "brilliant at the basics" recovery story built on service-level fixes, brand relaunches, and portfolio reshaping toward protein/fiber/clean-label. The subtext is a live negotiation with activist investor Elliott Management, whose playbook — much of which PepsiCo claims is already inside "Strategy 2030" — includes a Frito Lay margin target management pointedly declined to endorse. This is also a CFO transition call: Jamie Caulfield, 33-year veteran, is handing off to an external hire poached from PepsiCo's own largest customer.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Frito Lay overbuilt for a demand signal that never showed up.** CEO Ramon Laguarta admitted plainly: "the demand signal we had in '23 is different from the demand signal we have in '25," and the company is now closing its least-efficient manufacturing nodes, cutting warehouse footprint, and trimming go-to-market headcount to match reality — a rare direct admission of a self-caused capacity miss rather than a market-driven one.
2. **Volume declines in foods were partly a deliberate pricing choice, not just weakness.** Management shifted from deep single-brand discounting to broad everyday-low pricing across the portfolio, trading volume for better revenue realization and (they claim) a more balanced competitive position — a metric-shaping choice worth treating skeptically until it shows up in share data.
3. **"One North America" is being tested, not rolled out.** The combined snacks-and-beverage warehouse/distribution pilot in Texas — chosen for high snack share, low beverage share — is explicitly a nuanced, market-by-market experiment, not a national commitment; management repeatedly resisted framing it as a single solution.
4. **Elliott's activist pressure is being publicly minimized, with one pointed dodge.** Laguarta called engagement "constructive and collaborative" and claimed most of Elliott's ideas are already in Strategy 2030, but sidestepped the specific ask for a Frito Lay margin target, saying only that the company would "educate each other a bit more."
5. **CFO transition brings in an external hire from PepsiCo's biggest customer.** After 33 years, Jamie Caulfield is retiring; incoming CFO Steve is described as coming from "your biggest customer" (implicitly a major retailer), a notable signal about where PepsiCo wants channel/retail expertise sitting at the top.

## Economy & consumer
- **Global consumer is "stressed" and choiceful,** per Laguarta, with meaningful geographic divergence: China weak, India growth intact but hit by weather and beverage competition, Middle East "feeling good," Eastern Europe outperforming Western Europe.
- **Mexico is a proxy for US Hispanic-consumer stress via remittances** — Laguarta flagged this linkage explicitly and expects it to persist "for the next few quarters."
- **Affordability is structural, not a blip:** "when you look at low-income households or middle-income households, they're very stretched. Fixed costs of living are going up around the world" — framed as a durable, multi-year dynamic shaping pricing architecture.
- **Clean-label and ingredient-consciousness is called a "secular trend,"** driving the shift toward non-artificial reformulations (Lay's, Tostitos now; rest of portfolio through 2026) and a new "Naked" no-color, no-artificial platform.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Beverages grew volume and share, led by Pepsi;** international volume/net revenue for Poppy, Siete, and Alani Nu isn't yet reflected in organic growth figures as the company laps those acquisitions.
- **Foods (Frito Lay) volume declined by design** as pricing shifted to everyday-low-value strategy; management claims the underlying business "actually grew in the last four weeks" and expects flat organic growth in Q4 — a forward claim not yet visible in reported data.
- **Service levels recovered to "97, 98" fill rates** after early-2025 systems-transition disruption, which management credits as a real driver of the recent sequential improvement, not just easy comps.
- **International Q3 softness attributed to weather,** with September snapping back to "mid to high single digits" — plausible but a claim resting on one month of data.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Brand relaunches are the primary growth lever:** Pepsi (done, working — zero sugar and flavor innovation cited as drivers), Lay's and Tostitos (underway), Gatorade (Q1–Q2 2026), each pairing reformulation with added marketing spend funded by cost cuts elsewhere.
- **Protein/functional-hydration build-out is deliberately organic, not acquired** — Muscle Milk and Propel repositioned rather than bought, because "it's always a better ROI to develop internally than not" when an existing platform can stretch; acquisitions (Poppy, Siete, Sabra) were reserved for spaces PepsiCo had no existing platform to attack.
- **The binding constraint is North American fixed-cost structure**, not capital or demand — Frito Lay is actively divesting manufacturing nodes, consolidating warehouses (including combining with beverage in some markets), and shedding excess go-to-market labor as the labor market stabilizes.
- **Productivity gains are backloaded into 2026:** CFO Caulfield flagged "pretty significant carryover benefit" of 2025 cost actions concentrated in the first half of 2026.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management expects a return to long-term algorithm growth sometime in 2026 and continued margin expansion across PBNA, international, and foods.
- **Action backing it up:** real, dated commitments exist — Gatorade relaunch scheduled Q1-Q2 2026, Texas One North America pilot underway, Frito Lay productivity-per-FTE already back to prior-year levels after six-to-seven months of cuts.
- **The gap:** the growth-reacceleration timeline itself is unhedged on quarter ("is it Q3? Is it Q4? We see that happening during the year") — a real belief without a real commitment behind the timing, which is the tell that this is more aspiration than plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No specific quarter for algorithm-growth return** — deliberately left open across all of 2026.
- **No commitment to a single national franchising or One North America model** — explicitly "will not be a one size fits all solution," with decisions deferred market by market.
- **No direct answer on a Frito Lay margin target** despite being asked twice (once by an analyst, once implicitly via the Elliott question) — management redirected to "educate each other" rather than engaging the specific number.
- **No commitment on franchising/refranchising bottling or distribution assets**, despite an analyst raising it directly — Laguarta said only that the company is "open to all the ideas."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Volume declines dominated early questions** — analysts pressed repeatedly on whether foods/beverage volume weakness reflects deliberate pack-size/pricing strategy, category softness, or real share loss; management's answer leaned heavily on the pricing-strategy explanation, which is self-serving and hard to independently verify from this call alone.
- **Cost-cutting versus growth-investment tension** came up multiple times — how PepsiCo funds an expensive innovation and relaunch pipeline (higher COGS, more A&M) while simultaneously cutting Frito Lay's fixed-cost base.
- **Elliott Management's activist stake got a direct, pointed question** on the Frito Lay margin-target idea specifically — the one moment management visibly declined to engage on specifics.
- **Compressed worry:** are the volume declines a controlled pricing choice on the way back to growth, or early evidence of share loss that management is currently re-labeling as strategy?
