# CAT — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-01-29)

**The key idea:** Caterpillar closed its centennial year with record sales, a record $51B backlog (+71% y/y), and its power-generation business becoming the clear growth engine off AI-data-center power demand — but the company is candid that it is now supply-constrained, not demand-constrained, and is racing capex and capacity expansion against orders that are increasingly multi-year commitments rather than near-term deliveries (only 62% of backlog now ships within twelve months, below historical average).

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Data-center power demand is reshaping the whole company, not just Power & Energy.** Power generation sales-to-users grew 44% in Q4 driven by "large gensets and turbines used in data center applications," and management now frames data-center construction spillover as a genuine driver for Construction Industries too, not just an power-and-energy story.
2. **The company is explicitly capacity-, not demand-, limited in its fastest-growing segment.** Joe Creed put it plainly on the AIP mega-order and broader power-gen build-out: "it's not a demand issue for us... it's really going to be can we bring on supply faster" — the 2026 revenue guide reflects only what capacity is already committed, with upside contingent on pulling capacity online early.
3. **Tariffs remain the single largest drag on reported profitability**, with $1.7B net impact in 2025 growing to a guided $2.6B in 2026 (would be ~20% higher without mitigation actions), knocking Q4 segment margins down 220-600bps and pushing 2026 margin guidance toward the bottom of the target range even as underlying (ex-tariff) margin sits in the top half.
4. **Resource Industries is the visible soft spot** — sales-to-users down 7% in Q4 as mining customers exercise capital discipline against weaker coal prices — a reminder that the AI-power boom is masking real softness elsewhere in the portfolio.
5. **Backlog quality is shifting toward multi-year commitments**, with management now scheduling factory orders against customer project timelines rather than shipping ahead of need — a rational supply-chain move, but one that also means backlog growth is becoming a less pure read on near-term demand.

## Economy & consumer
- **North America construction running hot**: nonresidential and residential construction beat expectations in Q4, with IIJA infrastructure funding and accelerating data-center-adjacent construction spend cited as 2026 tailwinds.
- **China stays a drag but troughing**: EAME and Asia Pacific sales-to-users declined slightly in Q4 as expected; management expects "positive momentum in China off of low levels" in above-10-ton excavators in 2026.
- **Mining customers pulling back on capital discipline**: Resource Industries sales-to-users fell 7% in Q4 as customers responded to weaker coal prices, even as copper and gold demand is expected to support 2026 growth.
- **No traditional consumer exposure** — CAT's "consumer" is its dealer/end-customer base (construction, mining, energy, transportation); financial health there is strong, with Cat Financial past-dues at a record-low 1.37% and allowance rate at an all-time-low 0.86%.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record quarter, record year**: Q4 sales of $19.1B (all-time single-quarter record) and full-year sales of $67.6B (highest in company history), up 18% and 4% y/y respectively, with full-year adjusted operating margin of 17.2% landing within the target range despite tariff headwinds.
- **Power & Energy is the growth engine**: segment sales grew 23% in Q4 to $9.4B with margin up 30bps y/y to 19.6% despite a 220bps tariff drag — power generation and oil-and-gas turbine/gas-compression demand both beat expectations.
- **Construction Industries margin compressed hard**: Q4 profit fell 12% y/y and margin dropped 470bps to 14.9%, with tariffs alone costing ~600bps — the segment's underlying volume strength was largely offset by tariff cost and unfavorable price realization.
- **Resource Industries the weakest print**: Q4 profit fell 24% y/y with margin down 510bps to 10.7%, driven by tariffs (~490bps) plus higher incentive comp and unfavorable pricing.
- **Services revenue on track**: full-year services hit $24B, progressing toward the $30B-by-2030 target, supported by a connected fleet that grew past 1.6 million assets.
- **Autonomous hauling adoption accelerating**: CAT ended 2025 with 827 autonomous haul trucks in operation (up from 690 in 2024), against a 2030 target to triple the 2024 base; a new Vale mixed-fleet deal (90+ trucks) via Brazilian dealer Sotrak signals expansion beyond dedicated autonomous fleets into mixed operations.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capex stepping up to $3.5B in 2026**, driven by large-engine and industrial gas turbine capacity expansion — large-engine capacity doubling and turbine capacity more than doubling by 2030, phased with the first major step-up expected late 2026 into 2027 and turbine capacity coming online somewhat later.
- **The binding constraint is supply, not demand**, and it extends beyond final assembly into the supply base and component/machining capacity — management explicitly ties future services upside (overhaul cycles on continuously-run prime-power and gas-compression units) to getting this capacity right now.
- **Capital returned to shareholders stays aggressive**: $7.9B deployed in 2025 (84% of MP&E free cash flow) via $5.2B buybacks and $2.7B dividends (32nd consecutive year of dividend increases); a larger ASR than the $3B executed in early 2025 is planned for Q1 2026, even as 2026 free cash flow is guided slightly lower than 2025 to fund the capex step-up.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: management believes power-gen demand from hyperscalers and data-center customers will keep growing through 2030 (50GW target set at Investor Day) and that prime-power (customer-owned generation, as an alternative to grid power) is an accelerating trend, evidenced by four gigawatt-plus orders booked to date.
- **Action**: capex committed to double large-engine and more-than-double turbine capacity through 2030, with the AIP/Monarch Compute Campus 2GW order (reflected in Q1 2026 backlog, delivering late 2026 through 2027) as the largest concrete proof point; weekly contact with hyperscaler and large data-center customers to align capacity plans with real project timing.
- **The gap**: despite the bullish 50GW framing, management is explicit that 2026 revenue guidance reflects only currently committed capacity ("what we have high confidence in") — any acceleration in data-center demand converts to upside only if CAT (and its supply base) can pull capacity online faster than currently planned, an outcome management calls uncertain even as it markets the order pipeline aggressively.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to confirm backlog growth continues at its current pace**: "I don't know that you can count on repeat every quarter" regarding the record Q4 order intake, specifically flagging Resource Industries orders as inherently lumpy.
- **Would not size the battery-storage component of the AIP mega-order** beyond calling it "a small portion of the overall total," deflecting a direct ask for a revenue-mix split (recip engines vs. BESS).
- **Declined to pin down 2026 power-generation revenue growth rate**, refusing to confirm either a repeat of 2025's ~30% growth or reversion to the ~20% long-term CAGR — reiterating it depends entirely on how fast supply comes online, not a demand judgment.
- **No commitment on capacity coming online early**: management flagged they "were able to ship a little bit more at year-end... than we anticipated" but explicitly called sustaining that pace, and any acceleration of the broader capacity ramp, uncertain rather than planned.

## The street — what analysts asked
Questions clustered around three anxieties: (1) reconciling the disconnect between explosive order/backlog growth (36-44% y/y for near-term-delivery backlog) and a comparatively modest ~7% sales growth guide, with management attributing the gap to dealer-inventory timing and capacity-paced delivery scheduling; (2) price-cost protection on backlog now stretching into 2027+ given continued tariff volatility, which management addressed via frame-agreement inflation indices and standard escalators past twelve months; (3) overcapacity/boom-bust risk if data-center demand slows, given CAT and peers are all raising capacity simultaneously — management's answer leaned on weekly hyperscaler contact and backup/prime-power terms rather than a quantified derisking plan. A separate cluster probed Construction Industries' demand drivers (data-center spillover vs. normal seasonal reversion vs. share gain) and turbine/BESS mix details on the mega-orders, several of which got only partial or deflected answers. Compressed worry: is Caterpillar's capacity race against AI-power demand well hedged against a slowdown, or is a large share of this order book optionality that could evaporate as fast as it materialized.
