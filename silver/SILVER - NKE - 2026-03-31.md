# NKE — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-03-31)

**The key idea:** Nike is deliberately trading reported growth for marketplace health — pulling ~$4B of peak classic-footwear revenue and taking a 5-point reported hit this quarter alone — while betting that a "sport offense" playbook proven in Running (+20%+) can be replicated across football, sportswear, and geographies. The tension: North America is inflecting on schedule, but EMEA is decoupling (sportswear sell-through miss, Middle East disruption, elevated Q4 inventory) and China remains a multi-quarter sell-in drawdown, so management swapped quarterly guidance for a vaguer nine-month "trajectory" framework instead of committing to shape.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The inventory cleanup is the story, not a footnote.** Removing "unhealthy inventory of classic footwear franchises" cost roughly 5 points of reported growth this quarter, and management says AF1/AJ1 have merely "stabilized" while Dunks still need work — this is real, ongoing revenue destruction taken on purpose.
2. **North America is genuinely inflecting, but not painlessly.** Wholesale grew 11%, sell-through turned positive across all channels for the first time in two years, yet North America gross margin still fell 360bps and EBIT declined 11% reported — the qualitative turnaround is ahead of the P&L.
3. **The $230M severance charge is a confession about the old direct-first model.** Friend explicitly ties it to "pandemic" overinvestment in a "higher fixed cost base" for a bigger digital/direct business that no longer matches strategy — Nike is now unwinding infrastructure it built four-plus years ago, with payoff not arriving until fiscal 2027-2028.
4. **EMEA broke from the recovery script this quarter.** Sportswear sell-through missed sell-in expectations, partners and Nike itself got more promotional, and Middle East disruption added traffic risk — management still expects elevated EMEA inventory exiting Q4, an explicit admission the marketplace isn't clean yet.
5. **Guidance got vaguer just as visibility should be improving.** After five quarters of 90-day guidance, Nike shifted to a nine-month "trajectory" (down low-single-digits revenue, margin inflection in fiscal Q2, earnings "flattish" through year-end) rather than quarter-by-quarter numbers — a structure that lets them claim transparency while dodging the specific shape analysts wanted.

## Economy & consumer
- **North America sell-through inflected first time in two years:** "we drove positive growth in all channels in the geography for the first time in two years," per Friend — the clearest read that US demand for cleaned-up Nike product is real, not just supply-side noise.
- **EMEA consumer facing external shocks on top of internal cleanup:** "recent disruption in the Middle East" is cited as a driver of both softer traffic and the decision to carry elevated Q4 inventory in the region.
- **China consumer being managed down deliberately, not just weak:** sell-in is being throttled to match "full price demand" — Greater China Q4 revenue guided down ~20%, a supply-side choice more than a demand collapse per management's framing.
- **No mention of a broader US consumer pullback:** Friend states "we're not seeing a consumer reaction to what's going on in the Middle East at this point in time in North America" — worth flagging as a company actively looking for macro spillover and not (yet) finding it domestically.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Running is the proof-of-concept for the whole turnaround:** "Nike Running up over 20% for the quarter" after being first through the "sport offense" — the template Hill says football, training, and basketball will follow "over the next few quarters."
- **Sportswear remains the drag on the whole portfolio:** "declined low double digits in the quarter" per Friend, and it's explicitly still "less than half" offset by sports dimensions — this is the segment holding back the total-company inflection.
- **Digital is still bleeding and still too promotional:** Nike Digital down 9% company-wide (down 21% in Greater China specifically); Friend: "digital is still too promotional... markdowns across the marketplace remain elevated," a direct admission the D2C reset isn't done.
- **Wholesale relationships genuinely reset:** "our ways of working look very different than they did 12 months ago. Order books are growing and we are taking back shelf space" — named partners DICK's, Academy, Foot Locker, JD, Shoe Palace as reengaging.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$230M severance is the headline capital action:** concentrated in supply chain and technology, plus Converse right-sizing, explicitly reversing pandemic-era fixed-cost buildout; Friend flags "additional financial impacts in future quarters" are still possible, though this quarter is expected to be the largest single hit.
- **Innovation platform bets are being scaled, not shelved:** Nike Mind production doubled for two seasons after selling out in every geography with "more than 2,000,000 consumers" on the waitlist — real capital following real demand signal, a rare case of investment tracking demonstrated pull rather than a bet.
- **Tariffs are now a structural cost, not a one-off:** 300bps company-wide gross margin hit, ~650bps gross impact in North America specifically — binding through fiscal 2027-Q1 by their own timeline.
- **The real scarcity is management bandwidth for a portfolio-wide, multi-year rebuild:** Hill's own framing — "brand by brand, sport by sport, country by country, partner by partner" — describes an execution capacity constraint more than a capital or supply constraint.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: win-now actions complete by calendar year-end 2026.** Action: new leadership installed in EMEA (César García), Greater China, and Converse, with "structural changes underway" — leadership change is a real commitment, but the specific playbook for China and EMEA sportswear is still described as "clearer strategies taking shape," i.e., not yet finished.
- **Belief: gross margin inflects in fiscal 2027-Q2.** Action: tariff mitigation steps and completion of win-now inventory cleanup are the stated levers — but no specific mitigation tactics (sourcing shifts, pricing) were detailed on this call, leaving the belief ahead of visible action.
- **Belief: World Cup 2026 catalyzes the football and broader marketplace "for quarters to come."** Action: concrete commitments — Tiempo already launched, Mercurial coming in June, Aero-FIT kits including a Jordan Brazil away kit, and elevated presentation across "more than 5,000 football doors" — this is the most matched pair of belief and funded action on the call.
- **Gap flagged explicitly:** Hill's Camp Nou metaphor itself concedes the gap — "capacity right now is limited" while the team competes "in the present" — a rare instance of management naming the tension between near-term results and long-horizon rebuild rather than papering over it.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No full-year or long-term guidance until the fall Investor Day:** explicitly deferred, with Friend repeatedly redirecting quarter-shape questions back to the nine-month framework.
- **Declined to break out EMEA Q4 revenue guidance directly**, forcing analysts (Binetti) to triangulate it themselves from the North America/China numbers given.
- **No specific magnitude given for how much of the underperformance is "self-inflicted" versus external/macro**, despite Boss directly asking — Hill's answer ("a little bit of both") stayed qualitative.
- **No commitment on further severance/restructuring size:** Friend says they "continue to evaluate opportunities" that "could result in additional financial impacts in future quarters" without bounding them.

- **Guidance mechanics were interrogated hard** — analysts wanted the nine-month framework translated into quarter-by-quarter shape (November vs. December, calendar Q4 vs. Q1) and got only directional commentary in return, a mild dodge of the specificity requested.
- **D2C quality vs. wholesale growth was a recurring anxiety** — asked to reconcile declining direct revenue with claims of "quality" improvement; Friend's answer leaned on discount-rate and digital-demand metrics rather than reported revenue, effectively redefining "quality" away from the top-line number under scrutiny.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts are no longer questioning whether Nike's strategy is right — they're questioning whether the timeline keeps sliding and whether "trajectory" guidance is a substitute for accountability on the actual numbers.
