# AVGO — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2025-12-11)

**The key idea:** Broadcom just posted a record quarter almost entirely on the back of custom AI silicon and networking, and used the call to stack up new proof points — an $11B TPU follow-on order, a fifth XPU customer, and a 10-gigawatt OpenAI capacity alignment — while carefully avoiding any number that would pin them down for full-year 2026. The tension: management wants credit for a backlog that "keeps growing" and "never ends," but won't convert that momentum into guidance, leaving the entire investment case resting on trust in a moving, self-reported number.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI backlog is being framed as a floor, not a forecast.** Hock Tan repeatedly walks back the natural reading of "$73B over eighteen months" as revenue, insisting "do not take that $73 billion as that's the revenue we ship... we do expect much more as more orders come in." That's a bullish framing device, but it also means the headline number analysts are trading on is explicitly a minimum, not a plan.
2. **The business is quietly shifting from chip sales to full AI-system/rack sales**, which management confirms will dilute gross margin ("gross margins will be lower... but gross margin dollars will go up") even as they insist operating margin dollars keep growing. This is a real mix-shift, not just a modeling footnote — Kirsten Spears says it will show up "the second half of the year."
3. **Five custom-silicon customers now, with TPUs starting to be resold externally** — Google's TPUs are being used by Apple, Cohere, and SSI for AI cloud compute. Tan calls this a "substitution effect" against merchant GPUs, not against Broadcom's other custom-ASIC customers, since building your own XPU is "a long-term strategic move" that isn't abandoned mid-journey.
4. **OpenAI relationship has two separate, non-binding-sounding tracks**: a 10-gigawatt "alignment" for 2027-2029 (explicitly "not 26"), and a separate custom-XPU program Tan says is "at a very advanced stage" — but he declines to confirm the $1B fifth-customer order is OpenAI at all, twice.
5. **Non-AI semiconductor and enterprise IT remain stuck**, with Tan's own explanation being that "AI is sucking the oxygen... out of enterprise spending" — a direct, if informal, admission that AI capex is displacing rather than adding to overall corporate tech spend industry-wide.

## Economy & consumer
- **No consumer-facing commentary** — Broadcom sells to hyperscalers and enterprises, not consumers; omitted from the read.
- **Enterprise IT spend is flat and stuck**, with Tan noting only broadband is recovering while "all the other end markets were down as enterprise spending continued to show limited signs of recovery." Non-AI semis guided flat y/y for Q1.
- **AI is explicitly cannibalizing enterprise/hyperscaler budget elsewhere**: "I think maybe the AI is sucking the oxygen a lot out of enterprise spending elsewhere."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **AI semis are the entire growth story**: revenue up 74% y/y to $6.5B in Q4, guided to double y/y to $8.2B in Q1 — "a growth trajectory exceeding 10 times over the eleven quarters we have reported this line of business."
- **Networking/optics is outrunning even the XPU story**: Tomahawk 6 switch backlog exceeds $10B and is "one of the fastest-growing products in terms of deployment that we have ever seen"; DSPs, lasers, and PCIe switches are all booking at record rates.
- **VMware integration is fully realized**: infrastructure software operating margin hit 78%, up from 72% a year ago, "reflecting the completion of the integration of VMware," with TCV bookings of $10.4B vs. $8.2B a year ago.
- **Non-AI semis are the drag**: flat to down across every segment except broadband, with no sharp recovery expected — "we do not see it getting any worse. We do not see it recovering very quickly."

## Investing & scarcity
- **Singapore advanced-packaging fab** is the single named capex commitment, meant to partially insource multi-die XPU packaging "not just from the viewpoint of cost, but... supply chain security and delivery."
- **Silicon supply (TSMC 2nm/3nm) is "not constrained yet,"** per Tan, but he flags this could change: "time will tell as we progress and as our backlog builds up."
- **Packaging, not wafers, is the acute bottleneck today** — described as "a very interesting and technical challenge" driven by the shift to multi-chip customer accelerators.
- **Capital return, not capex, absorbed the balance-sheet upside**: 10% dividend increase to a record $2.60/share (15th consecutive annual increase) and a buyback extension through 2026, funded by $7.5B of free cash flow in the quarter (41% of revenue).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** AI revenue growth will keep *accelerating* through 2026 — "it's likely to be an accelerating trend as we progress through 2026" — off a base that already doubled in Q1.
- **Action backing it up:** two large concrete orders landed this quarter ($11B TPU follow-on, $1B fifth-customer XPU order) plus the OpenAI 10GW alignment and a shift to full system/rack sales that increases dollar content per deal.
- **The gap:** despite calling the trend "accelerating" and the backlog "never ending," management refuses to translate that into a 2026 revenue number — the actions (new orders, new fab, dividend hike) are all present-tense and reactive to backlog already in hand, not commitments sized to the accelerating forecast Tan describes verbally.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No full-year 2026 AI revenue guidance**: "I rather not give you guidance... it's hard for me to pinpoint what 2026 is going to look like precisely."
- **Won't confirm the fifth XPU customer is OpenAI**, deflecting twice ("I did not answer it. I'm not answering it either").
- **The OpenAI 10GW deal is called an "agreement... an alignment"** rather than a firm backlog commitment, explicitly separated from the (also unconfirmed) custom-XPU program with the same customer.
- **Declines to characterize how much of the $73B backlog beyond eighteen months will persist**, telling one analyst "your guess is probably as good as mine" on whether rack-level revenue continues past 2026.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: **is the backlog number real or a floor that will be exceeded** (asked at least three different ways), **how much will system/rack sales dilute margins**, and **whether custom silicon at hyperscalers threatens Broadcom's own franchise** (in-house tooling, TPU-as-merchant-product cannibalization).
- Multiple questions tried to pin down **whether the fifth customer and the OpenAI relationship are the same thing** — management dodged directly each time while giving adjacent color.
- Supply chain questions (wafers, packaging, HBM, CoWoS) got relatively confident, low-anxiety answers — the bottleneck concern was real but management projected control over it.
- **The compressed worry**: analysts don't fully trust that a backlog this large, this fast-growing, and this deliberately un-guided is fully binding revenue rather than a rolling snapshot of intent.
