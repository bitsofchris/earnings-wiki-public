# XEL — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-05)

**The key idea:** Xcel just doubled its publicly committed data center pipeline from 3GW to 6GW by 2027, backed by two major new partnerships (NextEra for co-development, GE Vernova for turbine/equipment supply), while explicitly telling investors the earnings benefit is a 2029-2030+ story, not a near-term one. The tension: management wants credit now for a demand pipeline whose capital and sales impact it can't yet quantify — repeatedly asking for "a little bit of time" — while simultaneously managing legacy liabilities (Marshall Wildfire settlement, under-earned Colorado ROE) that are actively suppressing current results.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Data center pipeline doubled, but the payoff is pushed to the next decade.** Contracted/high-probability data center capacity went from 3GW to 6GW by 2027 via the NextEra MOU and a new Upper Midwest ESA, but Brian Van Abel was explicit that "you might see a lot of these coming in maybe that '29 type period," with capital investment and sales growth ramping "into the 2030s" — this is a pipeline-extension story, not a near-term earnings catalyst.
2. **Twenty-one straight years of hitting guidance, but Colorado ROE is a known soft spot.** Full-year ongoing EPS of $3.80 beat 2024's $3.50, yet Van Abel conceded Colorado earned returns are "significant[ly] under earnings" and won't fully recover until the pending electric/gas rate cases flow through in 2027 — current results are propped up elsewhere while Colorado underperforms.
3. **Wildfire liability is converging to a manageable number.** Marshall Wildfire settlements are essentially done (only 3 of 4,000+ plaintiffs outstanding, $300M charge taken), and Smokehouse Creek's low-end liability estimate ticked up to $430M with $382M already committed in settlements — management frames this as "considerable progress," not an open-ended tail risk.
4. **CEO pushes back on the "bring your own generation" narrative.** Asked directly about data centers self-supplying power, Frenzel said hyperscalers "don't really want to own and operate their own generation" and prefer the utility to build and rate-base it — a notable divergence from the self-generation narrative circulating in other utility markets, self-serving but stated with conviction.
5. **PSPS (preemptive shutoffs) drew real public backlash, and management didn't budge.** Despite pushback from Colorado fire districts and lawmakers over a December shutoff, Frenzel said "we stand by our decision" and framed the criticism as a communication/awareness gap rather than a signal to change the policy.

## Economy & consumer
- **Affordability is the core political defense, not a macro read.** Colorado residential customers have "the lowest share of wallet out of all 50 states," and bills in Denver/Minneapolis have risen 40-80% less than peer regions since 2020 — this is deployed explicitly to preempt rate-case and election-cycle pushback, not as commentary on the broader consumer.
- **Energy assistance hit a record high.** ~200,000 customers received close to $200M in aid in 2025, "our highest ever one-year total" — a signal that even with low relative bills, affordability stress among vulnerable customers is rising, not falling.
- **No broader economic commentary.** The call contains essentially nothing on general business-cycle conditions or non-utility consumer spending — Xcel's "demand" discussion is almost entirely data-center-driven, not general economic activity.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Guidance track record intact.** $3.80 ongoing EPS for 2025 (21st straight year of meeting/exceeding initial guidance), reaffirmed 2026 guidance of $4.04-$4.16 and 9% average EPS growth through 2030.
- **Colorado is the drag.** Van Abel: Colorado earned returns are "significant[ly] under" authorized levels, driven partly by prioritizing a "constructive settlement" on Marshall over rate relief — full ROE recovery isn't expected until 2027 rate case impacts land.
- **SPS underperformed on weather.** Fourth-quarter weather challenges in SPS dented results; management expects a "get back closer to where it's been" via rider recovery and the pending New Mexico rate case.
- **O&M costs rose from wildfire mitigation and insurance.** $190M O&M increase driven by "accelerated wildfire mitigation costs in Colorado, excess liability insurance costs" and higher benefits/generation maintenance costs — the cost of the resiliency push is showing up directly in the expense line.
- **Winter Storm Uri (Jan 2026) was a reliability proof point.** Management touted flawless cold-weather execution and even exporting excess generation capacity during the storm — a direct callback to the 2021 Texas Uri failure that damaged utility credibility sector-wide.

## Investing & scarcity
- **$60B+ five-year capital plan, with an additional "$10B-plus" pipeline layered on top.** New capacity includes 7,000 incremental MW of renewables/gas/storage versus the prior plan, plus 760+ miles of newly awarded 765kV transmission (including a fresh SPP award worth $1.5B).
- **Two new strategic supply/development alliances lock in execution capacity.** The NextEra MOU (generation/storage/interconnection co-development for data centers) and the GE Vernova alliance (5 more gas turbines, 24 total on order, wind turbines bid into upcoming RFPs) are explicitly aimed at derisking the delivery bottleneck, not sourcing more demand.
- **~20GW of renewables equipment safe-harbored** to lock in expiring production/investment tax credits before they roll off — a direct, capital-intensive hedge against future policy risk.
- **The real constraint is execution speed, not demand or capital access.** Frenzel used the word "speed" repeatedly and asked analysts for "a little bit of time" to build organizational capacity with NextEra — the bottleneck is people, permitting, and construction throughput, not financing or interconnection requests.
