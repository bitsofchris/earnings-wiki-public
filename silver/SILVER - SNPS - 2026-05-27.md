# SNPS — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-05-27)

**The key idea:** Synopsys beat and raised on the back of AI-driven chip complexity, but the more consequential story is a deliberate pivot from *pure value creation* to *value capture* — new consumption-based pricing for agentic EDA, new business models for hyperscaler custom-silicon IP, and $400M+ in Ansys/Synopsys cross-sell synergies — all of which management pushed off to a September investor day rather than quantify now. Layered on top: an Elliott Management board seat arrives at exactly the moment the company is already mid-pivot on margin and monetization, and a chunk of this quarter's "beat" is an accounting reclassification, not organic growth.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **The IP "recovery" is capacity-gated, not demand-unleashed.** CFO Shelagh Glaser attributed sequential IP growth (12% Q1→Q2) partly to "title availability" freeing up as resources shift toward HPC — meaning the bottleneck was internal allocation, not customer appetite. That's a subtly different story than the "IP bottomed and is recovering" framing management leads with.
2. **A real business-model change is underway in IP and EDA, but it's not booked yet.** Ghazi described moving hyperscaler customers from usage fees to "subscription plus consumption" for agentic EDA and from per-use IP licensing to models capturing more dollar value as those customers commit to custom silicon (COT). None of this is in FY2026 guidance — it's pure optionality being teed up for FY2027 and the investor day.
3. **Elliott's arrival tracks almost exactly with management's own pre-existing thesis.** Ghazi says Elliott and new board member Jesse Cohn pushed on "monetize further" and "improve operating margin" — but insists these were already the company's talking points for "at least three quarters." Worth watching whether the September investor day content changes materially post-Elliott versus what would have been said anyway.
4. **The revenue beat has an accounting asterisk.** $12.5M of Q2 revenue (and $60M of the full-year raise) comes from reclassifying Ansys channel revenue gross instead of net — neutral to EPS and cash flow, but it inflates the topline growth optics precisely as analysts are trying to gauge organic Ansys growth (mid-teens ex-accounting, guided to ~10% for the year).
5. **AI is bifurcating design activity, not lifting it broadly.** Chip starts are rising sharply for anything AI-related; analog, industrial, and automotive design starts remain "fairly muted" even where those same customers report strong revenue — a real divergence between end-market health and future engineering pipeline.

## Economy & consumer

- **AI is the only segment pulling design starts higher.** "What we are seeing is a chip start increase or a design start increase in anything AI related... [analog/industrial/automotive] design starts are definitely not growing at the pace as we're seeing for the other cohort," per Ghazi — direct evidence that AI capex is not yet broadening into general semiconductor demand.
- **China remains structurally impaired, not just cyclically weak.** "The design start environment in China remains challenged given all the restrictions and the cumulative impact of the restrictions" — management says guidance already assumes no China recovery ("we're fairly pragmatic when it comes to our guide in China").
- No end-consumer segment; Synopsys sells to chip designers and system OEMs, so this key is omitted from ongoing tracking except as a proxy via design-start trends.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **EDA and hardware-assisted verification are the clear engine.** EDA revenue grew "slightly over 8%" YoY with "multiple strategic system wins" in emulation/prototyping (ZS5, ZeBu-200, HAPS-200) driven by hyperscaler and leading semiconductor customers scaling AI verification workloads.
- **Design IP is still down YoY despite the sequential bounce.** Revenue fell ~6% YoY even as it rose 12% sequentially; management's own framing — "we believe the IP segment bottomed in Q1" — is a claim about trajectory, not a claim of health yet.
- **Multiphysics Fusion is pre-revenue but showing strong early technical proof points**: "up to 3x faster design closure with higher ECO success rates and up to 2x faster turnaround times for complex analog designs" in trials, but commercial ramp doesn't start until second half of 2026 and monetization not until customers exit eval phase.
- **GAAP costs ran hot on accelerated restructuring**, pulling GAAP EPS down to $0.09 versus non-GAAP $3.35 — a real divergence worth tracking as the RIF continues.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Headcount strategy is bifurcated, not just cuts.** Synopsys is still working through the remainder of a committed 10% reduction in force, while simultaneously growing open requisitions for both "Synopsys Classic" and "Ansys Classic" roles several-fold since Q4 — Glaser: "we're also investing in critical areas... being very disciplined about the roles that we're hiring for."
- **The binding constraint in IP right now is internal capacity/allocation ("title availability"), not customer demand** — a scarcity of deployable engineering/licensing resources as the company shifts staff toward HPC-aligned high-value IP.
- **Capital is flowing to Multiphysics Fusion and agentic EDA build-out** ahead of any confirmed monetization, alongside continued Ansys integration spend and buybacks ($250M accelerated share repurchase, $50M open-market repurchase).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** Agentic EDA and new hyperscaler IP business models will materially expand monetization ("subscription plus consumption," dollar capture beyond traditional use fees). **Action:** Only 20 customers are evaluating agentic capabilities across 25+ agents, and management explicitly excludes any of this from FY2026 guidance — the belief is well ahead of the booked commitment.
- **Belief:** Ansys/Synopsys cross-sell will generate $400M in semiconductor-related revenue synergies (and eventually $1B) with confidence "higher" than before. **Action:** That revenue doesn't start until FY2027; today it's "limited set of partners" in eval, with the sales guardrail "one plus one must be greater than two" still being tested rather than proven.
- **Belief:** IP has bottomed and will show sequential growth every quarter this year. **Action:** The mechanism enabling that growth is internal resource reallocation ("title availability") rather than a demonstrated surge in external bookings — a thinner form of conviction than the guidance language suggests.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No FY2026 guidance credit for Intel Foundry 18A/14A pipeline**, despite acknowledging visibility into the engagement: "we're not accounting or taking into account or in our guidance any upside" until customers move from eval to production.
- **No synergy timeline beyond "more at investor day."** When pressed twice on remaining Ansys synergy magnitude and timing, Glaser deferred both times to the September 30 investor day rather than give a number.
- **No commitment on how new agentic/consumption pricing will be structured** — Ghazi called it "current thinking" and "early exploration with customers," explicitly not yet a defined contract model.
