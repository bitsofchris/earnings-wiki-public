# MDB — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-03-03)

**The key idea:** MongoDB closed FY26 with genuinely strong numbers — Atlas past $2B run-rate, non-Atlas's best growth in two years, a record $100M+ EA deal — but the call is really about a leadership handoff (new CEO's first full quarter, a CRO and Field Ops President both exiting) landing right as a single oversized bundled deal muddies the Atlas growth signal. Management's storyline leans heavily on AI-native logos and "agents will love MongoDB" positioning, while candidly admitting agentic AI isn't yet moving the revenue needle anywhere in the enterprise.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **One deal did a lot of narrative work.** A single >$100M EA transaction pulled enough revenue attribution away from Atlas that reported Atlas growth (29%) understated underlying growth (would've been ~30%) — CFO Berry called it "unique" and explicitly declined to project more of these, undercutting any read-through to trend.
2. **Go-to-market is mid-transition with real uncertainty.** CRO Paul Keppambesis and Field Ops President Cedric Pech are leaving after a decade each, a new CRO search is still open, and a new Chief Customer Officer was announced same-day — CFO admitted this "adds a level of uncertainty" baked into guidance, even while insisting no disruption is expected.
3. **On-prem (EA) is being reframed as strategic, not legacy.** Desai's customer conversations across financial services, healthcare, telecom, and government point to a real "keep mission-critical data on-prem" trend tied partly to AI-era security/sovereignty concerns — yet FY27 EA guidance is only low-to-mid-single-digit growth, a wide gap between the qualitative story and the number.
4. **AI is a pipeline, not yet a P&L line.** Vector search customers nearly doubled YoY and Voyage embedding usage doubled since acquisition, but Desai bluntly told a direct question that agentic workloads are "not yet" scaling anywhere in the Fortune 500 he's spoken to, and declined to predict when that changes ("it was supposed to be 2025... nothing meaningful in the enterprises").
5. **Guidance philosophy is deliberately sandbagged on EA.** Management restated its "more upsides than downsides" approach for EA specifically, meaning the record Q4 print is not meant to reset expectations upward — a discipline that reads as credible but also means the headline beat won't recur mechanically.

## Economy & consumer
- **No direct consumer read** — MDB's customers are developers and enterprises; management's closest macro comment was CFO Berry calling the back-half visibility caveat "more of a general macro comment... not specific to any set of workloads."
- **Guidance assumes stability, not acceleration**: "Our outlook assumes the business environment remains relatively stable, and we operate under similar conditions to what we experienced over the course of the past fiscal year" — a flat, non-committal macro stance.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Atlas broke $2B run-rate** with 29% YoY growth and a record $114M of net-new revenue in the quarter; net ARR expansion rate ticked up to 121% from 119% a year ago.
- **Non-Atlas had its best growth in two years (20%)**, driven by financial services, public sector, and tech customers signing multiyear EA commitments — RPO nearly doubled YoY to $1.47B.
- **Customer growth accelerating**: 65,200+ customers (up 60% YoY in full-year adds), with $100K+ ARR cohort up 17% and $1M+ ARR cohort up 26% — the upmarket motion is outgrowing the base.
- **What's not working**: the EA/Atlas bundling created forecasting noise that both surprised management and forced a mid-quarter explanation to investors; margin outperformance was explicitly attributed to revenue beat, not cost discipline.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital and engineering effort is going toward EA-Atlas feature parity**, Voyage embedding integration, a bigger Japan presence, and strengthening the U.S. federal business — all funded while still expanding operating margin ~100bps.
- **The real constraint is leadership bench depth in sales**, not compute or capital — CFO acknowledged the CRO/Field Ops transition as a guidance risk factor even as CJ Desai expressed "high confidence" in regional theater leads covering the gap.
- **Partner ecosystem is being deliberately narrowed**, not expanded: Desai (ex-ServiceNow) wants to concentrate on hyperscalers, "2 or 3" systems integrators, and AI framework/LLM partners rather than a broad partner sprawl — an implicit admission the prior partner strategy underperformed.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: on-prem/EA is structurally more important going forward due to AI-era data sovereignty and security concerns across regulated industries. **Action**: engineering investment to bring EA to Atlas feature parity — a real commitment, though FY27 EA revenue guidance (low-to-mid-single-digit) doesn't reflect much confidence in near-term acceleration, which management attributes to forecasting conservatism around multiyear-deal timing rather than to doubting the trend itself.
- **Belief**: agentic AI will eventually be a major growth driver ("not if but when"). **Action**: building "machine-friendly APIs," auto-scale/auto-shard capabilities, and repeating the SF dot-local event in August specifically to court AI-native founders — genuine product roadmap commitment, but explicitly not yet reflected in any revenue guidance or contribution.
- **Belief**: modernization onto MongoDB from legacy databases is accelerating alongside agentic coding tools. **Action**: this remains anecdote-driven (two customer conversations cited, in the UK and Spain) rather than a quantified pipeline metric — a belief still ahead of measurable action.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to project more mega-bundled deals**: Berry repeatedly stressed the >$100M EA transaction was "unique" and wouldn't commit to a repeatable pattern, despite it being the largest TCV deal in company history.
- **FY27 non-Atlas/EA guidance deliberately below momentum**: management chose low-to-mid-single-digit growth for EA and mid-to-upper-single-digit for Q1 specifically to avoid a negative surprise from multiyear deal timing risk they admit they "cannot forecast."
- **No commitment to specific AI revenue contribution or timeline**: Desai declined to predict when agentic AI becomes a growth driver, explicitly citing being burned by 2025 expectations that didn't materialize.
- **No detail offered on which customer or workload drove the mega bundled deal**: both executives kept the specifics vague when pressed twice on it.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Bundling mechanics dominated**: multiple analysts pressed on whether the large EA deal signals a structural shift pulling revenue from Atlas to EA, or was a one-off — management held firm it was one-off but conceded it reduces near-term Atlas growth visibility.
- **Leadership transition risk**: several questions probed whether the CRO/Field Ops departures reflect problems with the prior go-to-market approach (particularly partner ecosystem weakness) and whether guidance is now more conservative because of it.
- **Agentic AI's real enterprise impact**: recurring theme of whether coding-agent momentum is translating into enterprise modernization budget — Desai's answer stayed anecdotal and hedged, reinforcing that this is still a belief, not a measured trend.
- The compressed worry: **is the strong quarter representative of durable momentum, or was it flattered by one unrepeatable deal landing right as the sales leadership team turns over?**
