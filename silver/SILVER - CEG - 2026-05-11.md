# CEG — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-11)

**The key idea:** Constellation's quarter was a clean beat almost entirely inherited from Calpine, but the real story is a company sitting on an enormous, largely uncontracted demand pipeline (data centers, hyperscalers) that it insists it cannot fully monetize until PJM finishes rewriting its capacity market rules. Management is confident enough in the eventual outcome to buy back stock and disclose a multi-year free cash flow ramp, while carefully declining to commit new capital or specific deal terms until the regulatory picture — which they now say is moving at "unprecedented" speed — actually resolves.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Calpine is doing the heavy lifting on this quarter's numbers.** The $0.60/share year-over-year EPS beat was "mostly attributed to the EPS accretion from Calpine" (~$2/share full-year), while organic drivers were mixed — higher PJM capacity prices and lower stock comp offset by more nuclear outage days, softer ZEC pricing, and Winter Storm Fern costs.
2. **Regulatory clarity, not power, is the scarce resource.** Dominguez repeatedly frames PJM's rulemaking timeline (targeting a FERC filing by June, full clarity "by the end of the year") as the gating factor for converting a >400,000 MW ERCOT interconnection queue and surging hyperscaler capex into actual signed contracts.
3. **The buyback was a valuation statement, timed deliberately.** $335M repurchased at ~$285/share, executed before the first Calpine share lockup expires June 30 — management insists timing "shouldn't be read into" but confirmed the $5B authorization was sized partly with the lockup in mind.
4. **CEG is betting against the ERCOT forward curve.** Andrew Novotny: the 2028+ forward market appears to price in only 10,000-15,000 MW of load growth versus a queue "over 400,000 megs" — management is explicitly positioned for upward repricing.
5. **The deal mix is deliberately heterogeneous.** From full-curtailability data centers to round-the-clock CFE-style contracts (the Microsoft precedent), Dominguez rejects any single "ratio" framework — different customers will accept different reliability tradeoffs, and that flexibility is the commercial product.

## Economy & consumer
- **Hyperscaler capex is still accelerating, not slowing.** "Projected spending levels for 2026 are nearly 75% higher than last year and continue to be revised upward" — demand is not the constraint anywhere in this call.
- **Residential/commercial ratepayer relief is the political argument for large-load growth.** Management repeatedly ties data center connections to "stabilize and perhaps actually lower costs for all Americans" by improving system utilization of existing fixed grid infrastructure.
- **No direct residential-consumer read** beyond this framing — CEG's real "consumer" here is the C&I/hyperscaler counterparty, not a retail household.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Nuclear fleet stayed strong operationally.** 40 million MWh generated at a 92.3% capacity factor despite more planned refueling outage days than typical for Q1.
- **What's not working:** ZEC pricing softened across state programs, and Winter Storm Fern forced the grid operator to call on operating reserves, raising ancillary charges and cost-to-serve — a real, if modest, cost headwind acknowledged directly by CFO Shane Smith.
- **Customer margin expansion is real and broad-based**, per Smith: traditional C&I power margins expanded, carbon-free product demand added incremental margin, and the Calpine retail book skews toward "a higher mix of tailored products in attractive high-value markets," now covering ~275 million MWh and 800 BCF annually across 40 states, including over 80% of the Fortune 100.

## Investing & scarcity
- **New generation delivered from the Calpine pipeline, not fresh capital:** the 105MW Pastoria solar project and 460MW Pin Oak Creek gas peaker were both underway pre-acquisition — Dominguez explicitly credits this to validate the Calpine deal thesis rather than claim new organic execution.
- **~5,000 MW submitted to PJM's interconnection queue** (nuclear uprates, new gas, battery storage) — but when pressed on whether this represents a firm commitment to new build, management declined: "I don't know that at this point we're in a position to commit anything until we get a little bit more detail from PJM."
- **The actual binding constraint is regulatory, stated plainly:** "Customers want to get their data centers online as quickly as they can. They need regulatory clarity for that to happen."
- **Secondary constraint: transmission interconnection speed** for specific assets like Crane, where full capacity credit depends on FERC approving a CIR transfer from Eddystone — management is "working with the utilities involved here to shorten up these projects."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** PJM's backstop framework goes to FERC by June and "we'll have clarity on all these issues by the end of the year"; ERCOT's forward curve is undervalued for 2028+.
- **Act:** Bought back $335M of stock now, at today's price, rather than waiting for regulatory clarity to derisk the thesis — a real capital commitment made ahead of the catalyst it's betting on.
- **The gap:** Despite the confident PJM timeline and ~5,000 MW queued, CEG has NOT committed incremental new-build capital beyond what's already funded — Dominguez says twice they're waiting for "a little bit more detail from PJM" before deciding what actually gets built. The buyback is a funded bet on valuation; the generation buildout is not yet a funded bet on demand materializing into contracts.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No answer on which nuclear restart site (Calvert vs. Limerick) moves first** — Dominguez called it "a bit of a non-answer" deliberately, noting they'll "let our customers announce transactions when they're ready."
- **No incremental new-build capital commitment** pending further PJM detail, despite the 5,000 MW queue submission.
