# VZ — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** This is a change-of-leadership call, not an operating-results call. New CEO Dan Schulman (ex-PayPal, ex-AT&T) uses his first earnings appearance to declare the prior "price increases without subscriber growth" strategy dead, while the quarter's own numbers — postpaid phone net losses of 7,000, the worst since the pandemic — are the direct product of that strategy's exhaustion. Nearly every substantive question was deferred to January, so this call is best read as a thesis statement to be graded later.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A CEO transition dressed as a strategy pivot, with the substance withheld.** Schulman repeatedly promises detail "in January" — on cost cuts, portfolio exits, and the customer value proposition — while asking the market to trust a still-undefined turnaround. "I'm actually never going to go into specifics because I don't want to tip off our competitors," he says, which is also a convenient way to avoid commitments this quarter.
2. **Postpaid churn crossed into net losses despite record gross-add growth.** Gross adds up 8.4% year-over-year but churn of 0.91% still produced 7,000 net phone losses — an analyst notes this is the worst account result since Q1 2020. Convergence is the one lever shown to work: fiber-mobility bundled customers churn "nearly 40% lower" than the base.
3. **Management indicts its own recent playbook.** Schulman calls pricing "the last refuge of the marketing desperate," directly contradicting CFO Skiadas's own line minutes earlier crediting "continued ARPU growth from targeted pricing actions" for the quarter's wireless service revenue growth.
4. **Frontier is a drag on the very cash-flow story management is selling.** Frontier is "burning $1 billion of cash today" per an analyst's question, yet Skiadas insists 2026 free cash flow will grow "all in, including Frontier" — an assertion resting entirely on cost cuts not yet detailed.
5. **AI shows up as a marketing/efficiency tool, not infrastructure ambition — yet.** Schulman name-checks "AI infrastructure is booming, and we can be a part of that," but the concrete AI commitments discussed are internal: personalized offers, churn prediction, proactive service — not a compute or data-center build.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read offered.** The call contains zero commentary on consumer spending power, inflation, or broader demand conditions — unusual for a company this consumer-facing; the entire framing is internal execution, not external environment.
- **Four self-diagnosed reasons customers leave**, per Schulman: price increases, service friction, "value perception," and competitive intensity — a admission that the prior strategy actively pushed customers out.
- **Public sector wireless is shrinking** from "ongoing government efficiency efforts," offset by SMB and large-enterprise demand — a rare direct read-through of DOGE-style federal cuts into a private company's book.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broadband and prepaid are genuinely strong**: 306K broadband net adds, Fios's best quarterly result in two years (61K), prepaid revenue growth for the first time since the TracFone deal, and a fifth straight quarter of prepaid net adds.
- **Core mobility is not**: postpaid phone net losses of 7,000 despite 8.4% gross-add growth — churn is outrunning acquisition. Schulman states Verizon captures "somewhere between 0% and some very low percent" of new-to-industry postpaid subscribers.
- **Free cash flow and leverage are the bright spots on paper**: $7B quarterly free cash flow, "highest reported in our industry by nearly $2 billion," and net debt/EBITDA already at 2.2x — inside the long-term target ahead of the Frontier close.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital-light fiber expansion via partnership**: the Tillman deal lets Verizon build Fios-branded fiber outside its own and Frontier's footprint without owning the buildout — a structural shift from pure organic capex.
- **Bolt-on M&A continues alongside Frontier**: the Starry acquisition adds MDU (multi-dwelling unit) fiber capability even as the larger $10B Frontier deal is still mid-close (11 of 13 state approvals).
- **The real scarcity is share-capture, not capital or network.** Schulman is explicit the network and brand are already strong; the constraint binding growth is execution — an unusual admission that money isn't the bottleneck.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: churn can become "industry best."** Action so far: no new pricing structure, no named product — only a promise of "evidence... even beginning this quarter." The gap between the stated ambition and the disclosed mechanism is the widest divergence on the call.
- **Belief: cost cuts will fully self-fund customer investment with no margin trade-off.** Action: a January reveal of a "portfolio rationalization" that will exit unnamed "legacy" businesses described as "hemorrhaging margins" — real dollars, but the specific units and size are undisclosed.
- **Belief: 2026 free cash flow grows despite Frontier's cash burn.** Action: reiterated guidance and a lower-end 2025 capex outcome, but the offsetting Frontier-related and cost-transformation math is not shown.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No numeric churn target, no timeline**, only "lowest churn rate in the industry" as an aspiration.
- **No detail on which businesses get divested** or how much margin they represent — deferred wholesale to January.
- **Declined to discuss competitive tactics explicitly**, citing fear of "tipping off" AT&T and T-Mobile.
- **Declined to flex leverage guidance despite a direct question** on M&A opportunism; Skiadas reaffirmed the 2.0-2.25x target as unchanged, with only a temporary Frontier-related bump acknowledged.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Questions clustered on three anxieties: (1) how do you grow postpaid share without repricing the back book or spending heavily on promotions, (2) will 2026 free cash flow growth actually materialize given Frontier's cash burn and a comparatively slow organic fiber build relative to the size of the Frontier deal, and (3) whether the "customer-centric" pivot is a real strategy change or a relabeling exercise ahead of a January reveal that still contains all the substance.
- Management's answers leaned heavily on personal credibility (Schulman's PayPal turnaround story) rather than disclosed mechanics — the compressed worry: **is this a strategy, or a promise to have one in ten weeks?**
