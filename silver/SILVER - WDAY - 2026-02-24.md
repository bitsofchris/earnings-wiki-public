# WDAY — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-02-24)

**The key idea:** Aneel Bhusri returns as CEO declaring a new "Chapter 4" built on agentic AI layered on Workday's deterministic HR/finance system of record — but the FY27 guide (12-13% subscription growth, flat ~30% margin) is a self-described trough year, because the new agents are priced on consumption (Flex Credits) and management says the revenue won't show up until the back half of the year. The tension: genuine usage momentum (1.7B AI actions, $400M ARR from AI products) running well ahead of what's actually been monetized, while core enterprise deal cycles are lengthening.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A CEO return built entirely around a reacceleration bet.** Bhusri came back specifically to push agentic AI, framing the core apps as stable but slow-growing ("in some areas like HR, we have high levels of market share") and pinning all upside on agents: "AI is just a huge opportunity for Workday to reaccelerate growth."
2. **Consumption pricing means usage success won't show up in revenue yet.** Despite $100M+ new ACV from AI products in Q4 (>100% YoY) and 400+ customers live on new agents, Flex Credits is described as "probably more of a second half motion" for FY27 — a deliberate lag between adoption and recognized revenue.
3. **Enterprise deal friction is real but downplayed as timing, not loss.** Rob Enslin: large deals in "Fed, SLED and health care and across parts of the commercial market" pushed out of Q4, with the company insisting "we don't see them moving out of our pipeline. We just see them elongated" — a claim analysts pressed on but couldn't fully verify from the numbers given.
4. **Margin expansion is explicitly paused to fund the bet.** FY27 non-GAAP operating margin guided to ~30%, essentially flat, "albeit at a slower pace" than previously communicated — Zane Rowe framed this as intentional, not a shortfall.
5. **Platform-as-toll-collector strategy against third-party agents.** Bhusri was unusually blunt about competitors and ecosystem players "parasites on Workday" riding free on its system of record, and laid out a plan to charge any third-party agent (via APIs/data cloud) that touches Workday data — a monetization-of-the-moat play, not just a product story.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read given** — Workday didn't characterize the broader demand environment; all commentary was company-specific (deal cycles, retention).
- **Enterprise, not consumer, exposure**: relevant demand signal is deal elongation in Fed/SLED/healthcare/commercial large-enterprise segments, contrasted with medium-enterprise (ME) strength — ME drove "roughly 60% of net new ACV in FY26."
- **International still nascent**: Europe, ASEAN, Canada called out as "solid," India newly opened with "1 or 2 deals," but Rob Enslin acknowledged "there's still work to do" before international becomes a bigger share of growth.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: retention and expansion.** Gross revenue retention held at 97%; expansion contributed ~60% of subscription revenue growth for the year; AI-attached expansion deals ran "nearly 50% larger on average."
- **Working: inorganic agent adoption.** HiredScore, Evisort, Paradox, and Sana "all had great Q4s" — Bhusri argued these acquisitions are proof customers will now buy Workday's own organically-built agents.
- **Not working: net-new large-enterprise velocity.** Q4 volume of net-new deals was hit by elongated Fed/SLED/healthcare/commercial cycles — the company's characterization that this is a pipeline-timing issue, not demand destruction, is unverified by the numbers on the call.
- **Not working (leadership churn): sales chief departure.** Patrick Blair's exit prompted a flattened sales org reporting directly to Rob Enslin — framed as a speed/decision-making improvement, but a leadership gap during a strategic pivot nonetheless.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital reallocation, not new capital raised**: FY27 guide explicitly directs incremental spend into "accelerated pace of AI investment across both product and go-to-market" at the expense of margin expansion versus prior guidance.
- **Buybacks continued at scale**: $1.5B repurchased in Q4, $2.9B for FY26, with $2.9B authorization remaining — capital return unaffected by the AI investment pivot.
- **The real constraint is monetization timing, not resources.** Agents are built and in early access/GA already; the bottleneck is converting usage into recognized revenue under a new consumption model still ramping ("there's a delay before we see the actual revenue from it").
- **CapEx stays light** (~$270M guided for FY27) — this is not a compute buildout story like the hyperscalers Workday name-drops as its pricing analogy.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: Bhusri says agentic AI will "enable Workday to reaccelerate growth, increased customer satisfaction and set us up for long-term leadership," with the inflection concentrated in H2 FY27 and beyond.
- **Action behind it**: 12 organic agents reaching GA in FY27 (five in the March R1 release alone), Sana Core/Enterprise shipped GA in 3 months, Flex Credits live with ~50 anchor customers, forward-deployed engineers added to drive activation.
- **Gap**: the FY27 growth guide (12-13%) is only "in the range" previously set at Financial Analyst Day and management would only say the investment should push them "higher on that range than where we otherwise would have been" — a soft, unquantified claim that the stated conviction doesn't fully cash out into raised guidance now.
- **M&A pivot**: explicit deprioritization of acquisitions in favor of organic agent development ("that's not — our focus really right now is on the organic development of agents"), a real strategic shift from FY26's Sana/Paradox/HiredScore/Evisort acquisition spree.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No hard timeline on margin re-expansion**: Zane Rowe deferred specifics to "our next financial analyst update," despite direct questioning on why margin guide didn't move up more.
- **No sizing of Q4 deal slippage**: management would not quantify the cRPO or revenue impact of elongated Fed/SLED/healthcare deals beyond saying they remain in pipeline.
- **Guidance philosophy unchanged, not raised**: despite a new CEO and stated optimism ("I would hope that we significantly surpass that"), the FY27 growth guide itself was not raised above the 12-15% medium-term range set previously — optimism stayed verbal.
- **Inorganic contribution to FY27 growth left vague**: Zane said it's "not a material component" but declined to give a number.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Recurring theme: is the FY27 guide too conservative given the AI narrative?** Multiple analysts (Kirk Materne, Brad Zelnick) pushed on why the numeric guide didn't reflect the enthusiasm of the agentic pitch; answers leaned on "we tend to guide conservative and beat it" rather than new data.
- **Margin skepticism**: Brent Thill directly questioned why operating margin wasn't expanding given past trajectory — answered with "trust the growth investment" framing rather than a firm floor.
- **Numbers didn't add up on Q1 sequential decline**: John DiFucci's math (DIA contract impact + day-count couldn't bridge the full $25M sequential drop) went unresolved — Zane repeated the same explanation without reconciling the gap, the most concrete unforced tension on the call.
- **Platform/ecosystem risk**: Gabriela Borges and Alex Zukin both probed whether Workday's own data/API layer could get commoditized as third-party AI agents build on top — management's answer leaned on the defensibility of 20 years of business-process IP rather than a structural moat argument.
- **Compressed worry**: analysts are asking, in different words, whether the "reacceleration" is a real second-half inflection backed by verifiable numbers, or a story being used to explain away slower core growth and margin stagnation until the AI revenue actually shows up.
