# QCOM — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-04-29)

**The key idea:** Qualcomm's near-term numbers are being scrambled by an AI-driven memory shortage that's forcing Chinese OEMs to under-order chips relative to real consumer demand — a temporary, non-Qualcomm-caused drag management is confident bottoms next quarter. Underneath that noise, the real story is a pivot: Qualcomm is trying to reframe itself from "phone chip company" to "the CPU company for the agentic AI era," with automotive already delivering, and a first, still-undetailed custom-silicon data center deal as the proof point — with nearly every substantive question deferred to a June 24 Investor Day.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The memory shortage is real and it's Qualcomm's excuse for the quarter.** AI data-center memory demand is squeezing handset OEMs on price and supply, and Qualcomm says it's "materially undershipping consumer demand" as a result — guiding QCT handset revenue down to ~$4.9B next quarter, with China bottoming in fiscal Q3 by their own account.
2. **Automotive is the one segment firing on all cylinders.** Automotive crossed $5B annualized for the first time, grew 38% YoY this quarter, and is guided to ~50% YoY growth next quarter — Amon calls the car "a computing surface" and is banking on 3x CPU / 3x GPU / 12x NPU gains in the next chassis generation.
3. **The data center pitch is long on framing, short on facts.** A "leading hyperscaler" custom silicon deal ships in December, but management wouldn't say if it's a CPU, accelerator, or networking part, wouldn't size it, and repeatedly pointed to the June 24 Investor Day — a pattern that reads as pre-selling the event rather than disclosing a real business.
4. **Samsung is now a stabilized >70% share relationship, not a shrinking one.** Amon explicitly reset the historical 50/50 framing to ">70% share" as the new steady state for this year and next, pushing back on analyst suggestions that Samsung's in-house silicon is displacing Qualcomm.
5. **Agentic AI is the new master narrative across every product line** — phones, PCs, cars, robotics (Figure AI, NEURA), and the coming data-center chips are all being pitched through the same "CPU is the agent-orchestration bottleneck, and we have the best CPU" lens, including a direct performance claim against Intel's Panther Lake (~30% faster).

## Economy & consumer
- **Sell-through is intact; shipments are not.** "Sell-through held up. Our chip business materially undershipped consumer demand," Amon said — the licensing business's visibility into activations is management's evidence that this is a channel problem, not a demand problem.
- **OEMs are rationing memory toward premium devices.** Akash: "If you had to choose between which devices you put your memory allocation to, you would pick the premium and the high tier" — mid/low tier handset units are the ones actually declining.
- **Global handset units were flat YoY**, the steadiest signal in an otherwise noisy quarter.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **QCT handset revenue ($6B) held to plan, but the ceiling is capped by memory, not orders** — guided to $4.9B next quarter purely on the memory dynamic, not demand.
- **Automotive and IoT combined grew 20% YoY**, underscoring genuine diversification away from the smartphone cycle — this is the strongest organic growth story on the call.
- **A one-time $5.7B non-cash GAAP tax benefit** from a reversed valuation allowance (tied to new Treasury/IRS guidance on capitalized R&D deductions) padded GAAP results but was explicitly excluded from non-GAAP — a clean disclosure, not a spin attempt.
- **Capital return accelerated** to $3.7B for the quarter ($2.8B buybacks, $945M dividends) even as the core handset business is under near-term pressure.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Memory is the binding constraint**, and it's exogenous — driven by AI data-center memory demand, not anything within Qualcomm's control, per management's framing.
- **The data center buildout is the marquee new investment**, combining organic CPU/accelerator development with the Alphawave acquisition's connectivity and custom-ASIC IP; Amon: "you should assume that Qualcomm will play on merchant, on custom... it's going to be a bespoke business."
- **6G is being staged as a decade-long infrastructure bet** — prototypes in 2028, first silicon 2028, launch 2029, scale by 2030 — with Qualcomm positioning itself to capture new "AI-native network" revenue models beyond device chips (sensing, drone tracking, digital twins).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** agentic AI will drive a premium-tier smartphone upgrade cycle into fiscal 2027, agent orchestration favors Qualcomm's CPU advantage everywhere from phones to data centers.
- **Act:** shipping the fifth-gen automotive chassis platform, ramping a custom hyperscaler silicon deal for December, building dedicated data-center CPU and inference-accelerator silicon — real product commitments, concentrated so far in automotive (proven, shipping) versus data center (one named but unspecified customer).
- **The gap:** every data-center claim is qualified by "we'll share details at Investor Day" — the capital and R&D commitment is real, but investors are being asked to underwrite the strategy on faith for eight more weeks before getting a single hard number (chip type, revenue scale, margin).

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to characterize the December hyperscaler chip** — not CPU, accelerator, or networking specifics, "I can't provide a lot of details... we don't wanna front run June 24th."
- **Wouldn't guide September-quarter handset revenue direction**, citing offsetting factors (China recovery vs. Apple share step-down) without picking a side: "we're not specifically guiding it at this point."
- **Declined to forecast 2027 memory market conditions** despite building the agentic-phone thesis on assumptions about future memory availability: "It's a little early to talk about 2027."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Data center specifics dominated the Q&A** — chip type, competitive positioning against Arm, NVIDIA, Groq, and Cerebras, ASIC vs. merchant strategy, margin accretion — nearly all deflected to Investor Day.
- **China bottom-calling got pressed hard**, with one analyst directly asking whether handsets even grow sequentially in the September quarter; management wouldn't say yes.
- **Customer concentration questions** (Samsung share stability, Apple's 20% share assumption for fall launches, QTL royalty continuity through renegotiation) all got confident, consistent reaffirmations — no new news, no walk-backs.
- **Compressed worry:** is the "leading hyperscaler" data-center deal a real second growth engine being intentionally under-disclosed ahead of a big reveal, or a small pilot dressed up to carry the stock until June 24th?
