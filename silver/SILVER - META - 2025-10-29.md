# META — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** Meta delivered another strong quarter on the back of AI-driven ranking and ads improvements, but the call was dominated by a forward-looking capex story management explicitly would not size: 2026 spending will grow "notably" faster than 2025's already-raised guide, justified by compute scarcity in both the speculative superintelligence bet and the proven, monetizing core business. The tension is that the same "compute starved" argument that makes the frontloading sound rational also removes any obvious ceiling on it.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The core business is the real justification for the AI capex, not superintelligence.** Zuckerberg repeatedly leans on "we keep on having more demand to be able to use more compute... especially in the core business in ways we think would be quite profitable" — the speculative superintelligence bet is the stated north star, but the safety net cited for the spend is proven ad/ranking ROI today.
2. **A one-time tax charge, not operations, cratered the GAAP headline.** Net income fell to $2.7B ($1.05 EPS) purely from a noncash deferred-tax-asset write-down tied to new U.S. tax law; ex-charge net income was $18.6B ($7.25 EPS) — the underlying quarter was strong, but a careless reading of the headline would badly misread the business.
3. **Reality Labs' 74% revenue growth is an accounting/timing artifact, not demand strength.** The jump came from retail partners stocking Quest headsets early in Q3 (vs. a Q4 2024 Quest 3S launch comp); management flagged this explicitly and guided Q4 RL revenue down, with strong AI-glasses growth "more than offset by the headwinds to the Quest headsets."
4. **Capex guidance for 2026 is directionally alarming and numerically absent.** "CapEx dollar growth will be notably larger in 2026 than 2025" and "total expenses will grow at a significantly faster percentage rate" — both stated with conviction, neither quantified; the budget process is explicitly still "not baked."
5. **Off-balance-sheet financing (Blue Owl JV) is quietly absorbing part of the capacity build.** Meta takes a 20% ownership stake and books only its pro-rata share as "other investing cash flows" rather than CapEx — a structural choice that keeps headline capex lower than the true infrastructure footprint, worth watching as more JVs likely follow.

## Economy & consumer
- **Ad pricing up double digits, partly diluted by lower-value geos.** Average price per ad +10% y/y "benefiting from increased advertiser demand," offset by impression growth concentrated in "lower monetizing regions and services" — demand is strong but its mix is diluting blended pricing.
- **Engagement growth is broad and accelerating, not just video.** US Facebook/Instagram time spent grew double digits y/y with "healthy growth in non-video time on Facebook" alongside the video story — a sign core engagement isn't purely being propped up by Reels.
- **No macro/consumer-spending commentary at all.** Unlike a retailer or consumer-goods company, Meta gave zero read on discretionary spending health — its "consumer" signal is purely engagement and ad-spend proxies.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Ads AI stack compounding gains.** Lattice rollout to app ads: "nearly 3% gain in conversions"; new runtime ranking model on Instagram: "more than 2% lift in conversions"; Andromeda model consolidation: "14% increase in ads quality on Facebook surfaces" — each incremental, but stacking on a large base.
- **End-to-end automation crossed $60B run rate**, with Advantage+ lead campaigns now showing "14% lower cost per lead on average" — Susan Li frames this as still under-penetrated ("a lot of advertisers only use our end-to-end automated solutions for a portion of their campaigns").
- **Click-to-WhatsApp ads grew revenue 60% y/y** — business messaging is becoming a real monetization line, not just an engagement feature.
- **Expense growth accelerated 20 points q/q**, driven by legal charges (including a lapped accrual reversal), AI-talent comp, and infra/depreciation costs — the cost base is inflecting well ahead of any AI product monetization.
- **Reality Labs hardware momentum is real on AI glasses**: Ray-Ban Display "sold out in almost every store within 48 hours," forcing a manufacturing scale-up decision — but this is a small, still-subscale business next to the ads engine.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Compute is the binding constraint, stated plainly and repeatedly.** "We are sort of perennially operating the family of apps and ads business in a compute starved state at this point" — an unusual admission given the scale of the buildout already underway.
- **2026 compute needs "expanded meaningfully... including versus our own expectations last quarter,"** meaning the plan is being revised upward mid-cycle, not just extended.
- **Capital allocation strategy is explicitly optionality-driven**: staging data center sites to "spring up capacity quickly," and structuring the Blue Owl JV for "option value for future compute needs" spanning 2027-28, not just 2026.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: 2026 ad/engagement growth continues from ranking R&D.** Action matches — concrete 2026 roadmap items cited (foundational ranking model scale-up, LLM-based content understanding, broader topic surfacing) with committed headcount and compute.
- **Belief: superintelligence could arrive "in a few years" or take "5, 7 years or longer."** Action: capex is being frontloaded for the optimistic case regardless — Zuckerberg's own framing ("the right strategy to aggressively frontload... so if it takes longer, we'll use the extra compute to accelerate our core business") is a hedge dressed as a growth thesis — the downside case is explicitly "we were just slow building new infrastructure for some period."
- **Gap:** management is confident enough in compute-driven ROI to commit tens of billions in accelerating spend, yet unwilling to give any 2026 dollar figure, margin target, or cash target — the conviction in the narrative is not matched by conviction in a number.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 capex or opex dollar guidance** — "there are lots of moving pieces in the budget. It's not baked yet."
- **No cash-on-hand or net-cash targets**, despite being asked directly.
- **No margin framework for new AI products or wearables** — "I think it's too early to really understand what the margins are going to be for the new products that we build."
- **No timing commitment on the next frontier model or MSL product launch** — "I don't think we have any specific timing to announce."
- **Declined to characterize competitive positioning strategically** — Zuckerberg said he was "keeping this high level because I don't want to... get into what we're prioritizing" when asked about MSL's capability focus areas.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Capex/opex trajectory and ROI evidence dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts asked for early quantifiable signals justifying the spend, and for how 2026 capex growth splits across MSL, core AI, and non-AI; management gave qualitative color (value-weighted conversion growth outpacing impressions) but no hard 2026 numbers.
- **Reality Labs revenue quality was probed directly** ("how large is the headwind"), and management again declined to quantify it beyond directional commentary.
- **Off-balance-sheet financing mechanics** (Blue Owl JV, on- vs. off-balance-sheet mix) got a specific, technical question — a sign analysts are starting to scrutinize how much true infrastructure spend is being kept out of the CapEx line.
- **Monetization path for Meta AI and value-capture in the broader AI stack** were both raised without management naming any near-term revenue targets.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts are less concerned about whether Meta can build AI products than about whether there is any ceiling on the spend needed to get there — and management gave no number to anchor that ceiling.
