# KLAC — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-07-28)

**The key idea:** KLA is describing an AI-driven wafer equipment cycle that keeps re-accelerating mid-year — WFE guidance has been raised **four times in 2026** — and management is explicitly sizing supply chains for "bullish scenarios" rather than the consensus case. The tension: gross margin isn't expanding with demand, because memory input costs are rising faster than KLA can reprice existing orders, and management all but admits it won't push customers on pricing mid-cycle.

## Where they're going / what they're building

- **Advanced packaging has become a real second engine**, not an Orbotech-era afterthought: 2026 packaging process-control revenue was raised to **~$1.1 billion, up more than 70% year-over-year** — nearly double the packaging market's own growth rate, and the market itself was revised from ~20% growth to a **mid-to-high-30% growth rate** intra-year. Rick Wallace: "our folks who were right in the middle of it were surprised by the magnitude of that."
- **Supply chain planning has extended to 2029+** for critical optical components, since lead times on that gating hardware run **12 to 24 months**. Bren Higgins frames this as sizing for upside, not the base case: "what if demand was 10% higher? What if 20% was higher?"
- **HBM/DRAM process-control intensity is now rivaling advanced logic**, driven by base-die customization and stricter performance specs — a structural shift management didn't fully anticipate at the March Investor Day.
- **Foundry broadening is real and durable**: Intel pulling in 14A, Samsung signing new 2nm/3nm customers, Rapidus and new greenfield "Terafab"-style projects — Rick calls this the "inevitable" diversification the industry has wanted, and it underpins the 2027 setup as much as AI compute itself.
- **KLA reports company-wide lead times of ~12 months, with some products at 18–24 months**, and conversations with customers are already about second-half-2027 deliveries — visibility that's pulling backlog to an estimated **$12.5 billion**, growing "pretty consistently over the last couple of quarters."

## What's changing

- **WFE forecast raised for a fourth time in 2026**, to the "low $150 billion range" from "$140 billion-plus," with **2027 consensus settling around $190 billion** — a growth rate management says is "similar to the growth rate in 2026," i.e., no deceleration expected.
- **Second-half 2026 revenue is guided to grow ~20% over the first half**, with September-quarter revenue guided to **$4 billion**, up from $3.66 billion — supply constraints that throttled H1 shipments are now clearing.
- **Memory mix is shifting**: foundry/logic is expected near 73% of semiconductor systems revenue in Q3, but management flagged memory will be "a bigger percentage" starting in the December quarter and likely staying elevated through first-half 2027.
- **Services grew 17% year-over-year to $820 million** and is expected to accelerate toward the "higher end" of a newly set 13–15% long-term target range, underpinned by 80%-contract revenue and rising tool utilization.
- **Capital returns held steady** at $876 million in the quarter ($571M buybacks, $305M dividends) against $817 million free cash flow — payout essentially matching cash generation even as the company also increases working-capital and facilities investment.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)

- **Gross margin is not expanding despite record demand.** Guided flat sequentially at 62.5% even as revenue grows another ~10% into December — Bren attributes **~100+ basis points of ongoing headwind to rising memory component costs**, which he expects to persist through 2027.
- **Management dodged the pricing-power question directly.** Asked twice why KLA can't do what ASML is reportedly doing — raising prices to offset input inflation — Bren's answer was that it's "pretty hard to go back to your customers after you've taken orders and start to change prices," meaning pricing resets only happen at new-product introduction, not on the existing order book. This is a real margin drag management is choosing to absorb rather than fight.
- **Rick's own caveat on forecasting**: "we're really good at creating systems that are valuable to our customers... We are not very good at forecasting" — a notably candid admission for a company simultaneously asking investors to trust multi-year capacity commitments.
- **The 2030 target model ($215B WFE, $1.4T semi TAM) is already stale** four months after being set at Investor Day, but management explicitly declined to reset it ("we're in no position to reset anything"), instead noting it would likely be exceeded — leaves the official long-range target functioning as a floor, not a real forecast.
- **China competitive dynamics were only partially addressed**: Rick leaned on the technical moat (applications engineers, algorithm depth) but Bren's answer implicitly conceded share losses in China ("we've gained share at a time when a number of our competitors have been able to ship into fabs in China that we haven't") — export restrictions are costing KLA share, not protecting it.
- Conspicuous absence: no mention of China memory-import-ban scenarios materializing, despite an analyst directly raising the risk that a lifted ban could redirect Chinese memory WFE spend to domestic suppliers.

## Street anxiety (compressed)

Whether gross margin can ever re-rate given memory cost inflation and reluctance to reprice back-book orders · whether the 2027 WFE number ($190B) is achievable or just extrapolated optimism given KLA's own admitted forecasting weakness · how much of the 2026 upside is real unit growth versus price/mix (semiconductor revenue itself hit prices "we also couldn't have imagined") · whether component and capacity constraints (not demand) are now the actual ceiling on KLA's growth · what happens to the China share picture if import restrictions loosen · underlying worry: the AI/HBM cycle looks unambiguously strong on volume, but nobody — including management — is confident whether pricing, margins, or the components supply chain can keep pace with it.
