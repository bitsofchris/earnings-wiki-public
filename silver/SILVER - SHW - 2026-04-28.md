# SHW — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** Sherwin-Williams beat guidance across all three segments purely on share gains in a market management describes as offering "no help" for a fourth straight year — but the quarter's real story is what's coming: a Middle East-driven propylene/solvent cost shock that management has only partially priced in, betting that surgical, contract-protected buying and North American sourcing insulate them more than the raw math suggests.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Share gains are real and broad, but they're compensating for, not reflecting, market strength.** Res repaint is up mid-single digits in a flat-to-down market; commercial is up mid-single digits while completions are down double digits; Refinish is up low-teens with price gains "in every region." Heidi Petz calls this a "jump ball environment" and says the team is "out chasing new business aggressively" rather than waiting for the market to turn.

2. **The raw material story is a ticking clock, not a resolved issue.** Propylene, the base for 75% of SHW's basket, is forecast up "maybe up 50% more through the rest of '26" on Middle East disruption, yet full-year inflation guidance moved only from low-single to low-to-mid-single digits — a gap multiple analysts (Josh Spector, Jeff Zekauskas) pushed on without getting a fully reconciled answer beyond "our procurement is maybe tighter than some of the others" (Ben Meisenzahl).

3. **Pricing has shifted from blanket increases to surgical, contract-aware targeting.** Consolidated price mix guidance rose to the high end of low-single digits, "more than 2x the pricing now in this new guide than what we had in the original guidance," phased by region (Asia-Pacific/EMEA first) and channel (spot-heavy industrial before contract-heavy architectural). Management explicitly reserved the option for a mid-season increase: "If we need to go again, we will go again."

4. **The consumer is bifurcating.** Premium DIYers paying up and staying loyal to specialty stores; value-conscious DIYers pulling back toward home centers — a live read on discretionary spending stress that shows up as +5% specialty DIY sales against roughly -5% at the broader retail channel.

5. **Packaging's outperformance is regulatory-timed, not purely share-driven.** High-single-digit growth against a flat-to-down global food/beverage market is explicitly tied to the EU's BPA ban (effective Q2 2026) accelerating customer conversions — a tailwind management says has "well into 2027" of room to run, separate from the underlying share-gain narrative applied elsewhere.

## Economy & consumer

- **No market tailwind, again.** "This is our fourth year in a row we have been operating with the expectation of getting no help from the market" — Heidi Petz. New residential demand stayed "very challenging as we anticipated."
- **Sentiment is at record lows.** CFO Ben Meisenzahl cited consumer sentiment prints "much worse" than GFC and COVID lows as a driver of the softer full-year volume assumption baked into guidance.
- **DIY bifurcation is explicit and quantified.** Premium/specialty-store DIY is up mid-single digits (price and mix, not volume); the value-conscious, home-center-leaning DIYer is down roughly 5% — a direct read on discretionary household stress.
- **Structural housing underbuild is the long-term consumer bull case.** Management leans on a "structural deficit" narrative (underbuilding for years) as the reason pent-up demand must eventually convert to volume, even as near-term household formation "has slowed a little bit."

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Every segment beat guidance, but margin flow-through was muted.** Consumer Brands margin rose on global supply chain efficiencies and premium mix, but Performance Coatings' segment margin was flat despite mid-single-digit profit growth — "higher incentive compensation... along with the significant FX headwinds, drove segment SG&A higher, resulting in muted flow-through."
- **Refinish and Packaging are the standout share-gain stories.** Refinish up low-teens with double-digit growth in every region and direct installs also growing double digits; Packaging up high single digits against a flat-to-down underlying market, aided by the BPA-ban conversion cycle.
- **Store footprint optimization continues, not expansion for its own sake.** 21 new stores opened, 27 closed in the quarter (about half a percent of PSG stores), framed as "decades" of ongoing portfolio discipline tied to Return on Net Assets employed rather than growth-at-any-cost.
- **Suvinil (Brazil) diluted reported gross margin** even as it boosted Consumer Brands' headline growth (high-teens contribution); underlying legacy Consumer Brands ex-Suvinil grew only low-single digits, with North America down low-single digits.

## Investing & scarcity

- **Capital return stayed the priority: $773M in buybacks and dividends this quarter**, with net debt/EBITDA at 2.5x — no signal of a shift toward larger capex or M&A in the face of cost pressure.
- **The binding constraint is now supply-side, not demand-side: securing raw material supply and locking in cost certainty.** "Our first objective is certainty of supply," per Heidi Petz, with over 80% of consolidated revenue insulated by North American sourcing and roughly 50% of purchasing under contract rather than spot.
- **The scarcity is geographically uneven and time-lagged.** Feedstocks are squeezed first in APAC/EMEA (with 60-90 day shipping lag cited by Ben Meisenzahl), meaning the U.S. cost impact is explicitly expected to intensify into H2 2026 rather than hit immediately.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** raw material inflation will keep worsening through the year, landing hardest in H2, but current pricing "is enough... for what that inflation is" under their base case.
- **Action:** priced 2x the original plan already, held back from a blanket architectural increase, and explicitly reserved dry powder for a mid-season move if the Middle East situation deteriorates further — a real hedge against their own forecast being wrong.
- **The gap:** the magnitude mismatch between a 50% propylene forecast and only a modest full-year inflation guidance bump is the clearest talk/action tension on the call — management's confidence rests on procurement structure (contracts, sourcing mix) that wasn't fully quantified for investors.
- **Belief:** the housing/architectural downturn could persist "softer for much, much longer," but **action** on Paint Stores Group guidance didn't change — full-year guide "remains in line" with prior low-single-digit expectations, described by Ben Meisenzahl as "a barometer of confidence," not caution.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Refused to quantify the demand impact of the Middle East conflict**, calling it "difficult to predict... given the highly fluid nature of the situation," despite raising inflation guidance.
- **Declined to characterize architectural demand as structurally "impaired,"** preferring "under pressure" — while simultaneously confirming internal scenario planning for a much longer downturn.
- **Would not confirm whether the window for a mid-season architectural price increase has passed** — Heidi Petz kept it explicitly optionality-framed: increases would come "in the middle or the beginning or the end of the season," decided reactively.
- **No forecast offered on when housing policy "unlocks" (50-year mortgages, land-supply reform) would show up in results** — management said only that they're "watching that closely" and would "be ready to act."

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Raw material math didn't add up cleanly for several analysts.** Multiple questions (Duffy Fischer, Josh Spector, Jeff Zekauskas) probed why a 50% propylene forecast produced only a modest guidance revision, and management's answer leaned on procurement structure rather than a reconciled number.
- **Pricing mechanics and timing dominated the Q&A** — surgical vs. broad-based increases, whether the January window has closed, and whether competitors' larger price moves signal SHW is under-pricing risk.
- **Volume credibility was tested directly** (Ghansham Panjabi, Mike Sison): given record-low consumer sentiment and a lowered volume outlook offset by higher price, is the unchanged full-year EPS guide really unchanged, or is the mix just shifting risk from volume to price execution?
- **Compressed worry:** does Sherwin-Williams's pricing and cost-out plan actually close the gap if Middle East-driven feedstock inflation runs longer or deeper than the company's base case assumes — and how much of the current guide is priced-in confidence versus wishful modeling?
