# EXC — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-05-06)

**The key idea:** Exelon is threading a needle between an affordability backlash sharp enough to make it withdraw a live rate case in Pennsylvania, and a supply-demand crunch across PJM severe enough that management keeps warning of blackout risk by 2028. The resolution on offer is a capital reshuffle — less distribution spend, more transmission — dressed as discipline, while the actual generation shortfall that management says is the real driver of costs remains entirely outside EXC's control (and, in most states, outside its legal authority to fix).

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A rate case withdrawal framed as goodwill, not weakness.** CEO Calvin Butler pulled PECO's electric and gas filings after stakeholder pushback, insisting it "does not change our commitment to... long-term infrastructure investment." But PECO was already on negative outlook and under review for a credit downgrade before this — the goodwill narrative sits next to real credit-market consequences.
2. **The generation shortage is the load-bearing story, and EXC can't fix it directly.** Butler's most repeated line all call: "you cannot have a conversation about affordability without addressing the underlying shortage of generation." Customers paid $32B for PJM capacity over two years while supply fell 1.2GW — a structural indictment of the current market design, not of EXC's operations.
3. **Capital didn't shrink, it moved.** The four-year plan stayed roughly flat ($41.7B) but shifted $1.5B into transmission and pulled $1.1B out of PECO/BGE distribution — a bet that transmission (competitive, RTO-mandated, data-center-linked) is the safer growth lane right now versus state-regulated distribution caught in affordability politics.
4. **A leadership change at PECO lands right as the political heat peaks.** David Velazquez moved to an advisory role and Michael Innocenzo (PECO CEO 2018-2024) returned as interim head — a continuity move timed exactly with the rate case withdrawal and governor pressure, though management didn't connect those dots explicitly.
5. **Data centers are being de-risked before they're counted as growth.** The transmission upside case now explicitly excludes ~$1B of data-center pipeline collateral already secured via FERC-approved Transmission Security Agreements — EXC wants that load counted only once it's contractually locked, not speculative.

## Economy & consumer
- **Affordability is now the entire regulatory conversation.** Every state interaction described on the call — PA's governor's letter, Maryland's RELIEF Act, the PECO withdrawal — was driven by residential cost pressure, not reliability complaints.
- **The 80% five-year cost run-up is doing a lot of work in the narrative.** Butler cites Mid-Atlantic residential supply costs up "80% or more" over five years as the justification for why supply-side fixes, not utility cost-cutting, are the real lever — a claim that shifts blame toward PJM's capacity market design.
- **PA's governor set explicit new rules of engagement.** His letter demanded "justifiable returns," transparency in ratemaking, and cost-effective capital — Butler called this "nothing that we hadn't heard" but it's now a public marker regulators will hold EXC to in the next filing.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Reliability performance is the one unambiguous win.** All utilities hit top-quartile reliability, ComEd top-decile, even through "several high wind storm and storm events" this spring — operational execution isn't the problem here.
- **Q1 EPS beat estimates but declined year over year.** $0.91 vs $0.92 prior year — new rate lag (-$0.07) and ComEd revenue-shaping timing (-$0.04) outweighed favorable weather; guidance ($2.81-$2.91) reaffirmed regardless.
- **$350M of new 2027 O&M cuts are tied directly to projects being dropped**, not general belt-tightening — "if we're not gonna do certain projects, we're gonna pull those costs out of the business." Butler calls these largely sustainable, but concedes some opcos "will have to make deeper provisions" specifically because they're not investing.
- **PECO's credit standing is deteriorating in real time.** CFO Jeanne Jones confirmed PECO was already on negative outlook and under review for downgrade heading into this quarter — a fact that complicates the "timing, not retreat" framing of the rate case withdrawal.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Transmission is where EXC is leaning hardest.** Rate base growth there raised to 16% through 2029, with the $12B-$17B upside range unchanged and explicitly excluding the ~$1.9B of MISO Tranche 2.1 bids (with Invenergy) or any new solar/storage opportunities.
- **Distribution investment in PA and MD took the cut** — $1.1B of deferrals — even as management insists "critical work" remains needed there; Jones explicitly frames this as "a cost to investing, and a cost to not investing," acknowledging the tradeoff rather than pretending it's costless.
- **The real scarcity is generation, and EXC is legally boxed out of building it in most states.** Management repeatedly points to Charles River Associates' estimate that utility-owned generation could have saved PJM customers $9.6B-$20B in the 2028/29 delivery year and cut outage risk ~85% — a number used to lobby for legislative change (MD HB 1561, PA HB 1272/SB 897), not something EXC can act on unilaterally today.
- **Only 19% of interconnection-queue projects historically reach operation**, per PJM's own head of external and regulatory affairs on the call — a sobering ratio against the widely-cited "811 projects, 220GW" queue headline.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** PJM could face blackout risk as soon as 2028, and "we're halfway there" with no meaningful supply progress. **Action:** EXC is bidding competitively on transmission (2 MISO bids filed, 2 more expected this month) and lobbying for utility-owned generation authority — but cannot itself build the generation it says is the actual fix. The gap between the stated urgency and the tools available to act on it is the central tension of the call.
- **Belief:** Interconnection delays (e.g., the Crane restart not connecting until 2031) are a solvable process problem. **Action:** Limited to "partnering with PJM to see what we can do" — no concrete commitment or timeline offered.
- **Belief:** Affordability requires "an all-of-the-above approach" including utility-led, demand-side, and merchant investment. **Action:** Concrete near-term moves are entirely on the utility-led/cost-control side ($350M O&M cuts, $1B in customer savings) — the demand-side and merchant pieces remain aspirational and reliant on legislation not yet passed.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No details on any generation partnership or contracted-generation deal**, despite pointed questioning. Butler: "per our process and who we are as a company, I don't talk about those things with you... until we've got something to talk to you about that is signed."
- **No timeline for the next PECO rate case.** "We're assessing our future rate case filings in Pennsylvania" — deliberately open-ended, tied to unspecified further stakeholder alignment.
- **No commitment on whether PA legislative fixes (resource adequacy bills) happen this cycle.** Butler flatly said getting anything passed in an election year with divided government "is going to be a long shot," while still publicly backing the bills.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Pennsylvania dominated the Q&A**, with repeated attempts to get Butler to explain why PECO folded when peers with similarly aggressive filings (one with a "black box settlement") didn't — Butler declined to compare or conflate EXC's decision with any peer's.
- **Credit and capital-structure risk was the second cluster** — whether the governor's ROE/equity-ratio language could force legislation or just PSC discretion, and how rating agencies are actually responding (PECO already on negative outlook, under downgrade review).
- **O&M savings durability got specific pushback** — is the $350M sustainable cost discipline or deferred spend that resurfaces in the next rate case? Management's answer leaned on "sustainable" without fully closing the door on catch-up investment.
- **Compressed worry:** is Pennsylvania becoming a structurally harder jurisdiction for EXC to earn its authorized return in, and is today's "different plan for a different moment" quietly a down-payment on a bigger bill — in capital, credit cost, or blackout risk — that comes due after 2028.
