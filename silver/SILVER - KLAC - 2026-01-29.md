# KLAC — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-01-29)

**The key idea:** KLA delivered a record 2025 and is calling for an accelerating, back-half-loaded 2026, but the near-term story is entirely about scarcity — not of demand, but of customer cleanroom shells and KLA's own optical component supply, plus a DRAM commodity cost spike squeezing KLA's own gross margin. The company is defending a WFE growth number that looks conservative next to a peer's, and its explanation (packaging inclusion/exclusion) reads as much as messaging management as informing investors.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The industry is capacity-constrained, not demand-constrained.** Wallace's line — "if we could supply everything everybody wanted right away it still would be a limit" — because customers can't build fab shells fast enough. This is the dominant theme of the whole call and shapes every growth answer.
2. **KLA is eating a DRAM cost inflation problem it didn't expect three months ago.** The company uses DRAM chips in its own image-processing computers, and "this pricing environment has changed profoundly over the past two to three months," costing 75-100bps of margin through 2026 — a case of KLA being on the buy-side of the AI-driven memory shortage it otherwise profits from selling into.
3. **The WFE-growth-number dispute with a peer is a real tell.** Vivek Arya opened the Q&A pointing out KLA's high-single/low-double guide versus a peer's "23%" — Higgins's answer (aggregating core WFE + packaging to reconcile to "mid one thirties") is a defensible reconciliation, but the fact that KLA felt compelled to lead with it suggests the Street came in skeptical of KLA's number.
4. **DRAM process control intensity is rising toward logic-like levels, but management won't say it'll get there.** HBM's tight tolerances ("no ability to bend devices"), added metal layers, and EUV adoption are structurally raising DRAM's process-control share of wallet — a genuine multi-year tailwind — but Wallace explicitly declined to call it a convergence with advanced logic.
5. **China snapped from "modestly down" to "up" in three months** as affiliate-rule restrictions were lifted, with ~$300-350M of previously-blocked revenue "feathered through" the forecast — a meaningful, fast-moving policy-driven swing that isn't fully reflected in prior guidance.

## Economy & consumer
- No direct consumer read (KLA sells to chipmakers), but the **AI infrastructure build-out is the whole demand narrative** — Wallace: "the demand for AI infrastructure, coupled with KLA's industry leadership in process control, positions the company well" across advanced logic, HBM, and advanced packaging.
- **China WFE swung from negative to positive** within one quarter as the affiliate-rule restriction was removed — Higgins: "three months ago, we thought China would be... modestly down and now I think it's going to be up for us."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Process control systems revenue +19% and service +15% for 2025; broadband plasma inspection tied to HBM is described as demand exceeding KLA's own capacity additions.
- **Not working / under pressure:** Gross margin guided down to 61.75% (±1pt) for March — the trough for the year — due to the DRAM commodity cost spike hitting KLA's own bill of materials, not customer pricing power.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Advanced packaging systems revenue reached $950M in 2025, +70% y/y**, but 2026 growth guide of mid-to-high teens is explicitly capped by customer shell readiness, not demand: "part of what's slowing down the growth this year is their ability to have their factories built to support packaging" (Wallace).
- **KLA's own scarcity is optics.** Higgins: "the biggest long lead time aspect of our build of materials is optical components" — decisions shipping in H1 2026 were locked in "last summer."
- Capital return stayed aggressive despite the constraints: $797M returned in the quarter ($548M buybacks + $250M dividends), $3B over trailing twelve months, funded by record $1.26B quarterly free cash flow.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management says 2026 growth accelerates in H2 and 2027 setup is "pretty remarkable" for foundry logic, with new order conversations already centered on late-2026/2027 deliveries.
- **Action:** capacity investment is targeted narrowly — adding capacity in broadband-plasma inspection and prioritizing securing memory component supply for its own manufacturing — rather than broad-based expansion, because the binding constraint (customer fab shells, KLA's optics supply chain) can't be solved by spending alone.
- **Gap:** KLA is guiding mid-single-digit 2026 revenue growth against a WFE market it says grows low-double-digits — Higgins declined to explain the implied share-gain shortfall with precision when Stacy Rasgon pressed on the math, leaving a visible tension between the "we're gaining share" narrative and the arithmetic analysts were doing live on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- Refused to give more granular half-over-half revenue guidance when Arcuri and Rasgon both tried to pin down implied H2 dollar figures: "I'm not going to guide specifically with more detail."
- Declined to state whether DRAM process control intensity converges with advanced logic: "Not sure I'm ready to commit to something that looks like advanced logic given the nature of those devices."
- Would not quantify how much revenue is being left on the table by supply constraints when directly asked by Arya.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: (1) why KLA's WFE growth number looks weaker than a peer's, (2) how much upside is being suppressed by supply/lead-time constraints and whether KLA can quantify it, and (3) whether the implied H2 acceleration actually supports the stated share-gain story once the math is done.
- China's affiliate-rule reversal and the magnitude of returning revenue (~$300-350M) was another repeated thread, alongside DRAM/HBM intensity trajectory.
- **The compressed worry:** the Street isn't doubting AI-driven demand — it's doubting whether KLA's own guidance discipline and hedged numbers actually reflect the acceleration management keeps describing.
