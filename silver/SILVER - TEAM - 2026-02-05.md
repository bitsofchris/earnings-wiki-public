# TEAM — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-02-05)

**The key idea:** Atlassian posted its best quarter by several headline metrics — first $1B cloud quarter, accelerating RPO, record $1M+ deals — and the stock still traded down double digits, because the whole call is a referendum on whether seat-based SaaS pricing survives an agentic-AI world where a competitor (Anthropic's CoWork) can plug directly into Jira workflows. Management's answer is unusually direct: AI coding tools don't shrink Jira usage, they inflate it, because more AI-generated software output means more coordination overhead, not less — a genuinely counter-crowd claim backed by their own usage data.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The numbers were strong enough to make the stock reaction itself the story.** One analyst opened by noting the stock was down ~40% over five weeks with no company-specific news, then fell further intraday on a beat-and-raise-plus-buyback quarter — a sentiment/multiple problem, not (by these numbers) an execution problem.
2. **AI adoption correlates with MORE product usage, the opposite of the bear case.** Customers using AI code-generation tools show 5% higher Jira task creation, 5% higher MAU, and faster seat expansion than non-AI-tool customers — CEO's explanit: efficiency gains let teams ship more, and more software output means more coordination/governance/Jira issues, not fewer humans needed.
3. **Pricing philosophy is a deliberate, stated bet against the consumption-pricing trend.** CFO/CEO explicitly reject moving core seat pricing to usage-based, arguing enterprise buyers want "understandable predictable" costs and that Atlassian's job as an application vendor is to absorb AI compute cost volatility on their behalf — validated so far by AI gross margins improving for a 3rd-4th straight quarter even as Robo scaled past 5M MAU.
4. **Anthropic is framed as partner, not threat**, despite CoWork directly automating Jira-adjacent workflows — Atlassian points to its MCP server as the integration surface, and to Formula 1 sponsorship (Atlassian Williams Racing) as the relationship's public face, arguing multi-agent, multi-tool enterprise environments favor Atlassian's Teamwork graph as the connective layer.
5. **Data Center is being walked down deliberately, not just declining.** CFO pre-warns FY2027 DC revenue "meaningfully down" against a tough comp, while using DC price increases as a calibrated lever to push cloud migration — mid-to-high-single-digit contribution to cloud growth from migrations this quarter, expected to hold for the full year.

## Economy & consumer
No macro or end-consumer commentary — Atlassian sells to enterprise IT/business teams, not consumers, so there's no read-through on household demand here. The closest proxy to "the economy" on this call is the disconnect between reported fundamentals and stock price, which management attributes to market noise rather than any softening in enterprise budgets.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Cloud crossed $1B in quarterly revenue for the first time**, up 26% YoY with growth accelerating; RPO grew 44% YoY, accelerating for a third straight quarter — "a really fantastic vote of confidence" per CEO, since RPO reflects multi-year commitments through 2027-2029.
- **$1M+ ACV deals nearly doubled YoY** to a new record as enterprises standardize on the platform; NRR sits above 120% and rising for a third consecutive quarter.
- **Service Collection is now the fastest-growing product at scale**: passed 65,000 customers, 50% of the Fortune 500, enterprise segment up over 60% YoY, with more than 40% of new agentic workflows built in service (not software) contexts in the last six months — expansion into HR, finance, and non-IT use cases (two-thirds of Service Collection customers now use it outside IT).
- **What's not working / softening**: Data Center is flagged for a "meaningfully down" year in FY2027 on a tough comp — the legacy on-prem business is being actively wound down as a strategy, not merely fading on its own. JSM (service management) seat counts show some "like-for-like" efficiency/consolidation within existing workloads even as new-customer growth stays strong, an admission CEO validates ("you are certainly seeing efficiencies coming in some of those customers") without quantifying it.

## Investing & scarcity
- **The AI cost curve, not AI access, is the constraint being actively managed.** CFO/CEO repeatedly point to AI gross margin improving for a 3rd-4th straight quarter as evidence they're containing serving costs for 5M+ Robo MAU — framed as an execution win for engineering, and a prerequisite for keeping seat-based (not usage-based) pricing viable.
- **Capital continues into the Teamwork graph (100B+ objects/connections) and enterprise trust infrastructure** (security, governance, permissioning) as the moat against both AI-native entrants and legacy competitors — CEO calls this "years of thoughtful investment" now converting into the current growth numbers.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
Management believes AI increases collaboration surface area rather than shrinking it, and is acting on that belief by continuing to build AI deeper into existing workflows (Robo agents assignable inside Jira/JSM/Jira Product Discovery, GA of a new customer service app, assets moved into the core platform) rather than hedging with a parallel consumption-pricing track. The one real gap: CEO reiterates confidence in the FY2027 20%+ revenue / 25%+ margin targets but, when pressed twice (Zukin, Keirstead) on exactly how AI monetization bridges to accelerating cloud growth once DC comps go negative, answers with RPO/NRR trend color rather than a quantified model — belief is stated with more conviction than the acting evidence for that specific bridge.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- CFO declines to give a specific breakdown of seat expansion by new-logo vs. existing-customer vs. role type beyond "broad-based," and repeatedly falls back to "conservative and risk-adjusted" language on FY2026 guidance despite a clear beat-and-raise.
- No commitment to quantify how much of NRR/RPO acceleration is AI-driven specifically versus general platform consolidation — CEO concedes the 5%-higher-usage stat is "at least" 5% and does not clarify the actual magnitude of AI's contribution to expansion revenue.

## The street — what analysts asked
Every question but one circled back to the same core anxiety: does AI break the seat-based SaaS model, either by making developers so efficient they need fewer seats, or by letting agentic tools like CoWork disintermediate Jira entirely. A second cluster pressed on whether the accelerating RPO/cloud numbers can actually bridge to the standing FY2027 20%+ revenue guide once Data Center comps turn hard negative — management reasserted confidence without a new quantified bridge. Underlying worry, compressed: the fundamentals look excellent this quarter, but nobody on the call got a convincing answer for why that continues once AI reshapes what a "seat" is worth.
