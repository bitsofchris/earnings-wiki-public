# TSM — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-16)

**The key idea:** TSMC beat guidance again on both revenue and margin, riding leading-edge and packaging demand so strong that management now believes its own year-old AI CAGR forecast was too conservative — but every attempt by analysts to get a bigger, harder number (2026 CapEx, CoWoS capacity growth, updated CAGR, gross margin dilution from N2) was deferred to January. The tension: TSMC's own data (customers' customers requesting capacity directly, token growth "exponential every 3 months") points to acceleration, while the company's stated capital discipline framework insists revenue growth must keep outpacing CapEx growth, not the other way around.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The AI demand signal strengthened materially quarter-over-quarter.** CC Wei: "The AI demand actually continue to be very strong, it's more -- more stronger than we thought 3 months ago... Today, the number are insane." TSMC is now getting direct signals from customers' *customers* requesting capacity, a new practice this cycle.
2. **Capacity is still the binding constraint, unresolved from last quarter.** Front-end and CoWoS/back-end capacity are both described as "very tight," and management repeats verbatim from three months ago that they're "working very hard to narrow the gap" — no evidence the gap has closed.
3. **Margin quality is real, not just FX.** The 200bps beat versus guidance was driven partly by favorable FX but explicitly also by "better-than-expected cost improvement efforts," and overseas-fab dilution guidance was revised *down* (2-3% to 1-2% for FY25) — a genuine operational win, not accounting.
4. **CapEx discipline is the load-bearing philosophy.** Huang repeatedly insists CapEx for a company of TSMC's size is "unlikely to certainly go up significantly in any given year" and that the goal is revenue growth outpacing CapEx growth — even as CC Wei describes customer demand signals that sound like they'd justify a step-change.
5. **The token-growth-vs-revenue-growth gap is the most important unresolved question on the call.** An analyst pressed hard that exponential token growth implies TSMC's AI revenue CAGR should be rising faster than guided; Wei's answer — node migration plus customer design efficiency absorb the gap — was asserted, not quantified.

## Economy & consumer
- **Non-AI markets are troughing, not booming.** CC Wei: non-AI end markets "have bottomed out and are seeing a mild recovery" — a modest, not a recovery-driven, growth story outside AI.
- **Tariff risk is on the radar for 2026 planning, not yet in the numbers.** "We have not observed any change in our customers' behavior so far" but management will be "prudent in our business planning going into 2026," specifically flagging "consumer-related and price-sensitive end market segment."
- **No prebuild concern despite a sharp smartphone jump.** Smartphone platform revenue rose sharply q/q; Wei directly rejects the pull-forward/prebuild worry raised last quarter, citing healthy, seasonal inventory levels.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Revenue and margin both beat guidance again.** $33.1B revenue (above guide), gross margin 59.5% (200bps above the top of guidance), EPS up sharply y/y, ROE 37.8%.
- **Full-year 2025 growth outlook raised.** Now guided to "close to mid-30s percent" y/y in USD, versus a more conservative framing previously implied.
- **N3 tightness continues to strain the fab network.** Days of inventory fell due to "strong shipment in N3 and N5" — a sign of demand outstripping available capacity at the still-dominant nodes (N3 is 23% of wafer revenue, N5+N7 together 51%).
- **DCE (digital consumer electronics) segment shrank 20% q/q** — the one visibly weak platform line, though it's only ~1% of revenue.

## Investing & scarcity
- **2025 CapEx guidance narrowed upward:** $40-42B, versus $38-42B previously — signaling more confidence in the higher end, not a step-change.
- **Arizona is being accelerated and upgraded.** Faster technology upgrades to more advanced nodes than originally planned, a second large land parcel nearly secured, framed explicitly around "very strong multiyear AI-related demand," with an independent "giga fab cluster" ambition.
- **Advanced packaging capacity is being built two ways at once in Arizona** — TSMC's own two fabs plus reliance on an OSAT partner whose fab is breaking ground *earlier* than TSMC's, an explicit admission TSMC can't move fast enough alone.
- **CoWoS capacity plans for 2026 were requested three times by different analysts and declined each time** — "the real number, we probably update you next year" — the clearest sign that packaging supply/demand math isn't settled internally yet either.
- **Advanced packaging revenue "approaching close to 10%"** of total revenue, framed as newly significant enough to justify TSMC's "Foundry 2.0" repositioning (front-end + back-end + packaging as one category).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** AI CAGR is running above the prior mid-40s% guide. **Action:** No new number given; CapEx guide only nudged within its existing range, not raised categorically — a gap between conviction and commitment that management defends as discipline ("higher CapEx is always correlated with higher growth opportunity," but the actual 2025 raise was modest).
- **Belief:** N2 ramp will be faster in 2026, fueled by smartphone and HPC/AI. **Action:** N2 volume production is on track for this quarter, with N2P and A16 both concretely scheduled for 2H'26 — this is the most funded, dated commitment on the call, i.e., roadmap execution is ahead of financial commitment.
- **Belief:** Token growth is exponentially outpacing revenue CAGR. **Action:** No capacity or CapEx response disclosed beyond "working very hard" — management's answer was conceptual (efficiency gains absorb it) rather than an investment commitment.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **2026 CapEx number:** repeatedly deferred to January ("it's too early to talk about 2026").
- **CoWoS 2026 capacity growth:** asked three separate times, answered each time with "the real number, we probably update you next year."
- **Updated AI accelerator CAGR:** acknowledged as likely higher than mid-40s but "we will update you probably in beginning of next year."
- **Revenue-per-gigawatt of AI data center buildout:** explicitly declined — "we are not ready to share with you yet because of different from different projects."
- **N2 gross margin dilution trajectory:** Huang declined to map it to the traditional "quarters to reach corporate average" framework, arguing the framework itself is now "less meaningful" since corporate average keeps rising — a redefinition that conveniently avoids a hard commitment.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **CapEx trajectory dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pushed on whether the historically modest CapEx-to-revenue growth ratio can hold given the demand signals described; management held the line on discipline without giving numbers.
- **AI CAGR sizing and the token-growth gap** were probed hard, including a direct comparison to a customer's $3-4T infrastructure TAM claim; management reaffirmed conviction without new figures.
- **China/export-control risk** was raised as a potential cap on AI growth; Wei dismissed it, expressing confidence customer performance (GPU and ASIC) holds regardless of China access.
- **Competitive positioning versus Intel** came up directly, given a TSMC customer's reported investment in Intel; Wei deflected, noting the "competitor happened to be our customer, very good customer" and declined further comment.
- **Compressed worry:** analysts don't doubt the demand — they doubt whether TSMC's stated financial discipline can keep pace with it, and management's repeated deferral of every hard number to January is itself the tell.
