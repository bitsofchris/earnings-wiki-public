# TXN — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-21)

**The key idea:** TXN delivered a solid top-line quarter (+7% sequential, +14% YoY) but is now visibly paying for its own inventory strategy — cutting wafer loadings to keep inventory from growing further, which is compressing gross margin toward decade lows even as revenue sits well above prior troughs. Management's story is that this is a disciplined, patient bet on a "moderate, below-trend" recovery with data center as the lone bright spot; the tension is whether that patience is prudence or a hedge against a recovery that keeps failing to show up.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Margin is being deliberately sacrificed to protect inventory discipline.** Rafael Lizardi confirmed loadings (wafer starts) are being cut for a second consecutive quarter specifically to keep the $4.8B inventory position "flat to down" rather than let it grow at lower revenue — a direct trade of near-term gross margin for balance-sheet conservatism. An analyst calculated cash gross margin (ex-depreciation) at its lowest in roughly a decade; TXN didn't dispute the math, only reframed the "why."

2. **The recovery narrative downgraded mid-year, and tariffs are named as the reason.** Haviv Ilan walked back the "sharp slope" optimism from earlier in the year: "We are seeing the market getting back towards trend line, but still below trend line... one of the more moderate recoveries we've seen in the history." He directly links customer hesitancy to unresolved tariff rules — "Should I put my factory in this country or another one?" — concentrated mainly in industrial.

3. **Data center is the one unambiguous growth story, and TXN is about to break it out as a reporting segment.** Data center-related revenue is running at roughly a $1.2B annual pace, up over 50% year-to-date, spanning power delivery (industrial), compute/switches (enterprise), and optical modules (comms). Ilan: "That's the one market that we see CapEx going into. I'm not seeing any slowdown there." TXN plans to disclose it as a standalone market starting Q1 2026 — a signal of how much internal weight it now carries relative to the legacy five-market framework.

4. **Restructuring (250mm fab closures) is a real, quantified cost now, not a future promise.** $0.08/share restructuring charge tied to shutting two Texas 150mm-equivalent (six-inch) fabs, with the "last wafer" already run at both sites this month; benefits phase in gradually into 2026, not immediately. A separate, ongoing R&D site consolidation was disclosed as management admitting "some areas where our R&D machine is not generating returns."

5. **Capital return stayed dividend-first, buyback-light.** $1.2B in dividends vs. only $190M in buybacks this quarter, alongside a 4% dividend raise marking 22 consecutive years of increases — capital discipline extends to shareholder returns, not just factory loadings.

## Economy & consumer

- **Below-trend, tariff-slowed recovery, not a demand collapse.** Ilan: "The overall semiconductor market recovery is continuing, though at a slower pace than prior upturns, likely related to the broader macroeconomic dynamics and overall uncertainty." Customer inventory depletion is described as "behind us," which should be a tailwind but hasn't translated into acceleration.
- **Tariff/trade uncertainty is explicitly cited as capex-chilling for customers**, not just an internal TXN cost issue — Ilan describes customers in "wait and see mode" on where to site new factories given unresolved tariff rates.
- **No direct consumer read** — TXN's closest proxy, personal electronics, grew low single digits YoY (weakest of five markets), consistent with a soft-but-not-collapsing consumer electronics cycle. Automotive strength is framed as secular content growth, not a demand surge — "the trough was shallow ... it's kind of back to where it used to be."

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Broad-based growth across all five end markets**, with enterprise (+35% YoY) and comms equipment (+45% YoY) — both linked to data center — leading; industrial (+25% YoY) decelerated sequentially as expected after a strong Q2.
- **Inventory execution is a genuine operational win**: days of inventory fell 16 sequentially to 215, and management repeatedly emphasizes near-zero obsolescence/scrap given long-lived chip and finished-goods inventory — a real moat versus faster-cycling competitors.
- **Gross margin is the visible casualty**: down 50bps sequentially in Q3, guided down further (~250bps) into Q4 on lower loadings, rising depreciation ($1.8-2.0B full year, stepping up again in 2026), and lower revenue — Rafael: "as we do that... you have the hit on the lower loadings. That's how you get to the EPS range."
- **Pricing is unremarkable** — low-single-digit like-for-like price declines for the year, consistent with prior guidance, no unusual dynamics by market.

## Investing & scarcity

- **CapEx skewing toward the low end of the prior multi-year framework.** Ilan: "the probability of being lower is probably more probable than higher" — a real downward signal on the 2026 capital plan, even while Sherman and Lehi 2 fab construction proceeds unchanged.
- **Fab consolidation is capital discipline in action**: closing legacy 150mm fabs and some R&D sites frees capital/opex for the three markets management is doubling down on — industrial, automotive, and data center — "we plan to grow our portfolio in these three areas."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Belief:** moderate, below-trend recovery continues into Q4 and likely into 2026, with data center as the exception showing no slowdown. **Action:** loadings cut for two straight quarters, CapEx tilted toward the low end of guidance — a defensive posture, not one betting on acceleration.
- **Belief:** data center is TXN's fastest-growing, most CapEx-attractive market with no visible ceiling. **Action:** breaking it out as a standalone reported segment starting Q1 2026 and explicitly directing R&D toward it — a real, structural commitment, not just rhetoric.
- **Gap:** management says the current inventory/capacity buildout leaves them "ready for any scenario," including a sudden reacceleration — but the loading cuts and CapEx guidance are actively removing the ability to respond quickly to that upside scenario, which is a quiet contradiction the analysts didn't press hard enough on.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No market-level guidance, ever** — Ilan repeatedly deflected end-market-specific questions back to "we only guide at the company level," even when directly asked about industrial deceleration vs. auto acceleration.
- **No Q1 2026 guide**, only unattributed historical seasonality ("not unusual to see... typically down slightly") — explicitly flagged by Mike Beckman as "not a guide for what we're gonna see."
- **No firm 2026 CapEx number** — only a probabilistic lean toward the low end of the existing multi-year framework, with specifics deferred to the February Capital Management Day.
- **Declined to confirm or deny recurrence of China pull-forward activity** — Beckman: "we didn't see that same level of pull forward, at least evidence of it. Can't confirm that with certainty."

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Dominant theme: gross margin mechanics.** Multiple analysts (UBS, Bernstein, Deutsche Bank, Jefferies) independently converged on decomposing the Q4 margin guide — loadings, depreciation, inventory trajectory — with one bluntly noting cash gross margin hadn't been this low in about a decade.
- **Secondary theme: 2026 visibility**, specifically CapEx range and whether the "moderate recovery" framing implies a structurally lower-margin operating model going forward — management deflected all of it to the February Capital Management Day.
- **Tertiary theme: China and end-market linearity** — probing whether Q2's pull-forward dynamics repeated or reversed, and whether industrial/auto sequential swings signaled a real inflection versus normal cyclical noise.
- **Notably absent pushback**: no analyst directly asked management to reconcile "ready for any scenario" with actively cutting the loadings that would let them respond to an upside scenario. The compressed worry: **is TXN's inventory-and-margin discipline actually optionality, or is it a slow-motion admission that this recovery cycle isn't coming back to trend?**
