# CMCSA — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-23)

**The key idea:** This is a turnaround-in-progress call: new leadership (Croney running Connectivity & Platforms), a broadband business that stopped the bleeding for the first time in over four years, and a wireless business hitting record net adds — but nearly all of it was subsidized, either by a one-time marketing blitz (Legendary February) or by free wireless lines that haven't yet converted to revenue. Management is asking the market to trust that the underlying, non-promotional improvement is real and durable, while simultaneously absorbing peak NBA rights dilution and admitting the company "was not competitive enough" for years.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Half the broadband win was borrowed from a calendar event.** Croney and Armstrong both concede "a little over half" of the 117k y/y improvement in broadband net losses came from Legendary February (Olympics/Super Bowl/NBA All-Star) marketing spend, not durable structural change — a one-off that won't repeat every quarter.
2. **Wireless is the real convergence bet, and it's running at a loss today.** Free lines are ~50% of new connects; management is explicit this is a deliberate loss-leader — "our free line offer... is doing exactly what we intended" — banking on conversion to paid in H2 2026 to repair both wireless and broadband ARPU.
3. **NBA rights are a straight-line accounting drag that peaked this quarter.** Media posted a $426M EBITDA loss and Peacock lost $432M, explicitly the "peak dilution" quarter from NBA amortization with ~50% of games already played; Peacock is guided to "approach profitability" in Q2 — a sharp inflection claim worth checking next quarter.
4. **Convergence ARPA gap versus telcos is the explicit growth thesis.** Armstrong frames the entire wireless push around closing a 2x gap: Comcast's convergence ARPA is ~$85 versus telecom competitors running "roughly double this amount" — the whole broadband-plus-mobile bundle strategy is built on catching up to that number.
5. **Leadership admits the prior broadband strategy failed.** Croney's blunt "we were not competitive enough, and we need to adapt our approach and pivot the business" is a rare direct admission of fault, paired with concrete operational fixes: new head of sales, AI-driven retention/upsell models, simplified buy-flow and activation.

## Economy & consumer
- **No domestic pullback yet, but watching closely.** Cavanagh: inbound international travel to U.S. parks "has not ever gotten back to the level we saw pre-COVID," and while domestic attendance hasn't shown "any significant impact... caused by higher oil," he flags this could change depending on gas/airline price duration.
- **China-linked softness is real and localized.** Osaka parks are seeing pressure from "China-related inbound travel trends," and Beijing is described as navigating "a more challenging macroeconomic environment" — the clearest concrete China-exposure signal on the call.
- **Advertising demand held up at baseline, not just on tentpole events.** Cavanagh: excluding the Olympics/Super Bowl, underlying ad demand was "strong... at a baseline level," with a record upfront cited as supporting evidence.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Broadband losses narrowed for the first time since Q4 2020** (117k y/y improvement to 65k net losses), but ARPU fell 3.1% on absent rate increases, free-line dilution, and pricing migration — the two metrics are moving in opposite directions.
- **Wireless had its best quarter ever**, 435k net line adds, with Premium Unlimited uptake at ~30% of connects and the premium base "up roughly five-fold" since launch a year ago — genuine product-market fit in the premium tier, distinct from the free-line subsidy story.
- **Parks (ex-Epic pre-opening cost adjustment) grew EBITDA over 7%**, with Orlando "extremely well" driven by Epic Universe attendance and per-cap spending — the clearest unambiguous win on the call.
- **Media flipped to a $426M EBITDA loss** on first-year NBA rights amortization, the explicitly stated "high watermark" of dilution — a cost the company chose to take on and is now underwriting the recovery story against.
- **Business Services stayed steady growth**, revenue +6%, EBITDA +4%, with a new T-Mobile MVNO launch cited as expanding the enterprise/mid-market mobile opportunity.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Wireless subsidy at scale**: free lines (~50% of connects), Premium Unlimited expansion, and the newly launched Mobile Plus (industry-first free lifetime device protection) are all capital committed against future ARPU recovery, not present profitability.
- **Parks capital continues past Epic**: UK park in "final planning approvals," a kids park opening in Frisco, TX this summer, Fast & Furious: Hollywood Drift, and Pokémon-themed expansion in Japan — steady reinvestment in the one segment that's unambiguously working.
- **The constraint is competitive, not physical.** Croney: the network itself is "on par with fiber" and beats fixed wireless/satellite on capacity, so the binding constraint isn't infrastructure — it's price/packaging/customer experience, which is why the fix has been organizational (new sales head, AI models, restructured go-to-market) rather than capital-intensive network buildout.
- **Leverage is expected to tick up mechanically** as Versant exits the trailing-12-month calculation, with management targeting a return to 2.3x — a soft target, not a hard commitment.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** broadband ARPU pressure eases as the year progresses, once free lines convert to paid and pricing-migration comps lap themselves. **Action:** actively converting the "significant majority" of free-line cohorts to paid relationships starting in H2 2026 — a real mechanism behind the belief, not just hope.
- **Belief:** Peacock "approaches profitability" next quarter. **Action:** this rests almost entirely on NBA amortization mechanically declining post-peak, not on new monetization — a favorable accounting comp rather than a new lever being pulled.
- **Belief:** cable industry sentiment is overly negative and the stock is undervalued (Cavanagh: "we're undervalued, frankly"). **Action:** stays largely rhetorical — no announced buyback acceleration or M&A commitment tied to this claim beyond ongoing routine capital returns ($2.5B this quarter).

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No ARPU floor or specific recovery date** — only "relief as we exit this year," repeatedly hedged with "some incremental pressure" language for Q2.
- **On cable consolidation**, Roberts stayed deliberately noncommittal: "the bar is high," open to "creative situations" but explicitly "don't want to create a lot of distraction" — declining to engage with the regulatory-framework question Diffley asked directly about satellite reshaping antitrust views.
- **Leverage target (2.3x) has no firm timeline** given it's expected to "tick up a bit" mechanically as Versant rolls out of the trailing calculation.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Broadband ARPU durability and competitive attribution (FWA vs. fiber vs. satellite)** dominated — Moffett pushed specifically on how much lower ARPU could go and where the improvement actually came from; Croney's answer ("improvement across all of our competitive environment") was notably non-specific on the fiber-vs-FWA breakdown he was asked for directly.
- **Wireless-to-paid conversion mechanics** were probed twice (Rollins, Ng) — analysts clearly want proof the free-line strategy converts to revenue, not just subscriber counts.
- **Cable consolidation and regulatory landscape** came up twice (Hodulik, Diffley) in the context of satellite's emergence — management engaged on strategy but dodged specifics on M&A appetite or regulatory read-through.
- **Macro/consumer weakness spillover into parks and advertising** was asked directly (Petti) given "consumer sentiment at all-time lows" headlines — answer was reassuring but hedged ("we'll see what the coming quarters look like").
- **Compressed worry:** how much of this quarter's broadband and wireless improvement is real, repeatable operational change versus a one-time marketing sugar high from Legendary February, and can Peacock's "profitability" actually hold once it's not just an NBA-amortization accounting artifact.
