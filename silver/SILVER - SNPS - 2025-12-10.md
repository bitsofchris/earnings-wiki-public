# SNPS — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2025-12-10)

**The key idea:** Synopsys closed FY25 with record revenue and backlog and immediately guided FY26 organic growth (~8%) well below its own long-term double-digit/mid-teens targets, pinning the gap on a self-inflicted IP execution slip and a structurally impaired China business — while betting the ANSYS acquisition, a fresh $2B NVIDIA investment, and an emerging "physics + silicon" joint-solution roadmap will reopen the growth algorithm from 2027 onward.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript

1. **Management is guiding down its own growth story for a year.** FY26 revenue guide implies only ~8% organic growth for core EDA+IP even as CEO Sassine Ghazi insists "my confidence in our long-term mid-teens for our IT business only gets stronger" — the gap between the stated long-term algorithm and the near-term number is the whole call.
2. **The IP miss is an execution failure, not a demand failure — and management says so.** Ghazi admits headwinds trace to internal delivery: "we needed to do some work to accelerate our roadmap to deliver to customers," with leadership changes in both IP development and sales, and titles not expected to close the gap until "midyear FY26."
3. **China is now a structural, not cyclical, drag.** Classic Synopsys China revenue fell 22% ex-ANSYS for the year; management frames this as share loss to "organic local EDA or IP companies" on customers they're legally barred from serving, not competitive erosion against Cadence — but the effect on the P&L is the same.
4. **The NVIDIA investment is a monetization signal, not just a capital event.** CEO relays that Jensen Huang pushed for equity ("I want to endorse it with an investment because I know we can make money") after discussions moved from GPU acceleration to Omniverse-level physical AI — proceeds are being funneled straight into accelerated debt repayment, an unusually direct capital-allocation tell.
5. **Analysts are unusually persistent on one question: is EDA/IP's multi-year deceleration a monetization problem, not a demand problem.** Ghazi concedes the point directly — "as an industry, we can do better in capturing more value for the impact we're delivering" — which is a rare admission from a company whose entire pricing model (per-chip-start, not volume) is designed to capture AI upside.

## Economy & consumer

- **AI infrastructure build remains the dominant demand driver**, but it's bifurcated: "a number of companies... their roadmap is somewhat muted" outside AI infrastructure, versus AI/HPC customers "delivering chips... at the same pace."
- **China is a widening, not stabilizing, hole.** Full-year China revenue down 18% overall, down 22% excluding ANSYS; guidance assumes "not much change going into FY26" — i.e., no recovery baked in, only continued erosion on restricted customers.
- **Mobile and automotive demand strengthening**, industrial "broadly remain subdued" — a split Synopsys attributes to AI infrastructure spend crowding out other capital cycles.
- No direct end-consumer exposure; omitted from frontmatter as the business sells to chip and systems designers, not consumers.

## The business — what's working, what's not

- **Design automation (EDA + ANSYS + HAV) is the engine**: FY25 segment revenue up 26% (ANSYS-inclusive), hardware-assisted verification had "a record year with 12 competitive wins" in Q4 alone, and Graviton 5's design used Synopsys tools end-to-end.
- **Design IP is the sore spot**: FY25 segment revenue down 8%, explicitly called "a transitional year" for FY26 with "muted" growth guided, driven by internal delivery slips on specific titles rather than demand softness, per management's own framing.
- **ANSYS integration is proceeding on schedule but go-to-market stays split**: R&D merged into "one team" delivering joint solutions, but the non-semiconductor ANSYS sales channel is deliberately kept separate "so we don't miss a beat."
- **Backlog is a genuine positive signal**: $11.4B at quarter-end, up from $10.1B last quarter — bookings strength that management leans on repeatedly as evidence the IP slowdown is delivery-driven, not demand-driven.

## Investing & scarcity

- **$2B NVIDIA equity investment plus expanded partnership**, structured to fund GPU-accelerated tooling and Omniverse-linked physical-AI/digital-twin workflows (the Kronets bottling-line digital twin cited as proof point) — diluted share count rises to 192–194M shares to accommodate the issuance.
- **Debt paydown accelerated using the proceeds**: $850M term loan repaid in Q4, another $900M in November, remaining $2.55B balance targeted for prepayment in FY26 — explicitly guided as EPS-accretive.
- **The binding constraint is engineering/delivery capacity in IP**, not capital: management repositioned (not simply hired into) the IP workforce toward HPC titles, implying the FY26 muted growth is a headcount-allocation and execution problem rather than a demand or balance-sheet one.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it

- **Believe:** EDA returns to double-digit growth and IP to mid-teens growth as joint Synopsys-ANSYS solutions and agentic AI change the industry's monetization model. **Act:** merged R&D teams targeting "first wave" joint solutions in 2026, but by their own account the revenue impact is a multi-year adoption curve, not a FY26 event — a real gap between the stated long-term thesis and what's actually committed for the coming year.
  - Ghazi: "one plus one is greater than two for sure... that's where we will capture that monetization opportunity over time as they start adopting the technology."
- **Believe:** IP can renegotiate to an NRE-plus-royalty model with strategic customers to capture more upside. **Act:** in active discussions, expecting to "lock up some customers in that new business model" in FY26 — this is a genuine near-term commitment, contrasting with the vaguer joint-solutions monetization story.
- **Believe:** China environment stays impaired long-term with no policy relief assumed. **Act:** guidance already derisks China at current run-rate; no incremental investment or workaround strategy described beyond serving the customers they're legally permitted to serve.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **No specific FY26 IP growth number** beyond "muted" and "back half loaded" — CFO explicitly declines to quantify, attributing timing to title availability without a percentage.
- **No quantified uplift estimate for joint ANSYS/EDA solutions** — repeatedly described as "over time" and dependent on customer adoption, with monetization not modeled into near-term guidance.
- **No commitment on whether China erosion has a floor** — when directly asked if current China revenue represents a reasonable floor, Ghazi answers with "we believe we have derisked it" for FY26 specifically, sidestepping the longer-term floor question asked.
- **No specific royalty-model economics disclosed** — management confirms the pricing shift (NRE + use fee + royalty) is under negotiation but gives no expected magnitude or timing of revenue impact.

## The street — what analysts asked

- **Nearly every question first-half of the call orbited the same doubt**: is the guided ~8% organic growth (well below double-digit/mid-teens targets) genuine conservatism or evidence the IP and EDA monetization problem is structural, not transitory — asked from at least four different analysts in slightly different framings.
- **China was a persistent secondary thread**: analysts pressed on whether current revenue is a "floor," whether share is being lost to peers versus lost entirely to local Chinese vendors, and whether the ANSYS portfolio's China resilience offsets classic Synopsys's decline.
- **ANSYS synergy realism got specific scrutiny**: one analyst directly noted flat quarterly expense run-rates despite claimed cost synergies, forcing management to clarify synergies are embedded in the operating-margin trajectory rather than absolute expense reduction.
- **The sharpest, most direct challenge** came late in the call: a comparison to Cadence and the broader EDA sector's multi-year growth deceleration despite obvious AI tailwinds, which got Ghazi to concede a monetization gap rather than deflect to demand strength.
- **Compressed worry:** the Street isn't questioning Synopsys's AI exposure — it's questioning whether Synopsys (and the EDA industry broadly) actually knows how to get paid for it.
