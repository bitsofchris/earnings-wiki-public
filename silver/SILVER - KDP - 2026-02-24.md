# KDP — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-24)

**The key idea:** This is a transition-quarter call dominated by deal mechanics, not operations — KDP is mid-flight on the JDE Peet's acquisition and subsequent split into Beverage Co and Global Coffee Co, and nearly every operational data point (soft Q4 coffee margins, a Q1 EPS air-pocket, Mexico timing benefits) gets explained as noise en route to that structural transformation. The real tension: management is asking investors to look past a genuinely weak coffee profit picture (cost inflation outrunning hedges, double-digit brewer declines, retailer destocking) on the promise that both halves of the business get healthier once separated and once 2026 cost headwinds lap.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Refreshment beverages is carrying the company.** US refreshment beverages delivered low-double-digit sales growth and high-single-digit operating income growth, led by Ghost integration, energy share gains (+1.5 points), and CSD innovation — while coffee sales grew but operating income *fell* at a high-single-digit rate in Q4.
2. **Coffee's margin problem is explicitly a timing/hedging artifact, not a demand problem** — a "six to nine month time lag between market price changes" and P&L impact means Q1 2026 will see peak cost headwinds before easing "particularly in the back half," per CFO Anthony DiSalvestro.
3. **The separation financing plan hardened this quarter and dropped an option**: the beverage-co convertible preferred was upsized from $3B to $4.5B, the $4B coffee pod JV was finalized, ~$9B of new debt plus $5B of assumed JDE Peet's debt were added — and "we will no longer consider a partial IPO" of Beverage Co, a clear signal the debt/preferred structure fully replaced that path.
4. **Guidance embeds a deliberately back-half-loaded EPS ramp**: Q1 EPS guided to $0.36-0.37 vs. $0.42 a year ago (a decline), with management stating "good visibility that stand-alone Keurig Dr Pepper EPS growth will be positive in Q2 and accelerate further in the second half" — a lot is riding on that inflection actually showing up.
5. **Energy remains the structural growth story management leans on hardest**: Tim Cofer described a "$28,000,000,000 category that's growing in the teens" with KDP's four-brand portfolio (C4, Ghost, Bloom, Black Rifle) gaining share and expecting *both* the category and KDP specifically to gain shelf space — explicitly denying cannibalization of the rest of the portfolio.

## Economy & consumer
- **CSD category resilient despite "uneven consumer environment"** — Cofer credits innovation, brand activity, and "attractive value proposition," with value formats (mini cans, two-liter multipacks) cited as tools already in the playbook if pressure increases.
- **SNAP eligibility changes flagged as a live risk being modeled weekly**: Cofer reviews a "state by state analysis... every other week," distinguishing category-eligibility changes (likely just payment-method reallocation, "left pocket, right pocket") from broader benefit-magnitude cuts (potentially more impactful) — conclusion: "too early to draw firm conclusions... impact on the business is gonna be manageable."
- **Mexico's economy "began to find its footing after a challenging 2025,"** with cold drinks outperforming and share gains across Peñafiel and Dr Pepper — though Q4 international strength was partly a beverage-tax buying-ahead timing benefit that reverses into a "softer start" in Q1.
- **Coffee elasticity "tracking to our expectations, and it remains healthy"** even after "meaningful pricing" and passing through "unprecedented" green coffee costs plus tariffs — though brewer shipments fell 16.8% and pod shipments fell 2.8%, so the elasticity claim sits alongside real volume softness.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **US refreshment beverages: broad-based strength.** Low-double-digit net sales growth, high-single-digit operating income growth, Ghost fully integrated onto the DSD network, Electrolit ("largest share gainer" in sports hydration) and Vita Coco (+20% retail sales) both scaling as capital-light partner brands.
- **US coffee: top line held, profit did not.** Segment revenue +4% (or +3.9% per CFO detail) on share gains in both owned/licensed and Keurig-manufactured pods, but operating income declined at a high-single-digit rate as "significant cost pressure flowed through our P&L without a proportionate offset."
- **Gross margin compressed 150bps enterprise-wide** in Q4 as inflation outpaced net pricing and productivity; SG&A leverage (+80bps) partly offset it, but operating income growth (+4.8%) still trailed net sales growth (+9.9%), and EPS growth of just 1.7% (to $0.60) reflected below-the-line drag from interest expense and a higher tax rate.
- **International was a standout on paper (+16% constant currency sales, +20% operating income)** but management flagged this was "partly aided by timing" — Mexican retailers buying ahead of a beverage tax hike — meaning the real run-rate is softer than the headline.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Keurig Alta (next-gen brewer) and Keurig Coffee Collective (new premium pod line)** are the two flagship coffee investments, both funded "even as we navigate an inflationary period" — Cofer frames this explicitly as accepting near-term profit pressure for a longer-term bet, with Alta targeted for "a targeted late 2026 launch."
- **The binding constraint in coffee is the cost pass-through pipeline itself**: a structural 6-9 month lag from hedging and inventory turns means costs booked today reflect market prices from many months prior — not a controllable input, just a timing mechanic the company has to manage through.
- **Capital is being funneled hard into deal-closing infrastructure**: upsized convertible preferred ($4.5B), finalized coffee pod JV ($4B), ~$9B new debt — all engineered explicitly to "facilitate a timely separation" and enable "swift deleveraging post deal close."
- **2026 free cash flow guided to ~$2B, up from $1.5B in 2025** (which included a one-time $225M distributor termination payment) — cash generation is being emphasized because deleveraging capacity is the real gating factor for how fast the separation into two investment-grade standalone companies can happen.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **They believe coffee returns to volume-led growth once cost headwinds normalize** ("put us in a good position to see the coffee category return to solid top line performance with volume and mix meaningfully contributing") — and they're backing that belief by protecting brand marketing spend and the Alta/Coffee Collective launches through the inflationary trough rather than cutting to defend near-term margin.
- **They believe energy has multi-year structural runway** and are acting on it with 8.4oz small-can format expansion, new flavor lineups across all four brands, and active distribution/shelf-space negotiations already secured with retailers for 2026.
- **Gap to watch: SNAP risk is "baked in some allowance" but not quantified**, and the CEO search for Global Coffee Co is "in the final stages" with only a promise of "public announcement by deal close" — both are stated beliefs about being prepared without a hard commitment investors can verify yet.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Would not specify the debt split between Beverage Co and Coffee Co beyond directional allocation** — existing KDP debt stays with Beverage Co, new debt/JV/assumed JDE debt stays with Coffee Co, but no granular breakdown was given despite a direct analyst ask.
- **Declined detail on JDE Peet's 2026 category-level plans**, citing that "JDEP is still a stand-alone business" — the 8.5-8.7B revenue contribution and 6-7 point EPS accretion figures were given only in aggregate.
- **No firm timeline for the actual separation**, only that it depends on "market conditions" and other factors "within our control" — the end-of-2026 target remains a milestone, not a locked date.
