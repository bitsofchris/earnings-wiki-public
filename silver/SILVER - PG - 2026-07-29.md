# PG — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** P&G spent fiscal 2026 admitting its post-COVID playbook — price-led growth, uniform global execution — stopped working, and is now running a category-by-category repair job (value corrections in baby care, a two-decade product overhaul on Tide liquid, China's go-to-market rebuild) while betting an unquantified pile of "AI-enabled" internal tooling will eventually let it do this with a smaller organization. Fiscal 2027 guidance is wide and back-half loaded, with the low end explicitly there to absorb more macro deterioration.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Tide's price-held, performance-up relaunch is being treated as the template for the whole portfolio.** The biggest Tide liquid reformulation in "over two decades," at the same price, took the line from declining to high-single-digit growth — Jejurikar called it beating expectations and said the same logic will be pushed across brands like Head & Shoulders, where 85% of users are still on the base product.
- **Tide Evo (fiber-spun, no-plastic unit dose, 50+ patents) is the "bigger more" bet**, going into full national launch this fiscal year — proprietary manufacturing process positioned as a genuine new-to-world form factor, not just a SKU extension.
- **Retailers are being reframed as media and supply-chain partners, not just merchants**, with Jejurikar noting P&G's scale as the largest media spender is becoming a structural advantage as retailers themselves turn into media platforms — directly tied to anxiety about AI shopping agents reshaping how consumers discover products.
- **China's turnaround is a rebuilt go-to-market, not a market recovery** — the category is still contracting roughly 2%, yet P&G is growing share for the first time in 15 quarters by winning across both offline and online/social-commerce channels simultaneously, with baby care (silk-material diaper innovation) now the number-one brand in the category.

## What's changing
- **The growth algorithm is shifting back from pure pricing to a volume/price balance.** Schulten was direct: "we've had the post-COVID period 100% of growth driven by price," and fiscal 2027 assumes both levers contribute again — a tacit admission that price-led growth is exhausted as a strategy.
- **Promotion intensity is normalizing back toward pre-COVID levels** (Europe volume-on-promotion up roughly 5 points recently), which management is choosing to lean into partially rather than resist, while insisting it isn't a sustainable way to build brands.
- **U.S. share health is inflecting from a very low base**: the percentage of top customer/brand combinations holding or growing share went from under 10% in the first half of the fiscal year to about 50% in the second half — real progress, but the qualifier undercuts the "growing momentum" framing since half is still not a majority-strength position.
- **Sell-in vs. sell-out is diverging structurally, not just seasonally.** U.S. consumption ran +2% while shipments were -1%, a gap management attributes to P&G's own scale and shelf velocity making it the easiest brand for retailers to destock/restock — Schulten's blunt fix is "we have to accelerate growth," not any inventory-management change.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Fiscal 2027 guidance bakes in an ~$0.56/share, 8%-of-EPS headwind from oil, FX, and interest costs**, tied to Middle East conflict-driven Brent assumptions of $90/bbl, with Q1 EPS explicitly guided down "5% or more" — a rough start dressed as prudence.
- **Family care (Charmin/Bounty) was quietly admitted as a multi-year underinvestment**: management conceded the "vertical portfolio" that defends against private label was deprioritized during COVID demand spikes and is only now being reactivated, alongside price-point corrections that acknowledge P&G "gotten too far away" from competitive price points against store brands.
- **The 23-of-50 category-country combinations holding/growing share (down from prior periods) got no real breakdown** — when pressed, management pivoted to enterprise-market strength and China rather than naming the weak combinations directly, and admitted fabric care in Europe is losing ground to increased competition.
- **No mention of AI-driven consumer behavior change or agentic shopping risk to P&G's own retail relationships** beyond one line about retailers becoming media platforms — for a company explicitly worried about "shopping agents and AI-based search" reshaping purchase paths, there's no concrete plan disclosed for how P&G defends discoverability when an agent, not a human, is doing the choosing.
- **Jon Moeller's retirement from the board was announced same-day** with warm but brief remarks — a 38-year, multi-CEO-era executive departing during a stated "foundation-building" year invites the obvious unasked question of whether this signals broader strategic continuity risk.
