# QCOM — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Qualcomm is racing to replace Apple with everyone else — automotive, IoT, and a brand-new data center business — before memory-driven cost inflation and an accelerated Apple exit collide in the same two quarters. Management is asking investors to trust a non-handset revenue target that **nearly doubled at Investor Day** while simultaneously admitting gross margins are compressed and Apple's share is "materially lower than our prior estimate of 20%."

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **Data center as a four-product-line bet**: connectivity in fiscal 2026, custom silicon and AI accelerators in fiscal 2027, server-class CPUs in fiscal 2028 — a phased entry built on "decades of leadership in power-efficient compute" rather than a single launch.
- **Two custom-silicon hyperscaler engagements go revenue-generating in the December quarter**; wafer production has already started, with POs in hand — the first real proof point after quarters of roadmap talk.
- **HBC (High Bandwidth Compute) taped out Gen 1**, integrating compute directly with high-density memory to attack the industry's memory bottleneck; silicon demonstrations are promised "in the coming quarters" ahead of a mid-2027 launch — notable to anyone watching the compute-adjacent-to-memory architecture trend that's reshaping inference economics.
- **Automotive land-grab via platform deals, not sockets**: a new BMW agreement makes Qualcomm "the lead compute silicon provider" for next-gen ADAS and Digital Cockpit "well into the next decade," alongside a Stellantis deal reaching into the 2030s. Management frames this shift — from "socket-by-socket design awards to multi-generation strategic engagements" — as the real structural change in the auto business.
- **Automotive run-rate raised mid-cycle**: full fiscal-2026 exit target moved from $6B to **approximately $7B annualized**, with Q3 automotive revenue up 61% year-over-year — the one segment executing consistently ahead of plan.
- **Betting the phone becomes an agent endpoint**: smart glasses (Snapdragon START, ~600M eyewear units/year as the addressable base), PCs (Project Solara with Microsoft, Chromebook share with Gemini), and Samsung's Galaxy ecosystem (foldables, watches, Google-built eyewear) are all pitched as one thesis — that today's device install base "needs to evolve" for agentic AI, creating a multi-year forced-upgrade cycle.

## What's changing
- **Non-handset revenue target roughly doubled**: fiscal 2029 outlook moved from $22B to **$40B**, with data center alone reaching $15B and auto+IoT above $24B — a target increase that arrived within one earnings cycle of Investor Day, not gradually.
- **The growth math is now explicitly a replacement trade**: management said non-handset revenue growth in fiscal 2027 (guided **greater than 60% year-over-year**) is intended to replace the entirety of Apple product revenue lost that year — CFO confirmed a back-of-envelope ~$7.5B figure is "a fair range."
- **China handset demand is being called a bottom**, with QCT China revenue expected to return to double-digit sequential growth in Q4 — driven less by end-demand recovery than by channel inventory finally being drawn down to the floor.
- **Broad, double-digit price increases across end markets** are being pushed through as a pass-through of wafer, assembly, test, packaging, and memory cost inflation — CEO explicitly frames these as small relative to the memory bill-of-materials shock, not a demand-risk move.
- **Full-year seasonality is being rewritten**: with Apple's December-quarter weight gone, Qualcomm now expects sequential growth from December into March — the opposite of its historical pattern where Q1 (December quarter) was the seasonal high.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **Apple exit accelerated and reframed as a choice**: guidance now assumes Apple's iPhone silicon share is "materially lower" than the prior 20% estimate, with a **~50% sequential revenue decline** from September to December. When asked directly whether Qualcomm was "starving Apple" to redirect constrained supply elsewhere, the CFO didn't deny the mechanism — he called it a negotiation outcome ("where discussions ended up") rather than a unilateral supply decision, which is a notably careful non-answer to a pointed question.
- **QCT gross margins are running below the historical range**, and management's own timeline for recovery is vague — "over the next couple quarters," gated by working through existing contracts and product cycles, not resolved by the price increases already announced.
- **Data center margins are guided to be dilutive by design**: the CFO said the ramping custom-chip revenue will be "significantly lower" than baseline gross margin and will drag QCT's weighted-average margin by **1.5-2 points** — an admitted structural cost of entering the business, not a transitional one.
- **Handset TAM is being written down**: 2026 market now expected down "low teens" versus prior year, with QCT Android revenue guided down 20% year-over-year and an EPS hit of "greater than $1.50" — reframed by management as stored-up tailwind for whenever memory prices normalize, which is doing a lot of work as a silver lining.
- **Conspicuous absence**: no commentary on data center customer diversification beyond the same two hyperscaler engagements repeated throughout the call — when pressed on whether the addressable customer base is expanding, the CEO's answer was that new customers "want to see silicon" first, i.e., the current two are still the whole story until 2027.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Whether gross margin recovery actually lands before data center dilution offsets it · whether the Apple relationship is deteriorating faster than framed and whether "materially lower than 20%" bottoms out near zero · whether the $40B fiscal-2029 non-handset target is credible given it doubled in one quarter with only two customers actually paying for data center silicon · whether China's "bottom" is real demand recovery or just channel inventory arithmetic that reverses next quarter · whether U.S.-China export restrictions eventually touch the two hyperscaler engagements Qualcomm insists are unrestricted today. The underlying worry: Qualcomm is asking the market to underwrite a much bigger, much later diversification story right as its core handset franchise (Apple and China both) is visibly eroding in real time.
