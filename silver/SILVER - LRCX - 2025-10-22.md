# LRCX — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-10-22)

**The key idea:** Lam just posted an all-time-record quarter riding a domestic-China surge it didn't expect, even as a fresh US export restriction (the "50% affiliate rule") starts stripping ~$600M off calendar 2026 China revenue. Management is pivoting the growth story to AI-driven WFE — quantified as ~$8B of equipment spend per $100B of data center investment, more than half of it memory — while repeatedly declining to say by how much, deferring the real numbers to January. The tension: they insist China spend finally falls next year, having made and missed that exact call twelve months ago.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Record quarter, but the growth engine was the one item under restriction.** China hit 43% of revenue (up from 35%), "the majority of our China revenue continued to come from" domestic customers — the segment now getting cut by the new affiliate rule, worth ~$200M in Q4 and ~$600M for all of 2026.
2. **The AI-to-WFE translation math is now Lam's core pitch.** Archer: "we estimate these needs translate to roughly $8 billion of WFE spending for every $100 billion in incremental data center investment," with Bettinger adding "more than half of it is coming from memory" — HBM and enterprise SSDs, not GPUs themselves.
3. **NAND upgrades, not new capacity, are absorbing the AI-driven bit-demand surge — for now.** The previously-guided $40B upgrade TAM is running ahead of schedule ("it's accelerated" — Archer), but clean room space, not tool lead times, gates how fast anyone moves to new capacity.
4. **A live credibility gap on China.** Asked directly why 2026 China would finally decline after last year's identical (wrong) call, Bettinger's answer was essentially "this time we mean it" — offering no new mechanism beyond "the stack up of everybody's plans in China... is going to be less."
5. **Guidance discipline bordering on stonewalling.** On EPS growth, segment/geo splits for 2026, and CSBG margin premium, management explicitly declined to answer multiple times, banking every specific for the January WFE outlook call.

## Economy & consumer
- No direct consumer read (semicap equipment supplier); the "economy" signal here is entirely enterprise AI capex, described as accelerating: "we have seen an acceleration of activity" in data center-driven demand over "recent months."

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working:** Record revenue ($5.3B), record gross margin (50.6%, "the higher end of our guided range"), record operating margin (35%), record CSBG (spares/service/upgrades) revenue, foundry at a third consecutive record (60% of systems revenue, up from 52%).
- **Not working / softening:** Memory systems revenue fell to 34% of the mix from 41% "due to the timing of customer investment plans"; Korea revenue dropped to 15% from 22% sequentially; December guidance shows gross margin declining to 48.5% (from 50.6%) on adverse customer mix and rising tariff costs.
- **Guidance for the coming quarter already discounts China policy risk**: December revenue guide of $5.2B ± $300M "does contemplate roughly a $200 million revenue impact from the recently announced 50% affiliate rule."

## Investing & scarcity
- Capex remains disciplined and infrastructure-light: $185M in the quarter, aimed at "lab investments in the United States, along with expansion of manufacturing sites in Asia" — staying close to customer fabs rather than building speculative capacity.
- R&D is 68% of opex and funding named platforms (Vantex/Akara, Altus Halo, Dextro, Ether dry-resist EUV) explicitly targeted at gate-all-around, HBM stacking, and 3D NAND inflections.
- The binding constraint is physical, not technological: "Availability of clean room space will likely act as a limiter to the pace of NAND supply growth" — and Archer extends this logic across the whole AI buildout, noting Lam's own equipment lead times sit within typical facility construction time, so "we're probably not going to be the bottleneck."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** 2026 will be a "robust setup" and clear growth year for WFE, powered by AI-driven leading-edge foundry, DRAM, and NAND spend, second-half weighted, with China falling under 30% of Lam's revenue.
- **Action:** Concretely, Lam has only committed R&D dollars and a materials partnership (JSR) so far — no capex ramp, no guidance upgrade beyond the immediate December quarter. The gap: leadership talks about "billions of dollars of served available market expansion" from AI but is not yet putting capital behind it beyond incremental lab/R&D spend, consistent with a company that profits from *others'* capex decisions rather than making large bets of its own.
- Upgrade-first bias is itself an action/belief split worth flagging: Lam explicitly expects customers to defer new capacity build-outs in favor of cheaper upgrades "since it's the lowest cost and likely easiest way to achieve higher performance growth in bits through 2026" — meaning the loudly-announced AI capacity buildout is, in Lam's own telling, mostly not yet capacity at all.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- No 2026 WFE growth number or segment/regional breakdown — explicitly reserved for the January call ("We plan to provide our detailed 2026 WFE spending outlook and subsegment color on our January call, per our usual practice").
- Declined to say whether EPS would grow with or outgrow revenue in 2026: "You're funny, Krish. I'm not going to answer that question."
- Wouldn't quantify the semiconductor-content share of the $100B data-center figure, nor Lam's specific dollar slice of the $8B: "I don't know the precise answer to your first question... I don't know the precise number, but I know it's a decent amount."
- Wouldn't detail non-China offset by segment: "Let us leave a little bit in our pocket for next quarter."

## The street — what analysts asked
- Heavy focus on translating AI infrastructure headlines into real near-term orders — analysts pushed for evidence of "expedited meetings" or "actual orders," and management drew a firm line: announcements are a future opportunity signal, current demand is existing NAND/HBM/foundry technology transitions.
- Multiple analysts probed the WFE-outperformance math (Lam tracking ~40% tool-shipment growth vs. an assumed ~10% market) and the China "down next year" narrative given it was wrong the prior year — both got directional reassurance but no new supporting data.
- Second-half-weighted 2026 guidance drew repeated attempts to extract magnitude and segment attribution; all deferred to January.
- Compressed worry: **the entire AI-capex-to-WFE story is being told in ratios and trend lines, with every hard number pushed one quarter down the road, while the one region that actually drove this quarter's record is the one being cut by policy.**
