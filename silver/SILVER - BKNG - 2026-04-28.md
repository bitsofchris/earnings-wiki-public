# BKNG — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-28)

**The key idea:** A genuinely strong underlying quarter (room nights would have beaten the high end of guidance ex-conflict) gets overshadowed by the Middle East war, which management treats as a bounded, will-definitely-end disruption rather than a demand problem — while simultaneously using the noise as cover to start real cost discipline (hiring recalibration, discretionary spend cuts) alongside record-setting buybacks. The other live thread is Booking's bet that agentic AI search from OpenAI/Anthropic/Google becomes a new performance-marketing channel rather than a threat, mirroring its Google-search relationship of the past two decades.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The conflict math is oddly precise for something this uncertain.** Management sizes the Middle East drag at ~2pts of Q1 room-night growth and ~3pts in Q2, split roughly evenly between reduced new bookings and elevated cancellations (highest in the first month post-shock) — a level of confidence in an estimate for an event they admit could last anywhere from weeks to much longer.
2. **US acceleration is real and broad-based, not a fluke.** Fourth consecutive quarter of accelerating US room-night growth to low teens, "far, far, far" above overall market accommodation growth per Fogel, driven by direct-channel momentum (double-digit direct growth at Booking.com) and cross-vertical attach (flights +28%, attractions +25% globally) — years of deliberate US investment finally compounding.
3. **Buybacks at record scale while quietly trimming headcount growth.** $3.6B in Q1 repurchases — the largest quarterly amount in company history — alongside newly "recalibrating business-as-usual hiring" and "strictly managing discretionary spend." Capital return to shareholders is not being throttled even as the P&L gets defensive.
4. **AI framed entirely as opportunity, no defensive crouch.** Fogel: "AI, I believe, is an absolute positive for us, not a negative... I see it much more as an opportunity" — betting that a shift of frontier LLMs toward performance-marketing models (à la the Cloud Live ad placement) replays Booking's historic advantage in Google search-ads arbitrage, and that agentic AI simply expands the addressable non-digital-travel TAM.
5. **Genius/Connected Trip loyalty stacking is the real 2026 lever nobody gets details on.** Level 2/3 Genius members are >30% of the active base but >50% of room nights — management flags a "strengthen Genius" initiative for this year but declines to specify anything, calling it a "superpower" combination with Connected Trip.

## Economy & consumer
- **US low-end consumer stabilizing, not yet fully healed.** Ewout Steenbergen: booking window "now stable," ADRs "flat" versus down for many prior quarters at the lower end, though "trips are still slightly shorter" — a partial, not complete, recovery.
- **No broad demand softness detected outside conflict-affected corridors.** Steenbergen: "we don't see customers globally being cautious... outside of those areas that are impacted, actually travel demand continuing to do very well" — intra-Europe +high-single-digits, intra-Asia +low-double-digits.
- **Rising airfares and airline capacity cuts are a watch-item, not yet a read.** "Too early to draw any conclusions" on whether higher ticket prices from capacity reductions dent demand.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Ex-conflict, this was a beat-the-guide quarter.** "Excluding these impacts, our first quarter growth rates would have exceeded the high end of our guidance ranges across all key metrics" — the underlying business outperformed; the optics were purely conflict-driven.
- **Connected Trip attach is accelerating and paying off.** Connected transactions grew high-teens, "about three times faster than Booking.com's total transaction growth," now low-double-digit percentage of total transactions.
- **Merchant mix keeps climbing, adding margin.** Merchant gross bookings +24% YoY, now ~72% of total gross bookings (+5pts YoY) — a structural payments-revenue tailwind.
- **Rest of World (including Middle East) room nights declined** — the one clearly negative regional data point, versus growth everywhere else.
- **A one-time tax repeal padded margins.** $17M benefit from Canadian Digital Services Tax repeal flattered the sales-and-other-expense line; flagged transparently rather than buried.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Record buybacks continue the long-run playbook.** $3.6B in Q1 repurchases (all-time high), part of a strategy that has cut share count >40% since 2014 at an average price of $93 — Fogel frames this explicitly as "betting on ourselves."
- **New belt-tightening layered on top of continued strategic spend.** Targeted cost actions (discretionary spend, hiring recalibration) introduced this quarter specifically because of macro uncertainty, while management insists strategic investment (AI, Connected Trip, US, Asia) is protected from the cuts.
- **The stated constraint on AI rollout is testing and regulation, not capute or cash.** Fogel, directly asked what's blocking scaling agentic tools like Penny: "It's definitely not a compute problem. It's not a sense of cost... there are a lot of rules about AI now, particularly in Europe... AI rules, but also the privacy rules, and then if you're doing payments" — an explicit, unprompted disclaimer that this isn't an infrastructure bottleneck.
- **Localization investment in Asia is granular and channel-specific:** distribution build-out into KakaoTalk (Korea), LINE (Thailand/Taiwan), WhatsApp (India) alongside deep local-supply relationships (ryokans in Japan, independent properties in Indonesia/India/Vietnam).

- **Belief:** the conflict "will end" and travel demand "normalizes" as it always has post-crisis. **Action:** guidance midpoints were cut for a full four-month impact window, but the high end of gross-bookings and EPS ranges was left untouched — management is putting its money on a second-half recovery rather than hedging the upside case away.
- **Belief:** agentic AI/LLM-first search expands the travel TAM and won't disintermediate direct bookings. **Action:** heavy, unusually candid parallel testing across brands (Priceline's Penny, Booking.com natural-language search, OpenTable Concierge, KAYAK) with an explicit intent to consolidate resources into whichever wins — a real multi-brand R&D bet, not just rhetoric.
- **Belief:** the US is a long-term structural growth opportunity. **Action:** four consecutive quarters of accelerating investment-driven US growth data back this up — talk and action are aligned here, a rarer clean case.
- **Gap:** management talks up "strengthening Genius" as a 2026 priority but provides zero committed resourcing or timeline detail — pure talk for now, no visible action yet.
