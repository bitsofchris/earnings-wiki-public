# VRTX — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-12)

**The key idea:** Vertex delivered another clean quarter on the back of a CF franchise that just keeps compounding, but the entire call — and nearly the entire Q&A — was actually about povetacept, a drug that hasn't reported Phase 3 data yet. Management is explicitly betting that a single BAFF/APRIL fusion protein becomes a "pipeline-in-a-product" across IgA nephropathy, membranous nephropathy, and myasthenia gravis, while conceding under repeated analyst pressure that they don't yet know how it performs against a real placebo arm.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **CF is the fortress, renal is the bet.** CF still grew 7% for the year and funds everything else; Reshma Kewalramani called renal an "emerging" fourth vertical she expects to eventually "rival" CF in scale — a claim made entirely pre-data on the pivotal readout.
2. **Journavx's headline growth is subsidized, not organic yet.** 550,000+ prescriptions filled in 2025 sound strong, but Q4 revenue "does not yet fully reflect this strong prescription growth" because patient support programs are absorbing gross-to-net — real revenue conversion is deferred to late 2026/2027 when that program sunsets.
3. **Kasjevy's ramp is a patient-journey story, not a demand story.** 111 new patient initiations and 37 first-cell-collections in Q4 are the real leading indicators; management flagged "continued quarter-to-quarter variability" in infusions through 2026 because patients themselves control timing, smoothing out only in 2027+.
4. **The Q&A was a stress test of one drug.** Of eleven analyst questions, at least eight were about povetacept — safety signal (IgG dips), ADA/neutralizing antibodies, proteinuria magnitude, baseline GFR sensitivity, dose selection (80 vs 240mg), and placebo-rate assumptions — indicating the Street's entire near-term valuation thesis hinges on the RAINIER interim.
5. **WuXi licensing deal quietly expands the BAFF/APRIL bet.** A preclinical trispecific T-cell engager licensed from WuXi Biologics, with indications deliberately undisclosed, signals Vertex wants optionality across B-cell autoimmune disease beyond the three already-flagged indications.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read — this is a reimbursement-and-access story, not a demand story.** Growth commentary centered on state Medicaid access models, EU country-by-country reimbursement wins (UK, Germany, Denmark, Ireland, Norway, and a first-ever Italian CFTR modulator access decision for 1,500 patients), not consumer spending health.
- **Tariff risk dismissed but flagged as reversible.** CFO Charlie Wagner said Vertex does "not expect a material impact from tariffs given our diversified supply chain and large U.S. manufacturing presence, but this outlook is subject to change" — a hedge worth tracking given 2026 is a new tariff-policy year.
- **CF population growth is the only real "consumer" datapoint.** A steady ~3% annual increase in the diagnosed CF population over five years is treated as a structural tailwind independent of any economic cycle.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working: CF pricing and pediatric expansion.** US CF revenue grew 11% YoY on "pediatric uptake, ongoing strength in Trikafta and Alyftrek, higher realized net prices, and a modest benefit from channel inventory" — a mix of real volume growth and one-time inventory benefit that Wagner was careful to disclose rather than bury.
- **What's not working: Russia and gross-to-net drag.** International CF growth was just 2% YoY, partly offset by a previously-flagged $200M full-year decline in Russia sales — a rare acknowledged geographic headwind.
- **Journavx gross-to-net is the messiest line item on the P&L.** Management admits the patient support program is masking the true revenue trajectory and won't normalize until "late 2026, early 2027" — a multi-quarter gap between headline prescription growth and reported revenue that investors have to model around, not read off the income statement.
- **Margins are stable and guided flat.** Q4 gross margin of 85.7% was called "a reasonable proxy for what to expect in 2026," an unusually direct forward statement on margin from a CFO.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Field force is the lever being pulled hardest.** Doubling the Journavx sales force in Q2 2026 is a direct, funded commitment tied to hospital/retail prescriber expansion (35,000+ prescribers already, targeting 3x prescription volume).
- **Renal commercial infrastructure is being built ahead of approval.** 74 payer engagements covering 210 million lives, and "the first contingent of our field team is already trained and actively engaging customers" — real headcount and dollars committed before povetacept has an approved label.
- **Opex is rising faster than the CF base would require.** Full-year 2026 non-GAAP opex guided to $5.65–5.75B versus $5.1B in 2025, driven explicitly by "acceleration of late-stage clinical programs in renal medicine" and launch buildouts — the spending is unambiguously chasing the renal/pain bet, not CF maintenance.
- **The real scarcity is regulatory and reimbursement throughput, not physical capacity.** Every growth lever discussed — Kasjevy infusion timing, EU country reimbursement sequencing, Medicaid state-by-state adoption, Journavx payer coverage — is gated by administrative/payer processes rather than manufacturing capacity or clinical demand.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** Povetacept becomes a multi-indication franchise rivaling CF. **Action:** A myasthenia gravis Phase 2 is only just starting, membranous nephropathy is still in Phase 2 of an adaptive Phase 2/3, and the IgAN BLA submission itself is contingent — "if the Phase 3 interim analysis results are supportive." The commercial buildout (payer engagements, trained field reps) is running well ahead of any of these readouts, which is the clearest talk/action gap on the call: money and headcount are already committed to a franchise that doesn't have approved data yet in any indication except the one furthest along.
- **Belief:** Journavx becomes "another multibillion-dollar franchise." **Action:** Field force doubling and a first connected-TV ad campaign are real spend, but management is simultaneously choosing to keep subsidizing gross-to-net via patient support programs through 2026 — meaning the P&L won't show the belief materializing as revenue until the subsidy is deliberately turned off in late 2026/2027.
- **Belief:** Renal will "ultimately rival" CF's scale. **Action:** Nothing here yet beyond one drug (povetacept) in one confirmed near-term indication (IgAN) plus early-stage work in three others — a big verbal claim resting on a single asset's cross-indication potential, not on a diversified renal pipeline.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused CF product-line breakdown.** Both CFO and CEO declined to split CF revenue or guidance contribution between Alyftrek and Trikafta despite direct analyst requests, twice.
- **Withheld the new WuXi asset's target indications.** "I am going to keep that information under wraps for a little longer" — an unusually direct non-answer on a freshly announced BD deal.
- **No RAINIER baseline data pre-release.** Declined to share Phase 3 baseline characteristics ahead of the readout, deferring entirely to results disclosure.
- **Repeatedly hedged cross-trial comparisons.** On both the Chinese telitacicept ("wild-type tacky") comparison and competitor placebo-rate assumptions, management explicitly flagged these as directional analogs to "take with a grain of salt," not commitments to expected magnitude.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Povetacept safety and immunogenicity dominated.** Multiple questions probed hypogammaglobulinemia risk, IgG dip thresholds, and anti-drug/neutralizing antibody rates — management's answer leaned on a single 80mg RUBY-3 cohort with no serious infection events, a thin dataset to hang a safety narrative on.
- **Efficacy magnitude and trial-design comparability.** Analysts pressed hard on proteinuria reduction thresholds needed to look "best-in-class," standard-error variance versus rival UPCR data, dose selection (why 240mg wasn't pursued further), and how a placebo-controlled Phase 3 (RAINIER) might differ from the non-placebo Phase 2 (RUBY-3) — a clear signal the Street doesn't fully trust the Phase 2 read-through.
- **Secondary themes:** Alyftrek's international reimbursement-driven momentum, Journavx retail/hospital mix and its gross-to-net implications, and non-CF revenue guidance credibility (analysts explicitly asked what gives management confidence in a 3x YoY increase in non-CF contribution).
- **Compressed worry:** the entire valuation bridge to "renal rivals CF" runs through one placebo-controlled interim readout that hasn't happened yet, and analysts spent the whole call trying to reverse-engineer what that data will show from imperfect cross-trial analogs.
