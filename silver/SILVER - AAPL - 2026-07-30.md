# AAPL — fiscal 2026-Q3 call (2026-07-30)

**The key idea:** Apple posted a genuinely record-breaking quarter — iPhone +22%, Mac +29%, records in every geography — but the story going forward is about running out of room: advanced-node fab capacity and DRAM supply can't keep pace with demand that beat even Apple's own high expectations. Tim Cook's final call as CEO closes with a company simultaneously flexing its best product cycle in years and admitting it will be "scrambling on the supply side" through the December quarter, while quietly raising prices to absorb a "100-year flood" in memory costs.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **This was Tim Cook's last earnings call.** He hands the reins to incoming CEO John Ternus, who fielded one softball question and gave a notably guarded, non-committal answer on the AI-device competitive threat from OpenAI/SpaceX — a real changing of the guard mid-call, with almost no visibility yet into Ternus's own voice or priorities.
2. **The supply constraint is a demand story, not a weakness story.** Cook was explicit: "The root cause of it is not a regular supply issue. It's a demand forecast issue." iPhone and Mac are outperforming Apple's own high expectations, and the advanced-node fabrication capacity underlying their SoCs can't flex to match — pulled-forward supply is running out of runway.
3. **Memory pricing is now a material line item, not noise.** Cook called it a "100-year flood... with exponential increases in memory prices," paid materially more sequentially in March, June, and expects to pay "even higher" in September, only partly offset by carry-in inventory that's fading. It drove "more than 100%" of the sequential gross margin decline (Kevan Parekh), forcing Apple to raise iPad and Mac prices — something Cook called uncharacteristic and "reluctant."
4. **Guidance dropped ~500bps for September (9%-11%)** on the combination of FX headwind (~2.5pts) and supply constraints hitting iPhone, Mac, and iPad simultaneously — and management flatly refused to extend any color into the December quarter, leaving the Street's own harsher December estimates unaddressed.
5. **Siri AI launched to strong early reception** (developer/public beta) but management has zero clarity yet on what it will cost to run at scale — Cook: "it's obviously early going for us. I don't want to say that we have a complete plan for that" — an open compute-cost question they're hoping to partially recoup via iCloud+ upsells.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro read beyond company-specific dynamics** — Cook attributes the growth deceleration entirely to FX and self-inflicted supply tightness, not softening demand: "we continue to expect high levels of demand."
- **No visible demand pull-forward** ahead of price increases, per Cook, though he hedges that it's early to judge elasticity on the newly-raised iPad/Mac prices given channel inventory lag.
- **Customer satisfaction scores stayed near-saturation** (99% iPhone, 98% iPad, 95% Mac/Watch per 451 Research/Worldpanel) — consumer sentiment toward the products themselves is not the constraint; supply is.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **iPhone (+22%) and Mac (+29%) both hit June-quarter records** and gained share globally per IDC — Cook: "the most powerful and most popular iPhone lineup we've ever had."
- **Services decelerated to 12% growth**, below prior guidance, driven mostly by FX but also acknowledged "headwinds in mobile gaming" in the App Store and a tough comp against last year's F1 theatrical release — still hit records in every subcategory including cloud and payments.
- **Reported gross margin (50.1%) is partly a mirage** — Parekh disclosed ~2pts of the total-company beat and ~2.5pts of the products-margin beat came from one-time tariff refunds; excluding that, margin was roughly at guidance midpoint and September guide (47%-48%) still includes a ~1pt tariff-refund benefit.
- **iPad revenue fell 6% YoY**, the one soft spot, attributed to a tough compare against last year's A16 iPad launch rather than demand weakness.

## Investing & scarcity
- **New Broadcom deal (>$30B, multi-year)** for custom silicon and wireless connectivity — Apple's largest-ever American Manufacturing Program commitment, aimed at building "an end-to-end silicon supply chain" domestically.
- **Houston Advanced Manufacturing Center opening**, plus continued Arizona sourcing (100M+ components this year) under the $600B four-year US commitment — all funded partly by reinvesting tariff refunds.
- **The real constraint is advanced-node SoC fab capacity and a three-supplier DRAM market** — Cook, on memory sourcing flexibility: "if there were more suppliers, that would be good... It's unclear on the pricing side, it could help on the supply side. We're evaluating all options" — a notably non-committal answer to a direct question about diversifying away from current memory suppliers.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** demand for iPhone/Mac remains structurally strong and Siri AI will be a major franchise driver — "off the charts excited," "couldn't be more confident that the best is yet to come" (Cook).
- **Act:** yet they are guiding down ~500bps for the very next quarter and explicitly declining to project past it — the confidence in demand is not matched by confidence in their ability to *supply* that demand, and capacity investment (Broadcom, Houston, Arizona) is aimed at years out, not at relieving the immediate September/December crunch.
- **Believe:** Siri AI usage will be significant enough to matter for compute costs and iCloud+ upsell.
- **Act:** but there is no disclosed capex ramp or cost plan tied to it yet — Cook admits "it's obviously early going for us... I don't want to say that we have a complete plan for that." The talk of AI ambition is well ahead of any funded plan.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Flatly declined to guide or characterize December-quarter growth**, despite direct, repeated analyst pressure referencing Street estimates in the "8%-9%" range: "we're not providing any kind of color or guidance beyond the September quarter."
- **No formulaic pricing philosophy** — Cook refused to say whether the goal is protecting margin percentage or margin dollars: "It's not a mathematical formula that gets us to a specific result."
- **No commitment on Siri AI's effect on capital intensity** — hybrid cloud/on-device model described, but "what the balance of that is a bit uncertain at the moment."
- **Ternus deflected the competitive-landscape question** (OpenAI/SpaceX AI-native devices) with a generic non-answer about being "really focused on our plans" — no substantive read on the threat given.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Supply constraints dominated the Q&A** — multiple analysts pushed on how much of the 9%-11% September guide is FX versus supply, whether alternative silicon suppliers (an unnamed Arizona fab, understood to be TSMC) could relieve the SoC bottleneck, and how bad December could get. Cook repeatedly reframed it as a high-class problem (demand beating forecast) while declining to quantify the December impact.
- **Memory/pricing was the second major cluster** — sourcing diversification, elasticity risk from iPad/Mac price hikes, and the margin bridge from tariff-refund-adjusted 48.1% down to a 46.5% September midpoint, which Parekh confirmed is "more than 100%" explained by memory cost alone.
- **Services deceleration got a pointed follow-up** on whether AI is reprioritizing consumer time away from the App Store — management didn't fully rule it out, citing "headwinds in mobile gaming" alongside FX and comp effects.
- **The compressed worry:** can Apple's supply chain keep pace with a demand cycle so strong that management itself has been surprised by it, without repeatedly resetting growth expectations quarter to quarter?
