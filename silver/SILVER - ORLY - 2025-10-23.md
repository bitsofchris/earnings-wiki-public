# ORLY — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-23)

**The key idea:** O'Reilly delivered a strong headline quarter — comp guidance raised, EPS guidance raised, Pro comps over 10% — almost entirely riding a tariff-driven pricing tailwind (~4% same-SKU inflation) rather than pure volume strength. Underneath the raised guide, DIY transaction counts started softening mid-quarter for the first time this year, and management spent the call carefully threading the needle between "we're taking share" and "our own price increases may be pushing out our most price-sensitive customers."

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The comp beat is a pricing story as much as a share story.** Same-SKU inflation contributed just over 4% in Q3 and is guided to a "mid-single-digit" tailwind in Q4 — roughly in line with the implied Q4 comp guide itself, prompting an analyst to directly ask whether O'Reilly's comp is "as good as it gets" under a different model than the historical one. Jeremy Fletcher didn't dispute the framing, calling pricing "an important consideration" but not historically "what's driven our business."
2. **DIY cracked for the first time this cycle.** "We began to encounter modest pressure to DIY transaction counts midway through the third quarter" — the first admission of demand softness after multiple quarters of DIY resilience. Management attributes it to deferral of larger-ticket jobs (chassis, control arms) rather than failure-driven repairs, and explicitly says there's no trade-down to cheaper products.
3. **Pro remains the fortress.** Pro/DIFM comps grew just over 10%, ticket-count led, with Brad Beckham repeatedly emphasizing this customer is "less economically constrained" and largely non-discretionary — the clearest bifurcation on the call between a resilient commercial customer and a wobbling retail one.
4. **Supplier concentration risk (First Brands) was proactively addressed and downplayed.** Management volunteered extended commentary on supplier health before being asked, then quantified First Brands exposure at ~3% of COGS with dual/triple/quadruple sourcing as mitigation — a "nothing to see here" answer delivered with unusual thoroughness, which itself signals the Street was already nervous about it.
5. **Capex cut is a timing shift, not a signal of caution.** The $100M capex guidance reduction (to $1.1-1.2B) is explicitly attributed to project timing sliding into 2026, reinforced by an accelerated 2026 store-count target increase (225-235 vs 200-210) and a first-ever Canada expansion announcement — capital discipline language, but the underlying growth story is accelerating, not decelerating.

## Economy & consumer
- **Tariff pass-through nearly complete, but "we don't know what's next."** "We believe we have seen the lion's share of the cost impacts we are expecting as they relate to the tariffs currently in effect" — hedged immediately by Beckham: "we don't want to talk in absolute... because we don't know what's going to be in the headline next week or next month."
- **DIY consumer reacting to price, not just macro.** The deferral pressure is framed as "some degree of initial short-term reaction by DIY consumers in response to rising price levels" — a direct admission that their own pricing action is contributing to the softness, not just external economic conditions.
- **No trade-down, but a "trade-up for value" pattern.** Even lower/middle-income DIY consumers are "trading up... to get a better warranty" in categories like batteries — spending caution shows up as deferring jobs, not switching to cheaper products.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Gross margin held despite tariff shock.** 51.9% (+27bps YoY) via "prudent supply chain management and solid distribution productivity" offsetting Pro-mix headwind — full-year guide unchanged at 51.2-51.7%.
- **SG&A inflation is largely insurance-driven, not discretionary.** SG&A/store grew 4%, at the top of guidance, "again, centered around medical and casualty insurance programs" — management is explicit this is largely out of their control and won't chase artificial cuts to offset it.
- **Supply chain called "healthiest point since we emerged from the pandemic."** Strong in-stock availability across the tiered DC network cited as a core competitive advantage and share-gain driver.
- **China sourcing exposure "in the mid-20s" (% of COGS) and falling** — down hundreds of basis points over several years — though Brent Kirby notes tariff rates on alternative sourcing countries (Vietnam, Thailand, India, Mexico) are now comparably high, undercutting the diversification benefit.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Store growth accelerating into 2026:** 225-235 net new stores guided (up from 200-210 in 2025), plus the first announced entry into Canada in 2026, building on the existing Mexico expansion.
- **New DC capacity coming online:** Stafford, VA DC begins servicing stores in Q4 2025; Fort Worth, TX DC in progress — described as "full steam ahead" despite the capex trim.
- **Binding constraint is supplier/tariff cost exposure, not capital or people** — First Brands situation and broader tariff uncertainty dominate the risk discussion, not labor or compute/tech constraints (no meaningful AI/tech infrastructure commentary on this call).
- **Inventory growing faster than planned** — inventory/store up 10% YoY, ahead of the original 5%/store plan, described as deliberate investment in availability rather than price-driven stockpiling (LIFO accounting limits the price effect on reported inventory).

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** management believes the DIY deferral pressure is "short term" and industry-wide, not O'Reilly-specific share loss, and that Pro demand strength is structurally durable.
- **Action:** despite this stated confidence, they raised full-year comp guidance only modestly (midpoint) and explicitly built continued DIY pressure into the Q4 guide — a more cautious posture than the confident verbal framing suggests. **This is the clearest talk-vs-action gap on the call**: bullish rhetoric about "still feel very bullish about our opportunity," paired with a guide that assumes the softness persists rather than reverses.
- **Belief vs action on margins:** management says there's "not been any fundamental shift" in operating margin philosophy, yet is simultaneously accelerating store count and DC capex — a bet that volume/share growth outweighs near-term margin dilution risk from faster expansion.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Won't call tariff costs fully behind them:** "there's always a potential for some of that... the tariff environment... has the potential to move and change."
- **Declined to give a long-term U.S. store saturation number:** Beckham repeatedly said the ceiling estimate keeps rising each year but gave no fixed figure, citing ongoing industry consolidation as the reason the number keeps moving.
- **Dodged the SG&A-per-store return-to-2%-growth question directly:** Jeremy Fletcher called it "a challenge... to really address a hypothetical," reframing around philosophy (protect service levels, don't cut for cutting's sake) instead of giving a path or timeline.
- **No quantified competitive comparison on inflation exposure vs. NAPA/peers:** "we don't spend near as much time... looking at trying to parse the details of anybody else."

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Elasticity and inflation durability dominated:** multiple questions pressed on whether the ~4% same-SKU inflation is truly "the lion's share" or has more room to run, and whether DIY deferral is a genuine demand signal or noise from weather/timing.
- **Peer benchmarking on tariff exposure:** one analyst directly cited NAPA's lower same-SKU inflation figure, probing whether O'Reilly is worse-positioned on sourcing — management deflected to its own diversification story rather than engaging the comparison.
- **Supplier concentration (First Brands) got a direct, pointed question**, which management had already preemptively addressed in prepared remarks — a tell that this was a known Street concern going into the print.
- **SG&A leverage path was pressed twice** — both on what would restore ~2% per-store growth and on whether tech/automation investment could ease healthcare-driven cost pressure — both answered with philosophy, not commitments.
- **Compressed worry:** is O'Reilly's raised guidance simply riding a tariff price pass-through it can't fully control, while its more price-sensitive DIY customer quietly starts pulling back beneath the surface?
