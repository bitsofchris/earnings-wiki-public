# CAT — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-29)

**The key idea:** CAT posted record revenue and record backlog, powered almost entirely by an E&T power-generation boom tied to AI data centers, while tariffs quietly ate ~250bps of margin and forced management to explicitly defer any structural response until trade policy stabilizes. The tension: analysts want to know if 2026 is a margin story or a tariff-absorption story, and CAT wouldn't say — everything specific got pushed to next week's Investor Day.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Data-center power demand is the whole growth story.** Power generation sales to users jumped 33% y/y, "primarily due to demand for reciprocating engines for data center applications," and backlog growth was "driven by robust order activity in power generation and oil and gas" — construction and mining grew too, but E&T is doing the heavy lifting.
2. **Tariffs are a real, quantified, and worsening drag.** Q3 tariff impact landed near the top of the $500-600M range; Q4 is guided to $650-800M, with full-year impact now $1.6-1.75B — enough to push adjusted margin "near the bottom of the target range" despite ex-tariff margin being "in the top half."
3. **Management is deliberately not fixing tariffs structurally yet.** Creed: longer-term supply-chain moves "will require investments... and they will take time," and CAT wants "a greater level of predictability and stability" before committing capital it might have to reverse. This is a real, stated hedge against policy uncertainty, not evasion.
4. **A rare on-air correction.** IR walked back the CEO's framing seconds after the prepared remarks, clarifying operating profit actually fell 3-4% y/y rather than implied growth — a small but telling signal of how tightly the "resilient demand, tariff headwind" narrative was being managed.
5. **Prime power / turbine lead times are stretching but not yet capacity-constrained.** Solar Turbines lead times are extending, especially on the largest units (Titan 250/350), and CAT is "prepared to act when we feel like we need it" on further capacity — signaling this could become the actual bottleneck within a few quarters.

## Economy & consumer
- **North America construction beat expectations** on both residential and nonresidential activity, with IIJA-funded infrastructure projects still being awarded — a genuine demand-side positive, not just CAT share gains.
- **Asia Pacific was the one soft spot**, "below our expectations, resulting from softness in a few key subregions," with China excavator growth coming "from a very low level of activity."
- **Mining customers remain capital-disciplined** even as commodity prices (gold, copper) run high — Bonfield: order strength is concentrated in large mining trucks, while "coal... is probably a little bit of the drag... particularly in Indonesia" as declining coal prices park trucks.
- No end-consumer read-through; CAT's channel checks are dealer/rental fleet economics, and Cat Financial data show customer credit health at "the lowest third quarter in over 25 years" for past-dues — a genuinely strong signal on industrial customer balance sheets.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **What's working:** record revenue, record backlog (+39% y/y), E&T margin held roughly flat despite tariffs ("would have grown substantially without that" — Bonfield), and Construction Industries outperformed a "softer" industry via merchandising/financing programs.
- **What's not:** Construction Industries margin fell 300bps and Resource Industries margin fell 430bps y/y, both driven mainly by tariffs and unfavorable price realization; Resource Industries profit fell 19% y/y even as sales rose.
- **Financial Products segment profit dipped 2%** on higher credit-loss provisioning and equity-securities marks at Insurance Services — a minor but real crack given otherwise pristine customer credit metrics.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capacity investment is targeted, not broad-based:** Lafayette engine capacity expansion is already online and ramping; Titan 350 turbine capacity was sized with room to spare — "we obviously had room to produce those units... getting good uptake on them even sooner than we had thought."
- **CapEx guide unchanged at ~$2.5B** for the year despite record backlog — no acceleration signal yet, consistent with the "prepared to act" but not-yet-acting posture on further capacity.
- **The binding constraint is policy certainty, not physical capacity today:** tariff mitigation is being held back specifically because committing capital now risks having to reverse it later — an explicit real-options framing from Creed.
- **Turbine lead times extending** (Solar, especially Titan 250/350) is the closest thing to a physical scarcity signal, though management characterizes it as manageable, not binding, today.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** strong demand continues into Q4 and 2026, price tailwinds return as 2025's price hikes lap, and tariffs should stop worsening after Q4's peak headwind.
- **Action:** capacity additions already committed (Lafayette, Titan 350) match the stated E&T conviction — talk and action align there.
- **Gap:** on tariffs, the stated belief ("I remain confident that we'll manage the impact... over time") is not backed by committed capital — mitigation so far is limited to "no regrets" cost actions and sourcing tweaks, explicitly withheld from bigger moves pending policy clarity. That's a real, acknowledged gap between confidence and commitment.
- **Gap on backlog:** management frames rising backlog as unambiguously good but also admits they'd prefer backlog growth slow if it means shipping more product faster ("I'd like to try to at least get that stabilized or brought back in if we can") — a subtle tell that some of the backlog reflects capacity lag, not just demand strength.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 guidance** — repeatedly deferred to the January call and next week's Investor Day, even when analysts framed questions narrowly around specific puts/takes (price, capacity efficiency, tax rate).
- **Refused to disclose Solar Turbines' actual revenue** when asked directly to size it against a 2010 filing figure — Bonfield: "we are not going to disclose Solar revenues."
- **No commitment on tariff-driven pricing action** — "all factors are on the table" but nothing decided; explicitly waiting for "a greater level of predictability" before acting.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Power/data-center sizing** dominated: capacity at Solar, prime-power backlog size, Titan 350 order book, historical revenue comparisons — CAT gave qualitative color ("really excited," "healthy orders") but declined hard numbers.
- **2026 margin bridge** was the second cluster — price realization, tariff run-rate, capacity-expansion efficiency, tax rate — CAT declined to engage beyond directional comments, pushing everything to Investor Day/January.
- **Demand quality**: is the sales-to-users acceleration real end-market strength or CAT-specific merchandising/incentive programs? Management's answer leaned toward "our execution," which is itself a tell that the macro picture is murkier than the headline growth suggests.
- One-sentence worry: **is CAT's record quarter a genuine demand inflection or a company outrunning a still-soft global construction industry on financing incentives while an AI-power supercycle at Solar Turbines does most of the real work?**
