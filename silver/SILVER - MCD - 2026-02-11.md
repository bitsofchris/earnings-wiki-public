# MCD — fiscal 2025-Q4 call (2026-02-11)

**The key idea:** McDonald's closed 2025 with its strongest US quarter in years — value relaunch plus blockbuster marketing (MONOPOLY, Grinch) drove guest-count-led comps and the biggest gap to competitors in recent history — but management is already bracing investors for deceleration as the easy comps and one-off promo tailwinds fade into 2026. The subtext throughout: value is working, but it's expensive, and the real bet is that loyalty, a unified tech stack, and a faster-than-ever unit-growth pace can carry momentum once the promotional sugar rush wears off.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Q4 was genuinely strong, and franchisees noticed.** US comps hit 6.8% with *positive* guest counts — "the highest quarterly comparable guest count gap to near-end competitors in recent history" — and franchisee cash flow rose, per CFO Ian Borden. This wasn't just price/mix; traffic actually came back.
2. **Management is pre-announcing a Q1 slowdown before anyone asks.** Borden proactively guided Q1 2026 comps down sequentially in every segment — US, IOM, and IDL — citing weather (~100bps US drag), a lapping E. coli comparison, and the loss of Q4's MONOPOLY/Grinch tailwind. That's an unusually candid expectations-reset right after a beat.
3. **Value support is being weaned off franchisees, and the messaging is "the data made the decision for you."** CEO Kempczinski: "our support...is timely, targeted and temporary. We don't subsidize pricing on a permanent basis," adding that EVM support "rolls off" and franchisees looking at results should reach "a pretty easy conclusion" to keep funding it themselves — effectively transferring the cost of value leadership onto the franchise system.
4. **Beverages is the next big swing, learned from a scrapped brand.** The CosMc's standalone concept is being folded into McCafe rather than mentioned by name — a tacit admission that the spinoff didn't work — with a >500-store US test that "exceeded expectations" driving incremental snack/dinner/evening occasions and higher check, launching system-wide later in 2026.
5. **GLP-1s: no material impact yet, but management is clearly gaming out the menu implications.** Kempczinski: "we don't yet see evidence of it really having a material impact on our business," while noting behavioral shifts already showing up (less snacking, less sugary drinks) that are shaping menu R&D — a rare instance of a QSR openly discussing GLP-1-driven mix change before it's visible in the numbers.

## Economy & consumer
- **Bifurcated consumer persists.** "Industry-wide, we've seen traffic hold up pretty well with upper income consumers and traffic has been pressured with lower income consumers" — unchanged from prior quarters, but MCD claims to have arrested the low-income share loss in December.
- **Weather and macro drag flagged for Q1.** Severe US weather beginning late January "pressured the industry traffic, pressured our traffic," estimated at ~100bps for the full quarter; China and Latin America still under "continued macro pressures."
- **China holds share amid weakness.** MCD says it "maintained share" in China despite ongoing macro pressure, while still opening 1,000+ restaurants there in 2025 — expansion continuing independent of near-term same-store softness.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **3-for-3 formula (value, marketing, menu) delivered in Q4** — comps, revenue, and earnings all beat, with adjusted EPS up 7% constant-currency.
- **Marketing is doing outsized work.** The Grinch campaign "set new sales records, including the highest single sales day in our history," and combined with Minecraft and 2024 cup promotions to demonstrate marketing's power to spike volume — but this is inherently lumpy, not a durable baseline driver.
- **Margins were the tension point analysts pushed on.** Restaurant margins were "flattish year-over-year" for stretches of the year per an analyst's characterization; Borden's defense leans on Q4 improvement and higher owner-operator cash flow rather than disputing the earlier-year pressure.
- **International standouts:** UK returned to market share gains for the first time in over a year; Germany and Australia both "went 3 for 3" and gained share; Japan's new loyalty rollout and consistent strength stood out in IDL.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx stepping up again** to $3.7-3.9B in 2026 from $3.4B, consistent with the ~$300-500M/year increase telegraphed at the December 2023 Investor Day, funding acceleration toward 2,600 gross openings and the 50,000-restaurant-by-2027 target.
- **The real constraint is franchisee economics, not capital.** MCD itself has ample balance-sheet capacity; the limiting factor on value depth is how much margin franchisees will absorb once corporate-funded EVM support "rolls off."
- **Tech stack investment continues** (common consumer/restaurant/company platforms per the 2023 roadmap) — Borden: "We've got a little more work to do... but we feel really confident."
