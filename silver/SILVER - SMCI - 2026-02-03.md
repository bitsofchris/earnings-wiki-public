# SMCI — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-02-03)

**The key idea:** Super Micro posted record revenue (+123% YoY) but gross margin cratered to 6.4% as its business mix tilted toward a mega-customer (63% of revenue) with pricing leverage, plus new-platform expedite costs and component shortages. Management's answer to "how do we protect margin while chasing hyperscale volume" is DCBBS — a bundled data-center building-block product line management says carries >20% margins and is meant to pull the blended margin back into double digits, while burning through cash and new credit facilities to fund a >$13B order backlog in the meantime.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A record quarter built on a shrinking margin.** Revenue hit $12.68B, up 123% YoY and 153% QoQ, but non-GAAP gross margin fell to 6.4% from 9.5% the prior quarter — Charles Liang attributed it to "customer and product mix," expedited transportation, tariffs, and "ongoing component shortage."
2. **One customer is now the business.** A single large data-center customer represented **63% of total Q2 revenue** — a concentration level management repeatedly declined to characterize further ("hard to say" whether it stays 10%+, wouldn't confirm if it's a pre-existing named customer), even while insisting the customer base is "more diversified."
3. **DCBBS is the margin-repair story, and it's still tiny.** The data-center building-block bundle (CDU, chilled doors, power shelves, battery backup, water towers, switching, management software) is only 6 months into shipping, contributed just 4% of profit in the first half, but management wants it to double-digit profit share by end of calendar 2026, at reportedly ">20%" net margin — a big bet on a young, unproven product line offsetting a >300bps sequential margin decline.
4. **Cash is being consumed to fund growth, not returned.** Operating cash flow was roughly breakeven, inventory nearly doubled to $10.6B, and the company opened a $2B US revolving credit facility, a $1.8B Taiwan facility, and an AR factoring line — David Weigand: "we had well in excess of $13 billion of orders for purchase orders for delivery. And so we immediately had to use that."
5. **Guidance framed as deliberately conservative.** CEO called the $40B full-year revenue floor "relatively conservative," citing component-price growth as the reason for hedging rather than raising further — "if the shortage situation improve quickly, for sure, our revenue will be more than that."

## Economy & consumer
- **AI infrastructure demand exceeding supply, not a capacity problem.** Liang: "the shortage because the demand is getting so strong, not because of production capacity is reduced… that's a good sign" — framing memory/storage shortages as evidence of a hot market rather than a systemic risk to the business.
- No direct end-consumer exposure — this is a B2B/data-center infrastructure supplier; no consumer commentary applicable.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: revenue scale and OpEx discipline.** Non-GAAP OpEx fell to 1.9% of revenue from 4.1% the prior quarter even as revenue grew 153% sequentially — real operating leverage on the cost side.
- **Not working: gross margin collapsed.** 6.4% vs 9.5% prior quarter, driven by GP300 platform immaturity ("a lot of expedite transportation cost"), tariffs, and component shortages — management insists these normalize as the new platform matures.
- **Geographic mix swung hard to the US.** US revenue was 86% of Q2 sales (up 496% QoQ); Europe fell 51% QoQ and 63% YoY — a stark regional concentration shift alongside the customer concentration.
- **Working capital metrics improved even as balance sheet ballooned.** Cash conversion cycle dropped from 123 to 54 days and days of inventory fell from 105 to 63, even though the absolute dollar amounts of inventory and receivables both grew sharply — a sign of faster turns on a much bigger base, not necessarily a cleaner book.

## Investing & scarcity
- **DCBBS is the stated capital and engineering priority**, in year one of a multi-year build: 10+ subsystems shipped already, with 3-5 more categories (transformers, next-gen power generators, grid power replacement) planned for coming quarters.
- **Global manufacturing footprint expanding aggressively**: new or ramping sites in Taiwan, Malaysia, Netherlands, and the Middle East, explicitly framed as both regional-demand support and cost-structure optimization (implicitly, tariff mitigation).
- **The binding constraint is component supply (memory/storage) plus working capital**, not demand — management is now tapping $3.8B+ in new credit facilities and AR factoring to keep pace with a backlog rather than being demand-constrained.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: margins improve "quarter after quarter" from here.** Backed by only a modest guided step — Q3 gross margin guided up just 30 basis points from Q2's 6.4% — a much smaller near-term move than the confident verbal framing suggests.
- **Belief: DCBBS reshapes the margin structure.** Action matches here — real product breadth already built (10+ items in ~12 months) and a concrete profit-share target (double-digit by end of calendar 2026) with capital and headcount behind it.
- **Belief: customer base is diversifying.** Action doesn't yet support this — the same call disclosed a 63% single-customer concentration, up from a previously-disclosed lower level, and no diversification metric was offered to substantiate the claim.
- **Belief: next-gen platforms (Vera Rubin, Helios) are a growth catalyst for 2H26.** Action: "some good commitment from customers" already secured, but volume delivery timing is explicitly dependent on NVIDIA/AMD's own readiness — SMCI is a downstream dependency here, not in control of the timeline.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Refused to quantify the margin drag** from expedite costs, component costs, and tariffs individually: "we don't break those things out," per CFO Weigand.
- **Wouldn't confirm the identity or history of the 63% customer** — deflected twice to SEC filings rather than answering directly whether it was a previously-disclosed 10%+ customer.
- **Wouldn't forecast customer concentration going forward**, citing that large customers "sometimes shift their schedule of pull in or push out" — an explicit refusal to commit to a diversification trajectory despite claiming diversification is underway.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Margin trajectory dominated the Q&A** — nearly every analyst circled back to whether 6.4% is the trough and how fast DCBBS, DFM initiatives, and platform maturity translate to margin recovery.
- **Guidance conservatism** was probed directly — whether the $40B floor reflects real order softening in Q4 or deliberate sandbagging; management insisted it's the latter, tied to component-price uncertainty.
- **Customer concentration risk** came up from multiple angles (the 63% customer's durability, whether DCBBS penetration differs by customer size) and was met with vague, non-committal answers each time.
- **Compressed worry:** is Super Micro's headline growth actually a single hyperscale customer with pricing leverage renting SMCI's balance sheet and manufacturing capacity, with DCBBS as the unproven plan to win margin back before that customer's leverage grinds it down further.
