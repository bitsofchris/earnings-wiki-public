# LRCX — fiscal 2026-Q4 call (2026-07-29)

**The key idea:** Lam is riding a broadening AI capex wave — NAND upgrades this year, DRAM and advanced foundry next — and reframing itself from a WFE-cyclical equipment vendor into a structural share-gainer whose served market grows faster than the industry itself because every architecture (NAND, DRAM, foundry logic, packaging) is converging toward "3D NAND-like" complexity that favors etch and deposition intensity.

## Where they're going / what they're building
- **SAM expansion is running ahead of plan**: management now pegs served-available-market share at roughly 36–36.5% of WFE, already approaching the "high 30s" target set at the 2025 Investor Day — years earlier than originally outlined.
- **Advanced packaging guidance keeps getting revised up mid-year**: greater than 40% growth guided entering the year, raised to greater than 50% last quarter, now raised again to greater than 70%, spanning 2.5D, HBM, and foundry logic simultaneously — CEO Tim Archer called it "just everything."
- **Panel-level packaging is a deliberate early bet**: Lam has shipped 510×515mm panel systems into multi-geography development programs and will ship its first 310×310mm panel tool this year, positioning ahead of a shift away from wafer-based advanced packaging as AI packages approach nine times reticle size.
- **NAND's $40 billion upgrade opportunity is not a one-time event but a repeating cycle**: as the installed base moves 200→300→400→500 layers, "since greenfield additions have been made in that period of time, the next time it rolls through... it's an even bigger installed base" — SAM per wafer in NAND is expected to double from 128-layer to 500-plus-layer devices.
- **DRAM performance requirements are converging with leading-edge foundry logic**: low-k spacers, diffusion barriers, and EUV-driven patterning etch — capabilities Lam built for 2nm foundry — are now transferring directly into DRAM, which Archer frames as validation that "everything kind of became 3D NAND-like."
- **A new U.S. logic customer is in active dialogue** for equipment orders — Doug Bettinger confirmed engagement without naming the customer, notable given the concentrated nature of leading-edge foundry logic spend.
- **Automation (Dextro cobots) is expanding from NAND into DRAM**, with the number of automatable preventive-maintenance tasks doubling since the start of 2026 — management still calls this "very early stages of a multi-year rollout."

## What's changing
- **Calendar 2026 WFE outlook raised to the "low $150 billion range," up from $140 billion** — but explicitly no longer carrying "upside bias" language, meaning near-term surprise room has narrowed even as 2027 visibility improves.
- **NAND revenue more than doubled sequentially**, pushing total memory to a record 46% of systems revenue (up from 39%) — the clearest single-quarter mix shift on the call.
- **Gross margin hit 52%, a 20-year high**, and management raised the long-term target from the prior framework to mid-50s gross margin and mid-40s operating margin "over the next several years," driven by pricing, Malaysia-centered manufacturing efficiency, and new-product mix rather than one-off items.
- **China revenue fell from 34% to 26% of total as expected**, but within that decline, global multinational customers with China fabs grew sequentially while domestic Chinese customers declined — a bifurcation management expects to persist.
- **Tax rate is stepping up structurally**: non-GAAP rate was 11% this quarter but guided to "mid-teens" for September and expected to stay elevated "for the remainder of 2026 and likely beyond" as revenue shifts toward higher-tax U.S. jurisdictions and GILTI increases.
- **DSO jumped from 64 to 72 days** and inventory grew to $4.3 billion as Lam builds stock ahead of demand — though inventory turns still improved to 3.0x, the best in nearly five years, so the buildup isn't (yet) reading as a mismatch with demand.

## What's NOT working (or being talked around)
- **2027 numerical guidance was repeatedly declined** — asked four separate ways (by UBS, Bernstein, Morgan Stanley) whether growth could continue at this year's mid-30s-percent pace, management gave only qualitative "pretty darn good year" language, explicitly citing that clean-room capacity, not demand, is the near-term constraint.
- **Like-for-like pricing questions were deflected twice** — when pressed directly on whether unit pricing (not mix) is rising, Bettinger declined to quantify, folding it into a general "operational efficiencies, scale, product mix" answer both times, which reads as management not wanting to confirm real price increases on record margins.
- **China's structural direction remains vague**: "flat to slightly up" WFE view was repeated without new detail, despite China swinging from 34% to 26% of revenue in one quarter — a magnitude of move that got a soft qualitative answer rather than a forward number.
- **HBM4/HBM4E capital-intensity opportunity was asked about directly and declined**: "we haven't put numbers around that... I'm not prepared to do it right now" — a notable gap given HBM is repeatedly cited as a major DRAM growth driver.
- **NAND oversupply risk (raised via a third-party forecast citing weak consumer demand) got a non-answer on the actual demand question**, redirected instead toward Lam's diversification into DRAM and foundry logic — the oversupply/consumer-weakness premise itself was never directly addressed.

## Street anxiety (compressed)
Whether 2027 growth can match or exceed 2026's outperformance given clean-room space is the binding constraint, not demand · skepticism about how much of the record gross margin is real like-for-like pricing power versus mix and efficiency · concern that NAND fundamentals could invert into oversupply next year on weak consumer demand even as DRAM stays supply-constrained · whether China's multinational-vs-domestic bifurcation is a durable pattern or one quarter of noise · whether the AI capex cycle feeding into WFE is itself vulnerable to a slowdown in hyperscaler spending or a shift toward more efficient models — the underlying worry is that Lam's growth is being underwritten entirely by the AI buildout's momentum continuing uninterrupted, with capacity (not demand) as the only visible governor.
