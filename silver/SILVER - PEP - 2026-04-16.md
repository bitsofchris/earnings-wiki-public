# PEP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-16)

**The key idea:** PepsiCo is trying to prove its "hungry and thirsty for growth" turnaround is real — Frito-Lay inflected to positive volume and value share after a self-inflicted price/value miss, while PBNA's headline 9% growth is mostly acquired (Celsius, poppi) rather than organic. Layered on top: a live geopolitical cost shock (Iran) that management insists hasn't touched demand yet, funded by productivity gains it won't yet promise will show up as PFNA margin.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The PFNA turnaround is real but fragile and self-funded.** Volume grew 2%, occasions units 4%, and the company added "300 million new occasions... compared to Q1 of last year" — but this rests on productivity, not price power: "the cost for Foods North America went down in Q1, which is a remarkable achievement by the team."
2. **PBNA's growth story is mostly inorganic.** The touted 9% growth is "a combination of organic growth, a revenue growth of 2%, plus 7 points of additional platforms" (Celsius, poppi, energy brands) — underlying volume is only "almost flat" once a case-pack water exit is excluded.
3. **Inflation is assumed, not modeled.** Steve Schmitt: "our assumption is that inflation will come. The order of magnitude we're still working through" — yet guidance stands unchanged, an explicit bet that mitigation (growth, productivity, price-pack architecture) offsets whatever hits.
4. **Affordability and premiumization are running simultaneously.** Smaller packs and SNAP-exposed categories sit alongside reformulated "permissible" lines (no artificial colors/flavors) pulling lapsed consumers back — management frames both as accretive to occasions, not cannibalizing each other.
5. **Margin discipline is being kept deliberately vague at the segment level.** Repeated refusal to confirm PFNA margin expansion this year — "we're going to manage margin as a total company" — signals internal flexibility to keep spending on Frito-Lay's rebuild even if it dilutes segment profitability.

## Economy & consumer
- **Iran conflict flagged as cost risk, not demand risk (yet):** "We haven't seen an impact on demand since the war started," per Laguarta, with international momentum "actually... accelerating."
- **SNAP restrictions now live in 8 states**, hitting beverages/candy — Schmitt calls it "too early to come to any definitive conclusions," a wait-and-see stance on a real, expanding affordability squeeze.
- **Category-level recovery narrative:** savory snacks are "accelerating" and "already growing ahead of food" in some U.S. weeks, framed as consumers returning after having "lapsed" — an implicit admission the prior year's price-driven volume decline pushed real consumers out of the category.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Frito-Lay inflection is broad-based, not just Lay's:** volume share positive for "three or four periods," and now "positive share in value as well" — a KPI the company explicitly set for itself.
- **Lay's specifically remains the weakest link** despite being furthest along in restage — still volume-positive this quarter but the analyst pushback ("organic down pretty significantly") wasn't fully rebutted, only reframed around occasions and household penetration metrics.
- **PBNA growth is a portfolio story, not a core-brand story:** Celsius, poppi, Gatorade/Propel reformulation, and Mountain Dew flavor extensions are doing the work; core Pepsi is described only as "lapping" prior events.
- **Shelf resets and innovation are only half-deployed:** management expects to be "almost completed" by end of Q2, meaning the reported numbers understate the eventual run-rate — or overstate confidence if execution slips.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Productivity is the enabler of growth investment**, not a separate initiative: headcount reduction, plant closures, SKU cuts, AI-driven route optimization and digital ordering systems are funding the ability to "invest in the food business in a way that we couldn't do earlier."
- **Testing structural supply chain integration in Texas** as a next-generation cost lever — described as early-stage ("we're going to learn more in the next few quarters"), not yet a committed rollout.
- **The binding constraint is rollout pace, not capital:** shelf resets and innovation ACV are only ~40-50% complete, meaning the H2 acceleration thesis depends on execution speed, not additional spend.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Believe:** sequential PFNA acceleration, PBNA turning volume-positive ex-water, savory snacks outgrowing food/beverage as historically normal.
- **Doing:** committing marketing/advertising dollars behind Frito-Lay value plus innovation, building a global World Cup activation across Lay's/Quaker, expanding functional/energy platforms via M&A (Celsius, poppi) rather than organic brand-building.
- **The gap:** management is confident enough to reaffirm full-year guidance through a geopolitical cost shock, yet unwilling to commit PFNA to specific margin expansion — talk is uniformly bullish, committed action is hedged to "total company" flexibility.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No sizing of 2026 inflation impact:** "the order of magnitude... still working through," despite affirming guidance is unchanged.
- **No 2027 cost visibility offered:** "we've started to begin our work on 2027 scenarios... we don't have anything more to share on that today."
- **No segment-specific margin promise for PFNA** despite direct, repeated questioning — deliberately reserved as company-level flexibility.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Questions clustered almost entirely on PFNA: durability of the volume inflection, whether it was pulled forward by shelf-reset timing/winter storms, competitive pricing response risk, and the split between distribution gains vs. innovation.
- Secondary cluster on PBNA value share, bottler network strategy, and whether reported growth is organic or acquired.
- Management leaned on IRI data (not Nielsen) to support share claims and consistently redirected margin questions toward "total company" flexibility rather than segment commitments.
- **Compressed worry:** is Frito-Lay's rebound a genuine structural fix, or a temporary sugar-high from value investment and shelf resets that competitors will price right back out of once summer promotional intensity hits?
