# DXCM — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-07-30)

**The key idea:** Dexcom had a clean quarter — record new patient starts, margin recovery from last year's product-quality problems, and a $1B buyback — but the entire call is a bet on a regulatory event that hasn't happened yet. The CONNECT trial gives management a stronger hand to play with CMS, but they explicitly refuse to pull forward their mid-2027 coverage-effective-date assumption, so the market gets a "great quarter" wrapped around an 18-month wait for the actual growth driver.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Last year's quality crisis is over, and the margin recovery proves it.** Gross margin rose ~400bps y/y to 64.1%, and CFO Jereme Sylvain ties this directly to reduced scrap and freight costs from fixing the G7 out-of-box failure problems: "we've addressed all of that... complaint levels come down because of the enhancements we've been making."
2. **The whole future of the company rests on a coverage decision Dexcom doesn't control.** CEO Jake Leach calls the 25M-person U.S. type 2 non-insulin population the "Road to 100" opportunity, but CMS won't rule until "before the end of this year" with effect only "in the middle of 2027" — a full 18 months of runway with no revenue.
3. **CONNECT trial data is genuinely strong and management is using it aggressively.** 1.6% A1C improvement, 97% median CGM usage over 26 weeks, and results "even better than what we saw in our landmark DIaMonD and MOBILE studies" that won insulin coverage — but note the sales pitch: they're citing a controlled-trial 97% usage rate while real-world registry usage in this population is "well above 80%," a meaningfully lower bar.
4. **G7 15-day conversion is on track but the margin story gets more complicated before it gets simpler.** Management still expects ~50% of the U.S. base converted by year-end, but the Ireland factory ramp (hiring, training, depreciation before any output) is explicitly flagged to push Q3 gross margin down from a Q2-Q3 peak — a self-described "step down."
5. **Nutrisense is a technology acquisition dressed as immaterial to the model.** Leadership repeatedly stresses the deal adds "millions you can count on one hand" in incremental revenue while simultaneously describing it as core to G7/Stelo's future personalization roadmap — the acquisition is strategically important but they don't want it read into guidance risk.

## Economy & consumer
- **No macro framing at all** — the call is entirely reimbursement- and product-cycle-driven; DXCM doesn't talk about consumer spending power because CGM is largely covered/subsidized and demand is gated by insurance access, not household budgets.
- **Physician awareness, not patient demand, is the actual adoption bottleneck** — Sylvain: sales reps show "historical claims adjudication by payer" to convince doctors coverage exists, because doctors won't prescribe what they think won't be reimbursed.
- **~9 million already-covered U.S. patients aren't using CGM yet** — Leach frames this as evidence that double-digit growth is sustainable even before any coverage expansion hits.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Record-tying new patient starts, broad-based across segments** — "a step up actually from last quarter, even in T1 a little bit... across T1 and T2 intensive, basal as well" — no single driver, described as an ecosystem effect (NPS, wear time, algorithm, onboarding).
- **NPS scores up three consecutive quarters**, explicitly tied to fixing last year's field failures — margin improvement is offered as independent corroborating evidence of the same fix.
- **International organic growth of 16%**, led by newly-won tenders in France and Canada, but management flags **tougher year-ago comps in Q3/OUS** and a currency headwind (euro at $1.14 vs. ~$1.17-1.18 breakeven) that will bite H2 international revenue by about $15M.
- **Full-year guidance raised** to $5.18-5.25B (11-13% growth), with gross margin guide up to ~64% and operating margin to 23.5-24% — raise attributed to "underlying performance improvements," not to Nutrisense (explicitly called immaterial to the top line).

## Investing & scarcity
- **$1B buyback, $600M already spent in Q2** — capital return is now a headline capital-allocation pillar alongside reinvestment, a new posture formalized at the July Investor Day.
- **Ireland manufacturing facility is the next capacity bet** — hiring and training now, production later this year, with an acknowledged near-term margin drag before it pays off.
- **The real constraint is regulatory bandwidth, not capital or manufacturing** — every growth lever (non-insulin coverage, TEMPO pilot, international tenders) routes through a government or payer approval process outside Dexcom's control, and management is candid that timelines (mid-2027 for CMS) aren't moving despite better evidence.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **They believe CONNECT will "reshape diabetes care in the U.S."** and drive both CMS and international coverage decisions — but the only concrete action tied to that belief is submitting the data package and funding advocacy/KOL education, not any change to spending or hiring tied to an accelerated timeline.
- **They're acting now on things fully within their control**: G7 15-day rollout, Ireland capacity, Nutrisense integration, Stelo redesign — all proceeding on schedule regardless of the CMS outcome, which is the safer bet given the external dependency on the bigger prize.
- **The TEMPO FDA pilot is being talked up as strategically significant** ("first company chosen") but Leach is explicit it has no direct read-through to the CMS non-insulin coverage decision — enthusiasm without a committed dollar figure attached.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to

- **Wouldn't quantify Nutrisense's revenue contribution beyond "count the millions on one hand,"** deflecting further financial detail to the 10-Q disclosure of purchase price.
- **No single quarter identified as peak G7 15-day margin benefit** — Sylvain describes it as "a steady drip... over time" rather than committing to a specific inflection point, avoiding a number analysts can hold them to.
