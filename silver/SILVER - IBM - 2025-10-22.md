# IBM — fiscal 2025-Q3 call (2025-10-22)

**The key idea:** IBM printed its best growth quarter in years (7% revenue, 22% adjusted EBITDA growth) and is telling investors 2026 should be better still, built on three legacy-cycle mechanics — the z17 mainframe refresh, a Red Hat bookings reacceleration, and GenAI backlog conversion — plus a AI-driven internal productivity story that's now running more than double its original target. The tension: nearly every forward number offered was explicitly qualitative ("confidence," not guidance), while the one hard, real-time indicator that's gone the wrong way twice in a row — consulting signings and book-to-bill — got explained away as a mix-quality story rather than treated as a genuine headwind.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **A genuinely strong quarter, not just spin.** Revenue growth accelerated to 7% ("our highest growth in several years") with every segment accelerating sequentially, adjusted EBITDA up 22%, and nine straight quarters of operating margin expansion — this is real operating leverage, not a one-off comp.
2. **The mainframe cycle is doing a lot of the heavy lifting.** z17 delivered IBM Z's best Q3 in nearly two decades (+59% y/y) and "the strongest February launch in history," but transaction-processing (software riding on that hardware) actually declined 3% as clients "prioritize hardware spend" first — Jim explicitly models this as a repeating pattern from the z16 cycle, meaning TP growth is a bet on history rhyming, not new demand.
3. **GenAI monetization is real but concentrated and lopsided toward consulting historically, now shifting toward software.** The $9.5B inception-to-date GenAI book of business splits roughly 80/20 consulting/software historically, but consulting's Gen AI piece alone hit $1.5B in-quarter (up from doubling project count) — this backlog, not the shrinking core signings, is what's carrying consulting's "inflection" narrative.
4. **Consulting's book-to-bill is the number to watch, and it's not going the right way.** Signings down 5% for six straight quarters, with Jim's defense resting on "quality" (net-new penetration, backlog erosion improvement) rather than disputing the trend — a classic metric-reframe when the headline number disappoints.
5. **Government shutdown risk is dismissed almost entirely** — Arvind calls IBM's exposure "de minimis" because both its software (subscription, mission-critical: Social Security, VA payments) and consulting book skew toward essential-services work exempted from furloughs.

## Economy & consumer
- **Optimism reaffirmed, not just repeated.** "Last quarter, I said we had moved from being cautiously optimistic to optimistic... we continue to see broad-based demand from clients and remain optimistic" — Arvind Krishna holding the upgraded stance through a second quarter.
- **IT budgets outrunning GDP is the implicit macro tailwind.** Arvind: IT budgets have been "growing typically two to three points ahead of GDP growth" for four-to-five years, which he expects to continue and cites as the source of software/automation growth rather than share-shift from other IT spend.
- No direct retail/end-consumer signal — IBM's "consumer" is enterprise IT buyers, and their budget behavior is the proxy: spend mix is shifting from "70% run / 30% new" toward "60/40," i.e., more discretionary dollars freed for new (often AI) projects.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Software and infrastructure are clearly working.** Software +9% (accelerated ~3 points organically quarter-over-quarter), automation +22% (HashiCorp "delivered its highest bookings quarter in history"), infrastructure +15% with hybrid infrastructure +26%.
- **Consulting returned to growth but on thin margins of comfort.** +2% revenue, "a positive inflection point," with segment margin up 200bps to a three-year high — yet signings fell again and total backlog growth is a modest 4%.
- **Red Hat decelerated on a hard comp, not weak demand per management.** Revenue +12% (deceleration from prior quarters) blamed on lapping "abnormally" high consumption-based services growth and RHEL "trending back towards single-digit growth" after a double-digit prior year — bookings (+~20%) are framed as the leading indicator that matters more than trailing revenue.
- **Transaction processing is the visible casualty of a healthy cycle.** TP revenue down 3% in Q3, down 1% year-to-date, explicitly because clients are spending on z17 hardware first and software monetization lags — a pattern management says has repeated every mainframe cycle and should reverse in Q4.

## Investing & scarcity
- **M&A remains the primary growth lever, with real financial guardrails.** Arvind: targets must fit strategy (hybrid cloud/AI), deliver synergy ("growth rate inside IBM will be above what it was as a standalone entity"), and if "of size," become "accretive to cash by the end of the second year." Multi-year capital flexibility is "mid-twenties [billion] perhaps a bit more" over three years.
- **Internal AI productivity is now a material profit lever, not a side project.** Original 2023 target was $2B in productivity savings; run-rate is now guided to $4.5B exiting 2025 — Arvind: "I believe we have significant opportunity ahead of us to continue to become even leaner."
- **The binding constraint is conversion capacity, not client demand.** With backlog, bookings, and Gen AI pipeline all described as healthy, the gating factor Jim names directly is execution: "a lot of work still to go to sell and bill within quarter."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: Red Hat should reaccelerate to "mid-teens or close to" in 2026** — action behind it is real (20% bookings growth, OpenShift ARR +30% to $1.8B), but the 2026 growth figure itself is stated as an expectation, not a number underwritten by signed contracts beyond the six-to-twelve month RPO window.
- **Belief: TP returns to growth in Q4 and continues into 2026** — backed by concrete evidence (z17 shipping "over 100% more MIPS than the z16 cycle," 130% of the z16 program pace two quarters in), making this one of the more credibly-supported forward claims on the call.
- **Belief: enterprise AI is only in "the first innings," with private/self-hosted deployment growing as agents get more critical** — action is thin so far beyond partnership announcements (Anthropic, Grok/xAI, Granite 4.0); no committed capex or headcount numbers tied specifically to this shift, unlike the productivity and Red Hat figures.
- **Gap flagged:** consulting's "inflection to growth" claim is supported by revenue (+2%) and margin, but the leading indicator (signings) still points down — management is betting the market on the lagging (revenue) rather than leading (signings) metric.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No 2026 numeric guidance given**, repeatedly described instead as "qualitative statements about confidence" (Jim's own phrase) — Red Hat, TP, and consulting 2026 figures are framed as directional, not modeled.
- **M&A size and timing left fully open.** Arvind: "we will never rule anything out," but declined to say whether a "larger, transformative transaction" akin to Red Hat is under consideration, keeping optionality rather than committing to a scale.
- **Software growth for 2026 was decomposed piece by piece (Red Hat, TP, automation, data/AI) but not rolled into a single guided number** — Jim reiterated the standing "approaching double digits" software model rather than updating it for the new information provided in the call.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Free cash flow durability into 2026** dominated the open — is the $14B raise a one-time convergence of tailwinds or a repeatable engine? Jim's answer leaned on structural drivers (revenue growth, operating leverage, efficient balance sheet) and a "mid to high one-twenties" cash-conversion realization rate "for four years in a row," but sidestepped naming what could go wrong.
- **Software growth composition** (organic vs. M&A, Red Hat deceleration, HashiCorp lap in Q1 2026) came up from two separate analysts — the repeated question signals the Street doesn't fully trust the headline growth rate is clean of acquisition math.
- **Consulting book-to-bill below 1 for two quarters** was asked directly and pointedly, tying it to whether the "growth inflection" is durable — management's answer redirected to backlog and GenAI-mix quality rather than disputing the signings weakness itself.
- **Cloud/hyperscaler competitive threat to IBM Cloud** was raised once and answered by reframing IBM as a beneficiary-via-partnership (consulting/software riding on AWS/Azure/GCP/CoreWeave) rather than a competitor for cloud infrastructure share — an implicit concession that IBM isn't fighting for hyperscale workloads directly.
- Compressed worry: **is the growth acceleration real end-market demand, or is it several one-time cyclical tailwinds (mainframe refresh, Red Hat lapping dynamics, HashiCorp integration) converging in the same year and due to fade in 2026?**
