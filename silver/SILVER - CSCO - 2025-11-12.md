# CSCO — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-11-12)

**The key idea:** Cisco beat on every guided metric and leaned hard into an AI-infrastructure-and-refresh-cycle narrative, but the call spent much of its energy defending against a subtler concern: strip out hyperscaler AI orders and the "record" quarter is still solid (9% core order growth) but not exceptional, while a real security business is being obscured by an accounting-driven mix shift. The bet is that campus/branch end-of-life plus AI-driven network modernization compounds into a multi-year cycle big enough to outrun tougher comps in the back half.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **AI orders are real but still narrow.** $1.3B in hyperscale AI infrastructure orders, guided to 2x for the full year off the same customer/product cohort as last year — impressive, but Robbins admits "most of what we saw in Q1 was just a deepening of existing use cases," not new logos, and the fresh sovereign/neo-cloud/enterprise pipeline ($2B+) is explicitly excluded and not material to this year's guide.
2. **The base business isn't accelerating as much as headline growth suggests.** When an analyst calculated that stripping ~$1B of AI infrastructure revenue leaves the rest of the business growing only ~3.6%, Robbins countered with an unprompted, previously undisclosed data point — core orders excluding hyperscale grew 9% — and Patterson flagged that Q3/Q4 comps get materially tougher (+11%, +8% a year ago vs. Q1/Q2's -6%/+9%).
3. **Security's "decline" is an accounting artifact layered on real weakness.** Revenue fell 2% as Splunk's cloud-subscription mix jumped from roughly 50/50 to two-thirds cloud in a single quarter (ratable revenue recognition vs. upfront on-prem), but legacy-platform decline is also a genuine, ongoing drag — Robbins: "none of us are happy about where we are right now," while reaffirming a mid-teens long-term security growth target that this quarter's print does nothing to support yet.
4. **Supply is tightening and Cisco is stockpiling ahead of it.** DRAM, PCB, and optics have seen "significant price increases" and tighter supply; Cisco responded by growing inventory plus advance purchase commitments 38% (~$3B) year-over-year — a direct, funded hedge against an AI-order book it just promised to double.
5. **The campus refresh is a slow-moving, multi-year annuity, not a spike.** New Catalyst, Wi-Fi 7, and secure-router lines are "ramping faster than in prior product launches" against a Cat 4K/6K installed base Robbins says is still worth "billions and billions and billions" unrefreshed — a genuine multi-year tailwind, echoing the seven-year Catalyst 9K cycle that began in 2017.

## Economy & consumer
- **Sovereignty is now a demand driver, not just a talking point.** Public sector orders grew across all geographies, with the strongest growth (mid-to-upper teens) in EMEA and APJC rather than the U.S., driven by European defense/geopolitical spend and a broader push for "not just data sovereignty and tech sovereignty, but overall sovereignty."
- **U.S. federal grew despite the government shutdown** (high single digits), with management expecting upside once reopened given cyber-hygiene pressure on end-of-life federal equipment.
- **Onshoring is a stated structural tailwind for industrial IoT**, with orders up over 25% "driven by onshoring of manufacturing to The United States, the increase of AI workloads at the network edge, and the emergence of physical AI."
- No traditional consumer-demand commentary — Cisco's exposure is enterprise/carrier/government, not retail consumer.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Networking is the clear engine**: revenue up 15%, orders up to high-teens growth for a fifth straight quarter, with strength "led by high double-digit growth in service provider routing largely driven by revenue from AI infrastructure."
- **Security is the visible soft spot**: revenue down 2%, with next-gen products (secure access, XDR, HyperShield, AI Defense, refreshed firewalls) growing mid-teens but "partially offset by a decline in our prior generation platforms," compounded by the Splunk cloud-mix timing hit.
- **Splunk's underlying health looks fine on ARR/RPO** (both double-digit growth) even as the revenue line disappoints — Patterson: cloud offers "are stickier than the on-prem offers" and expected to recognize *more* revenue over time, just not this quarter.
- **Collaboration continues to shrink** (-3%, devices and WebEx weak) — a legacy drag getting no airtime relative to AI/networking.
- **Gross margin held up despite mix pressure**: 68.1% non-GAAP, product margin down 170bps on "negative impacts from mix and pricing," offset by productivity gains — margin durability under AI-driven mix shift is a live open question given DRAM inflation.

## Investing & scarcity
- **AI infrastructure orders accelerating sharply**: $1.3B in Q1, expected ~$3B in FY26 hyperscaler AI revenue, guided to at least 2x hyperscale AI orders for the full year versus FY25's cohort.
- **New silicon and product launches aimed squarely at the AI network layer**: the Silicon One P200-powered 8,223 router (51.2 Tbps, first-to-market for inter-datacenter AI traffic), a new NVIDIA Spectrum X-based N9100 switch (Cisco is "the first NVIDIA partner to offer networking with their cloud reference architecture"), and continued pluggable-optics wins — "all hyperscalers are now customers" of Cisco's optics.
- **Scarcity is shifting from demand-constrained to supply-constrained on components**: DRAM, PCB, and optics tightness with "pretty significant" price increases; Cisco's answer is advance purchase commitments plus inventory, up 38% YoY, both already reflected in guidance.
- **Silicon One is nearing full-portfolio penetration**: expected to be rolled out across the entire product line by 2028 ("2019" transcribed, evident typo — context implies roughly two-and-a-half years out), with a millionth chip shipping in 2026.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief**: AI will push inferencing and agentic workloads to the network edge, with agentic queries generating "up to 25 times more network traffic than chatbots." **Action**: launched Cisco Unified Edge (converged compute/network/storage) days before the call — a real product commitment, though Robbins hedges on how it scales: "we'll see how it scales and ramps."
- **Belief**: scale-across (inter-datacenter) networking is an "emerging" opportunity alongside scale-out/scale-up. **Action**: shipped the P200-based 8,223 router now, but conceded a scale-up Ethernet product is only roadmapped ("you should expect to see something from us over time") — belief is ahead of shipped product here.
- **Belief**: sovereign AI (Middle East, Europe) is a large future opportunity. **Action**: G42/AMD partnership expansion, European "sovereign critical infrastructure" portfolio launched — but management is explicit the orders aren't showing up yet, gated on export licenses, and "not material to this year's guide," so the action is partnership-building, not yet revenue-backed.
- **Belief**: security should be growing mid-teens long-term. **Action**: no new investment commitment disclosed to bridge the current -2% print to that target beyond organic new-product ramp (secure access, HyperShield, AI Defense) — the gap between stated belief and demonstrated near-term action here is the widest on the call.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Declined to break out the $3B AI revenue guide between optics and systems**, or to specify how AI-related margin compares to corporate average beyond generalities about a "broad portfolio and a range of margin."
- **Explicitly kept sovereign/neo-cloud/enterprise AI pipeline ($2B+) out of the 2x hyperscale order guide**, and called the sovereign build-out "not material to this year's guide" — declining to put a number on when export-license-gated orders convert to revenue beyond "probably the second half."
- **Would not attribute campus-refresh strength to competitive share gains with precision**: Robbins acknowledged "confusion in the marketplace" around competitors' Wi-Fi but said "I can't give you any specifics relative to whether that's a big part of it."
- **No firm timeline for security reacceleration to mid-teens** — only "probably... four quarters" to normalize the Splunk mix comparison, with no quarter-by-quarter bridge given.

## The street — what analysts asked
- **Diversification vs. concentration in AI orders**: multiple analysts pushed on whether AI order strength reflects broadening use cases (scale-across, inferencing) or just deeper spend from the same handful of hyperscalers — management conceded most of Q1's strength was "deepening of existing use cases," not new logos.
- **Is the "record quarter" story overstating underlying growth?** The bank analyst's back-of-envelope math (ex-AI base business growing only ~3.6%) forced Cisco to disclose an unplanned data point (9% ex-hyperscale order growth) it hadn't previously offered — a sign the framing question landed.
- **Security accounting vs. reality**: repeated questions on whether the Splunk-driven revenue miss is truly one-time and what "normalized" security growth looks like against the mid-teens long-term target Cisco keeps reiterating without recent evidence.
- **Supply/capacity risk**: whether Cisco can actually fulfill a promised doubling of hyperscale AI orders given tightening DRAM/optics/PCB supply — answered with a capacity-commitment metric (inventory + advance purchases) rather than a direct yes/no.
- Compressed worry: **is Cisco's AI narrative doing more work than the underlying order book, and is the security segment's growth story now running on trust rather than trailing numbers?**
