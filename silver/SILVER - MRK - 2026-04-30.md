# MRK — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-30)

**The key idea:** Merck posted respectable top-line growth (5% reported, 3% ex-FX) built almost entirely on KEYTRUDA and oncology, but the quarter's real story is capital allocation under pressure: a $9B non-cash charge for the Sedera deal pushed the company to a GAAP loss, and a second acquisition (Terns Pharmaceuticals, ~$5.8B additional charge) was announced with management publicly walking back the target's own efficacy claims mid-call. The company is betting heavily — a full commercial reorg, an AI infrastructure buildout with Google Cloud, and continued M&A — on a wave of "20-plus" new product launches to diversify away from Gardasil (down 22%) and eventual KEYTRUDA patent exposure, but near-term numbers are still being propped up by one-time timing benefits ($250M of pulled-forward KEYTRUDA purchases) that reverse later this year.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **KEYTRUDA's growth number is partly an illusion.** The 8% oncology growth included "approximately $250 million from the timing of purchases" that CFO Caroline Litchfield flagged will show up as "a corresponding headwind in the Q3" — the underlying growth rate is softer than the headline.
2. **Merck's own diligence undercut the Terns deal on the call itself.** Pressed on discrepancies in TERN-701's molecular response data, Dean Li conceded Merck's conservative read is "north of 50% MMR" versus Terns' publicly stated 75% — a material downgrade of the asset's headline efficacy claim, disclosed only because an analyst caught it, not proactively.
3. **Gardasil's China/Japan collapse (-22% global, driven by Asia demand) is the clearest external-demand signal on the call** — this is a demonstrated vaccine-demand air pocket in Asian markets, not a Merck-specific execution issue, and it's large enough to be a real drag on the vaccines franchise.
4. **The AI infrastructure buildout is broad but unquantified.** A "multi-year partnership with Google Cloud to scale advanced AI data and agentic capabilities," layered on Tempus AI (precision oncology) and Mayo Clinic (clinical/genomic data) deals — framed purely as a productivity and pipeline-speed play, with zero cost or ROI disclosure, a pattern increasingly common across pharma/tech AI tie-ups.
5. **The commercial reorg is a tell on internal complexity.** Splitting into Specialty/Pharma/Infectious Diseases, Global Oncology/MSD International, and a new Strategic Access/Policy unit — with three new executive appointments — signals management believes the existing structure couldn't handle 20+ concurrent product launches, not just a cosmetic rebrand.

## Economy & consumer
- **No broad macro read** — Merck doesn't comment on the general economy; the closest signal is regional demand divergence in vaccines.
- **Asia vaccine demand is the standout weak spot:** Gardasil "lower demand in China and Japan, consistent with our expectations" drove the bulk of a 22% global sales decline, while U.S. Gardasil softness was attributed to CDC purchase timing rather than underlying demand.
- **Patient uptake, not consumer spending, is the relevant "demand" metric** for a pharma company — WINREVAIR added "more than 1,600 new patients" in the U.S. this quarter, a genuine volume signal for a specialty drug.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Working: oncology breadth beyond KEYTRUDA.** WELIREG +43% to $199M and CAPVAXIVE (pneumococcal vaccine) +31% show the pipeline diversification is real, not just messaging.
- **Working, with an asterisk: KEYTRUDA.** $8B, +8%, but roughly $250M was pulled-forward wholesaler buying that reverses in Q3 — the clean growth rate is meaningfully below the reported number.
- **Not working: Gardasil (-22%) and Otivus.** The new COPD drug Otivus was "adversely impacted by the CMS reimbursement change, as well as Medicare deductible resets," with management only citing a March recovery in prescription trends as evidence the damage is temporary.
- **Not working: the P&L optics.** A $9B one-time Sedera charge pushed operating expenses to $15.2B and produced a **negative 43.5% tax rate** and a **loss of $1.28/share** — a genuine GAAP-level miss driven entirely by deal accounting, worth flagging plainly rather than waving off as "non-GAAP noise."
- **Steady: Animal Health (+6%)**, driven by livestock/poultry demand and price, plus the new Numelvi (dog dermatitis) launch — a quietly consistent diversified segment.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is going to M&A and pipeline defense, not buybacks.** Buybacks are capped "on pace for approximately $3 billion" — modest relative to the ~$15B being spent on Terns alone — while BD stays "a high priority."
- **The stated BD sweet spot is explicit and repeatable:** Rob Davis named "$1 billion-$15 billion" as the target deal-size range, with oncology, immunology, and cardiometabolic called out as the three most active search areas.
- **The scarce resource is organizational bandwidth to execute simultaneous launches**, not cash — the entire commercial reorg exists to solve for "speed... this opportunity demands" across 20+ new products, an unusually candid admission that structure was the constraint, not capital or science.
- **AI investment (Google Cloud, Tempus, Mayo) is scoped as a pipeline-speed and productivity lever**, explicitly tied to "reaching patients... with greater speed and with a higher likelihood" — infrastructure spend with no disclosed budget or expected payback timeline.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: $70B of new incremental revenue by the mid-2030s from 20+ launches.** Action: a full commercial reorg with three new executive leads announced this quarter — real organizational commitment behind the belief, not just guidance language.
- **Belief: TERN-701 has "multibillion-dollar commercial potential."** Action: committed ~$15B (implied by the $5.8B one-time charge structure) to acquire it — but on the same call, management quietly downgraded its own efficacy expectations for the asset relative to the seller's public claims, a gap between the confidence in the messaging and the confidence in the underlying data.
- **Belief: WINREVAIR can expand from PAH into a broader, sicker pulmonary-hypertension-plus-heart-failure population.** Action: still in FDA endpoint negotiations (time-to-clinical-worsening vs. PVR reduction) with no phase III start date given — belief is ahead of committed action here.
