# COST — fiscal 2026-Q2 call (2026-03-05)

**The key idea:** Costco delivered another strong quarter (comps +7.4%, EPS +14%) while explicitly choosing not to bank the wins — cutting prices on eggs, cheese, coffee, textiles and bedding even as tariffs and inflation gave it room to hold the line. The tension analysts kept probing: multiple growth engines (membership adds, renewal rate, core margin) are simultaneously decelerating toward what management calls their "long-term" steady-state, and the company is betting real estate creativity, digital personalization and AI-adjacent retail media can reaccelerate them without touching the core value proposition.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **Margin expansion is deliberately capped by design, not accident.** CFO Millerchip was explicit: "we are slightly expanding margin, but it's only slightly because... where we see meaningful benefit, we're reinvesting in the member." Core-on-core margin rose 22bps but reported core margin fell 3bps — the gap is Costco choosing lower prices and mix dilution (pharmacy, e-commerce) over flow-through.
2. **Three growth engines are cooling toward a "long-term rate" simultaneously.** Membership growth (4.8%, down from ~5%+), renewal rates (US -10bps, worldwide flat after prior declines), and new-country openings (fewer this year) are all normalizing — management frames this as maturation, not weakness, but it's a notable convergence in one quarter.
4. **Gold and unique high-ticket items are functioning as a member-engagement flywheel, not just a sales line.** A $150,000 diamond ring and near-200 luxury golf carts made the highlight reel, and CFO Millerchip credited gold bars specifically with driving traffic and cross-sell to online: "it's actually... helped elevate other parts of our business too."
5. **Real estate creativity is now a stated growth lever, not a footnote.** Parking-deck warehouses and mixed-use builds (residential above a Costco in LA) are explicitly named as what gives management "more confidence" in hitting the 30-warehouses-per-year target in space-constrained metros like LA and New York.

## Economy & consumer
- **Inflation cooled to low-single-digits in Q2**, per Millerchip, driven by deflation in produce, eggs, butter, cheese and dairy; beef and candy remained inflationary, and gold pushed nonfood inflation up modestly.
- **No major weather drag identified** despite a competitor citing a February headwind — Costco attributes lighter US traffic growth mainly to 55 Northeast warehouses closed for a full day, not a demand issue.
- **Consumer resilience framed as value-driven, not stretched:** "when you deliver on those things, we're seeing members are willing to and have the capacity to spend" — Millerchip ties consistent 6-7% comps to assortment newness rather than macro tailwinds.
- **Middle East instability flagged as a forward watch-item** for fuel costs and shipping schedules, though supply chain was "relatively stable" through Q2 with no disruption yet.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Fresh (+low double digits) and nonfood (+high single digits) comps led the quarter**, with meat, bakery, gold/jewelry and small electrics as standout categories; food and sundries (+mid-single digits) is being weighed down by ongoing egg deflation even as unit/share growth there is strong.
- **Digital engagement is compounding:** site traffic +32%, app traffic +45%, digitally-enabled comps +22.6%, with personalized recommendation carousels alone driving over $470M in e-commerce sales this quarter.
- **What's not working: renewal rate erosion, though stabilizing.** The structural driver — online sign-ups renewing at a lower rate than warehouse sign-ups — is a known drag management says is nearing "maturation," aided by targeted digital retention campaigns and auto-renew promotion.
- **SG&A crept up 13bps** partly from a one-time 6bps hit to general liability reserves for unsettled prior-year claims — a real, if modest, cost overhang flagged plainly rather than buried.

## Investing & scarcity
- **CapEx ~$6.5B for the year** funding warehouse pipeline, remodels, depot expansion, and digital experience — consistent with, not accelerated from, prior guidance.
- **International expansion pipeline (ex-Canada) is thin near-term** — only 3 new openings this year outside Canada — with management pointing to acceleration "from '27 on," an admission that near-term international unit growth is capital/pipeline-constrained today.
- **Cash is building toward prior special-dividend levels**, but management hedges on timing (see Hedges) — capital allocation priority order remains: reinvest in business, grow regular dividend, buyback to offset dilution, then special dividend as the residual use.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** membership and renewal-rate growth will settle near "long-term" structural rates (~5% membership growth) as the online-sign-up mix matures. **Action:** actively investing in targeted digital retention campaigns and promoting auto-renewal in-warehouse — a real program, but one management itself says only partially offsets the mix-driven decline rather than reversing it.
- **Belief:** AI will make Costco "better at who we are" for members and employees, not chase adjacent AI products. **Action:** concretely limited so far to pharmacy in-stock AI tools and digital checkout speedups — no infrastructure or model-development commitments disclosed; this is an early-innings capability build, not a stated big-dollar initiative.
- **Belief:** retail media/advertising is a "significant opportunity." **Action:** explicitly under-resourced relative to the stated opportunity — described as still testing "digital TV and targeted MVM amplifications" with over 1,000 suppliers participating, but no dedicated revenue target or investment figure attached, and profits are pre-committed to member reinvestment rather than margin.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **Special dividend: no plan disclosed.** Despite cash rebuilding to pre-dividend levels, Millerchip noted the yield math now requires a larger cash balance given the higher stock price, and explicitly said "there are no plans that we could share at this time."
- **Retail media sizing declined:** asked twice (by two different analysts) to quantify the media/advertising opportunity, Millerchip repeatedly redirected to qualitative framing ("meaningful amount," "significant opportunity") without a number.
- **IEEPA tariff refund process left fully open:** "it is not yet clear what the process will be, what refunds, if any, will be received and when this will happen" — a genuine unknown, not spin, but notably unresolved given how central tariffs were to the call.

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts clustered around **deceleration diagnostics**: membership growth, renewal rates, core-on-core vs. core margin divergence, and China — each time pressing management to distinguish structural slowdown from one-off/mix noise.
- **Capital allocation questions (special dividend, cash growth) got a polite but firm non-answer**, with management repeatedly falling back on "subject to Board approval" language.
- **AI and retail media drew genuine curiosity but shallow disclosure** — management's answers stayed philosophical (AI should "make us better at who we are") rather than quantitative, suggesting these are still pre-scale initiatives rather than P&L drivers today.
- The compressed worry: **is Costco's engine of compounding membership and margin gains structurally plateauing into a "mature company" growth rate, even as headline comps stay strong?**
