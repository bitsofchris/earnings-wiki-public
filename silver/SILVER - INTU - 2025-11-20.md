# INTU — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2025-11-20)

**The key idea:** Intuit posted an 18% revenue quarter and is explicitly betting its next growth leg on two moves: distributing its "system of intelligence" wherever customers already are (most notably inside ChatGPT via a new OpenAI partnership with no revenue share) and converting its AI-agent/data advantage into mid-market ERP share via accountant-firm partnerships. The tension: management insists the consumer and small-business economy is merely "stable," not weak — yet the entire tax-season and Credit Karma growth story leans on customers needing a "trusted platform" precisely because parts of that economy (real estate, lending, near-prime credit) are visibly under strain.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The OpenAI deal is a distribution bet with unchanged economics.** CEO Sasan Goodarzi was explicit: "There's no revenue share... the economics that we enjoy today when directly working with customers, we will enjoy tomorrow." Intuit's LLMs still train exclusively on customer data inside its "four walls" — this is channel expansion into ChatGPT's 800M weekly users, not a data or model concession.
2. **Mid-market is the growth engine, and the constraint is awareness, not product.** IES contracts grew ~50% quarter-over-quarter and online ecosystem revenue for QBO Advanced/IES grew ~40%, yet Goodarzi admits "very few people still know about Intuit Enterprise Suite." The response is a sales/marketing surge (conference presence, redirected headcount, accountant-firm partnerships) rather than further product investment.
3. **Mailchimp is the one visible drag, still unresolved.** Revenue was down slightly, and management reaffirmed a double-digit exit-rate target for fiscal 2026 while declining to say whether that comes from mid-market wins alone or requires smaller-customer recovery too — "early calendar year and springtime" is the earliest committed checkpoint.
4. **AI agents are shifting from novelty to measured ROI.** After four months, 2.8 million customers use the AI agent suite; accounting agent saves "up to twelve hours a month," payments agent gets customers paid "on average five days faster." Discovery/repeat engagement exceeds 80%. This is a genuine efficiency story with quantified customer-side ROI, not just AI marketing language.
5. **Management is telegraphing unusual conviction on tax season** — "I've never been more bullish" from a 20-year CEO — built on AI+HI concierge service design and a 50% expansion of the local office footprint (400→600 locations), explicitly framed as low-cost and asset-light.

## Economy & consumer
- **Small-business data shows stability, not strength across the board.** Payroll hours worked are up; profits/cash flows are "stable and up" — but "real estate and lending" are down "significantly" year-over-year, while IT services, construction, and manufacturing are up.
- **Consumer credit health is stabilizing after deterioration, not improving.** Near-prime/subprime credit scores "have gone down 10 plus points" over recent years but "sort of stabilized"; Gen Z credit card balances remain 20-30% higher than a couple years ago.
- **The platform is explicitly positioned as a downturn hedge.** CFO Sandeep Aujla: if the consumer comes "under pressure," they'll seek "a trusted platform" for debt consolidation — Intuit's growth thesis doesn't require a strong consumer, just an anxious one.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Mid-market and payments are the standout lines.** QBO Advanced + IES online revenue grew ~40%; total payment volume for payments/bill-pay customers grew 29%; a single large IES customer expanded from 200 to 246 entities in one quarter.
- **QBO pricing power is holding.** After July price changes, "customer attrition again came in below our expectations" — a rare direct admission of successful price-raising without churn blowback.
- **Desktop and Mailchimp are structurally declining/lagging.** Desktop guided to low-single-digit growth for fiscal 2026 with deceleration expected in H2; Mailchimp revenue fell slightly and needs a second-half turnaround to hit its own target.
- **Online services growth (17%) lags accounting growth (25%)**, inverting the historical pattern where payments/payroll outgrew accounting — CFO calls it a pricing lap, not a demand problem, but gave no numeric reacceleration target.

## Investing & scarcity
- **Capital is flowing to distribution, not core product.** Accountant-firm partnerships (Aphrio, Cherry Becker, Raymond, Cogan and Taylor), redirected sales headcount above the existing 250-person mid-market team, and a 50% expansion of tax office locations are the visible spend.
- **The binding constraint is market awareness/reach, explicitly named by the CEO**, not compute, talent, or capital — an unusual admission for a company this deep into an "AI-driven expert platform" narrative.
- **AI efficiency is funding margin, not just customer-facing features.** CFO cites AI lifting productivity across engineering, sales, customer success, finance, legal, and HR — margin outperformance versus Street consensus in H1 is attributed partly to this and partly to disciplined, tested marketing spend.

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief:** tax season will be a standout ("brightest days are ahead"). **Action:** concrete infrastructure behind it — 600 expert locations (up from 400), a flagship NYC store, AI-concierge triage before human handoff — funded at "relatively small" incremental opex per CFO.
- **Belief:** OpenAI partnership will be "game-changing" for new customer acquisition. **Action:** deep product integration into ChatGPT is live/imminent, but there's no committed metric, target user count, or revenue milestone attached — belief is running ahead of measurable commitment here.
- **Belief:** Mid-market network effects via accountants will accelerate growth. **Action:** four named accountant-firm partnerships signed with "many other opportunities of similar scale in the pipeline," plus dedicated headcount — but management is explicit this contributes to "the back half of the year and into next year," i.e., not yet in the numbers.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No usage split promised for the OpenAI deal.** When asked whether customers will use Intuit inside ChatGPT versus the native app, Goodarzi's answer was "only time will tell" — a genuine open question left unresolved rather than deflected with false precision.
- **Mailchimp's recovery path is unquantified.** CFO wouldn't attribute the double-digit exit-rate target to mid-market alone versus needing smaller-customer improvement too, pushing the real read-out to spring.
- **No magnitude given for H2 desktop or Credit Karma deceleration**, beyond directional language ("mid-single digits," "comps get harder").

## The street — what analysts asked
- Analysts converged on three anxieties: **channel risk from OpenAI** (data ownership, revenue share, cannibalization of the direct relationship), **mid-market execution** (sales productivity, headcount pace, when partnership revenue shows up), and **margin durability** (how much of the beat is AI efficiency versus one-time marketing timing shifts).
- Consumer-health questions were met head-on with granular data (payroll hours, industry breakdowns, charge volumes) rather than deflected — management came prepared for this line of questioning.
- The compressed worry: **is Intuit's mid-market and AI-agent growth durable and repeatable, or is it front-loaded by pricing actions and one-time partnership announcements that haven't yet shown up in the base numbers?**
