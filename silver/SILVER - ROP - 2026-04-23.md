# ROP — fiscal 2026-Q1 call (2026-04-23)

**The key idea:** Roper delivered a clean beat-and-raise quarter almost entirely powered by execution and buybacks rather than macro tailwinds — two of its largest sub-businesses (Deltek GovCon, DAT freight) remain explicitly un-recovered in guidance, and management is betting that a stalled M&A market (proprietary deals only, since the software valuation drawdown paused seller processes) will loosen later in the year as private-credit stress forces more assets out. The real story is the AI "accelerator team" moving from advisory mode into embedded builds, with early, hard-dollar ROI evidence (CentralReach, DAT Convoy, Vertafore) that management is using to justify continued high-conviction capital allocation toward vertical software.

## The read — 3-5 points from the whole transcript
1. **The beat was buyback-and-execution, not macro.** CFO Jason Conley conceded the DEPS raise "mainly flow[s] through the first quarter beat and then the buyback activity for the balance of the year" — core EBITDA guidance is essentially unchanged; the raise is a share-count story (102.4M shares, back to 2017 levels after $1.7B repurchased YTD).
2. **AI commercialization is bifurcating fast within the portfolio.** CentralReach and Aderant are "tip of the spear" with real agentic SKUs and revenue proof (75% of CentralReach's new bookings are AI-influenced, up from 0 two years ago); ~10-12 other companies are just now shipping past chatbot/search-level GenAI, with the rest still behind.
3. **M&A market dynamics flipped in Roper's favor, but the trigger hasn't pulled.** Public software valuation drawdown paused most active seller processes; Roper is betting maturing private-credit debt forces PE sponsors to sell "AA+ assets at differentiated values" later in the year — "timing... is still to be determined."
4. **Three businesses are explicitly NOT baked into guidance despite green shoots.** Deltek GovCon (federal procurement disruption, no OBBB benefit assumed yet), DAT freight (first carrier-count growth in years, but diesel spike hurting carrier margins), and Neptune (no recovery assumed) — all cited positively in commentary but zeroed out in the model, a conservative posture worth tracking against actuals.
5. **Input cost inflation is being absorbed via price, not surcharge, after a prior surcharge backfired.** Neptune's July 2025 tariff surcharge "had a negative demand impact" — customers said they'd "rather do it through regular way pricing" — so copper/bronze inflation (tied to data-center-driven copper demand) is now being recovered gradually through backlog repricing over multiple quarters.

## Economy & consumer
- **Freight shows a genuine but fragile inflection.** DAT's carrier-count grew for the first time in "a couple, 3 years," spot rates up 20-30% y/y, "real green shoots... particularly in the second half of the quarter" — but a diesel spike compressed carrier margins late in Q1, and management flagged prior "head fakes intra-quarter" as reason for caution.
- **GovCon remains the soft spot.** Deltek recurring revenue still grew mid-single-digit-plus off strong private-sector demand, but "continued softness in GovCon enterprise" persists from "last year's disruption to federal procurement, agency reorganizations, and broader budget uncertainty" — no OBBB benefit modeled since the money reaches Roper only after customers "win awards and invest in systems."
- **No B2C consumer read** — Roper's exposure is B2B/vertical software and industrial technology; the note omits a consumer answer as not applicable.
- **Middle East war has negligible direct effect.** Only "a small sliver" of Deltek's aerospace/defense-munitions customer base is affected, called "not material in the quarter" and "mostly U.S." exposure overall.

## The business — what's working, what's not
- **Headline metrics all beat.** Revenue +11% (organic +6%), EBITDA +8% (margin 38.1%, core margin down 70bps), FCF +11% (TTM FCF $2.5B, 19% 3-yr CAGR), DEPS $5.16 vs. $4.95-$5.00 guide.
- **Recurring software revenue is the real health signal.** Up 7% across software segments — Conley called this "the best indicator of business health and durability" — with enterprise gross retention "consistently in the mid-90s area."
- **Margin drag concentrated in two places.** Tech-Enabled Products core margin down 260bps from Neptune ingot cost inflation and NDI/Verathon's mix shift toward lower-margin, higher-recurring consumables; Network Software margin down 460bps (core down only 20bps) from Subsplash's lower starting margin profile and ongoing Convoy investment.
- **NDI hit a record quarter** on electromagnetic tracking demand in cardiac/neuro/ortho procedures — a genuine multi-year growth vector as procedure volumes and OEM catheter platforms expand.

## Investing & scarcity
- **AI accelerator team graduated from "listening tour" to embedded build partner**, starting with Vertafore (Velocity AI platform, six agents at launch) — CEO Neil Hunn cited "10X kind of productivity gains" on some joint development work, while cautioning "I don't want [the team] to hear this and think they've manifested fully."
- **Capital firepower is abundant ($5B+ over 12 months) but deployment is constrained by the deal environment**, not cash: the pipeline "leans more proprietary" after the public drawdown paused seller processes; Roper is positioning to buy when private-credit maturities force sponsor sales.
- **Refinanced a 5-year, $3.5B revolver at improved pricing** — framed explicitly as a competitive moat: "enhances our cost of capital strategic advantage in the face of an increasingly constrained private credit market" that rival acquirers face.
- **Input-cost scarcity (copper/bronze) is structural, not transient** — tied to data-center copper demand, and management's baseline assumption is that "this input cost is going to stay high for a while."

## Where they think it's going vs what they're doing about it
- **Belief: SaaS conversion accelerates from AI pull, not force.** Conley pegs the maintenance-to-cloud conversion (2/3 of products cloud-enabled, ~$1B maintenance still to convert) at "the first or second inning," worth 50-100bps of growth/year for 5-10 years; Hunn thinks AI-embedded cloud features could compress the timeline from "8-10 years" to "4-6." Action: heavy multi-year product investment already made to reach "basically feature parity" between cloud and on-prem — the SaaS pitch is now product-ready, not aspirational.
- **Belief: pricing power is "underutilized."** Hunn: expects "50 or 100 basis points over the portfolio" of incremental pricing lift over the next couple years, contingent on "earned" product value. Action: no portfolio-wide price increase — deliberately selective, business-by-business.
- **Belief: GovCon/DAT freight will eventually recover.** Action: guidance bakes in zero benefit from either — a real gap between stated optimism ("cautiously optimistic," "encouraged") and what's actually underwritten, which the company itself flags rather than hides.

## Hedges — what they wouldn't commit to
- **No single AI monetization model.** Hunn explicitly resists "one size fits all" — consumption pricing for DAT/SoftWriters where it matches customer unit economics, subscription-plus-overage elsewhere because "customers very much are saying very clearly they need to be able to plan for and budget."
- **No timing commitment on M&A pickup.** "The timing of this is to be determined" repeated twice regarding both private-credit-driven seller distress and overall pipeline reactivation.
- **Declined to underwrite any of GovCon inflection, OBBB benefit, DAT freight recovery, or Neptune recovery** into full-year guidance, despite citing positive indicators for each on the same call — deliberate conservatism rather than confidence.
